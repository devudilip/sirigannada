# Visual bible: Vijayanagara and the Deccan, 1509–1529 CE (ಕೃಷ್ಣದೇವರಾಯ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/vijayanagara-verified.md §9 (visual bible), §4 (Paes on the king, the
court, the city, the Mahanavami and the army review), §3j to §3l (Raichur, Nuniz), §6 (building), §12
(dignity). The king's face is NOT invented: it follows the Tirumala bronze group (f-tirumala-bronze),
which TTD 1930 p. 185 holds "must be expected to give a true and genuine representation of the three
royal personages", and Paes's eyewitness description of his person (f-paes-person). Reference images
(open them before every lead prompt): Wikimedia Commons "Bronze statues of King Krishnadevaraya and his
queen consorts, Chinna Devi and Tirumala Devi.jpg" (PD-India, from TTD 1930), "Annual Report of the
Archaeological Survey of India 1911-12 Plate 76, Fig 1, 2, 3 (page 352 crop).jpg" (PD-India-URAA, the
older photograph), "Metal images of Krishnaraya and his two queens.jpg" (PD, SEXC 1936), and for the
back and sides only, "Chinnadevi, Krishnadevaraya, Tirumaladevi statues at Chandragiri Museum.jpg"
(CC BY-SA 4.0, a modern replica). The cap: "the headgear which is typically like that of
Krishnadevaraya (bronze statue) at Tirupati ... a type for the wear of dignitaries like the Emperor and
his nobles" (SEXC 1936 p. 79); Federici's "long Hats on their heads called Colae" (Sewell n. 337:
KULLAYI). Every other face is an AI reconstruction, disclosed.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, early 16th-century Vijayanagara in the Deccan
(1509–1529): the granite boulder country of the Tungabhadra at Hampi, the red-black plains of the Raichur
doab, the hill forts of the east coast. Dramatic natural light, hard dry-season sun on granite, dawn
mist on the river, lamp and incense smoke in temple halls, gun smoke and dust on the battlefield; shallow
depth of field on people; high detail in fresh-cut grey and pink granite, polished pillars, crimson and
gold velvet, white muslin embroidered in gold, brocade, gilded horse plates, bronze, iron and brass.
Colour: warm grey and rose granite, dry ochre earth, deep green of palms and paddy, white and gold of
royal dress, crimson velvet, saffron, indigo, burnished gold and bronze. A living, crowded, prosperous
city, never ruins. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor,
not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

The lead must NOT repeat any earlier lead, and every prompt says so: NOT Mayurasharma (young, curly hair,
clean-shaven boy-scholar); NOT Pulikeshi (long shoulder curls, short beard, tall cylindrical kirita);
NOT Chavundaraya (square jaw, thick moustache with no beard, hair knot); NOT Amoghavarsha (long oval face,
thin moustache only, long loose hair, low diadem); NOT Vishnuvardhana (round wide-cheeked face, cropped
beard with upturned moustache, tall tiered conical crown); NOT Chikkadevaraja (long face, thin curled
moustache, small chin-beard, white turban); NOT Vikramaditya VI of Kalyana (broad high forehead,
deep-set eyes under heavy straight brows, short full beard, karanda crown); NOT Vikramaditya II of Badami
(clean-shaven HEART-SHAPED face with HIGH CHEEKBONES narrowing to a pointed chin, deep bronze-brown skin,
long straight hair with locks at the nape, low broad fan-fronted kirita). Krishnadevaraya shares only
"clean-shaven" with Vikramaditya II; every other feature must differ: a FULL SMOOTH OVAL face with a
soft rounded jaw (no prominent cheekbones, no pointed chin), HEAVY-LIDDED downcast almond eyes, a LONG
STRAIGHT nose, lighter warm wheat-gold skin, NO hair visible (all under the tall ribbed kullayi), and no
crown at all.

### LEAD: krishnadevaraya (one man at three ages)
Face type (the identity, from the Tirumala bronze, same at every age): a FULL, SMOOTH OVAL face, evenly
rounded at the jaw and chin, cheeks smooth and full but firm (not heavy, not jowly); a BROAD SMOOTH
FOREHEAD meeting the band of the cap; long, gently arched brows; HEAVY-LIDDED ALMOND EYES with large
lids, the gaze lowered and inward-looking at rest, steady and direct when he speaks; a LONG STRAIGHT
NOSE with a narrow bridge and a softly rounded tip; FULL, well-shaped lips with a faint calm smile at
the corners (the bronze's expression); CLEAN-SHAVEN, no moustache, no beard at any age. A LONG neck.
Skin: warm, light wheat-gold brown (Paes: "of fair complexion"); at most a few very faint pale marks
high on the cheeks, never visible as scarring (Paes's small-pox, optional; never disfigurement).
Build: medium height (Paes) but upright and commanding; slim, firm and athletic (the bronze; his dawn
routine of weights, sword, wrestling and riding): broad straight shoulders, flat hard stomach, strong
arms and forearms, a rider's thighs. Handsome, strong and calm at every age; never fat, never soft,
never ugly (owner rule; Paes's "rather fat than thin" is overruled by the bronze and kept to the
evidence page).
Signature headgear (every scene): the KULLAYI: a TALL CONICAL CAP, roughly one and a half times the
height of the face, with close VERTICAL RIBS or pleats running from base to top, narrowing gently to a
ROUNDED top (not a point, not a dome, not a turban), with a plain flat band at the brow. In court it is of
gold brocade covered with fine silk (Paes: "a cap of brocade in fashion like a Galician helmet");
in festival and temple scenes a richer one sewn with pearls (the kind he gave to Tirumala in 1521). It
hides all the hair. He takes it off and lays it on the ground before the god at the Mahanavami (Paes).
Dress:
- Court and city (scenes 2, 10, 11): WHITE. Paes: "clothed in certain white cloths embroidered with many
  roses in gold ... he wears a quantity of these white garments, and I always saw him so dressed".
  Rendered as a long, fitted white muslin tunic to mid-calf (the court CABAYA cut, which he gave as a
  gift with "a cap of the same fashion as the king wore"), closely embroidered with small gold roses,
  over a white lower cloth; a "pateca" of diamonds at the neck (a broad jewelled diamond collar or
  pendant); ear ornaments; gold armlets over the sleeves; BAREFOOT indoors (Paes). In ceremony a sword
  is carried, not worn.
- Temple and ritual (scenes 4, 5, 12, and the sheet's bronze view): as the bronze: BARE-CHESTED, a
  two-strand necklace with a small pendant, upper-arm bands and wrist bangles, a jewelled waist-belt with
  a hanging central ornament and fan-pleated cloth ends at the front, a close-fitting lower garment to
  the ankles, anklets, bare feet; palms joined in anjali at the chest.
- Dawn routine (scene 11 inset): a small loincloth only, oiled skin, holding earthenware weights or a
  sword; the cap off, hair bound in a small plain knot (the only scene where hair shows: short-to-medium
  black hair drawn back tight, no curls, no long locks).
- War (scenes 3, 6, 8, 9): a quilted tunic of layered leather with gilded iron plates (Paes), a gilded
  helmet with a neck-guard and face-piece worn over or in place of the kullayi (his face must stay
  readable, so the face-piece is lifted), a long straight sword, a large round shield carried by an
  attendant; on a white or grey Arab horse with a gilded forehead plate and crimson velvet housings.
Insignia: two umbrellas of state, gilded and covered with crimson velvet (Paes, the army review);
chowries; the throne "covered with silk cloths" with "lions all of gold" (Paes).

Ages:
- krishna-prince (scene 1): about 22. The same face, a little leaner in the cheek; a plain white cloth
  cap of the same tall ribbed cut, no jewels; a white upper cloth over one shoulder, a sacred thread;
  quiet, upright, unafraid.
- krishna-king (scenes 2–11): about 23 to 34 (as the sheet). The face full and smooth, the eyes heavy-
  lidded and calm; the body at its strongest (Raichur at about 33).
- krishna-elder (scene 12, only as glimpsed before the bronze or in the last-years paragraph if a second
  shot is needed): about 40 to 42. The same face, a faint line beside the mouth, a little greying at the
  temples (seen only when the cap is off, which it is not); still slim, upright and strong; never frail,
  never on a sickbed.

### timmarasu (scenes 1, 2, 5, 11; a breach in scene 12 is not shown)
Saluva Timma, "Appaji", a Brahmin minister and general; Paes calls him "an old man" in 1520. About 45 in
scene 1, 55 to 60 by scene 11. Invented face, distinct from the king's: a LONG NARROW face, a high bony
forehead, a strong aquiline nose, deep lines from nose to mouth, sharp intelligent dark eyes under grey
brows, a short close-cut white moustache only; lean and wiry, slightly stooped by scene 11 but alert.
Darker brown skin. A plain white kullayi of the same tall ribbed cut but shorter and unjewelled; a white
cabaya tunic with no embroidery, a sacred thread visible at the open neck, a white shawl; a single gold
ear-ring. Nobles make salaam to him (Paes). Shown as the king's elder and protector: never sly, never
punished on screen.

### tirumaladevi (scenes 4, 5, 12)
Senior queen, from the bronze (left of the king, the figures' own left; check against the photograph).
About 25 to 35. A full oval face with a calm, composed expression, broad brow, long eyes, a small straight
nose; warm medium-brown skin. From the bronze: hair in a HIGH ROUND HAIR-KNOT (bun) on the crown of the
head with a jewelled ornament, heavy ear ornaments, layered necklaces, a cloth across the breast and over
one shoulder, armlets, a jewelled girdle, an ankle-length lower garment falling in a fan of pleats at the
feet; palms joined in worship scenes. In life: silks of deep saffron or crimson with gold borders, a fine
veil-scarf, fully dressed by house rule. Regal and serene; slightly the elder of the two queens.

### chinnadevi (scenes 4, 5, 12)
The favourite queen, from the bronze (right of the king, the figures' own right). About 22 to 30. A
slimmer, finer face than Tirumaladevi's, a slightly pointed oval with large bright eyes and a small
smile; warm light-brown skin. The same bronze dress and high round hair-knot, the knot set a little
higher, with jasmine; silks of green or indigo with gold borders in life. A wedded queen in every image
(she performs the Ratnadhenu gift at Amaravati); never shown as a courtesan, dancer or in a harem.

### paes (scene 11; always from behind, over the shoulder, or in profile)
Domingo Paes, Portuguese, about 1520: a man of 35 to 45, short dark beard, weathered European face seen
only in profile or from behind; an early-16th-century Portuguese traveller's dress: a dark wool or
velvet doublet with slashed sleeves, a loose shirt, hose and riding boots, a short cloak, a soft flat
bonnet; a sword at the hip. He stands on the upper platform close to the king; in the review scene he
twists in the saddle to see. No ruff, no lace collar, no wide-brimmed plumed hat.

### nuniz (optional, evidence-page image only)
Fernao Nuniz, a Portuguese horse-trader who wrote about 1535: seen from behind at a lamp-lit table in a
Vijayanagara house, writing on paper with a quill, horses' bridles hanging on the wall. Never at
Raichur (he did not see it).

### figueiredo-and-gunners (scene 10; distant figures in scene 9)
Cristovao de Figueiredo: a Portuguese captain of about 40, full dark beard, an open steel cap and a
breastplate over a doublet, sword and dagger. His twenty Portuguese musketeers: bearded men in steel caps
and quilted jacks, carrying MATCHLOCK arquebuses with smouldering slow-match, powder flasks on cords,
forked rests. Smoke from muzzles. Not uniformed, not redcoats, no flintlocks, no tricorns.

### prataparudra (scene 6, distant; optional)
Prataparudra Gajapati of Orissa: a dignified middle-aged king seen only at a distance under his own
white parasol on an elephant, in white and gold; an invented face never shown close. His son
virabhadra (scene 5): a young prince of about 20, unbound, standing upright with dignity before the
king, a white upper cloth; spared, then given office. Never kneeling in humiliation, never in chains.

### adil-shah-army (scenes 8–10)
The army of Bijapur, 1520, shown as soldiers equal in dignity to the king's: Deccani cavalry in quilted
coats and long mail, pointed steel helmets with mail aventails, round shields, lances, Turkish composite
bows; heavy bronze and iron cannon on wheeled carriages with gunners; among them Portuguese renegades in
steel caps. Ismail Adil Shah seen once, far off, leaving on an elephant with Asad Khan beside him; no
face in close-up. No caricature, no sneering villains, no anachronistic Mughal court dress.

### vijayanagara-army (scenes 3, 6, 8, 9)
From Paes's review: horsemen in "quilted tunics ... made of layers of very strong raw leather, and
furnished with other iron (plates)", helmets "with borders covering the neck, and each has its piece to
protect the face"; horses "fully caparisoned", gilded or silver forehead plates, "fringes of twisted
silk", trappings of Mecca velvet, some plates shaped as "heads of serpents and of other large animals";
war elephants "completely clothed", castles with four fighters on each side, sharpened blades lashed to
the tusks, bells, "painted faces of giants" on their foreheads; shields huge, painted with flowers of
gold and silver or tigers, or black and polished like mirrors; archers with bows plated in gold and
silver; some musketeers with matchlocks; Muslim guardsmen of the king with shields, javelins and Turkish
bows. Footsoldiers in short cloths and quilted jackets.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### vijayanagara-city (scenes 1, 2, 11)
A LIVING CITY, not ruins: walls of "massive stonework" with gate towers, an outer belt of upright pointed
stones "as high as a man's breast" (Paes); inside, rice fields and orchards, gardens with groves,
"many conduits of water", tanks, palm groves; streets "lined throughout with rows of houses and shops",
houses with painted and carved decorations, crowds of every people; the giant granite boulders of Hampi
all around and the Tungabhadra running among them. The palace enclosure: walls larger than "all the
castle of Lisbon", courts and pavilions of stone and timber, halls of pillars "hung with cloths right up
to the top". Gopuras of fresh granite with brick-and-plaster towers in natural colours (no modern
whitewash).

### virupaksha-1510 (scene 2)
The Virupaksha temple at Hampi in 1510: the courtyard, the shrine door, a new pillared ranga-mandapa
going up with bamboo scaffolding and masons, a gopura before it under construction, the great outer
gopura being repaired; a stone tablet being inscribed in a corner (abstract Kannada and Sanskrit
letters, not legible).

### krishna-temple-1515 (scenes 4, 12 last beat)
The Krishna (Balakrishna) temple at Krishnapura, Hampi, new in 1515: a granite enclosure with a gopura
whose lower storeys are sharp-edged fresh stone, a pillared hall with carved piers, the shrine doorway lit
by lamps, nine-gem jewellery being offered. In scene 4 parts still under construction (scaffold on the
gopura's upper storeys). In the closing beat (today) it may be shown as it stands now, clearly as the
present day, in a single final shot.

### house-of-victory (scene 11)
The Mahanavami platform in September 1520: a tall square stone platform of several stepped tiers, its
stairs and faces carved with elephants, horses and processions, carrying on top a pavilion on pillars
"shaped like elephants", hung with crimson velvet and gold cloth; eleven velvet-covered stands around for
the nobles; the throne covered with silk with gold lions; below, the open ground for the horses, elephants,
wrestlers and dancers. Torches at night; white roses in baskets. NOT the bare stone platform of today.

### raichur-field (scenes 8, 9)
Open red-black plain before Raichur, dry scrub, a broad river with rocky banks behind the Bijapur lines
(Nuniz: they "leaped into the river"); the Vijayanagara camp of cloth tents like a city with market lanes;
cannon smoke, dust, heat. The fort on its granite hill on the horizon.

### raichur-fort (scene 10)
Massive granite walls and bastions on a rocky hill, embrasures, a great gate; heavy guns on the walls;
the town within with houses and a market.

### udayagiri (scene 3)
A steep rocky hill fort on the east, walls running along the crags, rock-cut steps and paths, a temple
inside; dry forest below.

### amaravati (scene 5)
The Amaresvara temple on the bank of the Krishna: a riverside courtyard, a great wooden balance hung on
gold chains, priests, the river broad and brown beyond.

### simhadri-potnuru (scene 6)
The coastal plain of the east: palms, paddy, sandy soil, a sea haze; a tall granite pillar being raised
with ropes and timber; the army drawn up.

### srikakulam (scene 7)
A stone mandapa of a riverside temple of the Andhra country before dawn: a lamp, a mat, the temple tower
dark against a deep blue sky with stars fading.

### tirumala (scenes 3, 12)
The hill temple at Tirumala: granite courts, the entrance gopura and its inner passage, the gilded
vimana over the sanctum (gilded from 1517–18), oil lamps and flowers. The three bronzes stand on the
inner side of the entrance gopura, north of the passage (TTD 1930 p. 185). The sanctum's image is never
shown.

### nagalapura-and-tank (scene 11)
The new town at the western entrance: walls and towers, one broad straight street lined with houses
(Nuniz: "the most beautiful street"), a tree-shaded road to the capital; south-west of it the tank works:
thousands of labourers "like ants" carrying baskets of earth up a long embankment, stone facing, lime
kilns smoking, a Portuguese mason with Vijayanagara overseers.

## PERIOD RULES
- Headgear: the tall ribbed KULLAYI for the king and nobles (shorter, plainer for nobles); cloth caps
  and bare heads for commoners; no turbans of later Mughal or Maratha type.
- Dress: the king in white muslin embroidered with gold roses; nobles in cabaya tunics with their hands
  in the sleeves before the king, the salaam with hands joined above the head; shoes "with pointed ends"
  for nobles outdoors, the king barefoot indoors; temple dress bare-chested as the bronze. Women in saris
  of the period, breast-cloths, high hair-knots with jasmine, heavy ear ornaments.
- Arms in 1520: MATCHLOCK muskets and blunderbusses (Paes's review; Figueiredo's twenty men); heavy
  CANNON on carriages (Bijapur's 400 heavy guns and 900 carriages; 200 on Raichur's walls); catapults on
  the fort; rockets possible (Ferishta). Swords, small battle-axes, javelins, spears, gold- and silver-
  plated bows, huge shields; quilted leather tunics with iron plates; helmets with neck-guards and face
  pieces. No flintlocks, no bayonets, no uniforms, no plate armour suits.
- Horses: Arab and Persian imports, with full caparisons of velvet, damask, brocade, gilded forehead
  plates, silk-fringed reins, horse-tail standards. Elephants with castles, cloth housings, bells, tusk
  blades, painted faces.
- Insignia: two crimson-velvet gilded umbrellas of state; chowries; gold-lion throne; no invented
  "Vijayanagara flag" or modern emblem banners.
- Writing: Kannada, Telugu and Tamil letters on stone and copper shown as abstract period strokes, never
  modern legible glyphs; no legible dates.
- Coins: small gold pagodas with a seated figure, no portrait of the king.
- Architecture: granite, fresh and sharp; brick-and-plaster gopura towers in natural colours; pillared
  halls; everything inhabited, clean and in use. Faiths side by side: Saiva, Vaishnava, Jain; Muslims of
  the country in the king's guard.
- Dignity: the king never fat, never in despair, never shown ordering a killing, never in a harem or
  drinking; the queens always as queens in public acts; no eyes or blinding; no sacrifice imagery; the
  enemy never caricatured.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No ruined Hampi (no fallen mandapas, no roofless halls, no bare Mahanavami Dibba, no Vitthala stone
chariot as it stands today) in any historical scene; no Talikota or 1565 imagery, no burning city; no
Tenali Rama or jester figure, no row of eight poets labelled Ashtadiggajas; no Purandaradasa at court; no
modern whitewash or painted gopuras; no flintlocks, bayonets, tricorns, ruffs or 17th-century European
dress; no Mughal or Maratha turbans and coats; no invented flags; no crown or kirita on the king, no
beard or moustache on the king; no eyes being put out, no goat's eyes, no blood in close-up, no
beheadings, no animal sacrifice, no sati; no harem or dancing-girl scenes around the king; no gods
depicted in the dream; no modern objects, no Bollywood-set look, no legible text.
