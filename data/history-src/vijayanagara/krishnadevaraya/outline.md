## Title

Recommended: **ಕೊಟ್ಟ ಉಂಗುರ, ನಿಂತ ಕಂಚು: ಕೃಷ್ಣದೇವರಾಯ** ('The Ring He Gave, the Bronze That
Stands'). Two images in the house pattern (ಕೊಟ್ಟ ಬೆರಳು, ಕಟ್ಟಿದ ನುಡಿ; ಅಳಿಸಿದ ಶಕ, ಎತ್ತಿದ ಕೊಡೆ): at
Raichur, when the first line broke, he took the ring from his finger for a page to carry to his queens
"in token of his death" (f-raichur-rally), and rode forward; at Tirumala he stands to this day in bronze
between those two queens, their names cut on their shoulders (f-tirumala-bronze). Both images are of
the king and his queens; the one is the day he expected to die, the other is how he chose to be
remembered. The recommended title is the war and the worship together, which is the book.

Teaser (en): His brother asked for his eyes; the minister showed him a she-goat's. Eleven years later at
Raichur, when the front line broke, he sent the ring from his finger to his queens and cried, 'Who
ranges himself with me?' A Portuguese visitor thought his city as large as Rome; in the poem he wrote, a
god called him 'Kannada king'. At Tirumala he still stands in bronze between his two queens, palms
joined.

## Arc

- Introduction (world): Vijayanagara in 1509, a city on the Tungabhadra more than a century and a half old; a
  third royal house, the Tuluvas, on the throne for six years; the testament of the old king naming three
  forts still unconquered (Raichur, Udayagiri, Kondavidu: f-narasa-nayaka). A younger son of Narasa Nayaka
  by his queen Nagala.
- Why (the wound and the promise): the brother's order and the minister's mercy (scene 1, Nuniz only,
  told as the story the capital told). The prince who said he wanted to be a jogi is made king, and
  Timmarasu, who "brought up the king and made him king", stands beside him for two decades. The book's
  question: what does a king do with a throne he did not ask for? His own answer, in his own verse, is
  the dharma line (scene 7), and the book lets his deeds answer it.
- How (the turn): the three forts of the testament fall one by one (Udayagiri 1514, Kondavidu 1515,
  Raichur 1520), and each victory ends not in a sack but in a gift or a mercy: the Balakrishna image
  "brought with great care", the Gajapati's son spared and made a governor, the defeated Bijapur army
  spared at the river, Raichur's citizens given back their goods. GOOSEBUMP 1 at Raichur (scene 9).
- Height (the city): Paes's eyes in 1520: the House of Victory, the white roses thrown to the horses, the
  king at dawn with oil and sword and horse, the city "as large as Rome" (scene 11).
- Ending: Tirumala, where the king and his two queens were cast in bronze, palms joined, names on their
  shoulders (c. 1517), and the Krishna temple at Hampi where the Udayagiri image was set up. Between them
  one dignified paragraph: the son born 1518, crowned at six, the father's obeisance, the boy's death;
  the breach with the old minister told in one restrained sentence; the last ride towards Raichur; the
  will naming Achyuta; the last record, 23 April 1529; death in 1529. Final beat: the bronze still stands
  where he placed it, and the Krishna temple still stands at Hampi.

Goosebump moments: (1) Raichur: "Who ranges himself with me?", the ring to the page for the queens, the
charge (f-raichur-rally); (2) at the river, the king calling his men back "out of compassion", because
"numbers had died who did not deserve death" (f-raichur-compassion); (3) the god in the dream-frame of his
own poem: "I am the Telugu king, O Kannada king!" (f-amukta-dream); (4) the closing: the crowned child
"ruling the earth" in 1524, and the bronze at Tirumala with the three names on the shoulders.

Legend rule for this book: the accession story (scene 1) is told inside the story with full arc as the
story Nuniz heard, and disclosed on the last page as Nuniz alone, c. 1535; never illustrated with eyes or
the goat's eyes. The dream at Srikakulam (scene 7) is the king's own poem: it is told as his own
account of how the poem began (record of the poem; the dream itself is literary), and the god is never
shown, only the waking king and the temple tower. Tenali Rama, the Ashtadiggajas as eight seated poets,
Purandaradasa at court, Vyasatirtha on the throne, the astrologers and the infant, the Tukka Panchakam:
not used in the story; named on the last page as later tradition.

## Content note
Adults (12+). The battle of Raichur is told plainly: guns, a broken line, men drowning in the river,
sixteen thousand dead on the king's side, the dead burned with honour. No gore in close-up. The
accession story involves an order to blind; it is told without any image of eyes. The prince's death is
told as illness. Poisoning, imprisonment and blinding (Timmarasu) appear only on the evidence page as a
disputed report.

## Dignity notes (verified file §12)

- **Scene 1, the accession.** The prince answers without fear and asks only for justice; Timmarasu's mercy
  is the beat. The dying brother is never shown; his order is reported. No image of eyes, a goat, a knife.
- **Scene 9, Raichur.** Never "despair" or "panic" in the prose; the first line broke, and the king
  rallied and charged. Sewell's "began to despair of victory" is Sewell's paraphrase and is not used. The
  order to slay the fleeing men is not in the story (evidence page only). The ring is a vow, not a
  surrender. Nobody is shown fleeing in disgrace on the king's side; the Bijapur soldiers in the river are
  men, not a mob; no caricature of the Adil Shah's army.
- **Scene 10, after Raichur.** The "kiss his foot" demand is stated plainly as the price of peace he set,
  without Sewell's moralising. The Salabat Khan execution is a story of the king deceived by Asad Khan,
  and is left to the evidence page. The Bahmani princes are treated as princes.
- **Scene 11, the king's person.** Follow the bronze: slim, strong, calm, handsome; Paes's "rather fat
  than thin" and small-pox marks on the evidence page only; marks, if at all, faint and never
  disfiguring. "Subject to sudden fits of rage" never quoted without Paes's whole sentence ("a great
  ruler and a man of much justice"). No harem: Paes's "twelve thousand women" is hearsay ("they say") and
  is not used; the queens are shown as queens in public acts (Amaravati, Tirumala, the Vitthala gates).
- **Chinnadevi.** A wedded queen: she performed a mahadana at Amaravati, stands in bronze at Tirumala,
  and is named in the records. The foreigners' 'courtesan' is on the evidence page with TTD's rejection.
- **Scene 12, the son and Timmarasu.** One paragraph, restraint: the child's crowning and death from
  illness, the father's grief, "a breach with the old minister" without accusation or punishment in the
  prose. The evidence page: Nuniz's poisoning charge, three years' prison, blinding; "Nuniz reports;
  historians doubt it" (SEXC 1936).
- **Achyuta.** Named heir by the king's own will; neutral, never Nuniz's "little honesty".
- **The Gajapati.** Prataparudra is a king who fought and made peace by marriage; his son is spared and
  serves; no fencing-match humiliation; the pillar's insult is not quoted.
- **Old age.** The king is about 42 at the end (by tradition); upright, never frail, no sickbed scene.

## Thin spots
- Nuniz is the only source for the accession story, the open-field offer, the whole Raichur narrative,
  the son's crowning ceremony and death, the Timmarasu breach, the last ride and the will. Raichur's
  chapters read like an eyewitness's, but Nuniz wrote about 1535 from the capital.
- The Raichur date is disputed (1520 Sewell; 1521 Ferishta; 1522 Nuniz; a river battle earlier in Telugu
  works).
- The Hampi Krishna temple records (ARE 25 and 26 of 1889) and the Tirumala records were read only as
  reported by Krishna Sastri and the TTD epigraphist, not in their own texts.
- The Kondavidu escalade is credited to Saluva Timma; the king's presence is not stated.
- The Orissa princess's name is unknown; the Srikakulam stay is undated (during the Kalinga campaign).
- The Hazara Rama attribution is contested and the modern study was not opened.
- The bronzes' date is c. 1517 by inference; right and left must be checked against the photograph.
- Death: year 1529 secure, day and place unknown; the Honnenahalli record unread.
- Kannada at his court is thin in verified sources: the Kannada birudas and portion of the coronation
  tablet, the Tirumala records in Kannada, Timmanna's Kannada Bharata (scholarship), 'Kannada raya' in
  his own poem. Purandaradasa and Kanakadasa cannot be placed at his court.
- No UNESCO page read (Wikidata only); the coin legend unverified; the Virupaksha 'raja-mudra' unverified.
- Birth year and age at death rest on tradition and on Sewell's arithmetic from Nuniz.
