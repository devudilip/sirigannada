# Visual bible: the founding of Vijayanagara, 1310 to 1377 CE (ಹರಿಹರ ಮತ್ತು ಬುಕ್ಕ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources (repo): data/history-src/research/vijayanagara-harihara-bukka-verified.md (cited "V§n"),
research/hoysala.md §6 (Hoysala friezes, hero stones and arms), research/vijayanagara.md §9.

What survives and what does not:
- NO portrait of Harihara I, Bukka I, Kumara Kampana or Ballala III exists: no sculpture, painting or coin
  face (Commons categories Harihara_I and Bukka_Raya_I empty, checked 2026-11-08; V§8). Every face here is
  invented and disclosed. Their coins ("HariHara I coin.png", "Bukkaraya I Coin.png", Commons, PD) are tiny
  thick gold pieces with a striding or standing figure and a short Kannada legend: props only.
- The brothers were not crowned kings: "maha-mandalesvara", "Hariyappa Vodeya" (Sewell ch. 3; EC VI Sg 1
  of 1346; EC II No. 344 of 1368 still calls Bukka mahamandalesvara). Harihara II (after 1377) is the first
  "Maharajadhiraja" (ASR 1907-08 p. 242). So NO CROWN, NO KIRITA, NO KULLAYI on either brother, ever.
- Dress and arms of the 1330s to 1370s in Karnataka: take them from the Hoysala friezes at Halebidu
  (horsemen with saddles and stirrups, infantry with round shields, war elephants; hoysala.md §6, Commons
  "KAHLB_221229_Friezes_11_rows_Brahma_Shiva_Vishnu.jpg") and from the Kannada hero stones (viragal) of
  the period, e.g. Kankaya's stone of 1342 (EC VI Kd 75, V§1d): men bare-torsoed or in a short quilted
  coat, a dhoti or short waist-cloth, heavy ear-rings, straight leaf-bladed swords, daggers, spears, round
  shields, bows. NOT the 16th-century Vijayanagara court dress of the Krishnadevaraya book (no white
  cabaya, no tall ribbed kullayi; that dress belongs to c. 1500 and later).
- Ibn Battuta on the raya of Kampili's last charge: "rubbed his body with sandal and put on his arms, but no
  shield" (Rehla p. 97; V§1c).
- Madhuravijayam (Gangadevi, c. 1370s; V§6): the Madurai Sultan's "jewelled tuft", his "fearful bow", the
  sword "hanging on the side of his horse's saddle", his "crow banner"; elephants with riders; bows and
  swords on horseback.
- Delhi armies 1310 to 1340 (hoysala.md §6): early Sultanate mail, conical steel helmets, composite bows,
  straight or only slightly curved swords; NO Mughal dress, no curved talwars, no guns. Barani: "Thirty-six
  elephants, and all the treasures" carried off (E&D III p. 203; V§1a).
- The Bahmani army of 1366 (Ferishta via Sewell; V§4d): Deccani cavalry, mail and quilted coats, pointed
  helmets; distant only. Firearms are NOT shown: Ferishta's "train of artillery" in 1366 is doubted by his own
  translator (Sewell n. 49: "Briggs ... considers it unlikely that the armies could have possessed artillery
  at so early a date"); keep every picture free of guns and cannon.
- The site (Sewell ch. 2 and 3; V§3d): "lofty hills near and far, formed almost entirely of huge tumbled
  boulders of granite", the Tungabhadra running north of them, Anegundi on the far bank. In the 1340s to
  1370s the city is NEW: rough granite walls laid between boulders, modest stone and timber buildings,
  a small old Virupaksha shrine by the river (Kp 25 "in the presence of Virupaksha"). The great Virupaksha
  gopura, the Vitthala temple, the stone chariot, the Mahanavami platform, the Lotus Mahal, the Hazara Rama
  temple and the elephant stables are ALL LATER and must never appear.
- Sringeri (EC VI Sg 1; V§3a): a matha on the Tunga river among the forested Western Ghats; the 1346 grant
  is "a stone sasana". The present Vidyashankara temple is later than 1346; show only a modest stone shrine
  and a matha of timber and tile.
- Shravanabelagola, Bhandari basti (EC II No. 344; V§5): for the evidence page only; scene 10 is set in the
  king's hall, not at the basti.
- Srirangam and Madurai (V§6d, §6g): the Kaveri, brick temple walls, coconut palms; Madurai's fort walls.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, 14th-century southern Karnataka and the Tamil country
(1310 to 1377): the granite boulder hills of the Tungabhadra at Hampi, dry ochre plains with neem and
tamarind, the forested Western Ghats at Sringeri, the palm-lined Kaveri and the plains of Madurai.
Dramatic natural light: dawn mist on the river, hard sun on granite, red dusk, firelight and oil lamps
at night; dust, smoke and spray; shallow depth of field on people; high detail in rough-cut grey and pink
granite, hand-woven cotton, quilted cloth, iron mail, bronze, gold, horse hide and elephant skin. Colour:
warm grey granite, ochre earth, deep green of palms and forest, white and indigo cotton, madder red,
saffron, dull gold. A young city being built, never ruins. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a
painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in bible/characters/)

The two leads (and Kampana) must NOT repeat any earlier lead, and every lead prompt says so. Earlier and parallel leads:
Mayurasharma (young, close-cropped, clean-shaven); Pulikeshi (long shoulder curls, short beard, tall
kirita); Chavundaraya (square jaw, thick moustache no beard, hair knot); Amoghavarsha (long oval face,
thin moustache, long loose hair); Vishnuvardhana (round face, cropped beard, tiered crown); Chikkadevaraja
(long face, thin curled moustache, small chin-beard, turban); Vikramaditya VI (broad high forehead,
deep-set eyes, short full beard); Vikramaditya II (clean-shaven heart-shaped face); Krishnadevaraya (full
smooth oval clean-shaven face, kullayi); Shivaji (long face, pointed beard); Veera Ballala II (long narrow
face, widow's peak, upcurled moustache, clean chin); Govinda III (broad face, upswept moustache, high
topknot, clean chin); Kanthirava (broad wrestler's face, looped moustache, clean chin, red turban);
Stambha and Kuvara-Lakshma (square faces, short dense beards). So: Harihara is the only lead with a
SQUARED MEDIUM BEARD on a LONG OBLONG face with the hair in a knot at the NAPE (revised 2026-11-08: the first
sheet's receding hairline and chest-length grey beard read as old and sage-like, which breaks the owner's
rule that leads are handsome and strong); Bukka is the only lead with a FULL ROUNDED MEDIUM-LENGTH BLACK
BEARD together with LONG LOOSE WAVY HAIR under a RED CLOTH FILLET and no crown.

### LEAD 1: harihara (sheet: bible/characters/harihara-sheet.png)
Face type (same at every age): a handsome long OBLONG face with high cheekbones and a strong straight jaw
under the beard; a FULL head of thick black hair (never balding or receding) with a few silver strands at the
temples, combed straight back into a firm knot at the NAPE; calm, commanding dark eyes under strong level
black brows; a long AQUILINE nose; a wide, thin-lipped, closed mouth; a neatly groomed SQUARED black beard of
medium length (a few fingers below the chin, never chest-length) with a few grey threads at the chin, joined
to a full black moustache (no curled tips); warm dark brown skin; heavy plain gold ear-rings. Build: TALL and
powerfully built, broad straight shoulders, a strong deep chest and muscular arms, flat stomach; a lion in
his prime, never frail, never fat, never old-looking. Handsome, grave, commanding; the planner.
Dress: bare torso with a white cotton upper cloth over the left shoulder, a sacred thread NOT worn (unknown
caste; do not show), a white dhoti with an indigo border to mid-calf, a dark red cloth sash, a straight
sword in a plain scabbard; in council a white cotton shawl; in war a quilted indigo coat to mid-thigh.
NO crown, NO turban, NO cap.
Ages: about 35 (scene 3), about 40 (scene 4), about 45 (cover, scene 6, the sheet),
about 58 (scene 7: hair and beard grey, still upright, strong and handsome).

### LEAD 2: bukka (sheet: bible/characters/bukka-sheet.png)
Face type (same at every age): a BROAD, OPEN face with full firm cheeks and wide cheekbones, a strong
rounded chin under the beard; WIDE-SET, LARGE, warm, laughing dark eyes with laugh lines; thick slightly
arched black brows; a BROAD nose with a slight wrestler's bend at the bridge; full lips that smile easily;
a FULL, THICK, WAVY BLACK BEARD of medium length (about a hand's width below the chin), rounded in shape,
with a broad moustache merging into it (no curled tips, no points); LONG, LOOSE, WAVY BLACK HAIR falling
to the shoulders, held off the brow by a TWISTED MADDER-RED CLOTH FILLET; a thin old pale scar along the
right cheekbone; warm medium-brown skin; gold hoop ear-rings. Build: powerful and deep-chested, broad
shoulders, thick strong arms and neck, a warrior-wrestler's body, hard muscle, never fat, a little shorter
than Harihara. Handsome, warm, fierce in battle.
Dress: bare-chested with a gold chain, gold armlets, a madder-red upper cloth, a white dhoti with a red
border, a broad cloth sash with a straight sword and a dagger; in war a quilted madder-red coat with iron
plates sewn on the chest and a round hide shield; in court (scenes 8, 10) a fine white upper cloth and
several gold necklaces. NO crown, NO turban, NO kullayi.
Ages: about 25 (scene 3), about 30 (scene 4), about 35 (cover), about 40 (scene 6, the sheet),
about 48 (scene 7), about 52 (scene 8), about 58 to 60 (scenes 9 and 10: grey at the temples and
streaks in the beard), about 66 (scene 12: hair and beard mostly white, still broad and upright).

### kampana (sheet: bible/characters/kampana-sheet.png; scenes 8 and 11)
Kumara Kampana, Bukka's son, about 25 in scene 8 and 35 in scene 11. Invented. Face type (distinct from
every earlier lead and from his father and uncle): a LEAN, ANGULAR face tapering from his father's wide cheekbones to a NARROW, firm chin; DEEP-SET, narrow, heavy-lidded dark archer's eyes under straight black brows; a STRAIGHT, NARROW, high-bridged nose; a thin DROOPING black moustache whose ends fall to the jawline, with a narrow neat CHIN-STRAP BEARD along the line of the jaw and a bare upper cheek; a small pale notch-scar through the outer end of the LEFT eyebrow; straight black hair drawn back and tied LOW in a long tail at the nape with a gold-thread cord; medium-brown skin; small gold ear studs.
Build: tall, lean and long-armed like an archer, flat-muscled, broad across the shoulders, never fat; handsome, calm, intent. War dress: a quilted saffron coat with mail at
the shoulders, a round-topped steel helmet with a cloth wrap (face open), a long bamboo bow, a quiver, a
straight sword on the saddle; a bay horse. From scene 8 on he wears a heavy gold necklace of large round gold beads with an oval gold pendant set with a red stone (his father's, put on him in scene 8).
Brave, calm, never cruel.

### ballala (no sheet; scene 5)
Ballala III, the last Hoysala king, about 80. Invented: tall and upright, lean and sinewy; long white hair
in a knot at the crown bound with a gold band (no tall crown in camp); a white moustache, clean-shaven
chin; deep lines, bright fierce eyes; dark brown skin; heavy gold ear-rings, a sacred thread (Amir Khusrau in E&D III p. 89, checked
2026-11-08 in archive.org historyofindiaas03elli: Ballala "was ready to give up all he possessed, except his
sacred thread"), a white dhoti with a gold border, a white upper cloth; a straight sword. Old
but never frail, never pitiable.

### kampili-raya (no sheet; scene 2)
The raya of Kampili, about 50. Invented: strong, broad-shouldered; a greying close-cut beard; hair in a
knot at the crown; his whole body smeared pale yellow with sandal paste (Rehla p. 97); bare torso, a white
dhoti hitched for fighting, a cloth sash; a straight sword and a dagger; NO shield. His men the same,
sandal-smeared, spears and swords, no shields.

### younger-brothers (scenes 3, 6)
The other three sons of Sangama, about 20 to 40: lean, strong Kannada men, two with short beards and one
clean-shaven with a moustache, hair knotted at the nape, white dhotis and coloured upper cloths, swords.
Plainly the brothers of the leads, never more prominent.

### sringeri-guru (scene 6)
The guru of Sringeri, about 60: head and face clean-shaven (a sannyasi), a saffron robe and upper cloth, a
bamboo staff (danda), rudraksha beads; serene. Seen from the side or three-quarter; his face is secondary.

### madurai-sultan (scene 11)
The last Sultan of Madurai, about 45. Invented, dignified and fierce: a trimmed black beard, an aquiline
nose, a steel helmet with a JEWELLED PLUME/TUFT at the front (MV concl. v. 23), mail coat over a quilted
green silk coat, a composite bow strung and drawn, a sword at the saddle (v. 32), a fast grey horse;
behind him a black banner with a CROW (v. 30), NOT a crescent. Brave, never mocked, never cowering.

### delhi-army (scene 1)
Early Delhi Sultanate cavalry of 1310 to 1330: riders in mail shirts over quilted coats, CONICAL steel
helmets with mail neck-guards, composite bows in cases, lances, straight or slightly curved swords; war
elephants with mahouts carrying treasure chests; no faces in close-up. Soldiers, not monsters.

### bahmani-army (scene 9, distant fires only)
The Gulbarga army of 1366: a camp of tents and hundreds of cooking fires across the plain at dusk; horsemen
in mail and pointed helmets in silhouette. No guns, no cannon.

### vijayanagara-army (scenes 9, 11)
Kannada and Telugu soldiers of the 1360s: archers in white and indigo cotton, quilted coats, round hide
shields, spears, straight swords, long bamboo bows; horsemen in quilted coats with iron plates, saddles and
stirrups; war elephants with riders.

### jain-elder and srivaishnava-acharya (scene 10)
Jain elder: a lay Jain merchant-elder of about 65, white hair and short white beard, white cotton dhoti and
upper cloth, no sect mark, a calm lined face. Srivaishnava acharya: about 55, shaven head with a small tuft,
the white U-shaped namam with a red central line on the forehead, a white dhoti and upper cloth, a sacred
thread. Around them: Jain householders in white; Srivaishnava acharyas and temple servants with namams;
working men and women of the Holeya and Madiga communities in plain cotton, standing among them as equals.

### harihara-ii (scene 12)
Bukka's son, about 25: tall, broad-shouldered, a short dark moustache and close beard, hair in a knot at
the nape, a white upper cloth with gold necklaces; he stands beside his father, listening.

### boys (scene 1)
A youth of about 15 and four younger boys of about 4 to 12, seen FROM BEHIND only, silhouetted on a boulder, in short white waist-cloths.

### workers and citizens (scenes 4, 12)
Stone-cutters with iron wedges and hammers, men hauling granite on wooden sledges with pairs of humped
white bullocks, women carrying water pots; plain cotton cloths.

## PERIOD RULES
- No crowns on the brothers; no kullayi, no white cabaya, no turban on them.
- No firearms, no cannon, no gunpowder smoke anywhere.
- No gopurams or tall temple towers at Hampi; small early shrines only. No Vitthala, stone chariot,
  Mahanavami platform, Lotus Mahal.
- No crescent on any flag; banners are plain cloth, or the Madurai crow banner in scene 11.
- Stirrups are correct (Halebidu friezes).
- Kannada lettering on stone is never legible.
- No gore in close-up, no severed head shown, no corpses in the foreground, no women in the fire.
- No king (including the Sultans and Ballala) humiliated, kneeling in disgrace, fleeing or mocked.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
Exclusions: No crowns, kiritas, kullayi caps or turbans on Harihara or Bukka; no 16th-century white court
cabaya; no Mughal dress, no Rajput or Maratha turbans; no firearms, cannon, matchlocks or gun smoke; no
tall gopurams, no Vitthala temple, no stone chariot, no Mahanavami platform, no ruins; no crescent or
star on any flag; no Hoysala soapstone temple at Hampi; no gore, no blood in close-up, no severed heads,
no corpses in the foreground, no women in a fire; no king or sultan kneeling, fleeing, mocked or
humiliated; no modern objects, roads, wires or signboards; no legible text.
