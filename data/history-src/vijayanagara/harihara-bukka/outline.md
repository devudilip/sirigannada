## The spine (one line)

ದಕ್ಷಿಣದ ಹಳೆಯ ರಾಜ್ಯಗಳು ಒಂದೊಂದಾಗಿ ಬೀಳುವುದನ್ನು ಕಂಡ ಸಂಗಮನ ಮಕ್ಕಳು ಬೀಳದ ರಾಜ್ಯವೊಂದನ್ನು ಕಟ್ಟಲು ಬಯಸುತ್ತಾರೆ;
ಹರಿಹರ ಅದನ್ನು ಕಡಲಿಂದ ಕಡಲಿಗೆ ಹರಡಿ ತಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ಒಪ್ಪಿಸುತ್ತಾನೆ; ಬುಕ್ಕ ಯುದ್ಧ ಸೋತರೂ ನಗರವನ್ನು ಹಿಡಿದು, ತನ್ನದೇ ಜನ
ಒಬ್ಬರ ಎದುರು ಒಬ್ಬರು ನಿಂತಾಗ ಅವರ ಕೈಗಳನ್ನು ಜೋಡಿಸಿ "ಭೇದವಿಲ್ಲ, ವೈಷ್ಣವರೂ ಜೈನರೂ ಒಂದೇ" ಎನ್ನುತ್ತಾನೆ, ಮಗ ಕಂಪಣ ದಕ್ಷಿಣದ ತುದಿಯವರೆಗೆ
ಗೆಲ್ಲುತ್ತಾನೆ; ಅದಕ್ಕಾಗಿಯೇ ಚಕ್ರವರ್ತಿಯ ಬಿರುದು ತೊಡದ ಆ ಅಣ್ಣತಮ್ಮಂದಿರ ರಾಜ್ಯ ಎರಡು ಶತಮಾನಕ್ಕೂ ಹೆಚ್ಚು ಕಾಲ ನಿಂತಿತು.

(en) Watching the old kingdoms of the south fall one by one, the sons of Sangama set out to build one that
would not fall; Harihara spreads it from sea to sea and hands it to his brother; Bukka loses a battle but
holds the city, and when his own people stand against each other he joins their hands and declares that there is no difference between them, while his son Kampana carries the kingdom to the far south; which is why the realm of brothers
who never took an emperor's title stood for more than two centuries.

## Title

**ಎರಡು ಕಡಲು, ಭೇದವಿಲ್ಲದ ನಾಡು: ಹರಿಹರ ಮತ್ತು ಬುಕ್ಕ** ('Two Seas, No Difference'). Both halves are the stones' own
words: Harihara's Sringeri stone (1346, "from the eastern to the western ocean") and Bukka's Shravanabelagola stone
(1368, Kannada text "ಭೇದವಿಲ್ಲವೆಂದು", "ವೊಂದು ಭೇದವಾಗಿ ಕಾಣಲಾಗದು"). The earlier title "ಒಂದೇ ದೇಹ" was dropped at review:
"(body)" is the 1923 translator's gloss and is not on the stone.
Subtitle: ವಿಜಯನಗರ ಕಟ್ಟಿದ ಅಣ್ಣತಮ್ಮಂದಿರು, ಕ್ರಿ.ಶ. ೧೪ನೇ ಶತಮಾನ (no 1336: the founding year is disputed).

## Dignity

Ballala reaches for his horse to fight, never pleads. The raya of Kampili dies with his face forward.
The Gulbarga Sultan holds Bukka to the payment of the draft ("a light word" must not be recorded of him), is moved by the envoys and swears mercy; his wine (Ferishta) is left out. Afif's mockery of the Madurai Sultan is left out. The Madurai
Sultan rides out himself, and in the poem his body still raises its hand; he is never mocked. Bukka's own
side is not spared: the Mudkal killings and the violence done to the women of Madurai are told plainly.
Bukka's defeat is told as a defeat, including his submission and the payment of the very draft he had sent back on an ass.
