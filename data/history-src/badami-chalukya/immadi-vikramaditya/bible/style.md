# Visual bible: Vatapi, Pattadakal and Kanchi, 8th century CE (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ವಿಕ್ರಮಾದಿತ್ಯ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/badami-chalukya-vikramaditya2-verified.md §9 (portrait, dress, arms,
Kailasanatha as of c. 735) and research/badami-chalukya.md §6 (architecture, emblem, dress). Dated
witnesses for dress and ornament: the Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna reliefs at Pattadakal (c. 740s),
e.g. Commons File:8th_century_mithuna_reliefs_at_Virupaksha_temple,_Pattadakal_Hindu_monuments_Karnataka_1.jpg
and File:HBPA_N_747_Relief_Roof_Entrance_Virupaksha_Pattadakal.jpg; Badami Cave III (578) as the earlier
analogue. The dress reading of each relief is `scholarship`: check per image.

No portrait, coin portrait or named sculpture of Vikramaditya II, of his queens or of Gunda is known (verified
file §9a; badami-chalukya.md §6.5: "No portrait of any Badami Chalukya king survives"). Every face in this
book is an AI reconstruction and the book says so.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, 8th-century Deccan and Tamil country, India (c. 730–745).
Dramatic natural light, volumetric haze, dust, torch smoke, river mist and sea spray where the scene calls
for them, shallow depth of field on people, high detail in cloth, skin, carved sandstone and metal. Colour:
red and buff Malaprabha sandstone, the grey-pink sandstone of Kanchi, dark river water, ochre and white
cotton, silk in madder red and indigo, dull gold, rubies, grey elephant hide, unpolished iron. No text,
captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

### vikramaditya (LEAD; scenes 1–12, cover)
Locked to the approved character sheet (video folder bible/characters/vikramaditya2-sheet.png); every
prompt says "FIRST open the file bible/characters/vikramaditya2-sheet.png and match this man exactly".
No real likeness exists; the face is an invented, consistent AI reconstruction, matched in dress, crown and
ornament to the Pattadakal reliefs of his own reign.
Handsome, strong and heroic: tall and long-limbed with wide straight shoulders and the lean, hard muscle of a
rider and spearman (NOT bulky, NOT heavy). Deep bronze-brown skin. A heart-shaped face: broad, high
cheekbones narrowing to a firm, slightly cleft chin; a wide clear forehead; large wide-set dark eyes under
heavy straight brows; a short straight nose with a gently rounded tip; a full, calm mouth. Fully clean-shaven,
no moustache, no beard (he is the only clean-shaven adult king among the leads). Long straight black hair,
drawn up and back under the crown, with a few straight locks falling behind the ears to the nape (straight,
never curly). Heavy round gold ear-rings (kundala) stretching the lobes, a broad jewelled collar and one
long pearl string, armlets with a leaf-shaped point, wrist-rings, sacred thread, bare chest with a narrow
silk sash crossing from the left shoulder, a patterned silk lower garment to mid-calf with a jewelled waist
belt and a sash tied at the hip with hanging ends (as on the Virupaksha mithuna reliefs).
Crown: a jewelled kirita LOWER AND BROADER than Pulikeshi's tall cylinder, a gold band at the brow with a
raised fan-shaped front plate set with rubies and a small rounded top (follow the crowned figures on the
Virupaksha reliefs; NOT a tall cylinder, NOT a tiered cone, NOT a turban).
Signature details that must read in every image: clean-shaven heart-shaped face with high cheekbones; the
broad low fan-fronted crown; a single large ruby pendant on the pearl string (the Kanchi rubies, f-trophies).
Ages: scene 1 = late 20s (no crown, a gold fillet band only; Yuvaraja); scenes 2–9 = 30–38 (as the sheet);
scenes 10–12 = about 40–45, a few grey hairs at the temples, still upright and vigorous. Expression: calm,
measuring, restrained; a quiet smile only in scenes 7 and 10.
On campaign: the crown replaced by a gold-banded cloth headwrap tied flat (not a Mughal or Maratha turban),
a quilted cotton jacket over the bare chest at most, a straight broad sword, a long spear, a round shield;
rides a war elephant under a white umbrella with the boar banner, or a horse with a cloth saddle and no
stirrups.
Distinct from the earlier leads, say so in every prompt: NOT Mayurasharma (young, curly hair,
clean-shaven boy-scholar look), NOT Pulikeshi (long curls, short beard, tall cylindrical crown), NOT
Chavundaraya (square jaw, thick moustache, hair knot), NOT Amoghavarsha (long oval face, thin moustache,
loose hair, diadem), NOT Vishnuvardhana (round face, cropped beard, tiered crown), NOT Chikkadevaraja (long
face, thin curled moustache, chin beard, white turban). Vikramaditya II is the mature clean-shaven king with
the heart-shaped face, high cheekbones and the low fan-fronted crown.

### kirtivarman (son; scenes 10, 12)
Age about 18–22 in scene 10. Clearly his father's son: the same heart-shaped face and high cheekbones, but
a slimmer, sharper jaw, a first faint moustache, longer hair tied at the back with a gold band; no crown (a
gold fillet as Yuvaraja), bare chest, armlets, a straight sword at the hip. Eager, respectful, proud.

### lokamahadevi (queen; scenes 11–12)
Senior queen, "of the race of the Haihayas", about 35. Invented face: oval face with a broad forehead, long
dark eyes, a small straight nose, medium-brown skin; a calm, commanding presence. Hair in a large low knot
at the nape wound with jasmine and a jewelled hair ornament; a small diadem band; heavy ear ornaments, layered
necklaces, armlets, bangles, a jewelled girdle over a silk lower garment, a fine silk upper cloth over one
shoulder, as on the Virupaksha mithuna reliefs (dressed modestly; no bare-breasted staging). Carries no weapon.
Patron's gestures: receiving, giving, pointing at the work.

### trailokyamahadevi (queen; scene 12)
Lokamahadevi's uterine younger sister, mother of Kirtivarman, about 30. The family likeness (same long
dark eyes) but a rounder face and softer chin, hair dressed high in a crown-knot with a gold ornament, indigo
silk upper cloth to distinguish her from her sister's madder red. Warm, quick.

### gunda (sutradhari; scene 11)
The master builder, "Tribhuvanacharya". Invented face: about 50, compact and wiry, sun-darkened brown skin,
a lined square face, close-cropped grey-black hair, a short grey beard, strong scarred hands. Plain white
dhoti, a cotton upper cloth, a measuring cord coiled at his shoulder (sutradhari = "holder of the thread")
and a palm-leaf plan. In the honour scene a cloth fillet of honour (patta) tied around his head. Dignified,
not servile.

### avanijanasraya-pulakeshin (scene 9)
Chalukya prince of Lata (south Gujarat), about 40. Invented face: long jaw, hooked nose, full black
moustache, hair tied in a top-knot under a gold-banded cloth headwrap; quilted cotton battle jacket, straight
sword, round shield, on a war elephant or horse with cloth saddle. Shown as a hero in his own right.

### nandipotavarman (Pallava king; scene 4, far off in scene 5)
Pallava king of Kanchi. His age is NOT recorded (the modern "13-year-old boy" is unverified): show a young
adult, face partly hidden by distance and dust. Tall Pallava crown, bare chest with armlets, straight sword,
on a war elephant; the Pallava bull banner. Dignified: he came out to meet the enemy in the open field; he
withdraws in good order, never shown fleeing in panic, captured or humiliated.

### tajika-army (scene 9)
Per the Navsari plate only: quick horses raising dust that darkens the sky; riders "vomiting forth arrows and
mudgaras" (maces), "very sharp swords", "coats of mail". Plain mail shirts, simple rounded or conical iron
helmets, lances, maces, short bows, swords; plain cloth under the mail; horses with cloth or felt saddles and
NO stirrups shown prominently. No modern Arab dress (no keffiyeh, no agal cord, no white thobe), no
crescent banners, no Ottoman or Mughal costume, no curved scimitars, no faces made sinister. They are a
formidable army, not caricatures.

### chalukya-army (scenes 4, 8, 9)
Bare-chested or quilted-jacket spearmen with round shields and straight swords; archers; war elephants with
cloth housings, bells and drivers with goads; drums; the boar banner. Chiefs with gold armlets.

### anivaritapunyavallabha (scribe; scene 7)
Court scribe, about 45: lean, shaven head with a tuft, white dhoti and upper cloth, a palm-leaf draft in hand,
directing a Kanchi mason who cuts the letters.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### pattadakal (scenes 1, 11, 12)
Kisuvolal on the Malaprabha: the river and its sandy bank, low hills, a group of red and buff sandstone
temples. Scene 1: the Vijayesvara (Sangameshvara) under construction with timber scaffolds, rope hoists,
earth ramps and masons; scene 11: the Lokesvara (Virupaksha) nearing completion with its east gateway;
scene 12: Lokesvara and Trailokyesvara (Mallikarjuna form) side by side, complete. Model on Commons
File:Virupaksha_Temple,_Pattadakal,_Karnataka.jpg and File:8th_century_Mallikarjuna_temple,_Pattadakal_monuments_Karnataka.jpg,
but shown fresh and complete (sharp carving, plastered and painted details allowed, no ruin, no modern
paths, fences, signboards or lawns). NO later Jain temple of the group, no Papanatha emphasis.

### vatapi (scenes 2, 10)
Badami: two horns of red sandstone cliff over the green Agastya lake, the cave temples in the south cliff,
the citadel on the north hill, a brick and timber town between. Early structural shrines on the north hill
may appear in the background. NO Adil Shahi walls, gun bastions, domes or mosques.

### vatapi-court (scenes 2, 10)
A timber pillared hall with square sandstone pillars and bracket figures, a low wooden throne under a white
umbrella, oil lamps, cloth hangings with the boar emblem; courtiers standing.

### lakshmeshwar (scene 3)
Pulikara: a Jain temple (the white Jinalaya) with whitewashed walls in a walled court, a small town of brick
and thatch, black-soil fields; the king's camp of cloth tents nearby.

### tondaimandalam-plain (scene 4)
Flat red-earth plain with palmyra palms, scrub and a tank bund; dust; two armies.

### chalukya-camp (scene 5)
Night, torches, open-sided cloth tent; trophies on display: a great conch on a stand, a long horn, a
staff-banner topped with a skull-club (khatvanga), rubies on a cloth, elephants tethered behind.

### kanchi (scene 6)
A large city in the plain: streets of brick and timber houses with tiled or thatched roofs, a town gate in a
brick-and-earth wall, temple towers of modest height on the skyline. NO towering gopurams (those are
Vijayanagara and later), no white-plastered Dravida skyline.

### kailasanatha-735 (scenes 7, 12)
The Rajasimhesvara (Kailasanatha) of Narasimhapotavarman as it stood c. 735: a low sandstone temple in an
enclosure of small shrine cells, the main shrine with its pyramidal tower, a pillared mandapa in front of the
shrine (Hultzsch: the Kannada record is on the back of one of its pillars). The later mandapa between the
shrine and the front mandapa does NOT exist yet and must NOT be drawn. Keep the entrance frontage modest (its
dating was not checked). Model on Commons File:Kanchi_Kailasanathar_Temple_at_Kanchipuram,_Tamil_Nadu_01.jpg
and File:Kailasanathar_Temple._Kanchipuram,_TamilNadu.jpg, minus later additions. In scene 12 the pillar
is shown as today: the letters in shadow behind a later wall, lamplight.

### southern-shore (scene 8)
A wide unnamed beach on the southern ocean at dawn, surf, pearl-oyster shells on wet sand, a plain tall
stone pillar being raised by ropes and timber shear-legs. No named temple, no Rameswaram.

### navsari-field (scene 9)
South Gujarat near the coast: flat alluvial plain, scrub and palms, dust storm from horsemen, a small river
behind; a fortified town (brick walls, timber gate) in the distance. No sea-fort, no ships.

## PERIOD RULES
- Weapons: straight double-edged swords, spears, bows, round shields, elephant goads, maces (mudgara) on
  the Tajika side; daggers. No curved talwars or scimitars on the Indian side; no firearms of any kind.
- Armour: Indian side bare-chested or in quilted cotton jackets; mail ("coats of mail") only on the Tajika
  army, per the plate. No plate armour.
- Elephants lead the Indian armies (cloth housings, bells, a driver with goad, a howdah-less pad or a simple
  railing for the king). Horses small and fine, cloth saddles; stirrups NOT shown (follow the Pulikeshi
  bible: "stirrups not shown"; the question is open and the image must not decide it).
- Crowns: low broad fan-fronted kirita for Vikramaditya II; tall Pallava crown for Nandipotavarman; no
  crown on the Yuvaraja (fillet only); diadem band and hair ornaments for the queens.
- Banners and emblems: the Chalukya boar (varaha), standing; the palidhvaja; the Ganga and Yamuna signs and
  the kettle-drum as Chalukya insignia; the Pallava bull and the captured khatvanga club-banner on the Pallava
  side. No lion, no gandabherunda, no garuda banner for Chalukyas, no crescent for the Tajikas.
- Writing: Old Kannada letters for the Kanchi pillar and the Pattadakal gateway records; Sanskrit charters in
  the southern alphabet on copper plates strung on a ring with a boar seal; the 754 pillar in local script and
  Nagari. Letters rounded and ancient, never modern Kannada, Tamil or Devanagari glyphs; shown as abstract
  strokes, never legible text.
- Religion: Saiva temples (linga, Nandi) at Pattadakal and Kanchi; the Jain temple at Lakshmeshwar shown with
  respect (seated Jina, monks in white). No mocking of any faith.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No gunpowder, cannon or firearms, no loopholed battlements, no Mughal, Maratha, Vijayanagara or Rajput dress,
no turbans on Chalukya royalty, no curved swords on Indian soldiers, no stirrups, no towering gopurams at
Kanchi, no later mandapa in the Kailasanatha, no ruined or roofless Pattadakal temples, no modern Arab dress,
keffiyeh or crescent banners, no lion, garuda or gandabherunda emblems, no modern script on stone or copper,
no modern objects, paths, fences or signboards. No gore: wounds and death implied, never in close-up; no
headless bodies, no executions, no captives in chains.
