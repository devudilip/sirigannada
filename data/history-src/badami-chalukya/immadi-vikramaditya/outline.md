## The arc (owner rule: introduction, why, how, ending)

- INTRODUCTION (scenes 1–2): the world. A dynasty at its height under a father who reigned more than thirty-four
  years and built at Pattadakal; the old wound of 642 still cut in Pallava letters on a Badami rock.
- WHY (scenes 2–3): the coronation resolve against the "natural enemy" who "robbed of their splendour the
  previous kings born from his race". A revenge story is set up, so the reader expects fire.
- HOW (scenes 4–10): speed (the march "with great speed"), courage in the open field, and then the
  reversal that is the story's heart: he enters the enemy capital without destroying it, inspects the
  treasure of the enemy's royal temple and gives it back, and has that act cut in Kannada in the temple.
  The southern sweep and the victory pillar on the ocean; the far frontier held at Navsari by a kinsman
  whom he honours with four names; the son who asks to be sent and comes home with gold for his father.
- ENDING (scenes 11–12): the queens turn the campaigns into stone at Pattadakal: Lokamahadevi's Lokesvara,
  with Gunda honoured as Tribhuvanacharya, and her sister's Trailokyesvara. One dignified paragraph only for
  the reign's end and the dynasty's fall (from the victors' own records, which still praise the Karnataka
  army); the last image returns to the Kannada letters on the Kanchi pillar, still there behind a later wall.

Goosebump moments: scene 6 (the gates open and nothing burns), scene 7 (the gold handed back and the
Kannada letters cut in the Pallava temple), scene 9 (the Navsari oath, "today we have paid for one birth
our debt to our lord"), scene 12 (closing on the pillar).

## ಪೀಠಿಕೆ

Vatapi and Pattadakal in the 730s: the red sandstone of the Malaprabha valley, a dynasty two centuries old,
a new temple of Vijayesvara rising at Pattadakal (Kisuvolal, "of the coronation"). To the south-east, Kanchi
of the Pallavas, the city whose king had broken Vatapi ninety years before. Sources in one breath: his
son's copper plates (two sets, the same words), a Kannada pillar inside the Pallava temple, the queens'
gateway inscriptions at Pattadakal, a Gujarat grant about an Arab army. What the reader will feel: a revenge
that ended in restraint, and two sisters who built it into stone. Map image: Vatapi/Pattadakal, Lakshmeshwar,
the Tondaimandalam and Kanchi, the southern ocean, Navsari in Lata far to the north-west.

## Scenes that risk showing the king badly, and how to avoid it

- Scene 2 (the vow): "uproot completely" can read as bloodlust. Tell it as a duty to the family's honour
  after the 642 wound; let scene 6 answer it. Never show him gloating at the Pallava rock.
- Scenes 4–5 (battle, trophies): the Pallava came out to meet him in the field; say so, to the Pallava's
  credit. Trophies are shown as objects, not as humiliation of captives. No prisoners, no executions.
- Scene 6 (Kanchi): the risk is the opposite, overclaiming virtue. Say only what is recorded: not destroyed,
  gifts to the twice-born, the distressed, the helpless. No "not a single house was touched".
- Scene 7: do not use the Pattadakal pillar's "bruiser of Kanchi" (vimardana) in the prose; it is on the
  evidence page with Fleet's own note against it.
- Scene 8: *pratapita* "scorched" is a metaphor of royal heat; do not show burning villages.
- Scene 9 (Navsari): the glory belongs to Avanijanasraya; do not put the emperor on that field or make the
  vassal a mere servant. The emperor's part is the honour. The Arab army is drawn only from the plate (mail,
  lances, maces, swift horses), never as a caricature; no "saved India", no religious framing. The plate's
  "headless trunks dancing" is not used, in text or image.
- Scene 10: the king is a proud father in his prime, not old or failing; the son lays the spoils at his feet.
- Scene 12: no death scene for the king; Kirtivarman is not shown defeated or humiliated; the fall is told
  from the enemy's records with their own praise of the Karnataka army; end on the Kanchi pillar.
- Kuram's "covered only by a rag" belongs to Vikramaditya I, not this king: never let the two blur.

## Where the verified file is thin (write carefully)

- Kanchi entry and the treasure scene: only the charter formula and the pillar's one sentence. No
  description of the city, people, priests or ceremony.
- The Pallava side: no Pallava record was opened; Nandipotavarman's age, exile, return are unverified.
- The southern sweep: four dynasties named, nothing else (no battle, place, date, king).
- The ocean pillar: no place, no surviving pillar.
- Navsari: the emperor's role is only the honour; no meeting, court or date of the honour; the overlord is
  unnamed on the plate.
- Order and dates of events: none of Kanchi, the south, the son's expedition or the temples is dated.
- The queens: Lokamahadevi's father unnamed; no marriage date; no year for either temple; their motives are
  not recorded beyond the "three times" line.
- Gunda: name, honours and titles only; whether the Sarvasiddhi titles are his is Fleet's reading.
- The king's last years and death: nothing.
- The look of the king: no portrait exists (bible/style.md).

## ಸಾರಾಂಶ (ten lines)

A father's long reign and a temple at Pattadakal; the 642 wound; anointed 733/734 with a resolve to uproot
the Pallava; a Jain gift at Lakshmeshwar in 735; the march into Tondaimandalam and Nandipotavarman put to
flight; Kanchi entered without destroying it; the Rajasimhesvara's riches given back and the act written in
Kannada in the Pallava temple; the southern kings and a victory pillar on the ocean; the Arab army stopped at
Navsari by a Chalukya kinsman whom he honoured; his son sent to Kanchi in turn; the queens' Lokesvara and
Trailokyesvara at Pattadakal, the architect Gunda honoured; the reign's end about 745 and, a decade later,
the end of the dynasty.

## ಇಂದು ನೋಡಬಹುದು
