# Visual bible: Vatapi and the Deccan, 7th century CE (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ಪುಲಿಕೇಶಿ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/badami-chalukya.md §6 and -verified.md §7–8. Dated witnesses for dress
and ornament: Badami Cave III (578), Aihole Ravanaphadi and Meguti, Pattadakal reliefs (c. 740) as
a late analogue, Ajanta paintings (5th c.) for court furniture and ships.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, 7th-century Deccan India. Dramatic natural light,
volumetric haze, dust, smoke and spray effects where the scene calls for them, shallow depth of field
on people, high detail in cloth, skin and stone. Colour: red Badami sandstone, dark lake water, ochre
and white cotton, dull gold, grey elephant hide, unpolished iron. No text, captions, logos or
watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

### pulikeshi-boy (scene 1)
Age 10–12. Dark brown skin, shoulder-length black curls, bare chest, white dhoti, a small gold
necklace, no crown. Alert, watchful.

### pulikeshi-prince (scenes 2–3 before crowning)
Age 20–25. Same face grown: dark brown skin, strong jaw, black curls to the shoulder, short beard,
bare chest, ochre dhoti with a patterned border, sacred thread, gold earrings, armlets; a straight
double-edged sword. No crown in exile.

### pulikeshi-king (scenes 3 onward, cover)
Age 30–45. Same face. Tall cylindrical jewelled kirita crown of the Badami Cave III type, heavy
earrings, pearl necklaces, armlets, bare chest with a cloth sash across it, patterned dhoti.
On campaign he rides an elephant under a white umbrella, with the boar banner. Expression: calm,
measuring. No turban, no Mughal dress, no Vijayanagara-style conical crown.

### mangalesha
Age 50–60. The uncle. Same family look, heavier, grey in the beard, kirita crown, a Vishnu-devotee's
vertical forehead mark, seated on a low wooden throne.

### harsha
Age 40. Emperor of Kanauj, north Indian: lighter brown skin, clean-shaven or light moustache, long
hair in a top-knot with a jewelled band, white and gold Gupta-style dhoti and shawl, pearl strings,
on a war elephant under a white umbrella; his banner plain (no emblem attested in this story).

### narasimhavarman
Age 30–35. Pallava king of Kanchi, Mahamalla. Tall crown of the Pallava Mamallapuram type, bare
chest, armlets, a straight sword; fierce.

### xuanzang
Age 40. Chinese Buddhist monk: shaven head, East Asian face, ochre and brown patched robes, a
back-frame of scrolls, a palm-leaf bundle and stylus. Calm, observant.

### ravikirti
Age 45. Jain court poet, white dhoti and uttariya, shaven head, holding a palm leaf; at the Meguti
temple wall with a mason.

### vikramaditya
Age 30. Pulikeshi's son: same family face, kirita crown, on a grey horse of a fine breed with a
cloth saddle and no stirrups, sword drawn, at the head of a column.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### vatapi (scenes 1, 3, 11, 12)
Badami: two horns of red sandstone cliff over a green lake (Agastya tirtha), four rock-cut cave
temples in the south cliff with pillared verandahs, a citadel of bare rock on the north hill reached
by boulder gorges and rock-cut steps, a brick and timber town between. NO loopholed battlements,
NO gun bastions, NO mosque, NO Bhutanatha-type towers in the foreground (later), NO Pattadakal.

### badami-court (scenes 1–2)
Inside Cave III's verandah or a timber pillared hall: massive square pillars with bracket figures,
a low wooden throne, oil lamps, cloth hangings with the boar emblem.

### banavasi (scene 4)
Moated brick city on the Varada bend with water birds; vast camp around it (as in the Mayura book).

### puri-harbour (scene 5)
A laterite rock headland fort on the Konkan coast at dawn, sea mist, a bay full of ships. Ships per
Ajanta Cave 2: three masts with oblong sails, a square jib on a bowsprit, high curved stem and
stern, twin quarter steering-oars, painted eyes on the bow, awnings amidships; oars on the smaller
craft. No stern rudder, no lateen, no outriggers, no cannon, no European hull forms.

### harsha-plain (scene 6)
A north Indian plain, the Ganga far off, a moving army of thousands of elephants, cavalry and foot
under white umbrellas and plain banners; dust.

### narmada-bank (scene 7)
A wide river with sandbanks, forested Vindhya hills behind, dusk; elephants on the sand, two hosts
across the water. No named battle structures.

### kaveri-crossing (scene 9)
A broad river in spate; a line of war elephants wading, the current piling against them.

### aihole-meguti (scene 10)
A flat-roofed sandstone Jain temple on a low hill above a village; a mason cutting letters on its
outer wall; Ravikirti with a palm leaf.

### mallikarjuna-rock (scene 11)
A boulder below the red cliffs on the lake's edge; a Pallava scribe cutting Grantha letters; smoke
in the distance.

## PERIOD RULES
- Weapons: straight double-edged swords, spears, bows, round shields, elephant goads. No curved
  talwars, no mail or plate, no helmets.
- Elephants dominate every war scene; horses are small with cloth saddles; stirrups not shown.
- Crowns: tall kirita on Chalukya and Pallava royalty; Harsha in Gupta-style jewelled headband.
- Banners: the Chalukya boar (varaha) with sun and moon; Pallava bull is allowed for Narasimhavarman's
  side; no lion, no gandabherunda, no Vijayanagara emblems.
- Writing: palm leaf with stylus; inscriptions in 7th-century southern Brahmi or Pallava Grantha
  (rounded ancient strokes, never modern Kannada or Tamil glyphs); copper plates on a ring.
- Buildings: red sandstone caves and small early temples, brick and timber town; no gopurams.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No gunpowder or cannon, no loopholed battlements, no Mughal, Maratha or Vijayanagara dress or
crowns, no turbans on Chalukya royalty, no curved swords, no chain mail, no helmets, no stirrups, no
European or Arab ship forms, no modern objects, no modern script on stone, no lion or gandabherunda
emblems, no Ajanta "Persian embassy" staging. No gore: wounds and death implied, never in close-up.
