## ಪೀಠಿಕೆ
Vatapi in the 6th century: a red sandstone fort on a lake, a dynasty forty years old. The Aihole stone,
cut in Pulikeshi's own lifetime (634) by the Jain poet Ravikirti, and a Chinese pilgrim who described
the same king from the other side. What the reader will feel: an exile who returned, stopped the
emperor of the north, and whose end nobody wrote down.

## ಸಾರಾಂಶ (ten lines)
Birth into a forty-year-old dynasty; uncle's usurpation; exile and return (c. 610); north and south
secured; the fleet at Puri; Harsha stopped; lord of three Maharashtrakas; Vengi to his brother; Kanchi
and the Kaveri; Aihole 634 and Xuanzang; the Pallava revenge c. 642 and the unrecorded death; the son's
recovery.

## ಇದು ಕಥೆ, ಇದು ಇತಿಹಾಸ (dramatised, by scene)
Boyhood in Vatapi (no record of his childhood); the council scene and companions in exile; where he
went in exile; the coronation scene; the look of Puri and the fleet (only "hundreds of ships" is
recorded); the Narmada as the site and every detail of the battle; Xuanzang never says he met the
king; the Persian letter's name is a restoration; the sack of Vatapi has no description; the death
is unrecorded; all dialogue.

## ಇಂದು ನೋಡಬಹುದು
Badami caves and fort, Agastya lake; Aihole Meguti temple and the inscription; Pattadakal; the
Mahamalla rock behind the Mallikarjuna temple at Badami; Kuram plates (SII I); Beal's Si-yu-ki online.
