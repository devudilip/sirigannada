# Visual bible: Kuntala and Kanchi, 4th century CE (ಮಯೂರಶರ್ಮ)

Every image prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and
the EXCLUSIONS. Sources for each rule are in research/kadamba.md §6 and research/kadamba-verified.md.
No Kadamba-period figural sculpture survives, so dress and arms follow the nearest dated neighbours:
Ajanta Vakataka paintings (5th c.) and Badami Cave III reliefs (578). They are period analogues,
disclosed as such in the book's illustration note.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, 4th-century Deccan India. Dramatic natural light,
volumetric haze, dust and smoke effects where the scene calls for them, shallow depth of field on
people, high detail in cloth and skin. Colour: laterite red earth, deep monsoon green, ochre cloth,
unpolished iron, dull gold. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon,
not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

### mayura-young (scenes 1–5)
Age 18–22. Dark brown skin, lean, shaven head with a small tuft (shikha) at the crown, no beard.
White cotton dhoti to the ankle, a plain white uttariya over the left shoulder, sacred thread,
no jewellery, barefoot. Carries a palm-leaf bundle or a wooden ladle. Expression: alert, proud,
quick to anger.

### mayura-king (scenes 6–9)
Same face ten to twenty years older, close-cropped hair grown out, short beard. Ochre dhoti,
a cloth sash, a plain cloth fillet (patta) tied around the forehead from scene 8 on, never a
metal crown. Simple gold earrings and one armlet after scene 8. Straight double-edged iron sword
with a round pommel, round hide shield. Still barefoot or in simple leather sandals.

### virasharma
Age 60+. Grey-white hair and beard, sacred thread, white dhoti and uttariya, a staff, a water
pot. Grandfather and teacher of Mayura. Gentle, slow.

### pallava-horseman
Age 30. Bare-chested, cloth turban-wrap (not a Mughal turban), short dhoti, iron sword, on a
small Deccan horse with a cloth saddle and no stirrups. Arrogant posture.

### pallava-king
Age 40–50. Tall crown (kirita) of the Badami/Ajanta type, heavy earrings, necklaces, armlets,
bare chest, patterned dhoti. Seated on a low wooden throne under a cloth canopy.

### kubja (scene 11)
The poet, age 40, with a visibly bent back (his name means hunchback), white dhoti, a stylus
and a palm leaf; a mason with a chisel beside him.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### kuntala-homestead (scene 1)
Western Ghats edge, Shimoga country. A thatched, mud-walled Brahmin house with a clean swept
yard, a square brick fire altar (vedi), kusa grass bundles, wooden ladles, earthen pots. A large
kadamba tree (Neolamarckia cadamba) in orange-ball bloom beside the house. Forest and mist behind.

### kanchi-4th-century (scenes 2–4, 8)
A large brick-and-timber city with a mud rampart and a moat, tiled and thatched roofs, wooden
pillared halls, a bustling market. NO stone temples, NO gopurams, NO Kailasanatha: nothing of
stone stood in Kanchi before c. 600 CE. The ghatika: a pillared timber hall where Brahmins recite,
palm-leaf manuscripts on low tables.

### sriparvata-forest (scene 6)
Dense deciduous forest on the Nallamala hills along the Krishna river gorge toward Srisailam,
a wooden frontier stockade, rock outcrops.

### rough-country-night (scene 7)
Broken hill country, boulders, a Pallava camp with cloth tents, picketed horses and a few
elephants, campfires. Monsoon night, torches.

### chandravalli-tank (scene 9)
A flat granite boulder beside a tank below the Chitradurga hills; a mason cutting Brahmi letters.

### talagunda-pillar (scene 11)
A small early stone shrine (square sanctum, no tower) on a brick base, a large tank beside it,
an octagonal grey-granite pillar on a square pedestal being inscribed; coconut and areca groves.

### banavasi-siege (scene 12)
A moated brick city on a bend of the Varada river with white egrets/swans on the water,
encircled by a vast camped army with elephants (this is the Chalukya army, c. 7th century;
use Badami-period dress for them).

## PERIOD RULES
- Weapons: straight iron swords, spears, bows, round hide shields, elephant goads. No curved
  talwars, no mail, no plate, no helmets with crests.
- Horses: small, cloth saddle, no stirrups.
- Writing: palm leaf with stylus; stone in box-headed southern Brahmi (the Talagunda ink
  impression is the model), never modern Kannada glyphs; copper plates on a ring with a seal.
- Buildings: brick, timber, thatch, mud ramparts; small early stone shrines without towers.
- Crowns: tall kirita only for kings in court; Mayura gets a cloth fillet.
- Banners: NO lion, NO gandabherunda, NO boar for the Kadambas (no early emblem is attested).
  Plain coloured cloth banners only.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No stone temples at Kanchi, no gopurams, no Hoysala or Vijayanagara architecture, no Badami caves
in Kadamba scenes, no lion or gandabherunda emblem, no metal crown on Mayurasharma, no stirrups,
no curved swords, no chain mail, no turbans of Mughal or Maratha type, no gunpowder, no modern
Kannada script on stone, no third eye, no film costumes. No gore: wounds and death implied, not
shown in close-up.
