# Visual bible: Belavadi and the Sampgaon plain, January–February 1678 (ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/belavadi-mallamma-verified.md §9 (Commons files checked through the API)
and §4 (the place), research/belavadi-mallamma.md §8 (dignity table) and §9 (visual bible), the Belgaum
Gazetteer (BG XXI, 1884) pp. 112 and 131 on local dress, the two c. 1680 Golconda-school portraits of
Shivaji on Commons (opened and looked at 2026-10-20), and mysuru-verified.md §13c (which lists only
those two portraits for the Maratha side; it names no period painting of Maratha soldiers). Nothing of
Belavadi fort survives and no portrait of Mallamma exists: the fort, the village and every face except
Shivaji's are reconstructions, disclosed as AI reconstruction.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, the Deccan plain of north Karnataka near Belagavi in
the dry winter of 1678 (January–February). Clear cold mornings with low mist over the fields, hard
bright afternoons, long golden evenings; red-soil dust in the air, cooking and camp smoke drifting flat
over the plain, torchlight at night. High detail in mud plaster, rubble walls, red earth, black soil
patches, harvested jowar stubble and cotton, handloom cotton, dull gold, blackened iron, worn leather.
Colour: red-ochre earth, straw gold, indigo and maroon cloth, white cotton, the pale winter sky. Shallow
depth of field on people. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not
watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

### mallamma (lead; scenes 1–12)
Locked to the approved character sheet (video folder
~/projects/ideas/sirigannda/history-video/veera-naariyaru/belavadi-mallamma/bible/characters/mallamma-sheet.png);
every prompt says "FIRST open the file bible/characters/mallamma-sheet.png and match this woman
exactly". The sheet shows her four times (front, three-quarter, profile, full length with bow); this
block describes exactly that sheet.
A Kannada woman of the Deccan plateau, about thirty (the tradition gives her an infant son; the
uncited 1624 birth year is NOT used). Tall, upright, strong, with a rider's and archer's shoulders and
strong forearms; beautiful, dignified, commanding; never fragile, never sexualised. Face: an OVAL face
with a STRONG JAW and HIGH CHEEKBONES, LARGE DARK EYES under STRAIGHT BROWS with a steady direct gaze,
a STRAIGHT NOSE with a small GOLD NOSE STUD on the left nostril, full calm lips, DARK BROWN skin. Hair:
long and black, drawn back into a FIRM KNOT AT THE NAPE, no loose hair (BG XXI p. 131: hair "in a knot
behind the head"). Marks: a level streak of WHITE ASH across the forehead with a small red KUMKUM dot
between the brows (BG XXI p. 131: "a level streak of white ashes", the Veerashaiva sect mark). At the
throat a small SILVER LINGA BOX (ಕರಡಿಗೆ) on a black cord (BG XXI p. 131).
Dress (the sheet): a nine-yard cotton sari in DEEP INDIGO with a narrow red border, worn in the warrior
way (ವೀರಗಚ್ಚೆ), drawn between the legs and tucked tight at the back so it falls in trouser-like folds
(BG XXI p. 112; the Yadwad figure as Nayak describes it), the end wound tight across the chest and over
the left shoulder; a short-sleeved fitted MAROON BODICE (ಕುಪ್ಪಸ); a wide striped red cloth SASH at the
waist holding a straight DAGGER in a brass-mounted scabbard on her right hip; plain gold ear studs, a
few gold and black-bead necklaces with the mangalsutra, gold bangles on both wrists; bare feet. Arms: a
long RECURVED BOW with thread-bound grip and tips, a leather QUIVER of arrows on the back with the strap
across the chest. A grey or bay horse with a padded saddle cloth when mounted (scenes 1, 9).
Widow's marks (the tradition widows her in scene 5): scenes 1–5 as the sheet (bangles, mangalsutra;
jasmine in the knot for scene 2 only); from scene 6 on, PLAIN WRISTS, no mangalsutra, no flowers, the
ash and the linga box stay; the kumkum dot stays (the sources do not settle widow's practice for her
community; disclosed as a choice). No theatrical grief make-up.
Ages: scene 1 = about 16 (same face, softer, slimmer, hair in a long plait, a simple cotton sari in the
same warrior tuck, no mangalsutra, no nose stud); scenes 2–12 = about 30, as the sheet.
NO crown, NO tiara, NO regalia of any kind: a Desai's wife wore jewellery, not a crown.
Distinct from every earlier lead, say so in every prompt: every earlier lead was a MAN (Mayurasharma,
Pulikeshi, Chavundaraya, Amoghavarsha, Vishnuvardhana, Chikkadevaraja, Vikramaditya VI, Vikramaditya II,
Krishnadevaraya); she shares nothing with them: a woman's oval face with a strong jaw, dark brown skin,
a gold nose stud, a hair knot at the nape, ash and kumkum, indigo sari in veeragachche. NOT the Kittur
Chennamma statue or its iconography (1824), NOT the modern Belavadi statue, NOT a Rajasthani
ghagra-choli, NOT a Mughal dress, NOT armour, NOT a modern sari drape with a long stitched blouse, NOT
ugly, NOT fat, NOT old, NOT a victim pose.

### shivaji (scenes 3, 10, 11; far off in 6, 7)
Matched to the two c. 1680 Golconda-school portraits on Commons, opened and looked at:
File:Shivaji British Museum.jpg (British Museum 1974,0617,0.11.12; 1680–1687; public domain) and
File:Shivaji Rijksmuseum.jpg (Rijksmuseum RP-T-1972-25; c. 1680; CC BY-SA 4.0), both bust portraits in
profile within an oval. What they show, and what every prompt must match:
- Face: a long face with a high forehead, a LONG STRAIGHT NOSE with a slightly hooked tip, a LARGE
  watchful almond eye under an arched brow, light wheat-brown skin, lines under the eye of a mature man.
- Beard and hair: a FULL DARK BEARD along the jaw TRIMMED TO A POINT below the chin, a full moustache
  joining it (lightly upturned in the Rijksmuseum portrait), and long dark hair falling as thick locks
  in front of the ear down to the jaw. NOT a squared beard.
- Turban: a ROUNDED, closely wound turban, sitting high and tilted back: olive-green with fine stripes
  in the British Museum portrait, green and gold striped in the Rijksmuseum one; on its front a
  jewelled ornament (sarpech) carrying a BLACK PLUME, with a hanging tassel of pearls (British Museum)
  or a gold tassel (Rijksmuseum) at the side. NOT a tall cone, NOT a crown.
- Dress: a long WHITE JAMA with a crossed, embroidered collar, patterned all over (small red flower
  sprigs with fine yellow stripes in the British Museum portrait; a gold lozenge pattern in the
  Rijksmuseum one); several long STRINGS OF PEARLS; a SHAWL over the shoulders (dark grey-black,
  British Museum; orange with gold borders, Rijksmuseum); a dark patterned waist sash (patka).
- Arms: a straight sword held upright by the hilt in front of him, point down, its basket-shaped gilt
  guard visible; at the waist a gilt gauntlet-hilted weapon (pata type) with an animal-head guard
  (shown in both portraits; its exact type is this pass's reading).
- Bearing: composed, grave, intelligent, kingly. Always dignified; never cruel, sneering or gloating;
  never fat.
Riding dress for scene 3: the same face, turban and beard, a quilted cotton coat over the jama, the
sword at the hip, a round shield on the back; on a strong horse with a cloth housing, an attendant
with a tall parasol nearby (the Yadwad rider as Nayak describes it: sword, shield, umbrella and staffs).
Scene 11: standing, palms joined, head inclined to Mallamma (the bow of honour); then seated on a low
rug with her infant on his lap and a cup in his hand. NEVER kneeling, NEVER at her feet, NEVER fleeing,
NEVER begging, NEVER shown giving an order to seize cattle or women, NEVER shown at a punishment.
Note: the existing shivaji-sheet.prompt.txt in the video folder asks for a "cone-shaped turban
(jiretop)" and a "beard trimmed close and squared under the chin"; both disagree with the two
portraits (rounded wound turban with a black plume; beard trimmed to a point). Rewrite that prompt from
this block before rendering the sheet.

### ishaprabhu (scenes 2, 4, 5)
Desai of Belavadi (tradition only). Invented face, distinct from Shivaji (who has a full pointed beard
and long side locks) and from Mallamma: about 35, broad-shouldered and tall, a BROAD SQUARE face, a
thick black MOUSTACHE and NO BEARD, short hair under a turban, warm brown skin, a calm proud look.
Veerashaiva marks: a level streak of white ash on the forehead, a silver linga box on a cord at the
chest (BG XXI p. 131). Dress: a white headscarf-turban (rumal) wound with a red band, a white
knee-length cotton coat or shirt with a shoulder cloth, a waistcloth, a cloth sash with a sword (BG XXI
p. 131 for the men's waistcloth, headscarf, shirt and shouldercloth); riding, a quilted coat. Scene 5:
carried wounded on a shield, eyes closed; no wound shown.

### sadashiva (her brother; scene 1 only, as a role, unnamed in the prose)

### sakhoji-gaikwad (scenes 3, 10, 11)
A Maratha commander (a Sakhoji Gaikwad led a force in this campaign in 1676, Mehendale). Invented

behind (scene 11). NEVER shown with Mallamma, NEVER shown doing the deed, NEVER shown being punished or
blinded, no close-up of his eyes.

### siddanagowda (scene 4)

grey-black moustache, no beard, ash streak, a white rumal turban, white shirt and shouldercloth, a

### fort-women (scenes 6, 7, 9, 10)
The women of Belavadi: varied ages, village women of the plain in short-sleeved bodices and cotton

### maratha-army (scenes 3–11)
Light horse and foot of 1678. NO period painting of Maratha soldiers was verified (mysuru-verified.md

### transport-bullocks (scenes 3, 5)
Strings of humped grey-white Deccani bullocks with gunny or cotton packs lashed on wooden pack-saddles,

### english-clerk and messengers (scene 8)

collar, writing with a quill. Two Maratha messengers in travel-stained cotton, seated on a mat. The
English dress of the 1670s is from general knowledge, NOT from a source in the research files:
UNVERIFIED; keep it plain and avoid any period-costume flourish (no wig, no lace, no tricorn).

## SETTINGS (locked)

### belavadi-fort (scenes 2, 4–10)
A small plains fort (ಗಡಿ, the Shivadigvijay's "Bhuikot"; Sarkar's "petty mud-fort"): low walls of
earth and rubble with mud plaster, perhaps a stone-faced gateway, four ROUND CORNER BASTIONS, a dry
ditch; a heavy wooden gate studded with iron spikes; a narrow wall-walk with simple parapets and arrow
slits. Inside: the Desai's two-storey wada of mud, timber and clay tiles with a pillared veranda,
granaries, a stone-lined well, the Veerabhadra shrine, a small open court. The walls are modest in
height: a man on the parapet is clearly visible from the plain. A reconstruction (dossier §9b); use
File:Kittur fort.jpeg (CC0) only as a guide to stone-and-mud building on the same plain, never as
Belavadi (Kittur's fort is 18th–19th century).

### belavadi-village and plain (scenes 1–5, 12)
Mud-walled houses with clay-tiled roofs (BG XXI p. 131), cattle pens of thorn and rails (the play's
ತುರುಹಟ್ಟಿ), red-soil fields of harvested jowar and cotton (BG XXI p. 236: red soils in the Belavadi
group), low ridges, neem and tamarind; the Malaprabha to the north with a wide sandy bed and a thin
winter stream (File:River Malaprabha.jpg, CC BY-SA 4.0, "near Bailhongal"). For a Bijapur-era building
on the plain if needed: File:Sampagaun Masjid Bailhongal.jpg (CC BY-SA 4.0).

### sode (scene 1)
Malnad forest country of Uttara Kannada: tall evergreen and teak, morning mist, a clearing with a
straw target. No Sode palace is shown (none verified).

### siege-camp and trenches (scenes 3, 7, 9, 10)
A Maratha camp on the open plain: small cotton tents in clusters, horse lines, transport bullocks,
cooking fires, a larger plain tent for the king with a cloth awning and side walls; zig-zag siege
trenches and earth mounds creeping towards the fort walls (the Tarikh-i-Shivaji's "siege trenches").
NO cannon batteries, no gabions of the European kind.

### rajapur-factory (scene 8)
A plain room in the English Company's house at Rajapur on the Konkan coast: whitewashed walls, a heavy
wooden table, an oil lamp, a ledger and loose letter sheets, a quill and an inkpot, a chest; wooden
shutters closed against the night; the sound of the sea implied by a glimpse of palms through a gap.
Reconstruction; the building's form is UNVERIFIED.

### yadwad-camp (scene 11)
An open camp on the plain about 10 km north of Dharwad: a cloth awning on poles over a low rug, a tall
parasol behind the king (the Yadwad slab as described), officers and Belavadi women standing, horses
beyond. Morning light.

### veerabhadra-shrine (scenes 2, 6, 12)
A small old stone temple: a plain square sanctum with a modest stepped tower, a pillared porch, oil
lamps; inside, a standing image of Veerabhadra with sword and shield seen dimly. The Belavadi temple's
real form is UNVERIFIED (no photograph found); keep it modest and regional. Regional guide only:
File:Godachi Bhadrakali Veerabhadreshwara Temple.png (Belagavi district, Commons), a modern photograph
of a different temple, not to be copied.

### belavadi-today (scene 12)
A present-day village of Bailhongal taluk at evening: red-soil fields, tiled and concrete houses, the
small old temple among them, cattle coming home, a dirt road. No statue (the modern Mallamma statue was
not seen), no signboards with legible text.

## PERIOD RULES
- Gunpowder exists but is minor in this siege: a few matchlocks on each side; no cannon batteries, no
  rockets, no flintlocks, no bayonets. Most fighting is bow, spear, sword and horse.
- The fort is mud and rubble, not dressed granite; no palace, no stone citadel.
- Dress: Belavadi people in Kannada village and Veerashaiva dress (BG XXI pp. 112, 131); Shivaji after
  the c. 1680 portraits; Maratha soldiers as the reconstruction above. Bijapur court dress, if any
  Adil Shahi figure is needed, after File:"Sultan Ali Adil Shah II of Bijapur with a Woman" 1660-75
  David coll.jpg (PD).
- Marks: the level ash streak for Veerashaiva men and women; linga boxes at the neck or upper arm.
- The Yadwad slab, if shown in scene 12, is a dark arched stone with two worn carved panels, smeared
  with ash stripes and kumkum from worship (as seen in the low-resolution Commons photograph); never a
  sharp, legible relief, never presented as a portrait made in 1678.
- Hero-stone carving style for any memorial stone: File:Hero stone with old Kannada inscription from
  the late Vijayanagara period.JPG (CC BY-SA 3.0, Bangalore Museum).
