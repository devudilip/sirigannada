# ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ: working outline (book and screenplay share this)

Series `veera-naariyaru` (ವೀರ ನಾರಿಯರು), story 1. Built to README §6 (storytelling template, 2026-10-20)
and the owner's direction for this story: embrace her as a hero; the fort became Shivaji's, the victory
was hers; Shivaji is never a villain, never fleeing or kneeling; the bow is honour, not surrender;
Mallamma is never a victim; no assault depicted or described; the blinding off-screen and attributed to
the chronicle on the evidence page.

Source base (the only one): research/belavadi-mallamma.md and research/belavadi-mallamma-verified.md
(V§n). Facts: facts.json (43 ids: 3 `inscription`, 28 `scholarship`, 12 `legend`). There is no
inscription and no eyewitness of the siege. The one contemporary record is the Rajapur letter of
28 February 1678 (f-rajapur-letter), hearsay from Shivaji's own men. The Maratha chronicles run from
1694 (Sabhasad) to 1810 (Chitnis); the Kannada tradition, the Samarotsava and the 1951 play are all
`legend`. This is a story where the record gives the frame (a woman Desai, a mud fort, 27 days, the
disgrace, the fall, the release) and the tradition gives the people (ಈಶಪ್ರಭು, the cows, the women,
the honour, the milk). The book tells both with confidence and the last page sorts them.

(en) Mallamma, the Sode girl who became the lady of Belavadi, wants her people's cattle and honour
respected; in the way stands Shivaji's army, the conqueror of so many kingdoms, camped on her plain;
her mud fort holds for twenty-seven days until his own men call it his worst disgrace; it costs her
her husband and her fort; and at the end the victor bows to her courage, punishes the man who wronged
her, honours her as a mother and gives her land back, which is why, three and a half centuries on, the
Veerabhadra temple at Belavadi and a carved stone at Yadwad still keep her name.

## Title

Recommended: **ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೇಳು ದಿನ, ಒಂದು ಬಟ್ಟಲು ಹಾಲು: ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ**
('Twenty-Seven Days and a Bowl of Milk'). Two images in the house pattern (ಕೊಟ್ಟ ಉಂಗುರ, ನಿಂತ ಕಂಚು):
the first is the record, her stand (f-27-days, f-rajapur-letter); the second is the tradition's own
monument, the king with her child on his lap and a cup of milk (f-milk, f-yadwad-slab). Together they
say what the owner wants said: she held him, and he honoured her, and neither of them is on their
knees. It names no defeat and no surrender, so it is fair to both, and it carries the goosebump
(twenty-seven days) on the shelf card.

## Age: 12+

Why 12+ and not 16+: the war is told plainly (Ishaprabhu falls in the first clash, men die at the
trenches) but without gore; there is no massacre, no execution, no mutilation on the page. The one
dark item in the sources, the Tarikh-i-Shivaji's 'greatly dishonoured' and the blinding of Sakhoji, is
handled by the dignity rule: in the prose Sakhoji only 'forgot the respect owed to a captive lady'
and is sent away under guard to prison; nothing is depicted or described; the blinding appears once,
in one plain sentence, on the evidence page, attributed to the chronicle (f-ts-sakhoji). Why not
below 12: a husband's death in battle, a captured woman wronged (however vaguely put), and the
need to read the evidence page with its three versions and its 'dishonoured'.

### 1. scene-01 ಸೋದೆಯ ಮಗಳು (The Daughter of Sode)
- **What happens.** The land: the red plain south of the Malaprabha, jowar and cotton, a small free
  state of villages under a mud fort at Belavadi, its Desais worshipping Veerabhadra; to the north
  and east, the great sultanate of Bijapur, old and failing. Then the girl: in the forest country of
  Sode, the ruler's daughter learns the bow, the sword and the horse beside her brother, a Veerashaiva
  girl with the linga box at her throat.
- **Facts.** f-belavadi-place, f-fort, f-veerabhadra, f-sonda-rulers, f-sode-daughter.
- **Dramatised (disclose).** Her parentage, her brother and her training are the tradition's; the
  Sode rulers are real but no source makes her their daughter. The training scene (a target in a
  forest clearing) is invented. Her caste is shown as the tradition gives it; the evidence page notes
  the 'Prabhu' reading of the Marathi texts.
- **Ends on.** ಕಾಡಿನ ಸೋದೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಬಿಲ್ಲು ಹಿಡಿದ ಈ ಹುಡುಗಿ ಬಯಲಿನ ಬೆಳವಡಿಗೆ ಹೇಗೆ ಬಂದಾಳು? (How would a
  girl with a bow in forest Sode come to the plain of Belavadi?)
- **Picture.** Morning in a Malnad clearing below tall trees: a girl of about sixteen drawing a
  recurved bow, an older brother on horseback watching, mist between the trunks.
- **Dignity.** Young, strong, focused; not a 'princess' in silks; no crown.

### 2. scene-02 ಬೆಳವಡಿಯ ಒಡತಿ (The Lady of Belavadi)
- **What happens.** She marries ಈಶಪ್ರಭು, the Desai of Belavadi, and comes to the mud fort on the plain;
  she rides the villages with him, sits beside him at the gate where disputes are heard, worships at
  the Veerabhadra shrine; a son is born. In ೧೬೭೪ her brother at Sode stops calling himself Nayaka and
  takes the title Raja, because Bijapur can no longer hold its chiefs. The small houses of the plain
  are free, and exposed.
- **Facts.** f-sode-daughter, f-ishaprabhu, f-sonda-rulers, f-woman-desai, f-veerabhadra, f-fort.
- **Dramatised.** The marriage, the husband's name, the son, the shared judging at the gate, the
  ride through the villages; all tradition or invention. 1674 and the title are record (Kanara
  Gazetteer); her being his sister is not.
- **Ends on.** ವಿಜಾಪುರದ ಹಿಡಿತ ಸಡಿಲವಾಗುತ್ತಿದ್ದ ಈ ಬಯಲಿಗೆ ಮುಂದೆ ಯಾರ ಕುದುರೆಗಳು ಬರಲಿವೆ? (With Bijapur's
  grip loosening, whose horses would come down onto this plain next?)
- **Picture.** The Belavadi gadhi at evening: mud walls and round bastions, the Desai's tiled wada
  inside, Mallamma and ಈಶಪ್ರಭು at the gate with village elders, cattle coming home in the dust.
- **Dignity.** A ruling couple, equals; she wears the married woman's bangles, mangalsutra and
  flowers here (removed in scene 6).

### 3. scene-03 ದಕ್ಷಿಣದಿಂದ ಬಂದ ದಂಡು (The Army from the South)
- **What happens.** ೧೬೭೮ರ ಜನವರಿ. Shivaji's army, coming home from the long southern campaign by Kopal
  and Lakshmeshwar, turns north after failing before Bankapur and spreads its camp over the Sampgaon
  plain: horse lines, cooking smoke, strings of transport bullocks. Shivaji himself is with it. His
  rules are read in the camp: no women in the army, no women or children taken, no cows taken,
  bullocks for transport only. Among his commanders is ಸಖೋಜಿ ಗಾಯಕವಾಡ. From the bastions of Belavadi
  the camp fires can be counted at night.
- **Facts.** f-route-south, f-shivaji-present, f-army-rules, f-sakhoji-officer, f-siege-date.
- **Dramatised.** The rules being read out in the camp (the rules are Sabhasad's; their reading here
  is invented); Sakhoji's presence in this camp (a Sakhoji commanded in the same campaign in 1676; his
  presence at Belavadi rests on the Tarikh-i-Shivaji); the camp fires seen from Belavadi.
- **Ends on.** ರಾಜನ ನಿಯಮ ಹಸುವನ್ನು ಮುಟ್ಟಬಾರದು ಎನ್ನುತ್ತಿತ್ತು. ಇಷ್ಟು ದೊಡ್ಡ ಬಿಡಾರದ ಪ್ರತಿಯೊಬ್ಬನೂ ಅದನ್ನು
  ಪಾಲಿಸುವನೇ? (The king's law said no cow was to be touched. Would every man in so large a camp keep it?)
- **Picture.** Dusk on the plain: a wide Maratha camp of small tents and horse lines, transport
  bullocks with packs, Shivaji on horseback with a few riders and an umbrella-bearer on a low rise,
  seen at middle distance, composed and kingly.
- **Dignity.** Shivaji introduced as a lawgiver and a great captain, never a raider. Sakhoji is a face
  in the camp, nothing more.

### 4. scene-04 ಖಾಲಿ ತುರುಹಟ್ಟಿ (The Empty Cattle-Pen)
- **What happens.** Foragers from the camp drive Belavadi's cows out of the village pens towards the
  camp. The farmers come to the fort. ಈಶಪ್ರಭು sends his commander ಸಿದ್ದನಗೌಡ to the camp to ask for them
  back under the king's own law. He is laughed at and sent back without the cattle and with an insult.
  Mallamma hears him at the gate; ಈಶಪ್ರಭು must decide.
- **Facts.** f-cause-versions, f-cows-siddangouda, f-army-rules.
- **Dramatised.** The whole scene follows the Kannada tradition and the Samarotsava (via Arur); the
  insult's words are not given (shown as the commander's silence and his face). The other two causes
  (her raid on the transport bullocks, Sabhasad; foragers killed, Grant Duff) are on the evidence page,
  and the first is folded into scene 5. Whether Shivaji knew of the seizure is left open; he is not
  shown giving the order.
- **Ends on.** ಹಸುಗಳನ್ನು ಮರಳಿ ತರಲು ಹೋದವನು ಅವಮಾನ ಹೊತ್ತು ಬಂದ. ಈಗ ಬೆಳವಡಿ ತಲೆ ತಗ್ಗಿಸುವುದೇ, ಕತ್ತಿ
  ಎತ್ತುವುದೇ? (The man who went for the cows came back carrying an insult. Would Belavadi bow its
  head, or raise its sword?)
- **Picture.** A village cattle-pen of thorn and wooden rails, broken open and empty, hoof marks in
  red dust; in the background ಸಿದ್ದನಗೌಡ riding back towards the fort gate alone.
- **Dignity.** The wrong is done by soldiers against their king's law; no Maratha caricature.

### 5. scene-05 ಮೊದಲ ಕಾಳಗ (The First Clash)
- **What happens.** Belavadi answers: its riders fall on the camp's transport bullocks on the road and
  drive a string of them into the fort, a debt for a debt. The camp answers in force. In the fight
  before the fort ಈಶಪ್ರಭು is struck down from his horse. His men carry him back through the gate. He
  dies inside the walls. Mallamma closes his eyes.
- **Facts.** f-cause-versions, f-sabhasad, f-sarkar-narrative, f-ishaprabhu, f-widow, f-duff.
- **Dramatised.** The reprisal on the bullocks joins Sabhasad's cause to the tradition's; the clash
  and ಈಶಪ್ರಭು's death in it are the tradition's (Samarotsava: wounded in a sortie; 1951 play: a
  treacherous blow). The record makes her a widow before the siege: disclosed. No Maratha general is
  named as his killer (the play's 'Venkoji' is invention).
- **Ends on.** ಈಶಪ್ರಭು ಇಲ್ಲ. ಕೋಟೆಯ ಬಾಗಿಲು ಈಗ ಯಾರ ಕೈಯಲ್ಲಿ? (ಈಶಪ್ರಭು is gone. In whose hands is the
  gate now?)
- **Picture.** Dust and evening light at the fort gate: men carrying a wounded chief on a shield
  through the half-open wooden gate, Mallamma coming down the steps towards them; horsemen and smoke
  at a distance behind.
- **Dignity.** His death plain and quiet, no wound shown. Her grief contained; no wailing pose.

### 6. scene-06 ಬಾಗಿಲು ಹಿಡಿದವಳು (She Who Held the Gate)
- **What happens.** The elders expect the gate to open; terms would be easy for a widow with a baby.
  Mallamma takes off her bangles, ties her sari in the warrior way, takes ಈಶಪ್ರಭು's place at the gate
  and gives the orders: the walls manned, the granary sealed, the well guarded, the boy given to the
  women of the inner house. The women of Belavadi come to her armed; she makes them a company. From
  the bastion she sends back the king's demand. The cry goes up: ಜಯ್ ವೀರಭದ್ರ. The army closes round
  the fort and Shivaji himself sits down before it.
- **Facts.** f-woman-desai, f-fort, f-women-army, f-veerabhadra, f-shivaji-present, f-siege-date.
- **Dramatised.** The council, the removal of the bangles, the demand and her answer (no words
  quoted as hers), the women's company (the Samarotsava's 2,000 + 3,000 are not given as numbers in
  the prose; 'the women of Belavadi'), the war cry (from the 1951 play). This is the scene that makes
  her greatness explicit: the choice to hold, shown, with what she stands to lose named (her son, her
  people, her fort).
- **Ends on.** ಇಷ್ಟೊಂದು ರಾಜ್ಯಗಳನ್ನು ಗೆದ್ದ ದಂಡಿನ ಮುಂದೆ ಒಂದು ಮಣ್ಣಿನ ಗೋಡೆ ಎಷ್ಟು ದಿನ ನಿಂತೀತು? (How many
  days could a wall of mud stand before the army that had conquered so many kingdoms?)
- **Picture.** On the gate bastion at first light: Mallamma in the indigo veeragachche sari, bow in
  hand, plain wrists, women archers on the wall behind her, the Maratha camp stretching across the
  plain below.
- **Dignity.** The hero shot of the book. Strong, beautiful, commanding; not sexualised; no crown;
  no tiger trophies.

### 7. scene-07 ಮುತ್ತಿಗೆಯ ದಿನಗಳು (The Days of the Siege)
- **What happens.** Trenches creep towards the walls; the fort answers with arrows by day and raids on
  the trench heads by night; the women carry water and stones and cook for the walls; the granary
  shrinks; she walks the walls every night and counts the days on a post by the gate. The days go
  past ten, past twenty. In the camp the great captains, who had taken forts in the south, wait before
  a mud wall.
- **Facts.** f-27-days, f-siege-date, f-fort, f-ts-sortie (the trenches), f-sarkar-prestige.
- **Dramatised.** Every incident is chosen to show a siege of a plains mud fort; the day-count post is
  invented as the scene's device. The length, 27 days, is record (Chitnis, Duff, Sarkar).
- **Ends on.** ಹೊರಗಿನ ಬಿಡಾರದಲ್ಲಿ ಅವನ ಸ್ವಂತ ಜನ ಈ ಕೋಟೆಯ ಬಗ್ಗೆ ಏನು ಮಾತಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು? (Out in the camp, what
  were his own men saying about this fort?)
- **Picture.** Night: the fort's dark walls with a few torches, a woman's silhouette on the rampart;
  zig-zag siege trenches and earth mounds in the foreground lit by camp fires; no bodies.
- **Dignity.** No starving close-ups; hardship shown through empty grain jars and tired hands.

### 8. scene-08 ರಾಜಾಪುರದ ಪತ್ರ (The Rajapur Letter): THE GOOSEBUMP
- **What happens.** Cut to the coast. In the English Company's house at Rajapur, men who have come down
  from Shivaji's own camp talk. A clerk writes by lamplight to Surat. ೧೬೭೮ರ ಫೆಬ್ರವರಿ ೨೮. The scene
  gives his sentence whole, in Kannada with the English beside it: he is at present besieging a fort
  where, by the report of his own men, he has suffered more disgrace than ever from all the power of
  the Mughals or the Deccan kings, and he who has conquered so many kingdoms is not able to reduce this
  woman Desai. The ink dries. Back at Belavadi the woman of the letter is on her wall and does not know
  she has been written into history.
- **Facts.** f-rajapur-letter, f-sarkar-prestige, f-shivaji-present, f-woman-desai.
- **Dramatised.** The room, the lamp, the clerk and the men from the camp are invented; the sentence
  is verbatim (the only quotation in the book that is a record). Timing disclosed: Sarkar's reckoning
  ends the siege about 23 February while the letter, dated 28 February, says 'at present besieging';
  the news it carries was some days old, so the cut 'back to Belavadi' places the reader inside the
  fort at the time the news left the camp, not on 28 February itself. The letter names neither her nor
  the fort; Sarkar identifies it by date and route.
- **Ends on.** ದೂರದ ಊರಿನ ಮಾತು ಕಣಜ ತುಂಬಿಸುವುದಿಲ್ಲ. ದವಸ ಮುಗಿಯುತ್ತಿರುವ ಕೋಟೆ ಇನ್ನೆಷ್ಟು ದಿನ? (Words in a
  far town do not fill a granary. How many more days for a fort whose grain was running out?)
- **Picture.** A 17th-century English factory room at Rajapur at night: a clerk at a plain wooden
  table writing with a quill in a ledger by an oil lamp, two travel-stained Maratha messengers seated
  on a mat, sea shutters closed.
- **Dignity.** The shame is Shivaji's men's word, not mockery; the clerk is neutral, the messengers are
  awed rather than gleeful. Shivaji is not shown in this scene.

### 9. scene-09 ಕಂದಕಗಳ ಮೇಲೆ (Over the Trenches)
- **What happens.** Grain and powder are nearly gone. Rather than wait to be starved, Mallamma opens the
  gate herself and rides out at the head of her people at dawn: the siege trenches are thrown down,
  the besiegers in them are scattered, and for one whole day she holds the field before her own fort.
  At dusk she brings her riders back through the gate. The trenches are broken; the army is still
  there.
- **Facts.** f-ts-sortie, f-ts-source, f-27-days, f-fort.
- **Dramatised.** The sortie is from the Tarikh-i-Shivaji only (`legend`, disclosed); its hour, her
  place at the head, the return at dusk are chosen. The Persian text says she 'fled vanquished' at the
  end of the day; the book has her ride back inside, which is consistent with the fort still standing
  for the assault in scene 10, and the evidence page gives the Persian wording.
- **Ends on.** ಕಂದಕಗಳು ಮುರಿದವು, ದಂಡು ಉಳಿಯಿತು. ನಾಳಿನ ಬೆಳಗು ಏನು ತಂದೀತು? (The trenches were broken; the
  army remained. What would tomorrow's dawn bring?)
- **Picture.** Dawn charge: Mallamma on a grey horse with bow drawn, riders and armed women on foot
  pouring out of the gate over half-filled trenches, Maratha foot falling back in dust; no gore.
- **Dignity.** Her bravest moment; she leads, not followed by a male rescuer.

### 10. scene-10 ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೇಳನೇ ದಿನ (The Twenty-Seventh Day)
- **What happens.** The army comes at the walls from every side. The mud wall gives way at one corner;
  the gate is forced; the fort is carried by assault. Mallamma is on the wall with her bow when it
  falls; she is taken standing, and brought out with her people. In the camp a commander, ಸಖೋಜಿ
  ಗಾಯಕವಾಡ, forgets the respect owed to a captive lady. Then she is brought before Shivaji. She stands.
  She does not lower her eyes. She tells the king, before his officers, what his commander has done,
  and asks him whether that is his law.
- **Facts.** f-sarkar-narrative, f-sabhasad, f-duff, f-27-days, f-ts-sakhoji, f-91-kalami,
  f-army-rules, f-shivaji-present.
- **Dramatised.** The breach, her taking, and the meeting are chosen (the record says only that the
  fort was stormed and she was captured). What Sakhoji did is NOT told or shown: one sentence, 'forgot
  the respect owed to a captive lady'; the Persian text's 'greatly dishonoured' is quoted only on the
  evidence page. Her speech to the king is invented (no words quoted as hers; reported speech only);
  it gives her the agency the record does not record but the tradition insists on.
- **Ends on.** ಇಷ್ಟು ರಾಜ್ಯ ಗೆದ್ದವನು ತನ್ನನ್ನು ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೇಳು ದಿನ ತಡೆದ ಹೆಂಗಸಿನ ಮುಂದೆ ನಿಂತ. ಅವನು ಏನು
  ಮಾಡುವನು? (The conqueror of so many kingdoms stood before the woman who had held him for twenty-seven
  days. What would he do?)
- **Picture.** Inside a large plain tent: Mallamma standing upright in her dust-stained indigo sari,
  hands free, facing Shivaji, who stands (not seated on high) in white jama and turban, officers on
  both sides; Sakhoji at the edge of the frame, face in shadow.
- **Dignity.** She is not bound, not on her knees, not dishevelled. Sakhoji is never shown with her.
  Shivaji is grave, listening.

### 11. scene-11 ತಲೆಬಾಗಿದ ರಾಜ (The King Who Bowed): THE TURNING POINT
- **What happens.** Shivaji turns to his officers: no man who breaks the king's law about women goes
  unpunished. ಸಖೋಜಿ is taken away under guard to a far prison and never comes back into the story.
  Then the king joins his palms and bows his head to her, before his whole camp: such courage he has
  not met in all his wars; she shall be to him as a mother. He sets her free and gives her back her
  land. At the camp at Yadwad, when she comes with her people, he takes her infant son on his lap and
  gives him milk from a cup with his own hand. She says he has won her not by his valour but by his
  generosity.
- **Facts.** f-mother-sister, f-chitnis, f-chitnis-estate, f-ts-sakhoji, f-martin, f-91-kalami,
  f-army-rules, f-milk, f-yadwad-slab, f-play-1951, f-samarotsava-editor, f-cows-siddangouda (Yadwad as
  the camp).
- **Dramatised.** This scene is the owner's ending and is built from: the release (Chitnis, record of
  1810) and the small estate (Chitnis via Mehendale); the punishment (Tarikh-i-Shivaji, 91-Kalami;
  the blinding is NOT in the prose, it is on the evidence page with Martin's unnamed brother-in-law and
  Pagadi's 'treason' reading); the bow and 'mother' (Samarotsava via Arur; the 1951 play); the milk
  (the Yadwad tradition and slab, undated); her line about generosity is a paraphrase of the 1951 play
  (in copyright, so not quoted). 'Gave back her land' is Chitnis's 'small estate' told large; the
  record does not say her whole state. Sabhasad's 'She was punished' is on the evidence page.
- **Ends on.** ದಂಡು ಉತ್ತರಕ್ಕೆ ಹೊರಟಿತು. ಆ ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೇಳು ದಿನಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಏನು ಉಳಿಯುವುದು? (The army marched north.
  What would remain of those twenty-seven days?)
- **Picture.** The Yadwad camp on an open plain at morning: Shivaji seated on a low rug under an
  awning with a small boy on his lap, holding a cup to his lips; Mallamma standing beside with bow and
  quiver, upright; Maratha officers and Belavadi women watching; a parasol behind the king (after the
  Yadwad slab as described).
- **Dignity.** The bow is honour: standing, palms joined, head inclined; never kneeling, never at her
  feet (the Samarotsava's 'fell at her feet' is excluded and its editor's admission is on the evidence
  page). Mallamma receives it as an equal. No image of the punishment.

### 12. scene-12 ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಇಂದು (Belavadi Today)
- **What happens.** The king rides north by Torgal; news from Bijapur turns him west to Panhala. Two
  years later, in ೧೬೮೦, he dies; among the forts he holds in the Belgaum country Belavadi is not
  named. Her own later life is not known; the tradition says she ruled for her son with her
  brother's help. The mud fort is gone. In Belavadi, a village of Bailhongal taluk, the Veerabhadra
  temple still stands, and at Yadwad, in the Hanuman temple, a dark stone shows a horseman and, below,
  a bearded man with a child on his lap and a cup in his hand. Three languages remember her: an English
  letter calls her 'this woman Desai', the Marathi chronicles call her Mal Bai and Savitri, and her own
  people call her ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ.
- **Facts.** f-bijapur-news, f-holdings-1680, f-unknowns, f-belavadi-today, f-veerabhadra,
  f-yadwad-slab, f-name, f-ambush (the escort to Torgal).
- **Dramatised.** The tradition that she escorted him to Torgal may be one clause (Samarotsava);
  'ruled for her son' is tradition; the modern statue is not described (no photograph checked).
- **Ends on.** ಈ ಕಥೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಎಷ್ಟು ದಾಖಲೆ, ಎಷ್ಟು ಐತಿಹ್ಯ? ಮುಂದಿನ ಪುಟಗಳು ಹೇಳುತ್ತವೆ. (How much of this story
  is record and how much legend? The next pages tell.)
- **Picture.** Belavadi at evening today: red-soil fields, a small old stone temple with a modest
  tower among village houses, cattle coming home; no statue, no text.
- **Dignity.** Not a travelogue: one paragraph of place, one of memory, then the question.

## ಸಾರಾಂಶ (bullets for the ten lines)
- Belavadi, a small free state of villages under a mud fort on the plain south of the Malaprabha.
- In the Kannada tradition, Mallamma, a daughter of Sode trained in arms, marries its Desai ಈಶಪ್ರಭು.
- January 1678: Shivaji's army, returning from the south, camps on the plain.
- Belavadi's cattle are taken against the king's own law; the commander sent for them is insulted.
- In the first clash ಈಶಪ್ರಭು falls (tradition); the record already calls her a widow.
- She takes the gate and holds a petty mud fort against Shivaji for twenty-seven days.
- His own men call it a greater disgrace than any the Mughals or Bijapur dealt him (Rajapur, 28 Feb 1678).
- After a last sortie the fort is stormed and she is taken.
- In the tradition and the 1951 play he bows to her courage, punishes the man who wronged her, calls her
  mother and gives her land back; the chronicle of 1810 says only that she was released.
- Why she is great, in one line: a widow with an infant and a mud wall chose to stand, and made the
  conqueror of kingdoms honour her.
