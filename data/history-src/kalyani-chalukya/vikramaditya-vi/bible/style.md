# Visual bible: Kalyana and the Deccan, 1055–1126 CE (ಆರನೇ ವಿಕ್ರಮಾದಿತ್ಯ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/kalyani-chalukya-verified.md §10 (no portrait; emblem; insignia; coins;
dress and court in words) and §6 (Itagi). Written evidence used for the look: the boar crest (EI XIII
p. 38); "the jewels, umbrella, and throne" and "the five big drums" (EC VII Sk 136); the pattabandha
fillet (Wadageri, Fleet p. 446); Bilhana's "blue parasol and a mast elephant" (Buhler p. 15) and the
parasol "borne aloft before the king" (Stein VII.935–937); war elephants and the king "on the back of
his maddened elephant" (Bhandarkar p. 218); hunting with hounds and arrows, bath rooms "cooled by
flowing water", sandal ointment (Buhler pp. 41, 43); Kalhana's Deccani "forehead-marks of sandal
ointment" and "splendid long daggers" (VII.926–927, context only); the Itagi tablet's top carvings
(EI XIII p. 37). Dress, crowns and architecture follow the surviving Kalyani Chalukya temples of the
period (Itagi 1112, Lakkundi, Gadag) as visual analogue only: they are not portraits and no face is
taken from them. The king's face is an AI reconstruction, disclosed (f-no-portrait).

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, late 11th to early 12th-century Deccan Karnataka
(the Chalukya kingdom of Kalyana: black-soil plains, rocky granite outcrops, the Tungabhadra, the
Krishna). Dramatic natural light, harsh dry-season sun, river mist at dawn, lamp and incense smoke in
halls, dust of elephants and horses on campaign; shallow depth of field on people; high detail in
finely carved grey-green and blue-black schist, black inscription slabs, cotton, silk, gold and iron.
Colour: blue-black and grey-green stone, black cotton soil, dry gold grass, white and indigo cotton,
madder red and saffron silk, dull gold, burnished iron. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a
painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

No relief, statue, painting or coin of Vikramaditya VI exists (f-no-portrait). Bilhana's only words
on his person: "a handsome and strong boy". Fleet's arithmetic makes him at least seventy in 1126:
age him across the book. The lead must NOT repeat any earlier lead, and every prompt says so:
NOT Mayurasharma (young, curly hair, clean-shaven); NOT Pulikeshi (long shoulder curls, short beard,
tall cylindrical kirita); NOT Chavundaraya (square jaw, thick moustache with no beard, hair knot, grey
temples); NOT Amoghavarsha (long oval face, thin moustache only, long loose hair, low diadem); NOT
Vishnuvardhana (round wide-cheeked face, close-cropped beard with upturned moustache, tall tiered
conical crown); NOT Chikkadevaraja (long face, thin curled moustache, small chin-beard, white turban);
and NOT the Badami Vikramaditya II being designed in parallel (check that sheet before locking this
one and change any feature that coincides).

### LEAD: vikramaditya (one man at three ages)
Face type (the identity, same at every age): a BROAD, HIGH, SMOOTH FOREHEAD with the hairline set far
back, the widest part of the face at the temples; a strong rectangular-oval face tapering to a firm
rounded chin; HEAVY straight dark brows set low over DEEP-SET, wide-spaced, dark eyes with a level,
steady gaze; a straight strong nose with a broad bridge (not aquiline, not hooked); full, firm lips; a
small vertical crease between the brows when he is still. Skin warm deep brown. Build: tall, broad
across the shoulders and chest, thick strong neck and forearms of a rider and elephant-fighter; lean
at the waist, never fat. Handsome, strong and heroic at every age; never ugly, never coarse.
Hair: long, thick, straight black hair drawn back off the high forehead and wound into a JEWELLED COIL
at the back of the crown (a low bun wrapped with a gold-and-pearl band), visible under or behind the
crown; never loose, never a top-knot.
Signature crown (king scenes): a KARANDA-MUKUTA of the Kalyani sculpture type: a broad jewelled gold
diadem band low on the forehead with a central gem, rising in three or four stacked, narrowing,
bowl-like gold tiers to a small finial; of medium height, NOT the tall Hoysala tiered cone and NOT a
tall cylindrical kirita; makara-headed ear flanges at the sides. In scene 6 a white cloth fillet
(pattabandha) tied under it. Forehead: a sandal-paste mark (horizontal stripes of Saiva ash in
ritual scenes, scenes 3–4).
Ornament and dress: heavy round gold ear-rings in stretched lobes; two or three necklaces (a pearl
string, a broad jewelled collar); armlets with a raised boss; wrist-rings; sacred thread; bare chest
with a silk uttariya across one shoulder; a madder-red or saffron silk lower garment, knee-length and
pleated for war, ankle-length in court, with a jewelled waist-belt; a long straight sword and a long
dagger at the belt. In court seated on a lion throne under a royal parasol, with chowrie-bearers and
the boar standard.

Ages:
- vikramaditya-boy (scene 1): about 10. Same broad high forehead and deep-set eyes already marked;
  a sturdy, long-limbed, athletic boy; hair in a small jewelled coil; no crown, no facial hair; a small
  bow; a white and saffron waist-cloth; one pearl string.
- vikramaditya-prince (scenes 2–6; also scenes 7–10 as young king, about 30–45): about 30. Full,
  SHORT BLACK BEARD trimmed close to the jaw line and joined to a full, straight moustache (not
  upturned); the forehead broad and bare; no crown as prince (a plain gold band), the karanda-mukuta
  from scene 6 on.
- vikramaditya-elder (scenes 11–12): about 65–70. The same face and build, still upright and broad;
  beard and moustache GREY-FLECKED, silvering at the chin; grey threading the coiled hair; two deep
  lines between the brows, crow's-feet; the gaze unchanged. Dignified and strong, never frail.

### someshvara-i (scene 1; scene 3 seen from behind or at a distance)
Ahavamalla, the father: about 55–65. A different face from the son's: a longer, narrower face, an
aquiline nose, a long grey-white beard; same family ornaments; karanda-mukuta. In scene 3 bare-headed
and in a white cloth for the river, gold being handed out; the face calm, eyes closed in meditation;
seen from behind or in profile, never in distress.

### someshvara-ii (scenes 4–6)
The elder brother, Bhuvanaikamalla: about 35–40. Shares the family's warm brown skin but NOT the
lead's forehead: a rounder, softer face, a fuller body, a clipped moustache only, hair in a high knot
under a karanda-mukuta. In scene 4 crowned, gracious at the gate; in scene 6 seated captive with
dignity, crown removed, a white shawl, unbound, under guard; never chained, never grovelling.

### jayasimha (scenes 1, 5, 8)
The youngest brother: a boy of about 12 in scene 5; about 30 in scene 8, slimmer and lighter than the
lead, clean-shaven with a thin moustache, hair in a side coil; on a war elephant, then kneeling,
unhelmeted, in scene 8.

### chandaladevi (scenes 9, 12)
Silahara princess of Karad, chief queen: about 25–35 in scene 9. Tall and graceful, an oval face with
a high clear brow, large dark eyes, a small gold nose-stud, warm light-brown skin; long dark hair in a
high rounded bun with jasmine and a jewelled pin; a small queen's diadem; silk sari-like lower garment
to the ankle with a fitted breast-cloth and a fine veil-scarf (dressed, by house rule); heavy girdle,
anklets. Composed, amused, regal; never coy, never in a harem setting.

### bilhana (scenes 9, 11)
The Kashmiri poet, about 45–50 by 1085: visibly northern: lighter wheat skin, a narrow face, a long
straight nose, a neat dark beard greying at the chin, hair under a wrapped Kashmiri cloth cap; a long
WOOLLEN robe (pheran-like, undyed cream or russet wool) over a cotton under-robe even in the Deccan
heat, a woollen shawl; palm-leaf manuscript in hand. In scene 11 on an elephant under a BLUE parasol.

### vijnaneshvara (scene 11)
The jurist of Kalyana: about 50–60, a Deccani Brahmin scholar: shaven head with a tuft, a broad
sandal mark, a white upper cloth and lower cloth, a sacred thread, a lean scholar's face, no
spectacles; seated cross-legged at a low wooden desk with palm leaves and a stylus, lamp beside him.

### mahadeva (scene 12)
General Mahadeva of Itagi, the king's Dandanayaka and minister: about 50, broad and soldierly, a
greying moustache, hair in a knot under a cloth cap with a gold band, a quilted cotton jacket over a
white lower garment, a sword at the belt; standing with the king, showing him the new temple.

### others
- harsha-of-kashmir (scene 9 optional shot): a northern king with a pointed moustache, long dark hair,
  in a hall of Kashmir; his courtiers with "big forehead-marks of sandal ointment" and "splendid long
  daggers" (Kalhana's words for Harsha's Deccan-fashion court); a portrait painted on cloth held up.
- lakshmadevi (scene 11): the chief queen, about 40, round face, granting a village before temple

- chola-envoy, rajiga (scenes 5–6): Tamil court dress, side hair-knots, the Chola tiger only on a
  banner at a distance; Rajiga seen only fleeing in the distance.
- achugi (scene 10, optional): Sinda chief on horseback leading cavalry.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### kalyana-court (scenes 1, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11)
A palace of timber and stone: a pillared audience hall with lathe-turned, polished black-stone pillars
and carved wooden brackets and ceilings, a raised lion throne under a royal parasol, chowries, oil
lamps on tall stands, a boar emblem carved on the throne back and over the gate; outside, a stepped
pool and a garden with swans; elephants and horses in the outer court. Plain black-soil country
beyond. Nothing of the later fort (see EXCLUSIONS).

### tungabhadra-kuruvatti (scenes 3, 5)
The wide Tungabhadra with rounded granite boulders and rock shelves, sand bars, dawn mist; a small
stone temple on the bank (shown as a modest shrine of the period, not the present building as it
stands); musicians with drums and horns, priests, a crowd at a respectful distance.

### krishna-bank (scene 4)
A broad, slower, brown river in flat black-soil country; an army camp of cloth tents, tethered horses
and elephants; a riverside rite with a lamp and a small fire.

### itagi-temple (scene 12)
The Mahadeva temple in its first years: dark grey-green schist, a pillared hall with lathe-turned and
richly carved pillars, a densely carved shrine wall and a stepped tower with fine miniature
pavilions, a tall flagstaff; a black inscription slab with carvings at its top (linga, standing
figures, cow and calf, sun, Nandi, moon) being set up; dry fields and palms around.

### lakkundi (scene 11 background, optional)
A prosperous town of stone temples and a stepped tank; a goldsmith's bench with small gold coins
(gadyanas) and scales; no legible legend on the coins.

### kashmir-court (scene 9, one shot)
A Himalayan hall: timber walls and carved deodar ceilings, felt rugs, woollen shawls, snow light from
a window, braziers; markedly different from the Deccan.

### narmada-crossing / vengi-field (scene 10)
The Narmada: a wide river between rocky banks and wooded hills, the army fording with elephants in
cloth housings and bells, horsemen, the boar standard. No enemy army shown (opponent unnamed). For any
Vengi or Kanchi shot: flat coastal plain with palms and paddy, distant brick-and-stone temples.

## PERIOD RULES
- Dress: men bare-chested with jewelled necklaces, armlets, ear-rings, sacred thread; pleated silk or
  cotton lower garments with sashes and jewelled belts; royals in karanda-mukutas or gold bands;
  officers in cloth caps with gold bands; quilted cotton jackets on campaign at most. Women in silk
  lower garments to the ankle, fitted breast-cloths and scarves, high buns with flowers.
- Arms: long straight swords, long daggers, spears, bows and arrows, round shields; war elephants
  with cloth housings, bells and a driver; horses with cloth saddles and breast ornaments. No plate
  armour, no face-guard helmets, no curved talwars.
- Insignia: royal parasol, chowries, lion throne, the boar standard and crest, drums ("the five big
  drums" of great vassals), jewels; the poet's BLUE parasol and his elephant.
- Writing: Old Kannada letters on black stone slabs and copper plates, shown as abstract ancient
  strokes, never modern glyphs; documents dated in "Chalukya-Vikrama" years after 1076 (never a
  legible Saka date except the Lakshmeshwar beat in scene 7, shown without legible numerals).
- Coins: small gold pieces, a boar or (for the Karnata type Harsha copied) an elephant; no portrait
  on any coin.
- Faiths side by side: Saiva and Vaishnava temples, a Jain basadi (Balligave), Buddhist viharas
  (Dambal); nobody destroying a temple.
- Legend scenes: the birth boon not illustrated; the svayamvara shown only as Bilhana reading.
- Dignity: the king never shown restrained, weeping uncontrollably, drunk, lovesick or in a harem;
  the captive brother never chained; no Chalukya rout at Kannegal.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No Basava or Basaveshwara figure, no Anubhava Mantapa, no Lingayat ishtalinga pendants on the court,
no Basavakalyan fort as it stands today (its walls, bastions and gates are later than the Chalukyas),
no gunpowder, firearms, cannon or rockets, no Vijayanagara gopuras or granite pavilions, no Hoysala
star-shaped temples at Kalyana, no Mughal, Maratha or Rajput turbans, coats or curved talwars, no
plate armour or face-guard helmets, no modern Kannada letters or legible Saka dates, no portrait
coins, no Bharatanatyam stage costume, no gore in close-up, no harem or drinking scenes.
