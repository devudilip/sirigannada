## Title

Recommended: **ಅಳಿಸಿದ ಶಕ, ಎತ್ತಿದ ಕೊಡೆ: ಆರನೇ ವಿಕ್ರಮಾದಿತ್ಯ** ('The Era Rubbed Out, the Parasol
Raised'). Two images in the house pattern (ಕೊಟ್ಟ ಬೆರಳು, ಕಟ್ಟಿದ ನುಡಿ; ಪಂಜಿನ ಎತ್ತುಗಳು, ಜಗದೇವನ ಮುದ್ರೆ):
the Gadag verb "rubbed out the Saka-varsha" (f-era-gadag) and Bilhana's parasol "borne aloft before
the king" (f-kalhana-parasol, f-bilhana-vidyapati). The one is the king's power over time, the other
his honour to a poet; together they are the book's two halves (the throne, the court).

Teaser (en): A father walked into the waves of the Tungabhadra; an elder brother was taken in battle; a
younger brother, caught in the jungle, heard only kind words. Then the king of Kalyana rubbed out the
Saka era and counted time in his own name, had a Kashmiri poet's parasol carried before him, and his
jurist wrote that there never was, is, or will be a town like Kalyana.

## Arc

- Introduction (world): a Chalukya house counted from 973–74, its capital made at Kalyana by the
  father; the boar crest; a second son, handsome and strong, viceroy of Gangavadi and Banavasi.
- Why (the wound and the question): the father's death in the river in 1068 puts the elder brother on
  the throne and the second son on the road; the poem's question is whether a younger son may take
  what the elder holds. The book does not answer it inside the story; the last page does (Buhler's
  verdict, Bhandarkar's, the fate "not known").
- How (the turn): the Chola marriage, Kanchi, Rajiga, the 1076 battle, the hesitation over the
  captive, the coronation festival; then the proclamation of the era (GOOSEBUMP 2).
- Ending: not a death but an age. The era on every stone, the court (Bilhana's parasol, Vijnaneshvara's
  colophon), Itagi dated "Chalukya-Vikrama year 37", and the quiet last date, 3 January 1126; the
  final beat is that stones went on counting his years after him, to the ninety-fourth (1169–70).

Goosebump moments: (1) the father wading into the Tungabhadra to music and waves, dated to the day by
the son's own brother's stone; (2) the command that abolished the Saka era: "Why should the glory of
the kings Vikramaditya and Nanda be a hindrance any longer?"; (3) the closing: records counting his
years long after he was gone.

Legend rule for this book: two passages are Bilhana's poem and are disclosed on the last page: the
birth boon (scene 1, told as what the palace told the boy, never illustrated) and the svayamvara
(scene 9, told as Bilhana's canto being read at court; the image shows the reading, not a
svayamvara). Gauda and Kamarupa are not used. The Siva dreams of scene 6 are told as the king's own
dreams and disclosed.

## Dignity notes (verified file §9)

- **The brothers' war (scenes 5–6).** Never a coup told as triumph, never the elder as a cartoon
  villain. Tell what the poem tells (the elder's alliance with the Chola usurper Rajiga, the younger's
  reluctance, the hesitation over the captive) with the elder seated with dignity, not chained or
  humiliated. No line that he was killed. The evidence page carries the stones' "seized in battle",
  "punished or confined", Buhler's verdict and "not known".
- **The grief on the Krishna (scene 4).** The poem says he had to be disarmed lest he harm himself:
  tell the grief, not the restraint.
- **Jayasimha (scene 8).** The clemency is the beat; the elephant charge shows courage, not rage; no
  punishment shown afterwards because none is recorded.
- **The Chola taunts.** Virarajendra's "the liar", "driven from Gangapadi over the Tungabhadra" and
  Kulottunga's "pursued from Nangali to Manalur" are enemy claims and stay on the evidence page. Inside
  the story the Chola is the father-in-law who offered his daughter and the Kanchi throne the king
  defended for his wife's brother.
- **Kannegal (scene 10).** The emperor is not said to be present. Two sentences: a night raid on the
  camp of twelve vassal lords, answered by Achugi; no image of a Chalukya rout. Never "never lost a war".
- **The lovesick king and the harem cantos.** Not used. Scene 9 keeps to the garland and the recital.
- **Harsha of Kashmir.** The mockery is Kalhana's and falls on Harsha; usable as proof of the queen's
  fame.
- **Old age (scene 12).** Grey-flecked, upright, dignified; never frail, never on a sickbed.
