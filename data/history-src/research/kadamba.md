Research file for the `kadamba` series. Written 2026-10-10 against `BRIEF.md`.

Nothing below is from memory; where a thing could not be sourced in this pass it is

## 1. Timeline and rulers (dates with source)

### 1.1 The dating problem, stated first

No early Kadamba record is dated in a known era. The Halsi plate of Kakusthavarma is dated
in "the eightieth year" of an unnamed "year of victory"; Kielhorn placed the Talagunda
pillar "roughly in the first half of the 6th century A.D." on metre, Gupta connection and
palaeography; later scholars pull it back to c. 450–470. So every absolute date below is a
historian's reconstruction and different historians differ by 20–40 years. Treat all dates
as `scholarship` unless marked otherwise.

- Kielhorn's own dating remark (first half of 6th c.): EI VIII p. 42 of the scan (printed
  p. 30 area), https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_08/032562/42 — `scholarship`
- Moraes 1931 p. 16 notes the Halsi plates of Kakustha are dated "the eightieth year of an
  unknown era, called the year of victory" — `inscription` (the date formula) / `scholarship`
  (its interpretation)
- Pollock dates the Talagunda pillar c. 455–470 CE (Sheldon Pollock, *Ideology and Status of
  Sanskrit*, Brill 1996, p. 214, cited at
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talagunda_pillar_inscription) — `scholarship`
- Karashima 2014 (*A Concise History of South India*, OUP, p. 369, cited at
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakusthavarma) gives Kakusthavarma c. 425–450 — `scholarship`
- G. S. Gai's "revised chronology of the Kadamba dynasty" places Ravivarman c. 484–519
  (Gai, "Sirsi grant", EI XXXII, 1957–58,
  https://whatisindia.com/inscriptions/epigraphica_indica/vol32_1957-1958/gai2.html) —
  `scholarship`
- The Kannada Wikipedia list (https://kn.wikipedia.org/wiki/ಕದಂಬ_ರಾಜವಂಶ) uses
  345–365 / 365–390 / 390–415 / 415–435 / 435–455 / 455–460 / 460–480 / 480–485 / 485–519 /
  519–530; the English page uses a different table (e.g. Ravivarma 497–537). Both are
  finding aids only.

### 1.2 Banavasi (Vaijayanti) main line

Order of succession is secure from the inscriptions themselves (Talagunda pillar verses
22–27, 34; the Halsi and other copper plates). Dates are the reconstruction ranges found.

- Succession Mayurasharma → Kangavarman → Bhagiratha → Raghu and Kakusthavarman (sons of
  Bhagiratha) → Santivarman (son of Kakustha): Kielhorn 1906, translation vv. 22–27, 34
  (https://whatisindia.com/inscriptions/epigraphica_indica/vol8_1905-1906/kielhorn22.html and
  kielhorn23.html) — `inscription`
- Kielhorn counts eight generations in the Talagunda genealogy against Rice's eleven
  (jainqq scan p. 42) — `scholarship`
- Santivarman "also known as Santivaravarmma"; Mrigesa "eldest son of Santivarmma" per the
  Halsi plates; Ravivarma's plates confirm: Moraes 1931 pp. 28, 31 (citing Fleet, IA VI
  p. 25) — `inscription` via Fleet
- Ravivarma's Nilambur plates: regnal year 5, issued from Vaijayanti; Sirsi (Ajjibad)
  plates: regnal year 35; "we might give 40 years as the possible extent of his reign":
  Moraes 1931 pp. 47–48 (citing EI VIII p. 148; Progress Report ASWI 1917–18 p. 35) —
  `inscription` for the regnal years, `scholarship` for the 40-year guess
- Harivarma's three copper-plate grants "do not go beyond his eighth regnal year": Moraes
  1931 p. 50 — `scholarship`
- Shivamandhatri (Mandhatrivarma) as an "intruder" who dispossessed the young Ravivarma:
  Moraes 1931 p. 47 — `scholarship` (Moraes's reading; the Kudgere plates of "Vijaya Siva
  Mandhatrivarman" exist: EI VIII Nilambur note, see
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilambur_plates)

### 1.3 Triparvata (southern) branch

| Ruler | ಕನ್ನಡ | Approx. reign | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Krishnavarma I (younger brother of Shantivarma; viceroy of the south, then king) | ಒಂದನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣವರ್ಮ | c. 430–460 | Birur plates of Vishnuvarma; Moraes 1931 pp. 28, 37–40 |
| Vishnuvarma | ವಿಷ್ಣುವರ್ಮ | c. 460–485/490 | Birur plates (yr 3, "spurious" per Siddham), Kora/Hebbata plates yr 5 |
| Simhavarma | ಸಿಂಹವರ್ಮ | c. 485/490–516 | no inscriptions of his own (Moraes 1931 p. 45) |
| Krishnavarma II | ಎರಡನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣವರ್ಮ | c. 516–540 | Bannahalli plates yr 7; Bennur plates; Sirsi plates (yr 19) |
| Ajavarma, Bhogivarma | ಅಜವರ್ಮ, ಭೋಗಿವರ್ಮ | 6th c., under Chalukya overlordship | en.wiki table; Dikshit 1980 p. 41 via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirttivarman_I |

- Birur plates of Vishnuvarma: grant made "with the permission of Santivarmma", whom they
  call his *jyeshtha-pitri* (father's eldest brother) and "master of the entire Karnata
  region of the earth, adorned by Vaijayanti, which was glorious with eighteen chieftains":
  Moraes 1931 pp. 28–29 (EC VI Kd 162); Kielhorn's genealogical table and his doubt about
  the plates' genuineness: EI VIII (jainqq scan p. 41) — `inscription` (text) / disputed
  (genuineness)
- Triparvata = "a city of three hills" near Halebid (Moraes 1931 p. 39; Jouveau-Dubreuil's
  suggestion from the Devagiri plates of Devavarma issued from Triparvata) — `scholarship`,
  location not settled
- Krishnavarma II "acquired the wealth of his kingdom by his heroism" (Bannahalli plates),
  "anointed at Vaijayanti during a horse-sacrifice" (Sirsi plates): Moraes 1931 pp. 53–54
  (EC V Bl 245; EI XVI p. 271) — `inscription`

### 1.4 The fall, c. 540–600

- Aihole prashasti of Pulakeshin II (Saka 556 = 634–35 CE, poet Ravikirti, Meguti temple):
  Kirtivarman (I) was "the night of doom to the Nalas, Mauryas and Kadambas" and broke
  "the multitude of the broad kadamba trees — the Kadambas" (Kielhorn, EI VI, 1900–01,
  pp. 1–11, vv. 9–10; cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aihole_inscription) —
  `inscription`
- The same prashasti, vv. 17–18: Pulakeshin II "besieging Vanavasi, which for a girdle has
  the rows of hamsa birds", a city which "by its wealth rivalled the city of the gods"
  (same source) — `inscription`
- Mahakuta pillar inscription (of Mangalesha): the ruler of Vaijayanti is among kings
  Kirtivarman defeated (Dikshit 1980, *Political History of the Chalukyas of Badami*, p. 40,
  via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirttivarman_I) — `inscription` via scholarship; the
  pillar's full conquest list is "obvious poetic exaggeration" (Sircar 1971 p. 165, same
  page) — `scholarship`
- Later Kalyani Chalukya records call Kirtivarman "an axe that cut at the very roots of the
  Kadambas" (Dikshit 1980 p. 40, same page) — `inscription` (12th-c. hindsight)
- The Kadamba king defeated by Kirtivarman is not named; "most probably Ajavarman, son of
  Krishnavarman II" (Dikshit 1980 p. 41) — `scholarship`
- Kirtivarman I's accession 566–67 CE (Amminabhavi and Badami inscriptions; Dikshit 1980
  pp. 39, 45) — `inscription`
- Moraes's reconstruction: Pulakeshin I's rise at Badami, "exactly in the centre of the
  northern provinces of the Kadamba kingdom", during Harivarma's weak reign; Krishnavarma
  II then took Vaijayanti and the elder line ended (Moraes 1931 pp. 51–53) — `scholarship`
- Xuanzang (c. 640) visited "Kong-kin-na-pu-lo" (Konkanapura), about 2000 li north of
  Dravida, with "about 100 sangharamas, with some 10,000 priests" and a jewelled tiara of
  Prince Sarvarthasiddha shown on fast-days; V. de St. Martin identified the capital with
  Vanavasi, Cunningham with Anagundi (Beal, *Si-yu-ki*, 1884, vol. II pp. 253–254, public
  domain: https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.84923/2015.84923.Buddhist-Records-Of-The-Western-World-Vol-Ii_djvu.txt)
  — `inscription` (eyewitness); identification `scholarship`, disputed

### 1.5 Later branches (brief)

- **Goa Kadambas (ಗೋವೆ ಕದಂಬರು), c. 960–1310/1345**: Shashthadeva appointed Mahamandaleshvara
  of Goa by Chalukya Tailapa II; took Chandrapura (Chandor) from the Shilaharas; capital
  later Gopakapattana; Jayakeshi I (c. 1050–1080) expanded the kingdom; the Jain
  *Dvyashraya* describes the port's trade with Zanzibar, Bengal, Gujarat, Sri Lanka; Malik
  Kafur looted the city; last ruler Kamadeva (1265–1310) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadambas_of_Goa,
  citing Moraes 1931 pp. 88–93, de Souza 1990, Gune 1979) — `scholarship`
- The Goa Kadamba story of Shashthadeva II's ship losing its mast at Panjim and being
  repaired by the Taji (Arab) merchant "Modamod/Madhumod" at Chimbel-Ribandar appears in a
  Goa Kadamba record per Goa heritage summaries found in search (pilarmuseum.org,
  panjim.org); **primary edition not reached in this pass** — mark `inscription (not
  verified)`
- Goa Kadamba gold coins: Commons has "Kadambas of Goa, anonymous, c. 1240–1310, AV pagoda,
  'Conqueror of the Malavas' issue, lion standing left" (file listed in
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Kadamba_dynasty; file page returned 404 on
  direct fetch, check the category) — `inscription` (coin)
- **Hangal Kadambas (ಹಾನಗಲ್ ಕದಂಬರು), c. 980–14th c.**: Chatta Deva (980–1031) helped the
  Kalyani Chalukyas overthrow the Rashtrakutas; Jayakesi III declared independence 1187–88;
  Tarakeshvara temple at Hangal; Old Kannada inscription of Kamadeva c. 1200 in its mantapa
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadambas_of_Hangal; Commons file
  Old_Kannada_inscriptions_of_Kadamba_king_Kamadeva_(c.1180)_and_Hoysala_king_Veera_Ballala_II_(c.1196)...JPG)
  — `scholarship`
- Later Kadamba titles: a 1110 CE Uchchangi Kadamba record begins "boon lord of
  Banavasi-pura — (performer of) eighteen horse-sacrifices, worshipper of the holy feet of
  the god Lunkesvara" (Moraes 1931 p. 233, quoting EC) — `inscription` (12th c.); the
  "eighteen asvamedhas" is a later-branch boast, not an early record (Moraes 1931 p. 16:
  "a little exaggerated, after a lapse of seven or eight centuries") — `scholarship`
- Other minor branches named: Bankapur, Bayalnad, Nagarkhanda (Somadeva received Banavasi
  1165), Uchchangi, Belur, Bandalike, Chandavar (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadambas_of_Hangal,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadamba_dynasty) — `scholarship`

## 2. Primary sources: what survives, where to read them, licence

### 2.1 Stone inscriptions

**(a) Talagunda pillar inscription (ತಾಳಗುಂದ ಸ್ತಂಭ ಶಾಸನ) of Santivarman, poet Kubja**
- Object: grey granite pillar in front of the ruined Pranavesvara (Pranavalingesvara)
  temple, Talagunda, Shikaripur taluk, Shimoga district. Pedestal 5 ft 4 in high, 1 ft 4 in
  square at top; octagonal shaft 6 ft 4 in high, faces 7 in wide; two vertical lines on each
  of seven faces, one short line (line 15) on the eighth; "Siddham || Namas Sivaya ||" on
  the pedestal (Kielhorn 1906, kielhorn10.html) — `inscription`
- Discovery: B. Lewis Rice, 1894; preliminary notice by Bühler in *The Academy*, Sept 1895;
  Fleet in *Dynasties* p. 286f; Rice's edition with photo-lithograph and translation in EC VII
  (Sk 176), p. 200ff (1902); Kielhorn's re-edition from Hultzsch's ink-impressions, EI VIII
  pp. 24–36 (1905–06) (kielhorn10.html) — `scholarship`
- Language/script: classical Sanskrit, "box-headed" southern alphabet closest to the
  Devagiri and Hitnahebbagilu plates of Mrigesavarman; 34 verses; the first 24 verses in a
  matra metre Kielhorn found unnamed in prosody works (kielhorn11.html, kielhorn13.html) —
  `scholarship`
- Online: EI VIII transcription at whatisindia.com (kielhorn10–23); scan at jainqq.org;
  Kielhorn's ink-impression photo (PD) at
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:455–470_CE_Talagunda_Pillar_Sanskrit_inscription,_Pranavesvara_temple_ruins,_Karnataka.jpg
  (PD-Art, CC Public Domain Mark); 23 3D-scan renders at
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Talagunda_450CE_Pillar_Inscription_of_Kakusthavarma
- Licence: Kielhorn (d. 1908) and Rice (d. 1927) editions are public domain; usable for
  quotation and reproduction.

  original now in the Government Museum, Bengaluru; fibreglass replica and memorial at
  Halmidi (https://kn.wikipedia.org/wiki/ಹಲ್ಮಿಡಿ_ಶಾಸನ;
  https://shastriyakannada.org/database/english/inscriptions/HALMIDI%20INSCRIPTION%20HTML.htm;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmidi_inscription) — `scholarship`

**(c) Chandravalli inscription (ಚಂದ್ರವಳ್ಳಿ ಶಾಸನ) of Mayurasharma**
- On a flat boulder beside the tank of the Bhairavesvara temple, Chandravalli, Chitradurga
  district; c. 6.5 × 3 ft; Brahmi (southern "cave" characters), Prakrit with some Sanskrit;
  records a tank made by Mayurasarman of the Kadambas who defeated Traikuta, Abhira,
  Pallava, Pariyatrika, Sakasthana, Sendraka(?), Punnata, Mokari (readings vary). Published
  MAR 1929 pp. 50–60 (M. H. Krishna); Krishna dated it 3rd c., later scholars 4th c.
  (Commons file page https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:4th_century_CE_Chandravalli_inscription_of_Mayurasarman,_Brahmi,_Prakrit_and_Sanskrit,_tank_near_Bhairavesvara_temple_2.jpg,
  CC0, photo of a 1910s print; Moraes 1931 p. 322 n.: "As the inscription is engraved in cave
  characters, and as it undoubtedly refers to a tataka or water reservoir associated with the
  Kadamba Mayurasarmma"; Kamath 2001 p. 31 via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurasharma) —
  `inscription`; the list of defeated peoples is `disputed` (see §5)
- Licence: MAR 1929 is a Government of Mysore publication; treat as PD in India (verify).

**(d) Gudnapur pillar inscription (ಗುಡ್ನಾಪುರ ಶಾಸನ) of Ravivarma**
- Found 1971 by B. R. Gopal and A. Sundara at Gudnapur, c. 5 km NW of Banavasi; pillar c.
  20 ft long, now lying on its side, top broken; Sanskrit in early Kannada characters; each
  line runs round the pillar (Deccan Herald "Message from time past",
  https://www.deccanherald.com/content/190427/message-time-past.html, via search snippet;
  page itself returned 403) — `scholarship`
- Content: dated in Ravivarma's first regnal year; records a Kama-jinalaya built by the
  king to the left of his palace (rajavesma), with grants for its worship; also a
  Kamadevalaya at Hakinipalli and a Padmavati temple at Kallili; the village Mukundi
  donated for their embellishment (Hampa Nagarajaiah, "Jina Parsva and His Temples in
  Inscriptions of Karnataka", *Nirgrantha* 2002, p. 3, https://jainqq.org/explore/269033/3,
  citing B. R. Gopal, *Corpus of Kadamba Inscriptions* [CKI], Mysore 1985, pp. 85, 87) —
  `inscription`
- Gopal's identification of the Kama-jinalaya as a temple of Bahubali (since Bahubali is
  called Manmatha) — `scholarship`, not settled; the record is also read as a Kamadeva
  (god of love) temple with the palace's dancing halls and women's quarters described
  beside it (Deccan Herald snippet; Cecil & Gomes 2021, "Kama at the Kadamba Court: The
  Gudnapur Pillar Inscription of Ravivarman as a Text-Monument", *Indo-Iranian Journal*
  64(1), pp. 10–50, https://www.jstor.org/stable/27072991; S. V. Sohoni, "Ravivarma's
  patronage of the performing arts", NCPA Journal 13(3), 1984, catalogued at sahapedia.org)
  — `scholarship`, disputed
- The Gudnapur record gives Mayurasharma's parentage (son of Bandhushena, grandson of
  Virasharma) and says he "took on the character of a Kshatriya" (Kamath 2001 p. 30 via
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurasharma) — `inscription` via secondary; **primary
  text not reached in this pass** (Gopal 1985 is in copyright)

**(e) Pranavesvara temple door-jamb inscriptions, Talagunda** (ASR Mysore 1911)
- Right jamb: invocation to Pashupati; names Kakustha of the Bhatari family (possibly a
  Kadamba feudatory) and his mother Lakshmi "of the Kadamba family"; money grant to feed
  thirty residents of Sthana-kunjapura. Left jamb: Queen Prabhavati, wife of Mrigeshavarma
  and mother of Ravivarma, of the Kekaya (Kaikeya) family (https://www.puratattva.in/talagunda/;
  Moraes 1931 p. 32 quoting the record: "she was born in the noble Kaikeya family, her name
  being Prabhavati; she was the beloved wife of Mrigesavarmma Dharma-maharaja") —
  `inscription`
- Commons: Talagunda_Left_Door_Jamb01–07 and Talagunda_right_Door_jamb_inscription1–5 in
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Kadamba_dynasty

**(f) Banavasi Naga-stone (pre-Kadamba, Chutu)**: Prakrit, Brahmi, 2nd–3rd c.; gift of a
naga, tank and vihara by Sivakhada-nagasiri, daughter of Chutu king Vinhukada
Chutukulananda Satakarni; still at a corner of the Madhukesvara temple; five-hooded snake
relief (EI XXXIV per https://puratattva.in/banavasi/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutu_dynasty)
— `inscription`. Relevant because Talagunda v. 33 says Satakarni and other kings worshipped
at the Talagunda Siva shrine (Kielhorn, kielhorn23.html).

**(g) Talagunda agrahara records, 1091 CE** (EC VII nos. 177, 178, 185, 186): the legendary
Mukkanna/Trilochana Kadamba brings 32 Brahmin families from Ahichchhatra to Sthanakundur
(https://www.puratattva.in/talagunda/; Moraes 1931 p. 290) — `legend` first recorded 11th c.

## 3. Candidate stories

### Story A — ಮಯೂರಶರ್ಮ: the sword drawn by a hand made for the ladle (c. 345)

**People**: Mayurasharma (ಮಯೂರಶರ್ಮ), his guru and grandfather Virasharma (ವೀರಶರ್ಮ); an
unnamed Pallava horseman at Kanchi (ಕಾಂಚಿ); the Pallava kings of Kanchi; the "Great Bana"
(ಬೃಹದ್ಬಾಣ); Shadanana/Senapati (ಷಡಾನನ, Skanda) who "anoints" him; later-born Kangavarma.

**Arc (8 beats, all from the Talagunda pillar unless noted)**
1. A Brahmin house of the Manavya gotra in the Kuntala country, named for the kadamba tree
   it tends; Vedic study, soma sacrifices, guests always welcome (vv. 4–8, Kielhorn
   kielhorn21.html) — `inscription`
2. Mayurasharma goes with his teacher Virasharman to the Pallava city (Kanchipura) and
   enters a *ghatika* "as a mendicant" to study the sacred lore (v. 10) — `inscription`
3. "A fierce quarrel with a Pallava horseman" (v. 11); his lament: "Alas, that in this
   Kali-age the Brahmans should be so much feebler than the Kshatriyas!" and that
   spiritual perfection should depend on a king (vv. 11–12) — `inscription`
4. The hand "dexterous in grasping the kusa grass, the fuel, the stones, the ladle, the
   melted butter, and the oblation vessel, unsheathed a flaming sword eager to conquer the
   world" (v. 13; wording per Moraes 1931 p. 15 quoting Rice/Kielhorn) — `inscription`
5. He overpowers the Pallava frontier guards, occupies the "inaccessible forest" up to the
   gates of Sriparvata (Srisailam), and levies tribute from the Great Bana and other kings
   (vv. 14–16) — `inscription`
6. The kings of Kanchi march against him; he strikes their army at night "while they
   marched or rested in rough country", relying on his sword-arm alone (vv. 17–18) —
   `inscription`
7. The Pallavas judge that ruining him would gain nothing, choose him as a friend; he
   enters their service, wins favour by bravery, and is crowned with a fillet they give
   with their own hands; he receives a territory on the western sea bounded by the
   "Prehara", "on condition that others would not enter it" (vv. 19–21) — `inscription`
8. "Shadanana (Skanda) anointed the father of Kangavarman" (v. 22): the Brahmin student
   becomes the ancestor of kings; the -sharma ending becomes -varma in his descendants
   (Kielhorn 1906 n. 6, kielhorn14.html) — `inscription` + `scholarship`

**Goosebump moment**: beat 4, the single verse where the priest's hand draws a sword. It is
recorded, in the dynasty's own words, a century after the event, by a court poet writing
for the founder's great-great-grandson. Evidence: Talagunda v. 13 — `inscription`.
Second candidate: beat 6, the night attack on the Pallava army (vv. 17–18).

**Dramatised vs recorded**
- Recorded: everything above; also that Kubja composed it for Santivarman (v. 34); that
  "the earliest known use" of the matrasamaka metre is in this poem (Kielhorn, kielhorn13.html).
- Dramatised (allowed, mark as such): the content of the quarrel (the inscription gives no
  cause); what the ghatika looked like (Kielhorn: "an establishment, probably founded by a
  king, for holy and learned men", n. 6 kielhorn21.html; Tieken & Sato 2000 and Sankaranarayanan
  offer other readings of ghatika — `scholarship`); any dialogue; the Banavasi capital
  (the pillar never names Banavasi for Mayurasharma; Vaijayanti appears only in later plates).
- Legend, must be labelled ಐತಿಹ್ಯ if used: born under a kadamba tree with a third eye
  (EC VII Sk 117; EC XI Dg 35, 10th–12th c.); Jain version with the Tirthankara's sister
  (EC VIII Sb 262); "Trilochana Kadamba" from Siva's sweat (Halsi and Degamve sasanas of the
  Goa Kadambas) (Moraes 1931 pp. 7–8) — `legend`; the "eighteen horse sacrifices" (Talagunda
  later record; Moraes 1931 p. 16) — `legend`; 144 brahmadeya villages granted to Talagunda
  Brahmins (attributed to the Halsi plates of Kakustha by Moraes 1931 p. 17 per en.wiki;
  **not verified against Fleet in this pass**); Ahichchhatra Brahmins (11th-c. records) —
  `legend`.
- Film: *Mayura* (1975, dir. Vijay, Rajkumar as Mayurasharma, from Devudu Narasimha
  Sastri's novel) invents a father Chandravarma killed by Pallava Sivaskandavarma, a
  Premavati, a wrestler Ranga Jetti etc. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayura_(film)) —
  none of this is in any inscription; list it under "sources to avoid for facts".

**Why it grips adults**: humiliation → self-reinvention; a scholar choosing violence and
being honest about the cost ("alas, in this Kali age..."); the ending is not a conquest
but a negotiated vassalage that later becomes a kingdom — morally ambiguous, true, and
told in the founder's own house's words. It is also the origin of the first dynasty to
use Kannada in a royal record (Halmidi, under his descendant).

**Evidence base**: strong. One long primary text (34 verses), edited twice by major
epigraphists, public domain, with a photographed ink impression and 3D scans on Commons.
Weak points: no date; Chandravalli list disputed; location of Prehara unknown.

### Story B — ಕಾಕುಸ್ಥವರ್ಮ: the king whose daughters were the sun's rays (c. 430–450)

**People**: Kakusthavarma (ಕಾಕುಸ್ಥವರ್ಮ); his brother and predecessor Raghu (ರಘು, "struck
down his adversaries despite wounds", a poet and donor, v. 26); his unnamed daughters;
Narendrasena the Vakataka (ನರೇಂದ್ರಸೇನ) and his queen Ajjhitabhattarika "daughter of the
king of Kuntala"; an unnamed Gupta prince; Srutakirti the general who "saved the prince";
Vija-arasa of Halmidi; son Santivarma (ಶಾಂತಿವರ್ಮ), poet Kubja (ಕುಬ್ಜ).

**Arc (7 beats)**
1. Raghu dies after "fierce battles" (v. 26); his younger brother Kakustha, "handsome,
   deep-voiced, devoted to liberation and the three aims of life" (v. 27), takes the
   throne — `inscription`
2. The Halsi plate: as *yuvaraja* at Palasika (Halsi) he rewards general Srutakirti with a
   field of the Arhats "for saving the prince" — which prince, and from what, is not said
   (Fleet IA VI p. 23; Bisschop & Cecil 2021 abstract; Moraes 1931 p. 25 n.) — `inscription`
3. Peace as policy: "As herds of deer tormented by the heat, entering into groups of trees,
   take refuge in their shade ... so relatives and dependents exposed to injury from
   superiors obtained comforts ... by entering this country" (v. 29; Moraes 1931 pp. 25–26
   quoting) — `inscription`
4. Wealth: "gateways scented with ichor from lordly lusty elephants, with the sweet sounds
   of songs, the goddess of fortune ... enjoys herself in his house" (v. 30) — `inscription`
5. The marriages: "the king is compared to the sun, whose rays are his daughters, who make
   the royal families of the Guptas and others bloom like lotuses" (v. 31) — `inscription`;
   one daughter = Ajjhitabhattarika, queen of Vakataka Narendrasena, "daughter of the king of
   Kuntala" (Balaghat plates of Prithivishena II, EI IX p. 271; Moraes 1931 p. 26; Sircar in
   *The Classical Age* p. 184) — `inscription` + `scholarship` (identification of Kuntala
   with the Kadambas is accepted but inferential)
6. Halmidi: in Kakustha's reign a Bhatari warrior, Vija-arasa, is rewarded with two villages
   for fighting the "Kekaya-Pallavas", in front of the Sendraka and Bana warriors — the first
   royal order in Kannada (§2.1(b)) — `inscription` (if Halmidi's Kakustha is this king;
   G. S. Gai dissents, §5)
7. He builds "a great tank at the temple of the god Bhava, a place of pilgrimage and Vedic
   recitation, which Satakarni and other kings had also honoured" (v. 33), and dies; his son
   Santivarman has Kubja carve the poem on a pillar before that temple (v. 34) — `inscription`

**Goosebump moment**: beat 5 — a Deccan forest-born house, four generations from a student
with a sword, marrying its daughters into the imperial Guptas, recorded in the same breath
as the deer-in-shade image of a peaceful country. Evidence: Talagunda vv. 29–31; Balaghat
plates for the Vakataka match — `inscription`.

**Dramatised vs recorded**: the identity of the Gupta bridegroom is open (Samudragupta —
Bühler; Chandragupta II — Dikshitar; Kumaragupta I — Tej Ram Sharma, Heras; Skandagupta —
N. Lakshminarayana Rao, Moraes 1931 p. 27) — write him unnamed. Kalidasa as Gupta envoy
to the "lord of Kuntala" (Kuntalesvaradautya) is a later literary anecdote linked to the
Kadambas by S. V. Sohoni 1979 (puratattva.in) — `legend`/`scholarship`, keep out or flag.
The Sanskrit "ghatika" and tank at Talagunda are recorded; the "palaces adorned with
gopuras" line in en.wiki (Nilakanta Sastri 2007 p. 220) is modern paraphrase — do not
illustrate as fact.

**Why it grips**: a king remembered for shade, not conquest; the women of the family
being the instrument of its greatest reach; and the oldest Kannada sentence being a
reward for a soldier in his reign.

**Evidence base**: good (two inscriptions naming him directly plus a Vakataka cross-
reference), but the Halmidi link is contested.

### Story C — ರವಿವರ್ಮ: the boy-king who uprooted "Chandadanda, Lord of Kanchi" (c. 485–519)

**People**: Ravivarma (ರವಿವರ್ಮ); his father Mrigeshavarma (ಮೃಗೇಶವರ್ಮ, "a very fire of
destruction to the Pallavas", "uprooted the family of Tunga-ganga"); mother Prabhavati of
the Kekaya house (ಪ್ರಭಾವತಿ); uncle Shivamandhatrivarma (ಶಿವಮಾಂಧಾತಿವರ್ಮ); cousin
Vishnuvarma of Triparvata (ವಿಷ್ಣುವರ್ಮ); "Chandadanda, Lord of Kanchi" (ಚಂಡದಂಡ); brother
Bhanuvarma (ಭಾನುವರ್ಮ); physician Nilakantha (ನೀಲಕಂಠ); the Jain sangha at Palasika.

**Arc (8 beats)**
1. Mrigeshavarma, scholar, rider of horses and elephants, builds a Jinalaya at Palasika
   in memory of his father and endows the Yapaniya, Nirgrantha and Kurchaka monks (Halsi yr
   8; Desai 1957 pp. 97–110 via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrigeshavarma) — `inscription`
2. He dies early; his son Ravivarma "perhaps a mere stripling"; the throne passes to
   Mandhatrivarma (Moraes 1931 pp. 46–47) — `scholarship` (Moraes's reconstruction from
   the Kudgere plates' existence)
3. The Pallava and Ganga kings "vanquished by his father" encroach, and "prevailed upon his
   relation Vishnuvarmma to make a bid for the sovereignty of the northern regions" (Moraes
   1931 p. 47) — `scholarship`
4. Ravivarma, "the Sun of the sky of the mighty family of the Kadambas, who having slain Sri
   Vishnuvarmma and other kings, and having conquered the whole world, and having uprooted
   Chandadanda, the Lord of Kanchi, had established himself at Palasika" (Halsi plate; Fleet
   IA VI p. 30; Moraes 1931 p. 47) — `inscription`. Fleet took Vishnuvarma to be Pallava
   Vishnugopa; Moraes to be the Triparvata cousin — `disputed`
5. Year 5: from Vaijayanti he grants two hamlets near Talakad, the new Ganga capital
   (Nilambur plates, EI VIII pp. 146–149; Moraes 1931 p. 48 reads this as a strike at the
   Gangas) — `inscription` + `scholarship`
6. Year 1 (Gudnapur): a Kama temple beside his palace, with dancing halls and the women's
   quarters described — a court of music and love, read by some as a Bahubali shrine (§2.1(d))
   — `inscription`, interpretation disputed
7. Year 11: his brother Bhanuvarma's gift for the bathing of Jinendra; an eight-day Karttika
   festival of Jinendra at Palasika funded from a village (Fleet IA VI pp. 27–29; Nagarajaiah
   2002) — `inscription`
8. Year 35, from Vijaya-Panktipura (Hangal): land to the Mahadeva temple at the request of
   "his beloved physician, the desa-amatya Nilakantha" (Gai, EI XXXII) — `inscription`; the
   Gudnapur record lists Punnatas, Alupas, Kongalvas and the Pandyas of Uchchangi among those
   subdued (en.wiki Ravivarma, citing T. Manjanna 2015 — weak source, **verify in CKI**)

**Goosebump moment**: beat 4 — the copper plate's single sentence: the young king who
"slew Sri Vishnuvarma" (his own kinsman) and "uprooted Chandadanda, Lord of Kanchi". A
Deccan king claiming to have beaten Kanchi, two centuries before Pulakeshin II. Evidence:
Halsi plate of Ravivarma, Fleet IA VI p. 30 — `inscription`. Chandadanda is unidentified
in any Pallava record (Sathianathaier via en.wiki) — say so.

**Dramatised vs recorded**: beats 2–3 are Moraes's narrative glue — mark `scholarship`
and do not stage a coup scene as fact. The Kama temple's "dancing halls" are in the
record per Deccan Herald/Sohoni but the primary text was not reached — flag.

**Why it grips**: family war (cousin against cousin), a Jain king who builds a temple to
Kama, a physician named in a royal grant, a reign of 35+ years ending with the dynasty's
widest claims (Sircar: Narmada to Kaveri from the Davanagere plates, 519 CE — en.wiki,
`scholarship`, **not verified**).

**Evidence base**: good on inscriptions (at least seven dated records in his name), weak
on narrative connections.

### Story D — ಹಲ್ಮಿಡಿ: the sword-washing (ಬಾಳ್ಗೞ್ಚು) of Vija-arasa (c. 450)

**People**: Vija-arasa (ವಿಜ ಅರಸ) of the Bhatari (ಬಟರಿ) family, "Pashupati of the Alupa
host"; governors Mrigesha and Naga (ಮೃಗೇಶ, ನಾಗ) of Naridavile-nadu; King Kakustha-bhattora;
the Kekaya-Pallavas; the Sendraka and Bana warriors; the Brahmins of Palmadi; the
*kurumbidi* concession.

**Arc (6 beats)**
1. "Jayati sri-parishvanga-sharnga-vyanatir Achyutah..." — a Sanskrit salute to Vishnu
   (line 1) — `inscription`
2. Under the Kadamba king "rich in generosity, enemy of the Kalabhoras", two officers
   "terrible as the lion-king and the serpent-king" govern Naridavile-nadu (lines 2–5) —
   `inscription`
3. A warrior of the Bhatari line, "a moon in the clear sky", called Pashupati, who "offered
   sacrifice of beasts in the sacrifice of hundreds of battles in the Dakshinapatha" (lines
   6–10) — `inscription`
4. He fights the Kekaya-Pallavas and wins (line 13) — `inscription`
5. "In the presence of the heroes of the two countries, Sendraka and Bana", the villages
   Palmadi and Mulivalli are given to Vija-arasa as *balgalchu* (lines 11–15) — `inscription`
6. "He who takes it away is a great sinner" (ಕೞ್ದೋನ್ ಮಹಾಪಾತಕನ್); a tenth share to the
   Brahmins (line 16) — `inscription`

**Goosebump moment**: beat 5 — the first time the Kannada language is used to give a
royal order, and it is to honour a soldier. Evidence: the stone itself, Government
Museum, Bengaluru (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Halmidi_stone_inscription-Original.JPG).

**Dramatised vs recorded**: battle location, Vija-arasa's wound or deed, and the ceremony
are not described; the word *balgalchu* (sword-washing) is the only hint. Date (450 vs
late 6th c.) and the identity of "Kakustha" (Kadamba king vs Bhatari chief per G. S. Gai)
are disputed (§5). Who the "Kekaya-Pallavas" are is not settled. A story that stays inside
the sixteen lines is safe; anything else is dramatised.

**Why it grips**: short, concrete, and the reader can see the stone. Best as a
companion piece to Story B or as the "ನಿಮಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತೇ" chapter of the series.

**Evidence base**: the object is secure; everything about its context is contested.

### Story E — The fall: Kirtivarman's axe at the roots of the kadamba (c. 540–600)

**People**: Harivarma (ಹರಿವರ್ಮ), "a moon to the blue lotuses that were the hearts of his
subjects"; Krishnavarma II of Triparvata (ಎರಡನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣವರ್ಮ); Sendraka Bhanusakti
(ಭಾನುಶಕ್ತಿ); Pulakeshin I (ಒಂದನೆಯ ಪುಲಿಕೇಶಿ) at Badami; Kirtivarman I (ಕೀರ್ತಿವರ್ಮ);
Ajavarma (ಅಜವರ್ಮ); Pulakeshin II besieging Banavasi; Ravikirti the poet.

**Arc (7 beats)**
1. Harivarma's year-5 Halsi plate spends its praise on his dead father and gives him one
   line (Moraes 1931 pp. 50–51, quoting Fleet IA VI p. 31–32) — `inscription` + `scholarship`
2. The Sendraka chief Bhanusakti still asks the Kadamba king for a village for the Jain
   monks (Halsi yr 5) — `inscription`
3. Krishnavarma II "acquired the wealth of his kingdom by his heroism" and is "anointed at
   Vaijayanti during a horse-sacrifice" (Bannahalli, Sirsi plates) — `inscription`; that he
   killed Harivarma is inference (Moraes 1931 p. 51: "This would probably imply...") —
   `scholarship`
4. Pulakeshin I founds Badami "exactly in the centre of the northern provinces of the
   Kadamba kingdom" (Moraes 1931 pp. 51–52; Fleet, *Kanarese Dynasties* p. 343) —
   `scholarship`
5. Kirtivarman I (acc. 566–67) is "the night of doom to the Nalas, Mauryas and Kadambas"
   (Aihole prashasti v. 9–10; Kielhorn EI VI) — `inscription`; the Mahakuta pillar names the
   ruler of Vaijayanti among his victims — `inscription`
6. The Kadambas survive as Chalukya vassals (Ajavarma, Bhogivarma) — `scholarship`
7. Two generations later Pulakeshin II besieges Vanavasi, "which by its wealth rivalled the
   city of the gods", its moat so full of his army that it looks like "a fortress in the
   water" (Aihole vv. 17–18) — `inscription`; Xuanzang sees Konkanapura's 100 monasteries
   soon after (Beal II p. 254) — `inscription`

**Goosebump moment**: beat 7 — the same Aihole stone that made the Chalukya series' pilot
shows Banavasi, the first Kannada capital, encircled by water and spears. Evidence:
Aihole prashasti, public-domain translation by Kielhorn — `inscription`.

**Dramatised vs recorded**: the civil war between Harivarma and Krishnavarma II is
Moraes's inference from one epithet; the Sendraka defection is also inference. Record
only what the plates say.

**Why it grips**: it closes the loop with the Chalukya pilot and gives the Kadamba series
a tragic end; adults will accept a fall told from the victor's inscription.

**Evidence base**: the Chalukya side is excellent; the Kadamba side of the fall is nearly
silent.

**Recommendation**: Story A as the first ಕದಂಬರು book (strongest single primary text, the
owner's own pick), with Story D folded in as the "ನಿಮಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತೇ" / sources chapter and
Story E as a short epilogue that hands over to the Chalukya series.

## 4. Little-known facts (one line + source + tier)

1. The Talagunda poem is the earliest known use of its metre (a species of matrasamaka),
   which Kielhorn could not find named in any work on prosody — Kielhorn 1906,
   https://whatisindia.com/inscriptions/epigraphica_indica/vol8_1905-1906/kielhorn13.html —
   `scholarship`
2. The inscription's own simile for the founder's dynasty: Shadanana (Skanda) "anointed the
   father of Kangavarman" — Kielhorn v. 22, kielhorn22.html — `inscription`
3. Mayurasharma entered the Pallava *ghatika* at Kanchi "as a mendicant" (*tarkuka*, a
   suppliant) — Kielhorn v. 10 and n. 7, kielhorn21.html — `inscription`
4. The founder's name ends in -sharman (Brahmin); every descendant's in -varman
   (Kshatriya) — Kielhorn n. 6, kielhorn14.html — `inscription` + `scholarship`
5. Kakusthavarma's Halsi plate opens "Jayati Bhagavan Jinendrah" and gives a field "of the
   holy Arhats" to a general — Fleet IA VI p. 23; Bisschop & Cecil 2021 — `inscription`
6. The Nilambur plates of Ravivarma were found in Kerala by a Kurumba gold-washer on the
   Chaliyar, yet grant villages near Talakad in Mysore — EI VIII pp. 146–149,
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilambur_plates — `inscription`
7. The Sirsi plates of Ravivarma, year 35, were issued from Vijaya-Panktipura, which Gai
   identifies with Hangal, not Banavasi — Gai, EI XXXII,
   https://whatisindia.com/inscriptions/epigraphica_indica/vol32_1957-1958/gai2.html —
   `scholarship`
8. The Talagunda Siva shrine was "honoured by Satakarni and other kings" before the
   Kadambas; a Chutu Satakarni Naga-stone still stands at Banavasi — Kielhorn v. 33;
   puratattva.in/banavasi — `inscription`
9. Queen Prabhavati, mother of Ravivarma, is recorded on the Pranavesvara temple's left
   door-jamb at Talagunda as "born in the noble Kaikeya family" — ASR Mysore 1911; Moraes
   1931 p. 32 — `inscription`
10. Fifteen of 51 early Kadamba inscriptions concern Jainism; twelve record grants to
    basadis; Palasika housed Svetapata, Nirgrantha, Kurchaka and Yapaniya monks — Hampa
    Nagarajaiah 2002 citing Gopal 1985, https://jainqq.org/explore/269033/3 — `scholarship`
11. Halmidi's gift is called a *balgalchu* (ಬಾಳ್ಗೞ್ಚು), "sword-washing", a reward paid to a
    warrior after battle — Halmidi line 14 (kn.wikipedia transcription) — `inscription`
12. The "eighteen horse-sacrifices" of the Kadambas is a title first found in records
    700–800 years after Mayurasharma (e.g. a 1110 CE Uchchangi Kadamba inscription) — Moraes
    1931 pp. 16, 233 — `legend`
13. Xuanzang records at Konkanapura a jewelled tiara of Prince Sarvarthasiddha (the Buddha)
    "somewhat less than two feet in height", shown on fast-days — Beal 1884 II p. 254 —
    `inscription` (eyewitness; the identification with Banavasi is disputed)

## 5. Disputed or uncertain points

1. **Absolute dates** of every early king: see §1.1. Give reigns as "c." and never a year
   for an event unless the record is regnal-dated (Nilambur yr 5, Sirsi yr 35 etc.).
2. **Date of the Talagunda pillar**: Kielhorn "first half of the 6th century"; Pollock
   455–470; Commons captions 450/455–460; Kannada Wikipedia "c. 450" — `disputed`.
3. **Who was the Gupta bridegroom** (v. 31): Samudragupta (Bühler), Chandragupta II
   (Dikshitar), Kumaragupta I (Tej Ram Sharma, Heras), Skandagupta (Lakshminarayana Rao,
   Moraes) — Moraes 1931 pp. 26–27; puratattva.in/talagunda — `disputed`.
4. **Prehara** (eastern boundary of Mayurasharma's grant): Tungabhadra (Rice/Fleet
   guess), Krishna (Lakshminarayana Rao), Pravara (Sohoni), Malaprabha (en.wiki), or
   "Premara = Malva" (Moraes 1931 p. 16); Kielhorn: "no a priori reason to mean a river"
   (kielhorn15.html) — `unknown`.
5. **Shadanana** (v. 22): Kartikeya (Kielhorn) vs a Pallava king, perhaps Sivaskandavarman
   (D. C. Sircar, *Successors of the Satavahanas* 1939) — puratattva.in — `disputed`.
6. **Did Mayurasharma serve as a Pallava dandanayaka?** Kielhorn's reading of v. 20 with
   v. 3 ("great lineage of leaders of armies") and v. 22 — `scholarship`, not stated in
   the text as such.
7. **Chandravalli's list of defeated peoples** (Traikutas, Abhiras, Pallavas, Pariyatrikas,
   Sakasthana, Maukharis/Mokaris, Punnatas, Sendrakas): readings differ; Moraes 1931 p. 322
   cites only "Abhira and Punata"; the inscription is a fragment — `disputed`.
8. **Halmidi date**: c. 450 (Krishna, on palaeography) to late 6th c.; **and its king**:
   G. S. Gai argues "Kakustha of the Bhatari family", not Kadamba Kakusthavarma
   (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmidi_inscription; shastriyakannada.org) — `disputed`.
   Also disputed whether Halmidi is the oldest Kannada inscription at all: the Talagunda
   "lion balustrade" record (c. 370?) was announced by ASI in 2017 as older (kn.wikipedia
   ಹಲ್ಮಿಡಿ ಶಾಸನ lead; en.wiki Kadamba dynasty) — **primary publication not found in this
   pass**; S. Settar argued a Ganga record is older (shastriyakannada.org).
9. **Kadamba origin**: Brahmin Vedic family (Talagunda; Sastri, Kamath, Moraes) vs a local
   Kadambu tribe (Chopra et al.) vs northern immigrants — en.wiki Kadamba dynasty — `disputed`;
   the Talagunda text is explicit that they were Brahmins of the Manavya gotra —
   `inscription`.
10. **Religion**: no inscription names a family sect; Talagunda invokes Siva; Kakustha,
    Mrigesha, Ravivarma, Harivarma patronise Jains; Sircar suggests some were Vaishnava —
    `disputed`.
11. **Vishnuvarma slain by Ravivarma**: Pallava Vishnugopa (Fleet) vs Triparvata cousin
    (Moraes 1931 p. 47 n.) — `disputed`. **Chandadanda**: no Pallava record names him.
12. **Shivamandhatrivarma as usurper**: Moraes's reading only — `scholarship`.
13. **How Harivarma's line ended** (died heirless and adopted Krishnavarma II, or was
    killed by him): Moraes 1931 p. 51 presents both — `disputed`.
14. **Kama-jinalaya at Gudnapur**: Bahubali temple (Gopal, Nagarajaiah) vs a Kama/Manmatha
    shrine with court-performance context (Sohoni; Cecil & Gomes) — `disputed`.
15. **Konkanapura = Banavasi**: St. Martin yes; Cunningham Anagundi; Fergusson another
    (Beal II p. 254 n. 40) — `disputed`.
16. **Birur plates** genuineness doubted by Kielhorn; Siddham labels them spurious —
    `disputed`; the Krishnavarma I viceroyalty rests partly on them.
17. **Dynastic emblem**: a lion is attributed to the Goa Kadambas (coins; Kadamba Transport
    Corporation logo since 1980, en.wiki Kadambas of Goa); **no early Banavasi Kadamba
    emblem is attested in any source found** — `unknown`. Do not give the early kings a
    lion banner as fact.
18. **Extent under Ravivarma "Narmada to Kaveri"** (Sircar on the Davanagere plates, 519
    CE) — en.wiki only; **not verified** — `scholarship`, flag.

Open items the next pass should close (search budget ran out): Fleet IA VI scan for exact
Halsi wording; Gopal 1985 CKI pp. 85–87 for the Gudnapur text; MAR 1929 pp. 50–60 for
Chandravalli; MAR 1936 for Halmidi; the 2017 ASI Talagunda "lion balustrade" report; the
Pampa verse numbers; Cecil & Gomes 2021 full text; the Goa Kadamba "Madhumod/Modamod" record.

### 6.2 What the texts themselves describe (use these as prompt facts)
- A Brahmin household with a kadamba tree in bloom beside it, fire altars, kusa grass,
  ladle, soma (Talagunda vv. 4–8, 13).
- The *ghatika* at Kanchi: an establishment for learned men, probably royal (Kielhorn n. 6).
- A Pallava horseman; frontier guards; a night attack on an army "marching or resting in
  rough country"; forest up to the gates of Sriparvata (vv. 11–18).
- A royal fillet (*patta*) tied on by the Pallavas' own hands (v. 20).
- Chiefs bowing so that their chowries shake the king's diadem (v. 23).
- Palace gateways scented with the ichor of elephants in rut; songs within (v. 30).
- A great tank beside a Siva shrine at Talagunda (v. 33) — the tank still exists per
  puratattva.in.
- A palace with a Kama temple to its left, dancing halls and women's apartments (Gudnapur,
  per secondary accounts).
- Halmidi: a sword washed after battle (*balgalchu*); Sendraka and Bana warriors as
  witnesses; two villages; a stone with a Sanskrit line to Vishnu on top.
- Konkanapura (if Banavasi): a great monastery beside the royal palace, a vihara "a hundred
  feet and more in height", a jewelled tiara in a casket (Beal II p. 254).

### 6.3 Dress, arms, animals: evidence status
- **No Kadamba-period sculpture of a king, soldier or courtier was found in this pass.**
  For 4th–6th-century Deccan dress and weapons the safest models are the Badami cave
  reliefs (c. 578, Chalukya) and Ajanta paintings (Vakataka, 5th c.) — both are neighbours
  and near-contemporaries; cite them as "period analogues", not Kadamba evidence.
- Elephants with ichor at palace gates (v. 30); horsemen (v. 11); chowries and diadems
  (v. 23); a sword as the royal weapon (vv. 13, 18) — `inscription`.
- Ships: Banavasi is inland; the only maritime note is Mayurasharma's territory "on the
  western sea" (v. 21). Goa-Kadamba ships are 11th–13th c. — anachronistic here.

### 6.4 Anachronisms to exclude in every prompt
- Lion or Gandabherunda emblems on early banners (lion is Goa Kadamba, 11th c.+; no early
  emblem attested).
- The Madhukesvara stone cot, the Sonda prakara, Hoysala-style soapstone carving, the
  11th-c. Pranavesvara doorway, the 1028 CE hero stone at Talagunda
  (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Virgal_(hero_stone)_in_Praneshvara_temple_at_Talagunda.JPG,
  Kalyani Chalukya).
- Vijayanagara-style gopuras, turbans and jewellery; Mughal dress; gunpowder; stirrups
  (not evidenced); European ship forms.
- Nagari script on stone (early records are box-headed southern Brahmi / early Kannada).
- Any "third eye" or four-armed founder except in a scene explicitly labelled ಐತಿಹ್ಯ.
- The 1975 film's costumes and its characters (Premavati, Chandravarma, Ranga Jetti).

## 7. Key people beyond the kings

- **Kubja (ಕುಬ್ಜ)** — court poet who composed and had engraved the Talagunda pillar poem
  "under the orders of Santivarman" (v. 34); Pollock calls its Sanskrit "wonderful literary
  Sanskrit" — `inscription`.
- **Virasharma (ವೀರಶರ್ಮ)** — Mayurasharma's guru (and grandfather per Gudnapur), who took

- **Prabhavati (ಪ್ರಭಾವತಿ)** — Kekaya princess, queen of Mrigeshavarma, mother of Ravivarma;

- **Srutakirti (ಶ್ರುತಕೀರ್ತಿ)** — general rewarded by Kakusthavarma with the Arhats' field

- **Damakirti (ದಾಮಕೀರ್ತಿ)** — royal priest whose village Kheta passed to his mother on his
  death under Santivarma (Moraes 1931 p. 29) — `inscription` via Moraes.
- **Kannaya** — built two temples whose opening Santivarma attended (Moraes 1931 p. 29)
  — `inscription` via Moraes.
- **Vija-arasa (ವಿಜ ಅರಸ)** of the Bhatari family — Halmidi grantee; fought the
  Kekaya-Pallavas — `inscription`.
- **Mrigesha and Naga (ಮೃಗೇಶ, ನಾಗ)** — governors of Naridavile-nadu in Halmidi —
  `inscription`.
- **Bhanuvarma (ಭಾನುವರ್ಮ)** — Ravivarma's younger brother, donor at Palasika in Ravivarma's
  year 11 (Fleet IA VI p. 29) — `inscription`.
- **Shivaratha (ಶಿವರಥ)** — Harivarma's paternal uncle, adviser of the year-4 grant (Fleet
  IA VI p. 30) — `inscription`.
- **Bhanusakti (ಭಾನುಶಕ್ತಿ)** — Sendraka chief, "glory of the family of the Sendrakas",
  feudatory who requested Marade village for the Jain Sramanas (Halsi, Harivarma yr 5) —
  `inscription`.
- **Nilakantha (ನೀಲಕಂಠ)** — Ravivarma's "beloved physician" and desa-amatya, at whose
  request the Sirsi grant was made (Gai, EI XXXII) — `inscription`.
- **Govinda-svami** — Kasyapa-gotra Brahmin, Yajurveda master, grantee of the Nilambur

- **Sivakhada-nagasiri** — Chutu princess of the Banavasi Naga-stone, pre-Kadamba (EI

- **Chandadanda (ಚಂಡದಂಡ)** — "Lord of Kanchi" uprooted by Ravivarma; otherwise unknown —

- **Xuanzang (ಹ್ಯೂಯನ್ ತ್ಸಾಂಗ್)** — Chinese pilgrim, c. 640, at Konkanapura — eyewitness.
- **Ravikirti (ರವಿಕೀರ್ತಿ)** — Jain poet of the Aihole prashasti (634) that records the
  Kadambas' destruction and the siege of Vanavasi — `inscription`.
- **B. L. Rice, F. Kielhorn, J. F. Fleet, M. H. Krishna, B. R. Gopal, A. Sundara, G. S.
  Gai, G. M. Moraes** — the modern discoverers and editors (dates in §2); useful for a

## 8. Sources to avoid (or use only as finding aids)

- **English Wikipedia "Kadamba dynasty" / "Ravivarma of Kadamba" / "Mayurasharma"**:
  internally inconsistent dates (Ravivarma 485–519 in text vs 497–537 in table; Talagunda
  450 vs 455); cites T. Manjanna 2015 (IJRAR, non-peer-reviewed) and kamat.com; the
  "Talagunda lion balustrade 370 CE" and "Narmada to Kaveri" claims are unverified there.
  Use only to find primary citations.
- **Kannada Wikipedia ಕದಂಬ ರಾಜವಂಶ**: ruler list has "ಬಗೀತಾರ್ಹ" for Bhagiratha and includes
  "ಒಂದನೆಯ ಪುಲಿಕೇಶಿ" in the Kadamba table; references are news items. Spellings only.
- **Kannada Wikipedia ಹಲ್ಮಿಡಿ ಶಾಸನ**: transcription is from a blog (appaaji.blogspot);
  page is tagged as needing citations; use the text only after checking against Krishna
  1936 / shastriyakannada.org.
- **newworldencyclopedia.org, clearias.com, lotusarise.com, indianetzone.com, historyfiles,
  nupam.com, kamat.com (Kamat's Potpourri)**: uncited popular pages; repeat the
  "18 asvamedhas", "first Kannada dynasty", "Havyaka Brahmins from Ahichchhatra" claims as
  fact.
- **Auction/dealer coin pages (legends.com.tw, numista listings)**: attributions are

- **Travel journalism (Deccan Herald, The Better India, enidhi.net)**: good for site

- **The film *Mayura* (1975) and Devudu's novel**: fiction; Chandravarma, Sivaskandavarma

- **Any page asserting a Kadamba lion/Gandabherunda emblem for the 4th–6th c.**: no early

- **Pallava-side Trilochana Pallava stories** (11th c.) and Nagarkhanda Kadamba "Nanda"

  translations freely (PD), but label his reconstructions `scholarship` and his
  Kielhorn-era dates as superseded.
