# Belavadi Mallamma (ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ), the siege of 1678: verification pass (verbatim sources)

Prepared 2026-10-18 as the check on `belavadi-mallamma.md`. Series `veera-naariyaru` (ವೀರ ನಾರಿಯರು), story 1.
Everything below was read this session in the editions named; nothing is from memory. Quotations are
verbatim from the archive.org OCR text (djvu.txt), with obvious OCR breakage silently repaired
(line-end hyphenation rejoined, stray marks and running heads removed, "Shiva's" for "Shiva’s").
Where the original uses a long dash it is printed here as " : " and flagged [dash]. Each item: URL
fetched, quotation, tier (`inscription` / `scholarship` / `legend`). Anything not reached is marked
UNVERIFIED. Source texts were saved in the session scratchpad, not in the repo.

Tier note for this story. There is **no inscription and no eyewitness** for the siege. The nearest
thing to a contemporary record is one English factory letter written from Rajapur while the siege was
going on (§3a); it is a report of hearsay: the letter's "their owne people come from him" are the Deccani (Bijapur) party's men returning from Shivaji's camp, not Shivaji's own men (correction 2026-10-20), so it is `inscription` tier only in
the README's sense of "contemporary record". The Marathi chronicles (bakhars) were written 16 years
(Sabhasad, 1694) to 130+ years (Chitnis, 1810) later; they are `scholarship`-weight sources for what
the Maratha side remembered, and their extra details are `legend` unless corroborated. Everything in
the Kannada tradition (Ishaprabhu, Sode, the cows, the women's army, the reconciliation) is `legend`.

## 1. How good is each Marathi source? (Sarkar's own judgement)

SARKAR 1920 p. 449 (Bibliography no. 101, Sabhasad):
> written in 1694 at Jinji, by order of Raja Ram ... A small book of barely 100 pages, composed from
> memory without the help of written memoranda or documents. The events are not arranged in the order
> of time ... Some of the statements are incorrect. Weak in topography, no dates.
and p. 449–450: "But the most valuable Marathi account of Shivaji and our only source of information
from the Maratha side."

SARKAR 1920 p. 450 (no. 103, Chitnis): "Incorrect, rambling or pure guess-work in many places. No
State-paper used, no idea of correct chronology."

SARKAR 1920 pp. 450–451 (no. 104, Shivadigvijay):
> Falsely described as written by Khando Ballal (the son of Shivaji's secretary Balaji Avji) in 1718.
> The published version was evidently fabricated at Baroda by a writer familiar with the style of modern
> vernacular novels
and: "the kernel of the book is some lost Marathi work composed about 1760-'75 and containing, among
many loose traditions, a few facts the truth of which we know from contemporary Factory Records. This
lost source was also the basis of the Persian Tarikh-i-Shivaji, which agrees with Shivadigvijay in many
passages."

SARKAR 1920 p. 455 (Persian no. 19): "Tarikh-i-Shivaji. India Office Persian MS. 1957 ... 'The work of a
Hindu based on Maratha tradition.'"

SEN SC p. 258 (Notes, on the author of Shivadigvijaya):
> he devotes page after page to an account of the gallant defence of Bhuikot of Belvadi by a Prabhu
> Kayastha lady.
(Same page: "It is certain that the author was a Prabhu Kayastha by caste".) The "Bhuikot" (ground
fort, i.e. a fort on the plain, not a hill fort) and the caste are the Shivadigvijaya author's; see §2c.

Use: Sabhasad is the anchor; the T.S. and Shivadigvijay details (sortie, dishonour, blinding) are late
tradition, tier `legend`, told only as "the Maratha chronicles of the next century say".

## 2. What happened: the record

### 2a. Sabhasad (1694), the earliest Maratha account
SEN SC p. 128 (Marathi text p. [91]):
> The Raje went to Kopal, accompanied by Anand Rav. Thence he went to Lakshmesvar. Thence he marched by
> stages to the province of Sampganv. There was a fort called Belvadi. There (ruled) a woman Desain.
> She carried off the transport bullocks of the Raje's army. The Raje learnt this news and laid siege to
> Belvadi, captured the fort, (and) arrested the Desain. She was punished. Then he returned to Panhala
> and made an enquiry about the state of the kingdom.
Tier `scholarship` (chronicle, 16 years after). No name, no husband, no length of siege, no Sakhoji.

SEN SC p. 128 note 152 (both scans agree):
> Grant Duff wrongly identifies it with Bellary, the chief town of a Madras district. Belvadi is a small
> village about 10 miles from Sampganv. The name of this brave lady was Saraswati Bai and she was a
> Prabhu by caste. (J. N. Sarkar.)
"Saraswati Bai" contradicts Sarkar's own printed text ("Savitri Bai", §2b) in all three editions read;
treat as a slip in a note passed to Sen. Do not use the name.

SARKAR 1919 p. 400 fn (= 1920 p. 353 fn), Sarkar's summary of the three chronicles' routes [dash]:
> His route is thus given in Sabhasad, 91 : Kolhar : Ballapur : Kopal : Lakshmishwar : Khangauda desai
> chastised : Sampgaon district : Balvada desain invested, captured, and "taught a lesson" : Panhala.
> Chitnis, 142 : Srirangapattan : Gadag : Lakshmishwar : Khangauda desai fled : Gadag : Balved desain
> Mal Bai besieged for 27 days, captured and released. Shivadigvijay, 347-357 : Savitri Bai of Belvadi
> besieged : Gadag : Lakshmishwar : Gaunda desai fled : Balved desain loots transport, is besieged and
> captured.
Tier `scholarship`. Note: **Chitnis names her "Mal Bai"** and says **27 days** and **released**.

### 2b. Sarkar's narrative
SARKAR 1920 p. 354 (= 1919 p. 401, running head "A WOMAN FIGHTS SHIVA"):
> Bankapur, 20 miles s. w. of Lakshmishwar, was besieged unsuccessfully, about the middle of January,
> 1678. (O. C. 4314.) From this place Shivaji retraced his steps northwards, and arrived near Sampgaon in
> the Belgaum district. At Belvadi, a small village 12 miles s. e. of Sampgaon and 30 miles s. e. of
> Belgaum, Savitri Bai, the widowed lady proprietor, plundered some transport bullocks of Shiva's army
> when passing by. Her fort was at once besieged, but she defended it most heroically for 27 days, after
> which it was carried by assault and she herself was captured.
Tier `scholarship` (Sarkar combining Sabhasad + Chitnis's 27 days + Dig./T.S. name).

SARKAR 1920 p. 354–355 (= 1919 p. 402):
> This long check by a woman, before a petty mud-fort, greatly lowered Shivaji's prestige.

SARKAR 1929 p. 316–317 repeats the narrative word for word (with "turned his steps northwards" and
"threatened" for "besieged unsuccessfully") and glosses *patelni* differently: "the patelni (village
headman's wife) of Belvadi" (1919/1920: "patelni (proprietress)").

SARKAR 1929 Chronology (pp. 425–426), 1678 [dash]:
> c. 16 Jan. Sh. at Lakshmishwar.
> c. 26 Jan. : 23 Feb. Sh. besieges Belvadi.
> 21 Feb. Siddi Masaud becomes wazir of Bijapur.
> c. 4 April. Sh. reaches Panhala.
Tier `scholarship` (Sarkar's reconstruction; 26 Jan to 23 Feb is 28 days, fitted to Chitnis's 27).

### 2c. The Tarikh-i-Shivaji: sortie, capture, dishonour, the blinding of Sakhoji
SARKAR 1919 p. 401 fn (= 1920 p. 354 fn, = 1929 p. 316–317 fn), Sarkar's translation of T.S. f. 38a:
> A woman named Savitri was the patelni (proprietress) of Belvadi. From the shelter of her fort she
> fought Shiva for one month. On her provisions and munitions running short, she made a sortie,
> demolished all the siege trenches, and dispersed and slew many of the besiegers. For one day she kept
> the field heroically, but at last fled vanquished, was captured and greatly dishonoured. Sakhuji
> Gaikwar was the doer of this evil deed. Shivaji, on hearing of his act, put out both his eyes and thus
> gave him his deserts. He was imprisoned in the village of Manauli.
Tier `legend` (a Persian text "based on Maratha tradition", from a lost Marathi source of c. 1760–75,
SARKAR 1920 p. 451). This is the **only** source read that names Sakhoji in this episode and the only
one for the blinding. It does not say what "greatly dishonoured" means; do not specify it.

ARUR thesis p. 105 (short quotation, modern):
> The text of 91 Qalami Bakhar speaks of the amarous nature of one Sakhuji Gaikwad, who coveted the
> Desain. On hearing of this Shivaji punished him severely by putting out his eyes.
and p. 105: "According to Pagadi, Sakhuji Gaikwad, the brother in law of Shivaji was blinded but for
some other reason - treason, who based this assumption from Martin's memories." Tier `scholarship`
(report of the 91-Kalami and of Setu Madhavrao Pagadi). The 91-Kalami text itself: UNVERIFIED (not
reached in English). Pagadi's book: UNVERIFIED (not opened).

SEN FBS pp. 333–334, François Martin's memoir (Pondicherry), in his entries for 1678:
> The Mogol none-the-less spared nothing for getting rid of him, Sivagy discovered a conspiracy by his
> brother-in-law against him which was said to have its source from the Mogol. The traitor was arrested
> and had his eyes put out.
Tier `inscription` (contemporary foreign memoir, hearsay). **Martin does not name the brother-in-law
and does not connect it with Belavadi.** That this is Sakhoji is Pagadi's inference (via ARUR).

MEHENDALE, "Campaign in the Gadag District": a "Sakhoji Gayakawad" commanded a force sent from Umbraj
to attack Khatav on the way to the Gingee expedition (1676), from a Marathi document of 1719. So a
Maratha officer of this name existed in this campaign. `scholarship`.

Shivaji's own rule on women, SEN SC p. 32 (Sabhasad):
> There should be no women, female slaves, or dancing girls in the army. He who would keep them should
> be beheaded. In enemy territories, women and children should not be captured. Males, if found, should
> be captured. Cows should not be taken. Bullocks should be requisitioned for transport purposes only.
> ... No one should commit adultery.
(SARKAR 1920 p. 417 gives the same rules in his own translation.) `scholarship`. Context for why a
punishment for misconduct towards a captive woman is plausible; it is not evidence that it happened.

### 2d. Grant Duff (1826): the earliest English narrative, with a different cause
DUFF 1826 vol. I pp. 283–284 (= 1863 reprint p. 206):
> When Sivajee's troops arrived in the neighbourhood of Bellary, a few of his foragers were killed by
> some of the people belonging to the fort, which was then in possession of the widow of a Dessaye. As
> satisfaction was refused, the outrage furnished an excuse for attacking the place, which was invested
> and taken after a siege of twenty-seven days. Sivajee next besieged and took Kopaul.
Tier `scholarship`. Duff puts it at "Bellary" and before Kopal; Sen (note 152) and Sarkar correct the
place to Belvadi near Sampgaon. Duff's cause: foragers **killed**; satisfaction refused.

### 2e. Mehendale (modern): Jedhe date, names, Chitnis's ending
MEHENDALE (short quotations; modern):
> The Jedhe Chronology says he arrived in the Gadag district in the month of Kartik of Shaka 1599 (17th
> October to 14th November 1677) and invested Belavadi.
> This village, about 20 km southeast of Sampgaon, boasted a gadhi : a small mud fort : held by the
> widow of the local Desai. [dash]
> Sabhasad says that she was punished, whereas according to Chitnis she was released and given a small
> estate.
Endnote 3316:
> Sabhasad does not give her name; Chitnis gives it as Malawai, and the A. K. Chronicle as Savitribai.
> According to local tradition, her name was Mallawwa. (Chhatrapati Shivajimaharaj Smritigrantha, p.24.)
Tier `scholarship`. The Jedhe line is via Mehendale only; Jedhe Shakavali itself UNVERIFIED (not opened).
Note the Jedhe month (Kartik 1599 = Oct–Nov 1677) clashes with the English letter of 28 Feb 1678 (§3a),
which has him still besieging; Mehendale himself places the siege on the way back to Panhala.

## 3. The only contemporary record

### 3a. Rajapur to Surat, 28 February 1677/8
The same letter (read in full 2026-10-20) also reports Shivaji's outstanding debt to the Company, the subject of its earlier lines; that is the "ಬಾಕಿ ಸಾಲದ ಸುದ್ದಿ" the story's scene-08 names.
ERS II no. 285 (F.R. Surat vol. 107 p. 60, "Rajapore to Surat, dated 28 February 1677/8", extract):
> They have likewise sent for Sevagee to be in person at their solem entrance for that as they stiled him
> to be their head, otherwise to send 8000000 Pagodas towards expences. He is at present beseiging a
> fort where, by relation of their owne people come from him, he has suffered more disgrace than ever he
> did from all power of the Mogull or Decans, and he who hath conquered soe many kingdomes is not able
> to reduce this woman Desy
Tier `inscription` (contemporary letter; hearsay from the Deccani party's men who had come from Shivaji's camp: "They" in the preceding sentence are the Deccanis under Sharza Khan and Siddi Masud). It supports exactly four things:
on or before 28 Feb 1678 Shivaji was besieging a fort; the fort was held by a woman; she was a desai
("Desy"); the siege was going badly enough for men who came from his camp to call it a disgrace. It does **not** name
her or the fort, does not say she beat him, and was written before the siege ended. Sarkar prints it
as "this woman Desai!" (1920 p. 355); the exclamation mark is Sarkar's. Sarkar's identification of
this fort with Belvadi (by date and route) is `scholarship`.

ERS II no. 284, Karwar to Surat, February 1677/8: advices from Hubli that "Sedde Mursude is in
possession of Vizapore for the Duccanees" (context: the Bijapur coup of 21 Feb).

SARKAR 1920 p. 356 (= 1919 p. 403):
> The news of the transfer of the Adil-Shahi capital to Siddi Masaud (21st February) reached Shivaji on
> his way from Belvadi through Turgal to Bijapur, and he swerved aside to the west and returned to his
> own stronghold of Panhala at the end of March or in the first week of April, 1678.
`scholarship`.

### 3b. Where Shivaji's Karnataka holdings stood afterwards
BG XXI p. 375: "in the year before [1679] Shivaji had taken several of the Bijapur-Karnatak districts".
p. 376: "At the time of his death in 1680 Shivaji held the Belgaum country south to the Harankashi a
feeder of the Ghatprabha, together with the forts of Pargad and Kalanandigad in Belgaum, Bhimgad in
Khanapur, Vallabhgad, Mahipalgad, Pavitragad in Chikodi, and Murgod, ... Kathdrigad, and Huli in
Parasgad." Belvadi is not in the list. `scholarship`.

## 4. The place

BG XXI p. 434 (Sampgaon survey, 1849–57): "Sampgaon was under the charge of a mamlatdar whose office
was at the town of Sampgaon, with a mahalkari stationed at Belvadi." (Belvadi was a sub-divisional
centre in the 19th century.) p. 236: red soils "in the Belavadi and Ambadgatti village groups or
khariyats of Sampgaon." p. 16: gold "associated with hematite beds in some of the streamlets about Bail
Hongal and Belovadi." pp. 51–52:
> gold was also found at Belevadi on the south side of the Malprabha, where the stream flows in a hollow
> between two parallel ridges of metamorphic rock.
and "The greatest proportion of gold, though the amount was small, was found in a small stream to the
west of Belevadi." `scholarship` (19th-c. survey). **BG XXI has no "Belvadi" entry in its Places chapter
and does not mention the 1678 siege or Mallamma anywhere** (searched in full).

BG XXI p. 600 (Sampgaon): an "old mosque though small and low-roofed is a beautiful building";
"The earliest mention of Sampgaon is in 1683 when Aurangzeb's son Prince Muhammad Maazzam is mentioned
as besieging the fort of Sampgaon"; "Towards the end of the seventeenth century the founder of the
Kittur Desai family, who came into the district with the Bijapur army, settled at Sampgaon."

BG XXI pp. 586–588 (Manoli, on the Malprabha, Parasgad): "The fort is a sandstone work standing on a
peninsula round which the river winds"; in 1786 "Parshuram Bhau took Manoli fort from the Torgal
chief". **Identification of T.S. "Manauli" with this Manoli is this pass's suggestion only: UNVERIFIED.**

BETAGERI 1951, closing note "ಬೆಳವಡಿಯ ಪರಿಚಯ" (p. 22): Belavadi is in Bailhongal taluk; apart from a
Veerabhadra temple no remains of the old capital are seen; the Belavadi state was finally destroyed by
"ಕಿತ್ತೂರಿನ ದೊರೆ ಮಲಸರ್ಜ"; the Chachadi, Rudrapur, Talur and Desai families are said to descend from it;
and "the people of that country say" memorial stones with figures of Mallamma and Shivaji still stand.
Tier `legend` (1951, local report). The Kittur claim: UNVERIFIED.

## 5. Her family: what can be checked

BG XV.2 p. 120 fn 3 (Kanara Gazetteer 1883, from the Sonda priest via Buchanan) [the family tree]:
> Imodi Arasappa Naik (1555-1598). Ramchandra Naik (1598-1618). Ragonath Naik (1618-1638). Madhu Linga
> Naik (1638-1674). Sadashiv Raya (1674-1697). Baswa Linga Raya (1697-1745). Imodi Sadashiv Raya (... 1763).
p. 121 (OCR prints "491"): "It was not till the decay of Bijapur power in 1674 that Sadashiv changed the dependent title of
Naik into Raja." `scholarship`.
This confirms that a **Madhulinga Nayaka ruled Sonda (ಸೋದೆ) 1638–1674**, followed by a **Sadashiva**:
exactly the father and brother the Kannada tradition gives her. **No source read says Madhulinga had a
daughter named Mallamma or that she married into Belavadi.** That link rests on the tradition (§6).

Husband: **no Marathi, Persian or English source read names him.** Duff (1826) and Sarkar (1919) call
her a widow at the time of the siege; Mehendale "the widow of the local Desai". The name ಈಶಪ್ರಭು
(Ishaprabhu) appears in the Kannada tradition (ARUR p. 102 "She married Isha Prabhu the chief of
Belavadi"; BETAGERI 1951 cast list "ಈಶಪ್ರಭು (ಬೆಳವಡಿಯ ದೊರೆ)"; Kannada Wikipedia "ಈಶಪ್ರಭು"). Tier
`legend`. Spelling used in this project: **ಈಶಪ್ರಭು** (not Ishwaraprabhu, not Yesaji Prabhu; "Yesaji"
appears in no source read).

Child: the Yadwad slab tradition (§7) and the 1951 play give her an infant son (the play names him
ನಾಗಭೂಷಣ). No record. `legend`.

Birth year 1624, death 1678 (English Wikipedia infobox): uncited there, and in no source read.
UNVERIFIED. (A 1624 birth would make her about 54 in 1678, which sits badly with the infant of the
Yadwad tradition; neither can be checked.)

## 6. The Kannada and late-Marathi tradition (all `legend`)

ARUR pp. 101–106 is the fullest account reached. Its sources: local tradition; Siddhanti Shivabasava
Shastri's *Turukari Panchamara Itihasavu* (Kannada; Kannada Wikipedia says first published 1929 and calls
it "ತರಾತುರಿ ಪಂಚಮರ ಇತಿಹಾಸ"); and *Shivaji Mallammaji Samarotsava* (Marathi), said to be by Shesho
Srinivasa, "an officer in Belavadi kingdom" (ARUR Preface p. III). ARUR's summary (short quotations):
- Family: "Mallamma was the daugther of the Prince of Swadi (or Sondha) in the present North Kanara
  district of Karnataka. She married Isha Prabhu the chief of Belavadi in Belgaum district."
- Cause: "the harrassment of the Belavadi farmers by Shivaji's men who carried away the former's
  livestock to their camp" (Kannada side) against the Marathi version that "the Belavadi Desai carried
  off the Rajas bullocks".
- Per the Samarotsava: the Desai sent his commander "Siddangaud Patil" to fetch back the cows; he was
  insulted; Mallamma "offered to lead her force of two thousand women soldiers, accompanied by three
  thousand female body guards"; on "1600 Saka (1678) Paushya, Purnima day" she attacked and "with in an
  hour two hundred men were wounded and ten to twelve men were dead"; then a siege of fifteen days;
  Ishaprabhu "mortally wounded" in a sortie; an informer "Shantayya" sends Shivaji to a Bhavani temple
  "at Lokur near Yadwad"; she ambushes him; "Mallamma looked like 'Jagdamba' (Goddess) to him and he
  began to pray"; "Shivaji raised the seige and honoured Mallamma treating her like his mother"; later
  she escorted him "uptil Torgal".
- Local tradition: "when Mallamma was wounded Shivaji arranged for her medical treatment."
- ARUR's own caution: "No serious mention is made in contemporary Maratha Bakhars."

Date check on the Samarotsava (this pass's calculation): Shaka 1600 began in spring 1678, so Paushya of
Shaka 1600 falls about December 1678 / January 1679, when Shivaji was back in Maharashtra (Sarkar: at
Panhala by April 1678; Mehendale: at Raigad by June 1678). Either the year is Shaka 1599 (Paushya =
Dec 1677 / Jan 1678, which fits) or the text is wrong. Do not use the date.

MEHENDALE endnote 3317, on the Samarotsava (short quotations):
> Recently, in 1989, a small Marathi book entitled Shivaji-Mallammaji Samarotsava was published by the
> Kannada Department of Mumbai University. The editor, Dr. K. N. Chitnis, calls it "An early 18th Century
> Manuscript."
The text claims to be an entry in a biography competition held by Tarabai, where "Shesho's book won
third prize"; the original is lost; a copy "in the Marathi language but Kannada script made after
Mallamma's death in 1717, is said to have been found at Belavadi." In it Shivaji, trapped, "fell at
Mallamma's feet and begged for forgiveness".
> The editor admits that the language seems modern, that there are several interpolations, and that the
> claim in the manuscript of Mallamma's victory over Shivaji is false.
> I have no doubt that is a modern fabrication and as certain details in it are without doubt taken from
> Shri-Shivadigvijaya Chronicle, it could not have been written before 1818.
Tier: the Samarotsava is `legend`; Mehendale's verdict is `scholarship`. The book itself: UNVERIFIED (not
reached). Note the conflict: ARUR calls it "contemporary"; its own editor and Mehendale do not.

Kannada Wikipedia (raw wikitext, finding aid) also names a Sanskrit *ಶಿವವಂಶ ಸುಧಾರ್ಣವ* by "ಶಂಕರ ಭಟ್ಟ",
"Mallamma's teacher", which it says records her victory and won Tarabai's first prize. Not found in any
source read; UNVERIFIED. It also says, correctly against the folk claim, "ಶಿವಾಜಿ ಮತ್ತು ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ ನಡುವೆ
ನಡೆದ ಯುದ್ದದಲ್ಲಿ ಶಿವಾಜಿ ಮಹಾರಾಜರು ಭಾಗವಹಿಸಲಿರಲಿಲ್ಲ" (uncited), and that Shivaji accepted her "as a
sister" (ತಂಗಿ), returned the fort and paid for the damage (uncited).

English Wikipedia (finding aid) adds the swayamvara (suitors to hunt tigers equal to their age plus one;
Ishaprabhu, 20, killed 21) and the teachers "Shankar Bhat" and "Ranavir Singh", marked "citation
needed", sourced only to Basavaraj Naikar's fiction *The Rebellious Rani of Belavadi and Other Stories*
(Atlantic, 2001). Tier `legend`; Naikar not opened.

BETAGERI 1951 (radio play, AIR Dharwad; `legend`, a 20th-century literary work): sets the opening in
"ಕ್ರಿ. ಶ. ೧೬೬೮", gives Belavadi 360 villages, has Shivaji's camp at ಯಾದವಾಡ, the cattle seized from the
"ತುರುಹಟ್ಟಿ", Ishaprabhu felled from his horse by a treacherous blow of Shivaji's general ("ವೆಂಕೋಜಿ" in
the play), Mallamma charging out with "ಜಯ್ ವೀರಭದ್ರ", and Shivaji stopping the war and calling her
"ತಾಯಾ" and "ಅಮ್ಮಾಜಿ". Her line to him: "ನೀನು ಶೌರ್ಯದಿಂದಲ್ಲದಿದ್ದರೂ ಔದಾರ್ಯದಿಂದ ನನ್ನ ಮನಸ್ಸನ್ನು ಗೆದ್ದೆ".
Shivaji promises protection and attends the crowning of the boy. The play's dates and names (1668,
Venkoji as Shivaji's general) are invention.

Films: no film titled *Belavadi Mallamma* was found (searches 2026-10-18; the Kannada Mallamma films found,
*Hemareddy Mallamma* 1946 and *Mahasadhvi Mallamma* 2005, are about the saint Hemareddy Mallamma). The
"1960s film" in the brief: UNVERIFIED, probably confusion with those titles or with *Kittur Chennamma*.
Folk ballads (ಲಾವಣಿ): none found. Shraddha L. Belavadi's Hampi PhD on women in Kannada folk narrative
songs (2019, https://archive.org/details/kuh.1049.shr.141630) was searched: no Mallamma ballad in it.
UNVERIFIED that any ಲಾವಣಿ exists.

## 7. The Yadwad slab (the only object)

MEHENDALE (short quotation; endnote 3319 cites *Chhatrapati Shivajimaharaj Smritigrantha*, pp. 23–25):
> Shivaji's chivalrous conduct towards this lady is indicated by a stone sculpture found in the village
> of Yadvad, about 10 km north of Dharwad. It is carved in a 3'x2.5' stone slate. A bearded man is shown
> sitting with a child in his lap and a glass in his hand. It is said that Mallawwa caused such
> sculptures to be put up in many villages in her jurisdiction ... But this is the only one that has been
> discovered so far.
ARUR Preface p. II lists "the sculptural panels of Shivaji and Mallamma of Belavadi, found at Yadwad near
Dharwar" and "Visual nos. 4 & 5" (photos not in the OCR); endnote cites G. S. Dixit, "A new portrait of
Shivaji", A. G. Pawar Felicitation Volume, p. 21 (UNVERIFIED, not reached).
Nayak 2021 (eSamskriti / DIPR, finding aid): in the Hanuman temple at Yadwad; black granite, three feet
high, two and a half wide, arched top, two panels; upper: Shivaji on horseback with sword and shield,
attendants with umbrella and staffs, a dog; lower: Shivaji feeding milk from a bowl to Mallamma's infant
on his lap, Mallamma standing with bow and arrow, sari in the "Veerakachche" style; "A few inscriptions
in Kannada are found at the top of this panel but they are illegible." Nayak calls it "the only
surviving image of Shivaji made during his lifetime": UNVERIFIED (no date for the slab in any source
read).
Tier: the slab exists (`scholarship`, several independent reports); its meaning and date are `legend`.
