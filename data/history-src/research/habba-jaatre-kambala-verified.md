# Kambala (ಕಂಬಳ): research brief (verified 2026-11-24)

## Read first

- **Solid:**
  - Twin slush tracks (ಜೋಡುಕರೆ) and four race classes: ನೇಗಿಲು, ಹಗ್ಗ, ಅಡ್ಡ ಹಲಗೆ, ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ. ನೇಗಿಲು and ಹಗ್ಗ are split into ಹಿರಿಯ/ಕಿರಿಯ, which is decided by the buffaloes' teeth.
  - In ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ, the winner is the pair that throws the water up to a cloth ನಿಶಾನೆ set at a height measured in ಕೋಲು. Prajavani 2023 spells it ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ (one word, ನ not ಣ).
  - The season runs November to March/April. The State Kambala Association's 2026-27 calendar has 28 events: Mysuru on 18 Oct, then Panapila on 7 Nov, and so on, ending at Kadandale Parari on 1 May 2027.
  - The law story has exact dates from primary texts:
    - The SC judgment of 7 May 2014 never mentions Kambala.
    - The HC interim stay was dated 18 Nov 2016.
    - The protests were in Mangaluru (27 Jan 2017) and Moodbidri (28 Jan 2017).
    - The Bill was passed on 13 Feb 2017 and returned by the President.
    - The President's instructions for the Ordinance are dated 3 July 2017, and the Ordinance was gazetted 20 July 2017.
    - Karnataka Act No. 2 of 2018 got the Governor's assent on 10 Feb 2018 and was gazetted on 20 Feb 2018, with effect from 20 July 2017.
    - The SC Constitution Bench upheld it on 18 May 2023.
    - The SC dismissed PETA's "coast only" plea on 10 Mar 2026.
  - Richest old record: Thurston, *Castes and Tribes of Southern India* vol. I (1909), pp. 157–162. It describes the plank with holes, the splash judged by eye, a "gallows" about 20 ft high for the splash, the pukare pole, Koraga drummers, the Kambla-asura belief, the podi Kambla at Vandar, and racing buffaloes "used for no purpose all the year, except racing".
- **Disputed:**
  - Srinivasa Gowda's Feb 2020 run is given as 142.5 m or 145 m, in 13.61, 13.62 or 13.46 s. The race date is given as "Jan 31" (one search summary, not verified) or 1 Feb (PTI).
  - The age of Kambala is claimed as "800-year-old", "more than 1000 years", "600-700 years ago", "fourth century" Alupa kings, "three hundred year history". All of these are claims; none is dated by scholarship.
  - Kane halage heights: DH wrote "6.5 and 7.5 meters". Prajavani says "6.5 ಕೋಲು". Kannada Wikipedia gives 1 ಕೋಲು = 2.5 ft. Use ಕೋಲು.
  - Whether race buffaloes plough: Thurston 1909 says no, except an occasional plough. Owners in 2017 and 2020 say they do plough them.
  - The 2017 Act's assent: the SC says "Presidential assent", while the Act text records the Governor's assent.
- **Not verified:**
  - The 2026-27 season's actual results for Mysuru, Sanoor and Kodange. Only the calendar and Mysuru's opening day are sourced. Panapila has a weak current-affairs source, and no race date is printed.
  - A Tulu word for the jockey. Kannada news uses ಓಡಿಸಿದವರು / ಓಟಗಾರ, and English uses "jockey/runner".
  - The Karnataka State Gazetteer, Dakshina Kannada (1973): not found on archive.org.
  - Any link between Kambala and the Jain Chowta rulers of Moodbidri.
  - A 2026 women-jockey outcome. Only a 2021 announcement was found.

## A. What Kambala is: track, pair, runner, start/finish, timing, season, prizes

1. **Kambala is run on two parallel slush tracks.**
   - Tier: scholarship (government page)
   - Source: Karnataka Tourism, "Kambala" (page last reviewed 18 Nov 2026)
   - URL: https://www.karnatakatourism.org/destinations/kambala/
   - Quote: "Kambala is performed on two parallel race tracks, filled with slushy water."

2. **The twin track (ಜೋಡುಕರೆ) is the modern form. The older single-track race was ಒಂಟಿಕರೆ. The first ಜೋಡುಕರೆ was the Lava-Kusha Kambala at Bajegoli near Karkala on 3 Feb 1970: a ticketed event, gold for winners, a 130.3 m twin track.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news feature)
   - Source: Deccan Herald, "Kambala's changing stride across Karnataka", 19 Mar 2026
   - URL: https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/kambalas-changing-stride-across-karnataka-3936834
   - Quote: "Traditionally, Kambala was held as a single-track race called Ontikare. A turning point came on February 3, 1970"
   - Second quote: "The race saw buffaloes run on a 130.3 metre twin track"

3. **Track length today is about 125–140 m. A season has about 20 events, each with 150–220 pairs. Races are on weekends in Dakshina Kannada, Udupi and Kasaragod.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)
   - Source: Deccan Herald, 19 Mar 2026 (URL as fact 2)
   - Quote: "Pairs of buffaloes race along 125 to 140-metre waterlogged tracks along with their jockeys."
   - Other figures (see Disputes): "142m" (AFP/Quint 2020), "100 to 150 metres" (Karnataka govt affidavit 2017, fact 40), "130-to-160m" (Open 2026, fact 9), and a "155-metre" Bengaluru track (Open 2026).

4. **The track is sunk below ground level. A mango-leaf ತೋರಣ marks the start, another marks the finish, and the buffaloes slow down by climbing a bank at the end called ಮಂಜೊಟ್ಟಿ.**
   - Tier: scholarship (encyclopedic, Kannada Wikipedia "ಕಂಬಳ"; unsigned, so treat as background and confirm by eye at a race)
   - URL: https://kn.wikipedia.org/wiki/ಕಂಬಳ
   - Quote: "ಅಲ್ಲಿ ಮೇಲೆ ಕಟ್ಟಿದ ಮಾವಿನ ಎಲೆಗಳ ತೋರಣವು ಓಟದ ಆರಂಭದ ಗೆರೆಯನ್ನು ಸೂಚಿಸುತ್ತದೆ"
   - Also: "ಮಂಜೊಟ್ಟಿ ಎಂದು ಕರೆಯಲಾಗುವ ಆ ಏರಿಯನ್ನೇರಿ ವೇಗ ಕಳಕೊಂಡು ನಿಲ್ಲುತ್ತವೆ"
   - Support: at Aikala, "Two large peepal trees at the finish point, called Manjotti, offer shade" (DH 2026, fact 2). There is also a modern mesh start gate called "ganti" (same article).

5. **A pair of male buffaloes (ಕೋಣ) is yoked together. The runner holds a rope or plough, or stands on a plank behind them. The buffaloes do the running and the man runs or "surfs" behind.**
   - Tier: scholarship (law and news)
   - Source: Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Karnataka Second Amendment) Act, 2017 (Karnataka Act No. 02 of 2018), s. 2, via Indian Kanoon
   - URL: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/77497365/
   - Quote: ""Kambala" means the traditional sports event involving Buffalo's (male) race normally held as a part of tradition and culture in the state"
   - News support: AFP/Gulf Times, 18 Feb 2020: "racers splash 142m through paddy fields holding a rope attached to two buffaloes, who run ahead of them." https://www.gulf-times.com/story/656212/buffalo-jockey-turns-down-sprint-trial

6. **How a heat ends: speed classes are won by the first pair to finish. Races run as league, semi-final and final, and results come out on the second day after the races run through the night.**
   - Tier: scholarship (encyclopedic, Kannada Wikipedia; URL as fact 4)
   - Quote: "ಉಳಿದ ಪಂದ್ಯಗಳಲ್ಲಿನಂತೆಯೇ ಲೀಗ್, ಸೆಮಿಫೈನಲ್, ಫೈನಲ್ ಓಟಗಳು ನಡೆಯುತ್ತವೆ."
   - Also: "ಕಂಬಳಗಳು ಆರಂಭದ ದಿನ ಬೆಳಗ್ಗಿನಿಂದ ರಾತ್ರಿ ಮುಗಿದು ಮಾರನೆಯ ದಿನದವರೆಗೂ ಎಡೆ ಬಿಡದೆ ನಡೆಯುತ್ತವೆ."

7. **Night races: tracks were first lit with Petromax lamps. Floodlit Kambala began "37 years ago" (counted from 2026) at the Adve Nandikoor Koti-Chennaiah Jodukare Kambala.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)
   - Source: Deccan Herald, 19 Mar 2026 (URL as fact 2)
   - Quote: "Races were originally held only during the day but the tracks later lit at night with Petromax lamps."
   - Second quote: "Floodlit Kambala was first introduced 37 years ago at the Adve Nandikoor Koti-Chennaiah Jodukare Kambala."

8. **Timing system, from stopwatch to laser: a stopwatch at Farangipete in the 1940s, laser beam sensors later, and a "third umpire" system from 2023.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)
   - Source: Deccan Herald, 19 Mar 2026 (URL as fact 2)
   - Quote: "In the 1940s, a Kambala at Farangipete used a stopwatch to record timings."
   - Second quote: "In 2023, the Zilla Kambala Samithi introduced a "third umpire" system to eliminate disputes over race outcomes."

9. **Photo finish arrived "about 20 years ago" (counted from 2026), and an end-point laser beam network is now used everywhere. This is an owner's statement.**
   - Tier: scholarship (magazine report quoting owner Nandalike Srikanth Bhat)
   - Source: Open Magazine, V Shoba, "Mudslinging Time…", 8 Aug 2026
   - URL: https://openthemagazine.com/art-culture/mudslinging-time-a-proposal-to-hold-a-coastal-buffalo-race-has-ignited-a-culture-war-in-karnataka
   - Quote: ""Photo finish came about 20 years ago. Now we have an end point laser beam network system everywhere"

10. **Season: November to March. One Kambala per weekend. 2026 rules: up to 150 pairs must finish within 24 hours, more than 150 pairs within 30 hours.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, Naina J A, "Kambala season to have 28 Kambala events…", 22 Jul 2026
    - URL: https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-kambala-season-to-have-28-kambala-events-check-dates-guidelines-and-more-4082465
    - Quote: "events with up to 150 pairs of buffaloes must be completed within 24 hours, while those with more than 150 pairs should conclude within 30 hours."
    - The season months also appear in Karnataka Tourism (fact 1): "held in various parts of Tulu nadu … between November and March."

11. **Prizes: winners get gold. In 1970 it was "the winners were awarded gold" (fact 2). Kannada Wikipedia says the owner customarily gets gold medals of 1 or 2 ಪವನು. In Bengaluru 2023 the prize money was Rs 1 lakh.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: The News Minute, "Why a Malekudiya man's win at Bengaluru Kambala shatters centuries of exclusion", 1 Dec 2023
    - URL: https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/why-a-malekudiya-mans-win-at-bengaluru-kambala-shatters-centuries-of-exclusion
    - Quote: "While announcing the prize money of Rs 1 lakh, Ashok said, "It's not about the money."
    - Kannada Wikipedia (fact 4 URL): "ಸಾಮಾನ್ಯವಾಗಿ ಚಿನ್ನ ದ ಪದಕಗಳನ್ನು (೨ ಪವನು, ೧ ಪವನು ಇತ್ಯಾದಿ) ವಿಜೇತ ಜೋಡಿಯ ಯಜಮಾನನಿಗೆ ನೀಡುವುದು ವಾಡಿಕೆ."
    - Contrast with 1909: "The racing is for no prize or stakes, and there is no betting" (Thurston, fact 22).

## B. The race classes

12. **Four classes, each with its gear:**
    - ನೇಗಿಲು: a light plough.
    - ಹಗ್ಗ: a rope tied to the pair.
    - ಅಡ್ಡ ಹಲಗೆ: the runner stands on a cross plank.
    - ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ: the runner stands on a round block with two holes, and the splash height decides the winner.
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, 19 Mar 2026 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "Kane Halage (jockey stands on a circular wooden block with two water-splashing holes. The height of the splash, measured using two white cloth strips, determines the winner)"

13. **ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ is judged at a cloth mark (ನಿಶಾನೆ) at a height counted in ಕೋಲು. In Bengaluru 2023 the winner threw water to the 6.5-ಕೋಲು ನಿಶಾನೆ. Spelling in this source: ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news, Kannada)
    - Source: Prajavani, "Bengaluru Kambala: 'ರಾಜ - ಮಹಾರಾಜ' ಜೋಡುಕರೆ ಕಂಬಳ ಕೂಟದ ಫಲಿತಾಂಶ", 27 Nov 2023
    - URL: https://www.prajavani.net/news/karnataka-news/bengaluru-kambala-results-2579099
    - Quote: "ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ ವಿಭಾಗ (6.5 ಕೋಲು ನಿಶಾನೆಗೆ ನೀರು ಹಾಯಿಸಿದ ಕೋಣಗಳು)"

14. **ಕೋಲು as a unit: 1 ಕೋಲು = 2.5 ft, and the contest usually starts at 6.5 ಕೋಲು (about 16 ft).**
    - Tier: scholarship (encyclopedic, Kannada Wikipedia; URL as fact 4)
    - Quote: "ಹೆಚ್ಚಾಗಿ ೬.೫ ಕೋಲು (ಒಂದು ಕೋಲು = ೨.೫ ಅಡಿ) ಎತ್ತರದಿಂದ ಈ ಸ್ಪರ್ಧೆ ಆರಂಭವಾಗುತ್ತದೆ."
    - The 1909 account (fact 22) mentions "a kind of gallows, perhaps twenty feet high" for the splash. That fits about 8 ಕೋಲು.

15. **The ನಿಶಾನೆ is a pole with white cloth. Kane halage is not a speed race.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news feature)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, "Kambala: a sport and a tradition", 6 Feb 2017
    - URL: https://www.deccanherald.com/content/595115/kambala-sport-tradition.html
    - Quote: "the buffaloes have to splash mud water to the maximum height possible at a pole that is tied with a white cloth. This pole is known as nishane."

16. **Tulu names for the classes, as printed in DH 2017 (fact 15):**
    - ಅಡ್ಡ ಹಲಗೆ = "addapalai"
    - ನೇಗಿಲು = "nayerdavvu"
    - ಹಗ್ಗ = "balluda gidapune"
    - Tier: scholarship (news feature; transliterations unverified against a Tulu lexicon)
    - Quote: "In Tulu, it is called as nayerdavvu (a plough)."
    - Second quote: "In Tulu, this is known balluda gidapune, which means racing with the help of a rope."

17. **Senior and junior (ಹಿರಿಯ/ಕಿರಿಯ) are decided by the buffaloes' teeth. "Senior" means permanent teeth, about six years old. Veteran jockeys run the plank classes.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, "A historical run", 21 Feb 2020
    - URL: https://www.deccanherald.com/spectrum/a-historical-run-806924.html
    - Quote: "Under Hagga and Negilu, senior (based on the teeth of a buffalo) and junior events are conducted."
    - Open Magazine 2026 (fact 9): "a single "senior" pair (the term for buffaloes with permanent teeth, which corresponds to about six years of age)"

18. **Pairs per class at Bengaluru 2023: ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ 7, ಅಡ್ಡಹಲಗೆ 6, ಹಗ್ಗ ಹಿರಿಯ 21, ನೇಗಿಲು ಹಿರಿಯ 32, ಹಗ್ಗ ಕಿರಿಯ 31, ನೇಗಿಲು ಕಿರಿಯ 62. Total 159. The plank classes are small and elite, and the junior plough class is the biggest.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Prajavani, 27 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 13)
    - Quote: "ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ: 07 ಜೊತೆ ಅಡ್ಡಹಲಗೆ: 06 ಜೊತೆ ಹಗ್ಗ ಹಿರಿಯ: 21 ಜೊತೆ ನೇಗಿಲು ಹಿರಿಯ: 32 ಜೊತೆ"

## C. Ritual Kambala and the old record

19. **Types of Kambala: Pookare, Baare, Arasu or Devara, and Adhunika (modern). Only Adhunika and Devara involve a race. The other two are rituals of Naga and Bhuta worship.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news feature)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, 6 Feb 2017 (URL as fact 15)
    - Quote: "There are four types of Kambala: Pookare, Baare, Arasu or Devara, and Adhunika."
    - Quote: "The other two types focus on ritual and cultural worship of Naga and Bhootas."

20. **Gunapala Kadamba's list: the ritual kambalas (Bare, Pokare, Arasu) prayed for a bumper crop. Hudi Kambala was held at kings' coronations. Devara Kambala is still held at the Vaidyanatheshwara Temple, Kokkada, "to help people in illness".**
    - Tier: scholarship (news, quoting Kadamba, five decades in the sport)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, 21 Feb 2020 (URL as fact 17)
    - Quote: "Kambalas were also conducted during coronations of kings (Hudi Kambala)."
    - Quote: "even today Devara Kambala is organised at Vaidyanatheshwara Temple in Kokkada to help people in illness."

21. **In ritual (non-competitive) Kambalas, buffaloes were led around an auspicious post, milk was offered, the Naga was worshipped, and people prayed for the land's fertility. "Dozens" are still held.**
    - Tier: scholarship (magazine, citing folklorist K. Chinnappa Gowda)
    - Source: Open Magazine, 8 Aug 2026 (URL as fact 9)
    - Quote: "buffa­loes were led around an auspicious post. Milk was offered, the Naga worshipped and prayers made for the land's fertility."
    - Note: the source has soft hyphens in "buffa­loes"; copy as plain "buffaloes".

22. **THE BEST OLD DESCRIPTION, 1909 (Thurston, quoting H. O. D. Harding), pp. 157–160:**
    - Every rich Bant had a kambla field.
    - Racing buffaloes cost Rs 150–500 and did no work but racing.
    - Each pair ran alone and the crowd judged pace, style and above all the splash.
    - The racing "car" came from the farm levelling plank: a small plank with a hollowed stool for one foot and holes for mud and water.
    - The driver held "one of the buffaloes' tails".
    - The course was a wet field "about 150 yards long".
    - Tier: scholarship (primary, 1909 ethnography)
    - Source: Edgar Thurston, *Castes and Tribes of Southern India*, vol. I (Madras, 1909), "Bant", pp. 157–160
    - URL: https://archive.org/details/castestribesofso01thuriala
    - Quote: "Each pair of buffaloes runs the course alone, and is judged by the assembled crowd for pace and style"
    - Quote: "In the plank, on each side, are holes to let the mud and water through."
    - Quote: "He holds a whip aloft in one hand, and one of the buffaloes' tails in the other."
    - Quote: "Sometimes a kind of gallows, perhaps twenty feet high, is erected on the course"

23. **1909: the races were a harvest festival before the second (sugge) crop, held in October and November. They opened with a devil dance, with a dancer on a hobby-horse circling the kambla field.**
    - Tier: scholarship (1909)
    - Source: Thurston 1909, p. 160 (URL as fact 22)
    - Quote: "They are a sort of harvest festival, before the second or sugge crop is sown, and are usually held in October and November."
    - Quote: "A painted, grass-crowned devil dancer, riding a hobby-horse, proceeds with music round the kambla field."

24. **1909, near Udupi: each pair went up to the start behind the Koraga band, Holeyas dancing with staves, and a man with a flag (nishani). The Ballala chief's banner flew on a bamboo at the far end.**
    - Tier: scholarship (1909, Thurston's own notes)
    - Source: Thurston 1909, pp. 160–161 (URL as fact 22)
    - Quote: "preceded by the Koraga band playing on drum, fife and cymbals, Holeyas armed with staves and dancing, and a man holding a flag (nishani)."

25. **1909 belief: every village has Kambla-asura, demons who rule the fields. Races are held to please them, and if the races are skipped the crop fails. The Koragas keep an all-night vigil (panikkuluni, "sitting under the dew"). The field is manured and ploughed the morning of the races and planted the next day.**
    - Tier: legend (belief as recorded in 1909)
    - Source: Thurston 1909, pp. 161–162 (URL as fact 22). Repeated in Thurston, *Omens and Superstitions of Southern India* (1912), pp. 298–299, https://archive.org/details/omenssuperstitio00thur
    - Quote: "In every village there are rakshasas (demons), called Kambla-asura, who preside over the fields."
    - Quote: "if they are omitted, it is believed that there will be a failure of the crop."
    - Caution: modern critics say the night rite involved degrading treatment of Koragas, and the Ajalu practice was banned in 2000 (fact 52). Handle it with care or leave it out of a children's book.

26. **1909: the pukare pole, decorated with flags, flowers and festoons of leaves, is set up in the field and worshipped through the races. Billavas tend it, and nobody else may touch it.**
    - Tier: scholarship (1909)
    - Source: Thurston 1909, p. 162 (URL as fact 22)
    - Quote: "a long pole, called pukare, decorated with flags, flowers, and festoons of leaves, is set up in the Kambla field"
    - Quote: "Billavas are in charge of this pole, which is worshipped, throughout the races, and others may not touch it."

27. **1909: at Vandar the race is run in a dry, ploughed field, so it is called podi (powder) Kambla. The field-owner's own pair must enter first, and precedence quarrels could hold up the races.**
    - Tier: scholarship (1909)
    - Source: Thurston 1909, p. 162 (URL as fact 22)
    - Quote: "At the village of Vandar, the races take place in a dry field … For this reason they are called podi (powder) Kambla."
    - Quote: "A pair of buffaloes, belonging to the field in which the races take place, should enter the field first"

28. **1909: the young men who handle the buffaloes are "Bannangayi Gurikara" (half-ripe-coconut masters). They may take tender coconuts and beaten rice for strength without asking the landlord. The days after the races are for cock-fighting.**
    - Tier: scholarship (1909)
    - Source: Thurston 1909, p. 162 (URL as fact 22)
    - Quote: "The young men, who have the management of the buffaloes, are called Bannangayi Gurikara (half-ripe cocoanut masters)"

29. **1894: the South Canara district manual records "kambla kónagalu", fine racing buffaloes kept by large Bant landholders. An ordinary pair of male buffaloes cost Rs 30–40. Buffalo-racing is listed among Bant amusements.**
    - Tier: scholarship (government district manual, 1894)
    - Source: J. Sturrock, *Madras District Manuals: South Canara*, vol. I (Madras, 1894), pp. 159 and 195–196
    - URL: https://archive.org/details/wbsl.16238
    - Quote: "Many of the large landholders among the Bants keep very fine racing buffaloes which are known as 'kambla kónagalu.'"
    - Quote (p. 159): "They are fond of out-door sports, foot-ball and buffalo-racing being amongst their favorite amusements"

30. **In Tulu, "kambala/kambula" is also the name of a big paddy field. In the Koti–Chennaya story (Burnell papers, 1894–97) the heroes hold a kambala: they ask a soothsayer for an auspicious Tuesday, order he-buffaloes and coolies, and plough before sowing.**
    - Tier: legend (oral epic as recorded in the 1890s)
    - Source: A. C. Burnell (papers), *The Devil Worship of the Tuluvas*, first in *Indian Antiquary* 1894–97; ed. A. V. Navada and Denis Fernandes, Karnataka Tulu Sahitya Academy, pp. 56–58
    - URL: https://archive.org/details/dli.language.1863
    - Quote: ""I am going to ask the opinion of a soothsayer for fixing a day for the celebration of a kambala," replied Buddyanta."
    - Quote: "They then said to one another that they ought to order some he-buffaloes and coolies for the kambala."
    - Editor's footnote 40 (p. 359): "This is an agricultural ceremony, and consists in racing with buffaloes and bullocks in a rice-field for luck"

31. **A story detail from the same epic: after the bully Buddyanta beat the borrowed buffaloes, Koti and Chennaya had them washed and fed boiled rice, and gave tender coconuts to those that would not eat fodder.**
    - Tier: legend (pp. 57–58; URL as fact 30)
    - Quote: "They then caused the buffaloes to be washed and boiled rice to be served to them."
    - Note: many jodukare tracks are named for the pair, as Koti-Chennaya (Moodbidri, Puttur, Adve). Kannada Wikipedia lists "ಕೋಟಿ-ಚೆನ್ನಯ ( ಮೂಡುಬಿದಿರೆ , ಪುತ್ತೂರು )".

32. **Another legend in the Burnell papers: four kambalas (large paddy fields) lay around the palace at Pohkule, and the bhuta Posa Maharaye was feasted after they were ploughed.**

## Disputes and gaps

- **Srinivasa Gowda's run (Feb 2020):**
  - AFP: 142.5 m in 13.62 s.
  - IANS/Quint: 145 m in 13.62 s.
  - DH, 15 Feb 2020: 145 m in 13.61 s.
  - DH, 22 Feb 2020: 142.5 m in 13.46 s, which works out to 9.44 s per 100 m.
  - Most outlets agree on 9.55 s per 100 m, worked out by the referees.
  - Date: PTI says Aikala on 1 Feb. One search summary said 31 Jan, not seen in a fetched page.
  - For the book: "about 142 metres in about 13.6 seconds" with the 9.55 per 100 m figure attributed to referees. Better still, use the fictional family and leave the record to an afterword.
- **Kane halage height:**
  - DH 27 Nov 2023 says banners at "6.5 and 7.5 meters".
  - Prajavani for the same race says "6.5 ಕೋಲು ನಿಶಾನೆ".
  - Kannada Wikipedia gives 1 ಕೋಲು = 2.5 ft.
  - DH's "meters" is almost certainly a unit error. Use ಕೋಲು.
- **Spelling:** ಕನೆಹಲಗೆ (Prajavani). ಕೆನೆ ಹಲಗೆ appears on Kannada Wikipedia. ಕಣೆ is not found in Kannada news. In Tulu, Wikipedia gives ಕಣೆ ಪಲಾಯಿ (unsourced).
- **Track length:**
  - 100 m by 7.5 m (2018 govt condition, which organisers wanted relaxed)
  - 100–150 m (2017 affidavit)
  - 125–140 m (DH 2026)
  - 130.3 m (Bajegoli 1970)
  - about 142–145 m (2020 reports)
  - 155 m (Bengaluru 2023)
  - "about 150 yards" (1909)
  - "130-to-160m" (Open 2026)
- **Age of Kambala:** "800-year-old", "more than 1000 years", "600-700 years", "fourth century" Alupa, and "three hundred year" (English Wikipedia). These are legend-tier claims. Scholarship offers only an undated Alupa-era inscription mention (DH 2017, not checked against the inscription) and paddana references.
- **Moodbidri protest, 2017:**
  - TNM: 28 Jan (Saturday), about 4,000 people, about 200 pairs.
  - Mangalorean.com: "January 29", "More than 100 pairs".
  - PTI: "200 bullocks" (sic).
  - Organisers had promised 70,000 people.
- **Assent to the 2017 Act:** SC 2023 says the State Acts "had received Presidential assent". The Act text (IK) records "assent of the Governor on the 10th day of February 2018". English Wikipedia claims President Kovind approved it on 19 Feb 2018, which is unsourced here.
- **Do race buffaloes plough?** Thurston 1909 says essentially no. 2017 and 2020 owners and Open 2026 say yes, for exercise.
- **Season months:** 1909 says October–November, before the sugge crop. Today it runs November–March/April, and Mysuru 2026 moved the opener to October.
- **Number of events:** "18 to 20" (2012), "20-22" (2017), "23-26" (Open 2026), "over 45 villages" (Karnataka Tourism), 28 in the 2026-27 calendar.
- **Not verified or not found:**
  - A sourced Tulu name for the jockey. Kannada news uses ಓಡಿಸಿದವರು ("who ran them"), and Thurston uses "driver".
  - "Kamba = splash pole" etymology (English Wikipedia only).
  - Any Jain Chowta/Moodbidri origin claim.
  - Karnataka State Gazetteer, Dakshina Kannada (1973).
  - The full text of the 2017 and 2022 state notifications of conditions.
  - Results of Sanoor and Kodange 2026.
