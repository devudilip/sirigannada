# The Rashtrakutas of Manyakheta (ರಾಷ್ಟ್ರಕೂಟರು, ಮಾನ್ಯಖೇಟ), c. 753–982 CE — research notes

Written to the shared BRIEF.md spec. Every claim carries a source and a tier
(`inscription` / `scholarship` / `legend`). Dates and names below come from the cited editions,
not from memory; where a point could not be verified online in this pass it is marked
**UNVERIFIED** and should be checked before it goes into a script.

Conventions
- EI = Epigraphia Indica (Archaeological Survey of India; public domain for the volumes used here).
- IA = Indian Antiquary (public domain).
- "jainqq EI18 p.300" means the page-scan viewer at https://www.jainqq.org/pagetext/Epigraphia_Indica_Vol_18/032572/300 ;
  the printed page number is given alongside. The viewer offset for EI XVIII is +59
  (viewer 300 = printed 241); for EI VI it is +21 (viewer 73 = printed 52).
- Kannada forms are given beside names on first use. Standard modern Kannada spellings are used;
  the Old Kannada forms in inscriptions differ (e.g. ಕನ್ನರದೇವ for Krishna III).

### 1.3 Origins, name, capital
- "Rashtrakuta" as a name of a line: the Sanjan plates v. 2 call Vira-Narayana "the progenitor of the lofty line of the good Rashtrakutas"; v. 3 names "prince Govinda, ornament of the earth, and son of Prichchhakaraja" of the Yadu line (EI XVIII p. 251–252, viewer 312–313). — inscription (dynastic self-description, Yadava claim is conventional).
- Indraraja (father of Dantidurga) "married the daughter of a Chalukya king at Khetaka (Kaira) by the Rakshasa form of marriage" (Sanjan v. 7; EI XVIII p. 238). — inscription.
- Samangad plates name his mother as Bhavanaga (Wikipedia "Dantidurga"; **UNVERIFIED** against Fleet's text).
- Earliest known dynastic name form "Ratta" appears in the Sirur inscription of 866 (Fleet, EI VII). — inscription.
- Capital: Mayurakhandi (ಮಯೂರಖಂಡಿ) under Govinda III (Wikipedia, uncited; **UNVERIFIED**); Manyakheta (ಮಾನ್ಯಖೇಟ, modern Malkhed/ಮಳಖೇಡ, Sedam taluk, Kalaburagi district, on the Kagina river). The Karhad, Deoli and Karda grants say Amoghavarsha I "constructed (not merely completed)" Manyakheta; the EI XVIII editors reject Fleet's idea that it became capital under Govinda III (EI XVIII p. 241 footnote, viewer 300). The Karhad plates v. 12–14 say Nripatunga "made Manyakheta rival the heavens" (EI IV, viewer 338). — inscription.
- Arab geographers call the capital Mankir and the king "Balhara" (= Vallabha-raja); see §2.4.
- Origin debate (Kannadiga / Maratha / Reddi / north-western): summarised Wikipedia "Rashtrakuta dynasty" §Origins; no primary evidence settles it. — scholarship (disputed).

### 2.4 Arab travellers and geographers (eyewitness / near-contemporary)
Elliot & Dowson, *The History of India as Told by Its Own Historians*, vol. I (1867), public domain:
https://archive.org/details/historyofindiaas01elli (full OCR text: /stream/historyofindiaas01elli/historyofindiaas01elli_djvu.txt).
- Merchant Sulaiman (account written 851 CE; section I, vol. I pp. 1ff): the kings of India and China agree there are four great kings — the Arab king first, then China, "After him comes the king of the Greeks, and lastly the Balhara, prince of the men who have their ears pierced." "Balhara is the title borne by all the kings of this dynasty. It is similar to the Cosroes (of the Persians), and is not a proper name." "Still the Balhara has the title of 'king of kings'." "in fact, among all the kings there is no one to be found who is so partial to the Arabs as the Balhara." "He gives regular pay to his troops, as the practice is among the Arabs. He has many horses and elephants, and immense wealth." "The Balhara has around him several kings with whom he is at war, but whom he greatly excels. Among them is the king of Jurz." "their kings live long, frequently reigning for fifty years." — inscription-tier (contemporary eyewitness; 851 falls in Amoghavarsha's reign).
- Ibn Khurdadba (9th c.): "The greatest king of India is the Balhara, or 'king of kings'." — same volume.
- Al-Masudi (visited 943 CE, i.e. under Krishna III's accession period): Mankir "the great centre of India" submits to the Balhara; the title passes to successors in that capital; he is "the greatest of the kings of India"; capital about eighty Sindi parasangs from the sea; troops mostly infantry "since the government is based in the mountains"; enemy of the king of Kanauj; exposed to attacks of the king of Juzr (Gurjara); "no king of India shows greater respect to Muslims than the Balhara"; "the people of Mankir speak the Kiriya language". — eyewitness.
- Istakhri (c. 951) and Ibn Hauqal (c. 976): Balhara's land runs from Kambaya to Saimur; Muslims live in its cities and have Friday mosques; the Balhara resides at Mankir. — contemporary.
Note: Elliot & Dowson is Victorian; use it for quotation but not for its editorial opinions.

## 3. Candidate stories

### Story A — "The left finger": Amoghavarsha Nripatunga (ಅಮೋಘವರ್ಷ ನೃಪತುಂಗ)
**People.** Sarva/Amoghavarsha (born c. 800 in his father's war-camp in the Vindhyas; crowned a boy of about 14); Govinda III (ಮೂರನೆಯ ಗೋವಿಂದ) his father; Arya Patalamalla (ಪಾತಾಳಮಲ್ಲ), the man who restored him; the Gujarat cousin Karka (Baroda plates 835); Srivijaya (ಶ್ರೀವಿಜಯ) the poet; Jinasena (ಜಿನಸೇನ) the Digambara teacher; Sulaiman the merchant of Siraf; Mahalakshmi of Kolhapur (ಕೊಲ್ಲಾಪುರದ ಮಹಾಲಕ್ಷ್ಮಿ).

**Arc.**
1. Birth in a camp: Govinda III, returning from the Himalayan campaign, winters "at the foot of the Vindhyas" in the little kingdom of Maharaja Sarva; a son is born and given the same name; astrologers foretell he will "rule the earth bounded by the Himalayas and the Setu" (Sanjan vv. 25–27). — inscription.
2. Govinda dies; the boy is crowned "to protect his ancestors' fame and destroy Kali's sinfulness" (v. 36). The Sirur record later fixes the year at 814–15. — inscription.
3. Collapse: "feudatories break their oaths, become independent, and seize power by killing appointed officers. Morality collapses" (v. 39); "the sun sets, and the moon and stars rise" (v. 40). — inscription.
4. Restoration: "The Rattas' lord, the sun, rises through the greatness of Arya Patalamalla" (v. 41) — a figure otherwise unknown. Other records (Baroda plates 835, "Surat records") say the Gujarat cousin Karka also fought for him (Kamath 2001 p. 78 via Wikipedia). — inscription / scholarship.
5. Wars: the Vengi Chalukyas crushed at Vingavalli, where he "pleased the god Yama with unprecedented morsels of cakes" (Cambay v. 12; EI VII p. 29). — inscription.
6. The calamity and the finger: "Jimutavahana, Sibi and Dadhichi gave their bodies or lives to individuals; Vira-Narayana presented his left finger to Maha-Lakshmi for the pacification of a calamity to the (whole) people" (Sanjan v. 47, EI XVIII p. 255). — inscription.
7. The merchant's verdict: in 851 Sulaiman lists the Balhara among the four great kings of the world, "king of kings", friend of the Arabs, paying his troops in cash. — eyewitness.
8. The king who kept leaving: the Sanjan plates say he "gave away his kingdom and himself several times and did not covet either" (v. 48, contrasted with a fratricidal Gupta donor); the editors conclude he "abdicated more than once before Saka 793 while still performing kingly duties". Digambara copies of the *Prasnottara-ratnamalika* say he composed it after giving up the throne (EI XVIII p. 242). — inscription + scholarship.
9. Coda: the *Kavirajamarga* — a book about how to write Kannada, with "Nripatungadevanumata" (approved by Nripatunga) in its text — defines the Kannada country from the Kaveri to the Godavari and praises a people who make poetry without being taught. — contemporary text.

**Goosebump moment.** Verse 47: the emperor whom the Arabs rank with the Caliph and the Emperor of China cuts off his own left finger and lays it before the goddess, not for himself but "for the whole people". Evidence: the king's own charter of 871, in his own lifetime, not a later legend.

**Dramatised vs recorded.** Recorded: finger, "calamity to the people", Mahalakshmi, the comparison with Sibi/Dadhichi, the collapse and Patalamalla, the abdications, Sulaiman's ranking. Not recorded: what the calamity was (plague? famine? v. 49 says under him "calamities to husbandry, plagues and famines cannot touch any of the six seasons", which hints at the fear); that the goddess was the Kolhapur one (the editors' inference from an unpublished inscription calling Kolhapur the "adya-pitha"); any scene of the act; Jinasena being present. **Disputed:** Settar and Desai read "bali" as a gift (dana), not a mutilation — see §5.

**Why it grips adults.** A 64-year reign that keeps failing and recovering; a ruler who twice walks away from power and comes back; a king whose greatness is measured by a foreign merchant and by a grammar book rather than by a conquest.

### Story B — "The hound and the prince": Takkolam, 949 (ತಕ್ಕೋಲಂ)
**People.** Krishna III Kannaradeva (ಮೂರನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣ); Butuga II (ಬೂತುಗ) the Western Ganga, his brother-in-law; Manalera (ಮಣಲೆರ) of the Sagara lineage, "lord of Valabhi", Butuga's champion; the hound Kali (ಕಾಳಿ); Rajaditya (ರಾಜಾದಿತ್ಯ), crown prince of the Cholas, son of Parantaka I and a Chera princess; Vellan Kumaran the Chera commander.

**Arc.**
1. Krishna III, newly crowned (939), kills the Ganga Rachamalla and installs Butuga, his sister's husband, as lord of Gangavadi 96,000 (Atakur subsidiary record: Butuga "had killed Rachamalla, son of Ereyappa"; Ron stone 942 shows Butuga governing Gangavadi, Belvola, Puligere). — inscription.
2. By 944 Krishna is in Tondaimandalam (Siddhalingamadam plates per Kamath). — scholarship.
3. 948–9, Takkola in North Arcot: the Chola prince Rajaditya faces the Rashtrakuta–Ganga army. The Chola elephant is "the fortress of the Chola"; Manalera "with the help of Hari and Lakshmi struck the forehead of the Chola's elephant so it burst open" (Atakur main record, EI VI p. 58, viewer 79). — inscription.
4. Rajaditya dies on his elephant. Atakur: Krishna III "attacked the Muvadi-Chola Rajaditya, and having fought and killed him at Takkola, was going in triumph" (ll. 4–5: "Takkoladol kadi kondu bijayam-geyyutt-ildu"). The subsidiary record says Butuga slew him "treacherously" ("without mincing matters", Fleet). The Cholas remember him as "Yanai-mel-tunjiya", he who died on the elephant's back (Leiden/Tiruvalangadu). — inscription (two hostile versions).
5. Reward: Krishna gives Butuga "the Banavasi twelve-thousand province" and the Belvola 300, Purigere 300, Kisukad 70, Bagenad 70. Butuga, "pleased with the prowess displayed by Manalera", gives him the Atukur twelve and Kadiyur as a "sword-washing" (balgachchu) grant — and his favourite hound Kali. — inscription.
6. The hound: Manalera looses Kali on "a mighty boar on a hill in the western quarter of Belatur village, Kelale district"; "the boar and hound killed each other". — inscription.
7. The stone: Manalera sets up a memorial "before the temple of the god Challesvara at Atukur", with land "yielding two kandugas of grain", and a curse: any cultivator or governor who destroys the land, or any Gorava manager who fails to worship the stone, "incurs the guilt of the sin committed by the hound". The sculpture shows hound and boar fighting. — inscription.
8. Aftermath: Krishna takes the title "Kannaradeva who took Kachchi and Tanjai"; the Karhad plates (959) say he "uprooted the Cholas, distributed their territory among his followers" and "planted his fame at Ramesvara" (via Wikipedia summary of EI IV). — inscription.

**Goosebump moment.** The warrior who broke open the Chola war-elephant's forehead asks his lord for one reward — a dog — and then carves the dog's death into stone with a curse as solemn as a king's land grant. Evidence: Atakur stone, Saka 872, in Old Kannada, still standing.

**Dramatised vs recorded.** Recorded: every beat above. Not recorded: whether the arrow (Wikipedia-derived pages) or the dagger "while embracing him" (Wikipedia "Rajaditya Chola" quoting the subsidiary record) killed Rajaditya — the two readings of the same record must be checked against Fleet's text at viewer p. 78; the Chola records give no killer. Nothing records Manalera's feelings; the stone's sculpture is the only "emotion".

**Why it grips adults.** Treachery openly admitted by the winners; a prince's death remembered by his enemies' hound.

### Story C — "The gods in their cars": Krishna I and the Kailasa at Ellora (ಎಲ್ಲೋರಾದ ಕೈಲಾಸ)
**People.** Krishna I Subhatunga (ಒಂದನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣ), uncle and successor of Dantidurga; the nameless architect; the Gujarat cousin Karka whose charter preserved the memory; (legend) the queen and the architect Kokasa.

**Arc.**
1. Dantidurga, at Ujjain, performs the Hiranyagarbha gift with "a Gurjara lord and others made door-keepers" (Sanjan v. 9; Ellora cave 15 inscription gives the Ujjain gift) and takes the Chalukya sovereignty; his Dashavatara cave (15) at Ellora is the family's first mark on the hill. — inscription.
2. Krishna I "takes the Chalukya family's fortune in battle" (Sanjan v. 10) — the Karhad plates add Dantidurga "wrested supreme sovereignty from the Chalukyas" (v. 9). — inscription.
3. The temple: the Baroda plates (812/13) say Krishnaraja "caused to be constructed a temple on the hill at Elapura, of a wonderful structure"; the gods "who move in celestial cars" are struck with astonishment; the architect himself "was suddenly struck with astonishment, saying, 'Oh, how was it that I built it!'" (R. G. Bhandarkar's translation, IA XII p. 229). — inscription (written two generations later, by a collateral branch).
4. The work: shrine top 32.6 m above the court; court 82 × 46 m; Dhavalikar estimated 250 labourers at 4 cu ft/day would need about 5.5 years (Dhavalikar 1982 p. 35 via Wikipedia). — scholarship.
5. The models: the main shrine "resembles, though is much larger than, the Virupaksha at Pattadakal, which replicates the Kailasa at Kanchi"; Dhavalikar suggests Krishna brought the Pattadakal workmen (incl. Pallava artists) to Ellora. — scholarship.
6. Later hands: Goetz 1952 thought the work ran through Dantidurga, Krishna, Dhruva, Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Krishna III; the Ravana panel is dated 3–4 decades after the main shrine (Dhavalikar) or to Krishna III (Goetz). — scholarship (disputed).
7. Legend (Marathi, *Katha-Kalpataru*, c. 1470–1535): a queen's vow for a sick king; architect Kokasa carves the sikhara top-down in a week; temple named Manikeshwar. — legend, first recorded ~700 years later.

**Goosebump moment.** The architect looking at the finished mountain and not believing he made it — a line written c. 812 about a building that still stands. Evidence: Baroda plates, but note the identification of "Krishnaraja" with Krishna I is an inference (no dedicatory inscription at the temple).

**Dramatised vs recorded.** Recorded: the astonishment verse, the Chalukya victory, the Ujjain ceremony. Not recorded: Krishna visiting the site; the number of workmen; the sequence of cutting; the queen's vow (legend). The temple has no inscription of its own.

### Story D — "The year the horses drank the Yamuna": Indra III at Kannauj, c. 915–917
**People.** Indra III Nityavarsha (ಮೂರನೆಯ ಇಂದ್ರ); his Vemulavada feudatory Narasimha II (ನರಸಿಂಹ) and Narasimha's son Arikesari II (ಅರಿಕೇಸರಿ); the Pratihara Mahipala; Pampa, who turned the campaign into poetry a generation later; Govinda IV, whose 930 charter is the record.

**Arc.**
1. Indra's father Jagattunga dies without reigning (Karhad v. 15–16); Indra is crowned at Kurundaka (Reu). — inscription / scholarship.
2. The march north: "the courtyard of the temple of the god Kalapriya became uneven by the strokes of the tusks of his rutting elephants"; "his steeds crossed the unfathomable Yamuna which rivals the sea"; he "completely devastated that hostile city of Mahodaya" (Cambay v. 19). Bhandarkar: Kalapriya = Mahakala of Ujjain; Venkataramanayya: Kalapriya = Kalpi on the Yamuna. — inscription (disputed geography).
3. Pampa: Narasimha "took the goddess of victory from the Gurjara king's arms"; "Mahipala fled as if struck by thunderbolt"; Narasimha pursued and "bathed his horses" at the Ganga (Puri 1957 p. 81; Venkataramanayya 1953 pp. 24–30). — contemporary poetry (a feudatory's court).
4. Bhandarkar's caution: the "total destruction" of Kannauj "rests on a pun" (Mahodaya/Kusasthala) and "is not a historical fact"; Kannauj remained a capital. — scholarship.
5. The Rashtrakutas hold Kannauj only "until c. 916" (Altekar via Kamath p. 80), then go home; Govinda IV's palace is nevertheless "served by the Ganga and Yamuna" (Cambay v. 28). — scholarship / inscription.
6. The fall at home: Govinda IV "did not wrong his elder brother, nor was he guilty of incest, nor of cruelty" (Sangli 933) — a denial that Reu reads as evidence of the accusations; Arikesari II and other vassals rebel and set Amoghavarsha III on the throne; Pampa, the rebel's poet, writes the Mahabharata with Arikesari as Arjuna (941). — inscription / scholarship.

**Goosebump moment.** The elephants' tusks scarring a temple courtyard a thousand kilometres from Manyakheta — a Deccan army at the Yamuna. Evidence: Cambay plates, 930, a son's record fifteen years after the event; Pampa's independent poetic testimony.

**Dramatised vs recorded.** Recorded: Kalapriya, the Yamuna crossing, Mahodaya, Mahipala's flight, horses bathed in the Ganga. Not recorded: the route, dates, numbers, any battle description; where Kalapriya is.

### Story E — "The last Rashtrakuta on the hill": Indra IV at Sravana Belgola, 982 (ಶ್ರವಣಬೆಳಗೊಳ)
**People.** Khottiga (ಖೊಟ್ಟಿಗ); Harsha Siyaka of Malwa; Karka II (Kakkala); Tailapa II Ahavamalla (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ತೈಲಪ), the Tardavadi feudatory; the Ganga Marasimha (ಮಾರಸಿಂಹ) and his nephew Indra IV; Dhanapala the lexicographer; Pushpadanta the poet.

**Arc.**
1. 965: "Ahavamalla Tailaparasa of the Satyasraya family" governs Tardavadi-1000 under Krishna III (Narsalgi stone) — the usurper is already inside the house. — inscription.
2. 967: Krishna III dies; Khottiga rules. — scholarship.
3. 972–73: the Paramara Harsha Siyaka sacks Manyakheta; Dhanapala dates his dictionary by it; Pushpadanta says Khottiga died in the fighting (per Wikipedia, **UNVERIFIED**). EI XXIX notes Fleet thought the attacker might have been Munja. — contemporary literary / scholarship.
4. 973: Tailapa defeats Karka II "after a fierce battle in which Karka's two wicked ministers were killed" (Altekar 1960 p. 299) and takes Manyakheta; the Silahara Rattaraja, still loyal in memory, names the fallen king "Kakkala" (Kharepatan). — inscription / scholarship.
5. Marasimha crowns his nephew Indra IV (Sravana Belgola no. 38: "holding festival at the coronation of Indra Raja") and fights on; defeated, he takes sallekhana at Bankapura (Kuge Brahmadeva pillar; Melagani inscription gives Saka 896). — inscription.
6. 982, Saka 904, Chitrabhanu: "Indra Raja, praised by all people, attained to the wealth of the king of all the gods" — "the son's son of the illustrious Krishna Rajendra" dies by the Jain vow "on the hill" (no. 57). — inscription.
7. Tailapa marries the Rashtrakuta princess Jakavve and rules from Manyakheta; the old feudatories (Rattas of Saundatti 980, Nolambas 981) accept him. — inscription / scholarship.

**Goosebump moment.** A grandson of the man who took Kanchi and Tanjore ending the dynasty not in battle but by fasting to death on Chandragiri, with the epitaph calling him Indra come to Indra's heaven. Evidence: the 982 stone itself (Rice 1889 no. 57).

**Dramatised vs recorded.** Recorded: all dates above except Khottiga's death-in-battle; Indra's vow and lineage. Not recorded: the sack of Manyakheta in any detail; whether Karka died or fled "to rule near Soraba until 991" (accounts differ, Sastri 1960 p. 320); Indra's words.

## 4. Little-known facts (one line + source + tier)

1. The Sanjan plates say Dantidurga made "a Gurjara lord and others" his door-keepers (pratihara) at the Hiranyagarbha at Ujjain — a pun on the Pratihara dynasty's name. EI XVIII p. 238–239, v. 9. — inscription.
2. Dhruva seized "the white umbrellas of a Gauda king fleeing between the Ganges and Jumna"; the editors identify the fugitive as Dharmapala (or Gopala). EI XVIII p. 239, v. 14; R. C. Majumdar first showed the Baroda verse refers to Dhruva, not Govinda III. — inscription / scholarship.
3. Govinda III, on being crowned, released the Ganga prince his father had chained, "apparently against his councillors' advice", telling them "Ye are (unto me) like (my) father"; the Ganga rebelled again and "ended up with fetters around his neck". EI XVIII vv. 16, 18. — inscription.
4. Govinda III brought two statues "of the Lord" from Lanka to Helapura and set them up as "columns of fame" before a Siva temple at Kanchi. EI XVIII v. 34, p. 241. — inscription.
5. Amoghavarsha was born in a feudatory's camp and named "Sarva" with a pun: Maharaja Sarva the host, Maharaja-Sarva the future emperor. EI XVIII vv. 26, p. 240. — inscription.
6. The Sirur stone of 866 is dated by a total solar eclipse (16 June 866) and is the first record to call the family "Ratta". Fleet, EI VII. — inscription.
7. Sulaiman (851) says the Balhara "gives regular pay to his troops, as the practice is among the Arabs", and that in his kingdom kings "live long, frequently reigning for fifty years" — written in the 37th year of Amoghavarsha's reign. Elliot & Dowson I. — eyewitness.
8. Masudi (943) says the Balhara's army is mostly infantry "since the government is based in the mountains" and that the people of Mankir "speak the Kiriya language" — presumably Kannada ("Kannadiya"?) ; the identification is the translator's problem, not stated in the text. Elliot & Dowson I. — eyewitness.
9. Govinda IV weighed himself against gold and gave Brahmans 600 agraharas and 3 lakh suvarnas, and temples 800 villages, 4 lakh suvarnas and 32 lakh drammas, at a pattabandha at Kapitthaka on the Godavari. Cambay plates ll. 46–49, EI VII. — inscription (figures are the charter's own).
10. The Sangli plates (933) protest that Govinda IV "did not wrong his elder brother, nor was guilty of incest, nor of cruelty" — Hultzsch and Reu read this as proof the charges were current. EI VII p. 45; Reu 1933 p. 81. — inscription / scholarship.
11. A Kannada inscription of Krishna III stands at Jura near Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh — the northernmost Kannada record of the empire. EI XIX no. 51/52; Kamath 2001 p. 83. — inscription (**text not fetched**).
12. The Atakur stone warns the Gorava (Saiva priest-manager) who "fails to worship the stone" of a hound that he "incurs the same guilt" as the hound — a dog's memorial with a Brahmanical curse formula. EI VI p. 58. — inscription.
13. Tailapa II appears as a Rashtrakuta district governor in a 965 stone eight years before he ended the dynasty. SII XI.1 no. 40. — inscription.
14. Dhanapala finished his Prakrit dictionary "in the year Harsha Siyaka sacked Manyakheta" — a lexicon is the firmest date for the fall of the capital. Bühler 1878. — contemporary text.
15. The Wani-Dindori seal (807) is the first Malkhed seal that is not Garuda or Siva but Lakshmi with sun and moon; Indra III's Sanjan plates (Saka 848) carry a recumbent bull. EI XXXII discussion. — inscription (seal iconography).
16. The Sanjan plates editors wondered whether a rock-cut "royal ascetic" figure at Badami portrays the twice-abdicated Amoghavarsha — "only a surmise". EI XVIII p. 242. — scholarship (speculative).

## 5. Disputed or uncertain points

1. **The finger (bali) at Kolhapur.** EI XVIII v. 47 says Vira-Narayana "presented his left finger to Maha-Lakshmi"; the editors and Kamath read a real self-mutilation to avert a public calamity; S. Settar (*Inviting Death* 1989 pp. 105–106) and P. B. Desai (1957 pp. 10–15) argue "bali" here means a gift/offering (dana), not amputation. Also uncertain: whether the goddess is Kolhapur's (editors' inference) and whether that shrine was then a Jain Padmavati shrine (Soundara Rajan 1981 via Wikipedia). Popular pages disagree on "left finger"/"little finger"/"thumb"; the charter says left finger.
2. **Who wrote the Kavirajamarga.** Colophons: "the production of Srivijaya on the lines laid down by king Nripatunga" (EI X). Views: (a) the king himself; (b) the court poet Kavisvara; (c) Srivijaya (Mysore Univ. encyclopaedia says (c) has "the strongest evidence"; shastriyakannada.org says the debate is "finally settled in favour of Srivijaya"). Wikipedia says co-authorship "with Jinasena" — no source supports Jinasena as co-author; treat as error.
3. **Samangad plates (Saka 675).** Palaeographic doubts (Sukthankar, D. R. Bhandarkar); Altekar and the EI XVIII editors keep it as usable. Dantidurga's early titles and his mother's name rest on it.
4. **Did Govinda II reign?** Fleet said no; Hultzsch (EI VII pp. 28–29) and Bhandarkar showed he did; Karhad plates say Dhruva replaced him for "misconducts" (Kamath p. 75) — the winners' story.
5. **Ganga–Yamuna emblem.** Baroda plates: Dhruva (per Majumdar) or Govinda III (per Fleet) "took the Ganga and Yamuna from his enemies" as insignia; Govinda IV's palace "served by Ganga and Yamuna" (Cambay v. 28) — inherited or re-won from Indra III's campaign; Hultzsch disputes Fleet's chain Guptas→Chalukyas→Rashtrakutas. The emblem in physical form (door-jamb river goddesses? standards?) is not described in any record read here.
6. **Kalapriya.** Mahakala of Ujjain (D. R. Bhandarkar, EI VII p. 30) vs Kalpi on the Yamuna (Venkataramanayya 1953 pp. 28–29). Date of the Kannauj raid: 914, 915–16 or 916–17 in different modern accounts; the Cambay plates give no date.
7. **How Rajaditya died.** Rashtrakuta: Krishna III "fought and killed him" (main record) / Butuga "slew treacherously" (subsidiary record) / Manalera burst the elephant's forehead; Wikipedia-derived pages add "an arrow" or "stabbed with a dagger while embracing him" — verify against Fleet's translation at EI VI p. 56–57 before using either. Chola: died on his elephant, no killer named.
8. **Extent of Krishna III's Chola conquest.** "Kachchi and Tanjai" taken (inscriptions), "fame planted at Ramesvara" (Karhad); but Rashtrakuta records are absent south of Tondaimandalam, so Sathianathaier doubts control beyond it (Kamath p. 83). The "pillar of victory at Rameswaram" is Wikipedia wording; the Karhad verse (as summarised) says "planted his fame".
9. **Kailasa attribution and duration.** No inscription at the temple; "Krishnaraja" of the Baroda/Kadaba grants is identified with Krishna I by consensus but not proven; Goetz's multi-reign theory vs Dhavalikar's single-reign-plus-additions.
10. **Who sacked Manyakheta in 972.** Harsha Siyaka (standard, Paiyalacchi) vs Munja (Fleet's suggestion, EI XXIX).
11. **Khottiga's and Karka II's ends.** Khottiga "died fighting" (Pushpadanta per Wikipedia, unverified); Karka killed in battle (Altekar) or fled to Soraba till 991 (Sastri).
12. **Amoghavarsha's religion and "retirement".** Jain patronage (Jinasena, Gunabhadra, Mahaviracharya) is certain; the Sanjan plates also have him offering to Mahalakshmi and granting to Brahmans; "sallekhana" at his death is speculation (Altekar pp. 50–55 via Wikipedia, marked "speculative" there). The *Prasnottara-ratnamalika* is also claimed for Vimalacharya and for Sankara (manuscript note).
13. **Accession year of Amoghavarsha.** 814 vs 815: Fleet's eclipse computation gives Saka 736 or 737; the Sirur record says 52nd year in 866.
14. **Pampa's birthplace** (Vengi vs Annigeri) and the dating of the Kurkyala inscription (945 per some, undated in Wikipedia).
15. **Kiriya language** of Masudi: not identified in the sources read.

### 6.4 Dress, armour, weapons, animals, ships
- War elephants are the central image in every record: Rajaditya's elephant "the fortress of the Chola" (Atakur); Indra III's rutting elephants' tusks on the Kalapriya courtyard (Cambay); Govinda III's elephants bathing in Himalayan springs (Sanjan v. 23); Masudi: "troops and elephants innumerable, but mostly infantry". Use Ellora Cave 16 elephant base and the Mahabharata battle friezes as the style reference for elephant armour and howdahs.
- Horses: Sanjan v. 23 (Himalayan springs), Cambay v. 19 (crossing the Yamuna), Pampa (bathed in the Ganga); Sulaiman: "many horses". The "Gold Dinara" horseman (if genuine) shows a rider with curved sword.
- Weapons named in the records read: arrows "rained" by Dhruva (Sanjan v. 12); sword (Ellora-era "Khadgavaloka" = "sword-glance", Dantidurga's title); dagger (Rajaditya, Wikipedia reading — verify); "sword-washing" (balgachchu) grants. Sculptural reference: Ellora Cave 16 Mahabharata/Ramayana reliefs; the Atakur stone's own carving of hound and boar.
- Dress/ornament: the Ellora murals (above) and Cave 16 Gajalakshmi; Sulaiman: "the men who have their ears pierced" (the Balhara's subjects wear ear-ornaments). No portrait of any Rashtrakuta king survives; the Badami "royal ascetic" is a guess (EI XVIII p. 242).
- Ships: the Arab accounts (Siraf merchants trading to the Balhara's ports Kambaya to Saimur; Friday mosques in his coastal cities) — depict Arab dhows at Konkan ports, not Indian warships; no Rashtrakuta naval record was found.
- Hound: the Atakur stone depicts Kali (a hunting hound) fighting a boar — use the relief itself for the breed look.

### 6.5 Anachronisms to avoid
- Hoysala-style lathe-turned pillars and star plans (12th c.); Vijayanagara gopurams; Bahmani/Deccan sultanate domes at Malkhed.
- Kannada script: use southern Old Kannada/Kadamba-derived letter forms of the 9th–10th c. (see Nilgund, Atakur photos), not modern Kannada or Devanagari on Kannada stones; copper plates are in Nagari-type script.
- Islamic-era armour (chainmail hauberks, Mughal helmets), stirrups-with-plate saddles, cannons, gunpowder.
- Curved talwars; Chola bronzes of the 11th c. as "Rashtrakuta" art.
- A finished Kolhapur Mahalakshmi temple in its present (later) form for a c. 860s scene; a Gommateshvara colossus at Sravana Belgola for the 982 scene (the statue is dated 981–983 — **check** before including it in Indra IV's death scene; it would be brand new or unfinished).
- Any crown/portrait of Amoghavarsha; any "Garuda flag" of a specific design (no description survives).

## 7. Key people beyond the kings

- Srivijaya (ಶ್ರೀವಿಜಯ) — court poet; per the colophons the actual composer of the *Kavirajamarga* "on the lines laid down by Nripatunga" (EI X; Mysore Univ. encyclopaedia). Two poets named Srivijaya are known to Kannada literature.
- Jinasena (ಜಿನಸೇನ) — Digambara acharya, Amoghavarsha's guru; *Parsvabhyudaya*, *Adipurana* (completed by Gunabhadra), co-author of the *Jayadhavala* with Virasena (Kamath; Mysore Univ. encyclopaedia).
- Gunabhadra (ಗುಣಭದ್ರ) — pupil of Jinasena; *Uttarapurana*, *Atmanusasana*; his colophon is the source for Krishna II's early reign (**date not verified**).
- Mahaviracharya (ಮಹಾವೀರಾಚಾರ್ಯ) — Jain mathematician; *Ganitasarasangraha* dedicated to Amoghavarsha, "the best of kings" (Pingree 2001 pp. 9–10 via Wikipedia).
- Sakatayana (ಶಾಕಟಾಯನ) — grammarian; *Sabdanusasana* with the commentary *Amoghavritti* named for the king.
- Patalamalla (ಪಾತಾಳಮಲ್ಲ) — "Arya Patalamalla", through whose greatness the boy-king's sun rose again (Sanjan v. 41); otherwise unknown.
- Bankesha/Bankeya (ಬಂಕೇಯ) — Amoghavarsha's general; defeated by the Ganga Rachamalla at Rajaramadu (Kamath p. 78 via Wikipedia; **UNVERIFIED**).
- Silamahadevi (ಶೀಲಮಹಾದೇವಿ) — Dhruva's queen, a Vengi Chalukya princess; issued grants with imperial titles (Jethwai 786).
- Chandrabbalabbe and Revakanimmadi — daughters of Amoghavarsha married to Ganga princes Butuga I and Ereganga (Kamath p. 78 via Wikipedia).
- Vatsaraja — composer of the Sanjan charter; per a social-media claim (unreliable) chief justice and general; **UNVERIFIED** in the EI text read.
- Narasimha II of Vemulavada (ನರಸಿಂಹ) — Chalukya feudatory who chased Mahipala to the Ganga for Indra III (Vemulavada inscription; Pampa).
- Arikesari II (ಅರಿಕೇಸರಿ) — Narasimha's son, Pampa's patron, "Gunarnava"; rebelled against Govinda IV and enthroned Amoghavarsha III; Pampa's Arjuna.
- Pampa (ಪಂಪ), 902–955 (dates vary) — *Adipurana* and *Vikramarjuna Vijaya* (941); "ಆದಿಕವಿ"; born in Vengi or Annigeri; buried in memory at Banavasi: "ಆರಂಕುಸಮಿಟ್ಟೊಡಂ ನೆನೆವುದೆನ್ನ ಮನಂ ಬನವಾಸಿ ದೇಶಮಂ" (Wikipedia "Adikavi Pampa", citing Hampana 2010); died at Bodhan.
- Jinavallabha — Pampa's younger brother; Kurkyala/Bommalagutta inscription naming their parents and a tank called Kavitagunarnava.
- Ponna (ಪೊನ್ನ) — Krishna III's poet, "ಉಭಯಕವಿಚಕ್ರವರ್ತಿ"; *Santipurana*, *Bhuvanaika-Ramabhyudaya*, *Jinaksharamale*; from Vengi/Punganur, migrated to Manyakheta (Wikipedia; Narasimhacharya 1988 pp. 20–22).
- Pushpadanta — Apabhramsa poet at Manyakheta under Bharata, minister of Krishna III; *Mahapurana* (965).
- Halayudha — Sanskrit prosodist at Krishna III's court (*Kavirahasya*).
- Trivikrama Bhatta — *Nalachampu* (915) at Indra III's court.
- Butuga II (ಬೂತುಗ), Ganga — brother-in-law of Krishna III; killer of Rachamalla and (per Atakur) of Rajaditya; rewarded with Banavasi 12,000 etc.
- Manalera (ಮಣಲೆರ) — Sagara-lineage warrior, "lord of Valabhi", Butuga's champion; broke the Chola elephant; master of the hound Kali.
- Marasimha II (ಮಾರಸಿಂಹ), Ganga — crowned Indra IV, fought Tailapa, sallekhana at Bankapura (Saka 896 per Melagani; 974/975 in modern accounts).
- Rajaditya (ರಾಜಾದಿತ್ಯ) — Chola crown prince, son of Parantaka I and a Chera princess; "Yanai-mel-tunjiya".
- Vellan Kumaran — Chera commander with Rajaditya (EI XXVII).
- Sulaiman al-Tajir of Siraf — merchant, account of 851; Abu Zaid of Siraf his continuator; Al-Masudi (visited 943); Ibn Khurdadba; Istakhri; Ibn Hauqal.
- Harsha Siyaka — Paramara king who sacked Manyakheta 972–73; Munja his successor, later captured and killed by Tailapa (Kauthem 1003 "imprisonment of Utpala"; Merutunga's tale of Mrinalavati is legend-tier).
- Tailapa II (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ತೈಲಪ) — Tardavadi governor 965; overthrew Karka II 973; married the Rashtrakuta Jakavve; "Panchalamardana Panchanana" for killing the Ganga Panchaladeva.
- Rattaraja, Silahara of south Konkan — his Kharepatan plates (Saka 930) keep "his love and regard for the late Rashtrakutas" and name Kakkala.
- Kokasa — the architect of the Kailasa in the *Katha-Kalpataru* legend (legend only).

## 8. Sources to avoid (or use with care)

- Exam-prep and listicle sites (prepp.in, unacademy.com, anantamias.com, gktoday) — uncited; repeat "pillar of victory at Rameswaram", "Ashoka of the South", "Kokasa built it in a week" as fact.
- X/Twitter and Medium posts on Amoghavarsha's "thumb"/"little finger" — the charter says left finger.
- Wikipedia "Amoghavarsha": useful finding aid but (a) says the *Kavirajamarga* was co-authored "with Jinasena" (unsupported), (b) cites a Kannada novel (Ta. Ra. Su., *Nrupatunga*) as a source for the king's retirement, (c) gives 814 and 815 for accession without reconciling. Follow its footnotes to Kamath/Altekar/EI instead.
- Wikipedia "Rashtrakuta dynasty" ruler table — rendered off-by-one in one fetch during this research; always re-check against §1.1 anchors.
- Wikipedia "Battle of Takkolam"/"Rajaditya Chola" — the arrow vs dagger detail is unresolved there; go to Fleet, EI VI pp. 53–57.
- Wikipedia "Kailasa Temple, Ellora" — mixes the medieval legend and modern "rock removed" estimates; the only quantified estimate is Dhavalikar's labour estimate.
- Elliot & Dowson's editorial notes (colonial framing); quote the translated travellers only.
- Jyotsna Kamat / kamat.com popular pages and Kannada-nationalist blogs (telangana360.com is fine for photographs of Bommalagutta but not for dates).
- Any page giving Kannada verse text of the *Kavirajamarga* without naming the edition — the Kannada Wikisource has no main-namespace text of the work.
- Auction-house coin attributions (Classical Numismatic Gallery) copied onto Commons as "Govinda III" — provisional.

### B.1 Before Dantidurga
- Sanjan vv. 3–6: Govinda (I), son of Prichchhakaraja, "of the Yadu line"; successor Karkaraja gets only conventional praise ("chests adorned with the Kaustubha gem", archery). EI XVIII pp. 238, 243, 252. — inscription.
- Sanjan v. 7: Indraraja "married the daughter of a Chalukya king at Khetaka by the Rakshasa form of marriage", i.e. seized her in battle; editors: Khetaka = Kaira, north Gujarat, and the bride was of the Gujarat Chalukya branch. EI XVIII p. 238. — inscription.
- The EI XVIII editors conclude from the Antroli-Chharoli grant (Saka 679) that Rashtrakutas already held south Gujarat before Dantidurga. p. 238. — scholarship.
- EI VI (Fleet, "fleet64") cites a Dashavatara record naming Indra I as father and Dantivarman I as grandfather of Govinda I — an earlier generation than Sanjan gives; the two genealogies must be reconciled by the writer only as "ancestors". — inscription.

### B.2 Dantidurga (ದಂತಿದುರ್ಗ)
- Sanjan v. 8: "defeats many elephant-riding hosts and subdues kings from the Himalayas to the Setu" (panegyric). v. 9: Hiranyagarbha at Ujjayini with "a Gurjara lord and others" as door-keepers. EI XVIII pp. 238–239, 252. — inscription.
- Ellora cave 15 inscription gives him the second name "Maharaja-Sarva" and records the Ujjain gift; editors use it to place the Pratiharas' original seat at Ujjain, not Bhinmal. EI XVIII p. 239. — inscription / scholarship.
- Samangad (Saka 675) titles: Prithivivallabha, Khadgavaloka (Fleet: "the preamble of the prose passages does establish for Dantidurga the birudas of Prithivivallabha and Khadgavaloka"); Fleet also observed that in an earlier record he "had not as yet assumed the imperialistic titles which appear in the Samangad plates". — inscription (via EI discussion in search results).
- Karhad plates v. 9: Dantidurga "wrested supreme sovereignty from the Chalukyas" — "an addition not found in the Deoli plates" (R. G. Bhandarkar). EI IV, viewer 334. — inscription.
- Cambay v. 7: Dantidurga "arose in the Yadu race as the moon in the clear sky". EI VII p. 28. — inscription.
- Vakkaleri plates of 757 show the Chalukya Kirtivarman II still holding southern provinces then (Wikipedia "Dantidurga"; **UNVERIFIED**). — scholarship.
- Succeeded by his paternal uncle Krishna I (Cambay commentary, EI VII p. 28). — inscription.

### B.3 Krishna I (ಒಂದನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣ), Subhatunga, Akalavarsha
- Sanjan v. 10: "Subhatunga-Vallabha takes the Chalukya family's fortune in battle." v. 11: Akalavarsha "destroyed kings and chieftains" and "became a royal sage (rajarshi)". EI XVIII pp. 239, 252. — inscription.
- Cambay v. 8: "destroyed the Chalukya race, as the sun dispels darkness". EI VII p. 28. — inscription.
- Karhad plates: the family "sprung from a family known as Tunga, which is why many princes' names end in that word"; Krishna I = Subhatunga, Govinda III = Jagattunga, Sarva = Nripatunga. EI IV viewer 334. — inscription (etymology is the charter's).
- Baroda plates (Karka, 812/13): "Krishnaraja" built the Siva temple at Elapura; Bühler and Fleet first mistranslated it as a hill-fort; R. G. Bhandarkar corrected it (IA XII p. 228: "the two stanzas form one sentence" whose principal clause is "yena rajna kirtanam akaryata"). — scholarship on an inscription.
- From the Baroda inscription "Krishna I may have had to subdue another claimant to the throne, perhaps a son of Dantidurga" (Wikipedia "Krishna I"; **UNVERIFIED**). — scholarship.

### B.4 Govinda II (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ಗೋವಿಂದ) and Dhruva (ಧ್ರುವ)
- Sanjan v. 12: "Prabhutavarsha and then Dharavarsha reign; Dharavarsha rains arrows on the battlefield." v. 13: "a king's drum is compared to Death belching, and his warriors drink enemy blood." EI XVIII p. 252. — inscription.
- Sanjan v. 14: Dhruva "seizes the white umbrellas of a Gauda king fleeing between the Ganges and Jumna"; editors: Dharmapala (or Gopala), who "had left his own territory to help the Kanauj ruler". p. 239. — inscription / scholarship.
- v. 15: "Dhruva's fame reached the heavens, implying that he had died." — inscription (editors' reading).
- Kamath (via Wikipedia "Dhruva Dharavarsha"): Dhruva deposed Govinda II, who "had become unpopular on account of his various misconducts" (Karhad plates); defeated Vatsaraja of the Pratiharas; defeated Dharmapala "in the Ganges-Yamuna doab"; imprisoned the Ganga Shivamara II and made his own son Kambarasa governor; the Pallava Nandivarman II "paid handsomely with many elephants"; campaigns at Kanchi 785 and against the Gangas 788; married Silabhattarika, daughter of the Vengi Chalukya Vishnuvardhana IV (Jethwai 786). Kamath 2001 p. 75. — scholarship (**verify each against EI before scripting**).
- Titles (EI XVIII): Kalivallabha, Srivallabha, Dharavarsha, Maharajadhiraja, Paramesvara. — inscription.
- Dhruva "was a sworn enemy of the Gangas" (Paithan plates verses, via Jain-hosted source in search). — inscription (secondary).

### B.5 Govinda III (ಮೂರನೆಯ ಗೋವಿಂದ), Jagattunga, Prabhutavarsha, Kirtinarayana, Tribhuvanadhavala, Vikrama
- Sanjan v. 16: crowned as "son of Nirupama"; restores feudatories; releases the imprisoned Ganga. v. 17: a revolt of vassals who shifted allegiance — the confederacy of twelve kings under Stambha (= his elder brother Ranavaloka-Kambhadeva). v. 18: "the Ganga, earlier freed from foot fetters, ended up with fetters around his neck." EI XVIII pp. 239–240, 252–253. — inscription.
- v. 21: ruler compared to the Sun in its northern course (udagayana) — a pun on the northern march. v. 22: "carries away the fame of kings Nagabhata and Chandragupta" (Nagabhata = Pratihara, son of Vatsaraja of Avanti; Chandragupta = Pandava-line ruler of Kosala at Sripura/Sirpur). — inscription / scholarship.
- v. 24: along the Narmada he takes "Malava, Kosala, Kalinga, Vanga, Dahala, Odraka" and "assigns these lands to his servants". v. 25: "sets up a capital at the foot of the Vindhyas" and "builds temples". — inscription.
- Other charters (Radhanpur, Wani-Dindori): after Malava submitted he marched to the Vindhyas, where "a prince named Marasarva offered him choice heirlooms", and he "spent the rainy season at Sribhavana". EI XVIII p. 240. — inscription.
- vv. 30–32: the Dravida campaign: "terrifies the Kerala, Pandya and Chaulika kings, withers the Pallava, drives the Kalinga and Magadha kings to fast to death, and humbles the Gurjjara leader"; compared to Rama; title Vikrama. v. 33: "disloyal Gangas are fettered and killed"; the camp kept clean "by paid labour, and the lord of Vengi by unpaid labour". v. 34: the Lanka statues to Helapura and Kanchi; editors ask whether Helapura is Velapura/Belur in Hassan district since the Radhanpur grant places him on the Tungabhadra. EI XVIII p. 241, 254. — inscription.
- Sirur (866): Govinda III "conquered the Keralas, Malavas, Sautas, Gurjaras and some other dynasty at the hill-fort of Chitrakuta". Fleet, EI VII. — inscription.
- Baroda plates (IA XII p. 157): "speaks of him as taking away from his enemies" the Ganga and Yamuna — but see Majumdar's re-assignment to Dhruva (EI XVIII p. 239 fn.). — inscription (attribution disputed).
- Nesarika grant, EI XXXIV pp. 123–137, vv. 21–23: emblems and tributes including "the bull from the Pallava king". — inscription (via Wikipedia).
- Commons photos of his stones at Mavali (797 and 800): File:Old Kannada inscription (left-800AD and right-797 AD) of Rashtrakuta King Govinda III at Mavali.jpg.
- Jain patronage: Virasena and Jinasena's *Dhavala* commentary (Williams 1963 via Wikipedia). — scholarship.
- Queen Gamundabbe named in the Someshvara inscription of 804 (Wikipedia, uncited; **UNVERIFIED**).

### B.6 Amoghavarsha I (ಅಮೋಘವರ್ಷ ನೃಪತುಂಗ), Sarva, Viranarayana, Atisayadhavala, Lakshmivallabhendra, Martanda
- Titles in Kannada per Mysore Univ. encyclopaedia: ಶರ್ವ, ನೃಪತುಂಗ, ಅತಿಶಯಧವಳ, ವೀರನಾರಾಯಣ, ಲಕ್ಷ್ಮೀವಲ್ಲಭೇಂದ್ರ, ಶ್ರೀವಲ್ಲಭ, ಮಾರ್ತಾಂಡ. — scholarship (CC BY-SA text).
- Sanjan v. 36: rules "to protect his ancestors' and relatives' fame and to destroy Kali's sinfulness". v. 37: palace drums proclaim "no other earth-lord protects the humble, conquers enemies, gives gifts and upholds custom as he does". v. 38: "the new kingdom of sixteen principalities"; Kali "flees inward and corrupts the feudatories, ministers and relatives". vv. 42–46: body-as-kingdom allegory (soul king, mind minister, senses feudatories); hostile lords "kept waiting outside his assembly hall" and despair when they see "their courtesans and elephants, now in his possession, will not be returned". v. 49: under him "calamities to husbandry, plagues and famines cannot touch any of the six seasons". EI XVIII pp. 254–255. — inscription.
- Sanjan prose: "staying at his capital Manyakheta" he gives Jharivallika (Zaroli) in the Twenty-four-village group of Samjana (Sanjan, Thana district) to four Karhada Brahmans of the Bahvricha school and Bharadvaja, Vaddamukha and Vatsa gotras; boundaries Kalluvi/Kalu river, Uplat, Nandgaon, Dhanoli. pp. 238, 242–243. — inscription.
- Sanjan v. 48: the fratricidal Gupta donor "made her write a deed for one lakh, one crore" vs the Rashtrakuta who "gave away his kingdom freely"; editors identify the Gupta as Skandagupta-Vikramaditya (editorial notes dissent). p. 242, 255. — inscription / scholarship (disputed identification).
- Cambay v. 12: Vingavalli victory over the Vengi Chalukyas ("probably in the Vengi territory", Hultzsch). EI VII p. 29. — inscription.
- Kamath 2001 pp. 77–79 (via Wikipedia "Amoghavarsha"): revolts 818–820 put down by 821; the Ganga Shivamara II killed 816; Bankesha defeated at Rajaramadu; daughters married to Gangas; Vengi wars with Vijayaditya II and Vishnuvardhana V (killed 846?); Alupas subdued under prince Vimaladitya 870; friendship with the Pallava Nandivarman III whose son was named Nripatunga. — scholarship (**each date UNVERIFIED** against EI).
- Sirur/Nilgund (866, "52nd year"): "worshipped by the lords of Vanga, Anga, Magadha, Malava and Vengi". Fleet, EI VII. — inscription (panegyric).
- Kumsi stone 876 (Commons). — inscription.
- Sulaiman 851 and Masudi 943: see §2.4. — eyewitness.
- *Ganitasarasangraha* praise verses (Pingree 2001 pp. 9–10; Datta & Singh 1935 pp. 30–35 via Wikipedia); Rangacharya's 1912 Madras edition is public domain — **not fetched**. — scholarship.
- *Prasnottara-ratnamalika*: Digambara copies say he composed it after giving up the throne; other copies omit him; later translated into Tibetan (Wikipedia). EI XVIII p. 242. — scholarship (disputed).
- Jain buildings claimed for the reign: Jain Narayana temple at Pattadakal, a basadi at Konnur, Neminatha basadi at Manyakheta (Deccan Herald 2007 via Wikipedia). — weak.
- Succeeded by his son Krishna II; Wikipedia says retired c. 877, died 878 (Narasimhacharya 1988 p. 1). — scholarship.

### B.7 Krishna II (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ಕೃಷ್ಣ), Akalavarsha
- Cambay v. 13: the Eastern Chalukya Gunaga-Vijayaditya III "drove Krishna II from Manyakheta" (Hultzsch's reading); Krishna's ally Kokkala of the Chedi family and Kokkala's son Arjuna. EI VII p. 29. — inscription / scholarship.
- Karhad plates: Krishna "honoured by the Gurjaras, Gaudas, Kalingas, Magadhas and others" (v. 14 region, viewer 338). — inscription (panegyric).
- Gunabhadra's *Uttarapurana* colophon at Bankapura under Lokaditya — **not verified in this pass**.
- Krishna II's gold gadyana with Garuda (livehistoryindia) — provisional.

### B.8 Indra III (ಮೂರನೆಯ ಇಂದ್ರ), Nityavarsha
- Cambay vv. 17–18: born to Jagattunga and Lakshmi (Chedi princess). v. 19: Kalapriya, Yamuna, Mahodaya (see §3 Story D). EI VII pp. 29–30. — inscription.
- Bhandarkar (EI VII p. 42): the Kannauj ruler "Kshitipala" was defeated and ousted; the "Harshadeva" who restored him is read as a Chandella prince. — scholarship.
- Narasimha II of Vemulavada: "conquered the Latas", "reduced the seven Malavas to ashes", defeated Mahipala "on the Ganges and bathed his horses there"; Mahipala had earlier "set his sword in a stone victory pillar at Kalapriya"; Rashtrakuta elephants "damaged its walls and flags" (Venkataramanayya 1953 pp. 24–30 via Wikipedia). — scholarship on inscriptions.
- Pampa calls Mahipala "Gurjararaja" (Wikipedia "Mahipala I"). — contemporary text.
- Married Vijamba of the Kalachuris (Wikipedia, uncited). Trivikrama's *Nalachampu* 915 at his court (Sastri 1955). — scholarship.

### B.9 Amoghavarsha II and Govinda IV (ನಾಲ್ಕನೆಯ ಗೋವಿಂದ)
- Bhadana grant: Amoghavarsha II reigned "about a year"; Hultzsch: Govinda IV "likely hastened his death and usurped the throne". EI VII p. 45. — scholarship.
- Cambay findspot: a husbandman's plough struck a rotten wooden box at Cambay (Khambayat); the plates went to a Gujarati at Petlad; Prof. A. V. Kathavate helped Bhandarkar obtain them; three plates 13⅝" × 10⅜" on a 4⅜" ring, uncut when sent to Hultzsch. EI VII p. 26. — editorial.
- Govinda IV's titles: Paramabhattaraka Maharajadhiraja Paramesvara, Suvarnavarsha, Prithivivallabha, Vallabhanarendra, Vikrantanarayana, Nripatitrinetra, Nityakandarpa, Sahasanka ("because of his unmatched heroic deeds", v. 22); "called Suvarnavarsha because he rained gold" (v. 23). EI VII pp. 46, 52. — inscription.
- Pattabandha at Kapitthaka near the Godavari; weighed against gold; the gift figures in §4 no. 9. ll. 46–49. — inscription.
- Dandapur inscriptions 918 and 930; Sangli plates 933 (Reu 1933 p. 80). — scholarship.
- Eastern Chalukya records of Amma I and Bhima: Bhima "repulsed" Govinda's armies (Reu p. 80). — inscription (via Reu).
- Arikesari II and other vassals rebel, Govinda "killed in battle" (Hultzsch EI VII p. 45) or died prematurely (Reu); Amoghavarsha III (Baddiga) enthroned 935/936. — scholarship (conflicting).
- Patron of the Kannada poet Ravinagabhatta (Wikipedia, uncited; **UNVERIFIED**).

### B.10 Krishna III (ಮೂರನೆಯ ಕೃಷ್ಣ), Akalavarsha, Kannaradeva
- Birudas on the Soratur stone 951: Anevedanga, Madagajamalla, Chalakenallata; Atakur footnotes add Vanagajamalla, Gandamartanda, Kachchega. SII XI.1 no. 39; EI VI p. 57 fn. — inscription.
- Karhad grant 959: village Kankem in Karahata (Karhad) district, Kalli twelve, to Gaganasiva "versed in all the Sivasiddhantas", for ascetics; dated Wednesday 9 March 959, Phalguna ba. 13, Kalayukta, Saka 880; found while digging house foundations in Satara district, passed via Hari Narayan Apte to R. G. Bhandarkar. EI IV viewer 333–334. — inscription.
- Karhad vv. 15–16: Jagattunga "died without obtaining the kingdom"; v. 17: Amoghavarsha (III) "compared to Rama"; v. 18: "Govinda Raja … takes the kingdom after his brother's death". — inscription.
- SII XI.1: Ron 942 (Butuga's governorships); Kurtakoti 946 (grants under Nanniya-Ganga Butuga II); Naregal 950 (tank gift to a basadi built by Padmabbarasi, queen of Butayya, entrusted to Gunachandra-Pandita); Soratur 951 (vidya-dana and matha during a lunar eclipse, bodyguard Ruddapayya administering Saratavura); Narsalgi 965 (Tailapa at Tardavadi). — inscription.
- Jura (c. 964) Kannada prasasti near Jabalpur; Marasimha's records (965, 968) say his forces "destroyed Ujjayani"; Altekar: Siyaka rebelled; K. N. Seth: "no evidence" Siyaka fought Krishna III. — inscription / scholarship (disputed).
- Masudi's 943 visit falls at the start of the reign: Balhara "greatest of the kings of India", "enemy of the king of Kanauj". — eyewitness.
- Ponna given "Ubhaya-kavi-chakravarti" (Narasimhacharya pp. 20–22). Halayudha, Pushpadanta (965) at the capital. — scholarship.
- Somadeva's *Yasastilaka* (959) "reportedly describes" the Chola victory (Reu p. 86). — contemporary text (not read).
- Religion: P. B. Desai calls him Jain; his inscriptions open by naming the donee institution, not a deity (Desai 1957 via Wikipedia). — scholarship.
- Gave Tardavadi to Tailapa II — "which later proved damaging" (Wikipedia, uncited; the Narsalgi stone is the evidence). — inscription.

### B.11 Khottiga, Karka II, Indra IV, Tailapa II
- Khottiga = Amoghavarsha IV, Nityavarsha; installed a panavatta at the Danavulapadu Jain temple in 968 (Shah 1987 p. 156; Nandi 1973 p. 35 via Wikipedia). — scholarship.
- EI XXIX (Venkataramayya): Arthuna inscription of Chamundaraja — warrior Kankadeva "beat the army of the Karnata king on the Narmada bank and died fighting for Sri-Harsha of Malava"; the author places it in a Khottiga campaign against his former Paramara feudatory (the "Khalighatta" fight). — inscription / scholarship.
- Tailapa's backers: Yadava Bhillama II, Chalukya Baddega II, possibly the Kalachuris (Altekar 1960 pp. 298–299). — scholarship.
- Tailapa's later records: Sogal 980 (Rattas of Saundatti submit); 980 inscription calls him "Indra's thunderbolt against the Chola mountain"; Nolamba grant 981; Kogali 992 — defeated the Chola king at Rodda and seized 150 war elephants; Kauthem 1003 — "imprisonment of Utpala" (Munja); 997 Bhadana (Silaharas shift to Chalukyas). Sastri 1960 pp. 320–323 via Wikipedia "Tailapa II". — inscription (via Sastri).
- Merutunga's *Prabandhachintamani* (14th c., from the lost *Munja-rasa*): Munja defeated Tailapa six (or sixteen) times, crossed the Godavari against his minister Rudraditya's advice, was captured; loved Tailapa's sister Mrinalavati; made to beg door-to-door and executed. — legend, first recorded c. 400 years later.
- Indra IV: Rice 1889 no. 57, Saka 904 Chitrabhanu, "son's son of the illustrious Krishna Rajendra", vow "on the hill". Fleet (EI VI "fleet76/78") notes the epitaph gives Krishna III's name in verse as "Krishnarajendra". — inscription.
- Marasimha: no. 38 "holding festival at the coronation of Indra Raja"; Melagani inscription: died Saka 896. — inscription.
- Panchaladeva, Marasimha's Ganga successor, styled "Chalukya Panchanana" (975); Tailapa "cut off his head on the battlefield" → "Panchalamardana Panchanana" (Sastri 1960 pp. 320–321; Desai 1970 p. 156). — inscription (via Sastri).

### B.12 Administration, economy, society (for texture)
- Provinces: rashtra / vishaya / nadu; the records read use "Banavasi twelve-thousand", "Gangavadi ninety-six-thousand", "Belvola three-hundred", "Purigere three-hundred", "Kisukad seventy", "Bagenad seventy", "Tardavadi thousand", "Atukur twelve", "Bempur twelve", "Kalli twelve" — the numbers are village counts (Atakur, Ron, Narsalgi, Karhad). — inscription.
- Sulaiman: troops paid regular wages; Masudi: mostly infantry; Istakhri/Ibn Hauqal: Muslim merchants govern their own quarters in Balhara's coastal cities and have Friday mosques; Sulaiman: Balhara "most partial to the Arabs". — eyewitness.
- Govinda III's camps "kept clean" by feudatories' corvée (Sanjan v. 33); the Vengi lord did it by unpaid labour. — inscription.
- Coinage denominations per Kamath (§2.6); the 0.28 g elephant dramma shows the actual scale of a silver coin. — scholarship / object.
- Grant types: agrahara to Brahmans (Sanjan, Karhad), basadi tanks (Naregal), matha and vidya-dana (Soratur), "sword-washing" reward grants (Atakur), hero-stones with land for worship (Atakur; Ron hero-stone of Pampayya). — inscription.
- Religion in the records: the same Amoghavarsha offers to Mahalakshmi (Sanjan), endows Brahmans (Sanjan), and is Jinasena's disciple; Krishna III grants to a Saiva Siddhanta ascetic (Karhad) and a Jain basadi (Naregal). Do not write any king as exclusively one faith. — inscription.
