## 1. Birth, youth, the father, and the river death of 1068

### 1a. Birth and boyhood: the court poem's version
BUHLER 1875 pp. 28-30 (summary of Vikramankadevacharita II.25-91, III.1-25):
> But in the midst of his victories and his prosperity, Ahavamalla was tormented by a deep sorrow.
> For he had no heir. At last he formed the resolution to lay aside for a while all princely pomp
> and to perform, together with his queen, severe penance in order to obtain a son through Siva's
> favour. ... the king, one morning when engaged in his prayers, heard a heavenly voice, which
> announced to him that Siva, pleased with his faith and penance, was willing to grant him three
> sons, the second of whom would surpass in valour and virtues all the princes of the olden times.
> (p. 30) The child's marvellous lustre, which announced its future greatness, induced the king to
> call him Vikramaditya. He throve and grew up a handsome and strong boy, the favourite of his
> father. Early he showed in his plays that he was destined to be a mighty warrior and conqueror. He
> loved to chase the royal swans, the Rajahamsas, and to tease the lionwhelps in their cages. Later
> he acquired the various Lipis or alphabets and the art of using the bow. "Sarasvati also, the
> giver of poetry and eloquence, kissed his lotus-mouth."
Buhler's caution, p. 29 n. 1: "in this and subsequent portions of Bilhana's narrative there is an
evident design of representing Vikrama as the special favourite of the gods and as such entitled to
oust his elder brother from the throne."
Tier: contemporary court poem (birth boon = `legend` in effect; the naming, the bow and letters are
plausible and can be told). Birth year: NOT RECORDED anywhere read. FLEET 1896 p. 447 infers age:
he was governing provinces in 1055-56, so "Even if he was then but a mere child, governing only
nominally, he must have been at least seventy years old in A.D. 1126."

### 1b. Parents: Someshvara I Ahavamalla and Bachaladevi (one mother for three brothers)
FLEET 1896 p. 438: "Jayasimha II. was succeeded by his son, Somesvara I., who had the double biruda
of Trailokyamalla-Ahavamalla. His wives were, - Bachaladevi, who was the mother of Somesvara II.,
Vikramaditya VI., and Jayasimha III., and was probably his first wife; Chandalakabbe or
Chandrikadevi, who had the title of piriyarasi, or chief queen in A.D. 1047-48; Mailaladevi ...
and Ketaladevi." Note 1 gives the Gadag Trikutesvara verse (A.D. 1102, the 27th year of the
Chalukya-Vikrama-kala) naming Bachaladevi as mother of Bhuvanaikamalla, Tribhuvanamalla and the
Nolamba prince, and adds: "The Gadag inscription of A.D. 1098 also names Bachaladevi as the mother".
FLEET 1896 p. 440 n. 3: "the Gadag inscriptions say distinctly that they were uterine brothers, born
of one and the same mother ... Bilhana's account is to the same purport. And the attribution of
particular titles in each instance is to be explained by the territorial administration which each
of the brothers held." Tier: inscription. (This corrects Rice's "Ganga mother" for Vikramaditya.)

### 1c. Prince and viceroy
FLEET 1896 p. 440: "In A.D. 1055-56, his second son Vikramaditya VI. was ruling the Gangavadi
ninety-six-thousand and the Banavasi twelve-thousand, with Harikesarin, of the family of the
Kadambas of Hangal, as his subordinate in charge of the latter district." n. 4: a Bankapur record
gives him "the Western Ganga titles ... Ganga-Permanadi; but the latter is qualified by the prefix
Chalukya, - 'Chalukya-Ganga-Permanadi'". Tier: inscription.
EC VII 1902 Intro p. 21-22 (Sk 124): the Permmadi basadi at Balligave was "one established by
himself when he was a Prince (kumara), and viceroy of Banavasi." Tier: inscription (as summarised).

### 1d. The yuvaraja offer and refusal (court poem)
BUHLER 1875 pp. 30-31 (III.25-59): Ahavamalla "conceived the plan of making him Yuvaraja ... But, as
soon as he opened his mind to the prince, the latter, respectfully but firmly, refused the offered
favour, alleging that the dignity of Yuvaraja belonged by right to his elder brother." n. 1: "looks
as if it had been touched up in order to whitewash V.'s character and to blacken that of his enemy."
Tier: contemporary court poem, partisan. Can be told with dignity as the poem's account; disclose.
Independent Chola evidence on the same question (hostile), SII III: Virarajendra "tied the necklace
on 'the liar's' neck and appointed him to the dignity of Vallabha"; Hultzsch: "the necklace
bestowed on Vikramaditya VI. was taken away from his elder brother Somesvara II., and ...
Virarajendra I. appointed the former as heir-apparent of Ahavamalla in the place of the second."
Tier: inscription (Chola, hostile; see §9 dignity flags).

### 1e. The father's death in the Tungabhadra, 29 March 1068
EC VII 1902 Sk 136 translation, pp. 101-102 (Balligave, dated 1068):
> ... the muscles of his arm and the energy of his mind not being exhausted, he fought against Indra,
> defeated him, and on his giving tribute, made friends with him, and returned alone, a universal
> emperor. In the year reckoned by sky, nine and number (990) of the Saka-kala, the year Kilaka, on
> the 8th day of Chaitra bahula, Sunday (29th March 1068 A.D.), thereby increasing his fame, he
> performed in Kuruvartti the rites of supreme yoga, and in the Tungabhadra the master of the world
> king Ahavamalla ascended to heaven.
Same record: Somesvara assumed "with the troops of elephants and horses, the jewels, umbrella, and
throne, the honours of the kingdom" on Friday, Vaisakha suddha 7 (11 April 1068); then the Chola,
"saying 'A new reign; (a kingdom) fit for a hero; this is the time to invade it; I will surround Gutti
and besiege it'", was driven off by the cavalry. Tier: inscription.
EC VII 1902 Intro p. 20 (Rice's paraphrase of Bilhana): Ahavamalla "was seized with a severe fever,
and feeling that his end was approaching, had himself conveyed to the banks of the Tungabhadra.
Bathing in the river and giving away much gold in charity, he waded in until the water reached his
neck, and amid the din of the waves and of all manner of musical instruments deliberately drowned
himself"; footnote: "This mode of death, Dr. Bhandarkar says, is called Jalasamadhi."
BUHLER 1875 p. 32 (IV.31-68): the messenger told "the dismal story of Ahavamalla's sudden illness and
death. The king, he said, had been supremely happy on learning his son's success against the Chola,
Pandya and Simhala. In the midst of his rejoicings he had been attacked by a malignant fever.
Finding that all remedies were of no avail, he had resolved to finish his life in the Tungabhadra,
the Ganga of the south. With the consent of his ministers he had travelled to the sacred stream and
had died in its waves, meditating on Siva." n. 4: "Verse 58 contains a regular confession of
Ahavamalla's faith in Siva". p. 24: "One of the most touching is the description of Ahavamalla's
death". Tier: inscription (date, place) + contemporary court poem (fever, ministers, river).

### 1f. The son hears the news on the Krishna
BUHLER 1875 pp. 31-32: Vikrama "had come as far as the Krishna, when he suddenly was disquieted by
the appearance of unfavourable omens ... He stopped his march and performed on the banks of the
river Santis or propitiatory ceremonies"; on the news "Vikramaditya was deeply affected and loudly
manifested his grief. At first he refused to be consoled and had to be disarmed lest he should
attempt his own life. After a while he recovered and performed the funeral ceremonies on the banks
of the Krishna." p. 33: "he set out for Kalyana in order to console his brother. Somesvara came out
to meet him and received him affectionately." Tier: contemporary court poem. DIGNITY: tell as the
depth of a son's grief (the poem's own intent); avoid showing him restrained like a madman.

## 2. The brothers, the 1076 accession, and the Chalukya-Vikrama era

### 2a. The quarrel and the break (court poem)
BUHLER 1875 p. 33 (IV.69 - V.9): "Somesvara fell into evil courses. Pride obscured his judgment, he
became suspicious, cruel, and avaricious ... He even tried to do harm to his brother. When the latter
saw, that he was unable to restrain the king from evil and had to fear for his life and good name,
he left Kalyana together with all his followers. He, also, took with him the youngest son of
Ahavamalla, Jayasimha, as he did not think him safe, whilst living near the king." Pursuing armies
were destroyed "'like one mouthful'". Tier: court poem, partisan (the elder brother's "cruelty" is
the winner's charge).
EC VII Sk 136 (1068), Someshvara II's own record, shows the brothers in rank order at the start:
"Junior is king Vikrama-Ganga to me; to that Permmadi-Deva the next junior: Vira-Nolamba-Deva"
(Rice's translation p. 102). Tier: inscription. A strong, quiet scene for the opening of the reign.

### 2b. The Chola marriage, Kanchi, Rajiga, and the battle (poem + both sides)
BUHLER 1875 pp. 34-35 (V.28 - VI.25): the Chola king "sent an ambassador ... to ask for his
friendship, to cement which he offered his daughter's hand"; they met on the Tungabhadra, "the former
was humble and the latter generous to his former foe ... Vikrama's marriage with the Chola princess
was duly celebrated." On the father-in-law's death Vikrama "entered Kanchi and put the rebels there
under his heel", secured the throne for his wife's brother, stayed a month; "'a few days' after his
return, he learned that his brother-in-law had lost his life in a fresh rebellion and that Rajiga,
the lord of Vengi, had taken possession of the throne of Kanchi."
SII III (Hultzsch): Virarajendra's "daughter was given in marriage to the Western Chalukya king
Vikramaditya VI. - his son and successor was Parakesarivarman alias Adhirajendradeva". Tier:
inscription (independent Chola confirmation of the marriage).
BUHLER 1875 pp. 36-37 (VI.26-99): Somesvara allied with Rajiga; "It was only at the express command
of Siva, who, appearing to him in a dream, ordered him to fulfil his destiny ... that he consented
to an appeal to arms. ... the victory finally remained with Vikrama. Rajiga fled and Somesvara was
taken prisoner. ... He, at first, intended to restore his captive brother to liberty and to the
throne. But Siva interposed a second time and angrily commanded him to assume the sovereignty."
FLEET 1896 p. 444, the inscriptions' own words: Somesvara II "became intoxicated with pride, and
neglected the sufferings of his subjects, whereupon Vikramaditya, being virtuously minded, punished
or confined him, and became king" (Kalige record); and "by the strength of his own arm, Vikramaditya
seized in battle the sovereignty of Somesvara while it was still of no long duration, and made
himself emperor" (Gadag 1098). p. 445: Hemadri's Vratakhanda says Seunachandra II "saved
Vikramaditya from a coalition of his enemies and placed him on the throne of Kalyana"; "What
ultimately became of Somesvara II., is not known." Tier: inscription.
Scholarly verdict (cite on the evidence page only): BUHLER 1875 p. 38 n.: "Considering all these
points I am inclined to give the verdict against Vikrama and Bilhana and to assume that the former
designedly used his superior talents to oust his weaker brother." p. 37 n.: "It looks very
suspicious that the poet finds himself obliged to bring in Siva three times". BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 217:
"Bilhana, who is, of course, anxious to show his patron to be guiltless in this fratricidal war".
DISPUTED (chronology): FLEET 1896 p. 445 n. 3: "There is, however, some anachronism here that
requires to be cleared up" (Rajiga = Kulottunga's annexation of the Chola throne seems to fall in
1070 by Eastern Chalukya records, against Bilhana's placing just before 1076).

### 2c. The accession year and the coronation festival
FLEET 1896 p. 446: "the year A.D. 1076-77, the Anala or Nala samvatsara, Saka-Samvat 999 current,
commencing with Chaitra sukla 1, which corresponded, approximately, to the 9th March, A.D. 1076, was
reckoned as the first year of his reign. ... an inscription at Wadageri ... records grants that were
made towards the close of the same year, on Phalguna sukla 5, corresponding to the 31st January,
A.D. 1077, on account of the festival of the pattabandha or coronation. ... whether the record fixes
the coronation day, or an anniversary of it ... is not clear." Same record: he was "then reigning,
not at Kalyana, but at Nadaviyuppayana-vidu". (OCR prints "990 current"; 998 expired = 999 current,
as on p. 443; read 999.)
BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 217 n. 5 disagrees with Fleet's anniversary idea: "this is a mere assumption ...
The date in this inscription refers to the grant, and does not, in my opinion, show at all the day
on which the coronation ceremony took place." Tier: inscription; exact day DISPUTED.
Last date of the previous reign: Someshvara II's records run "to the month Bhadrapada (Aug.-Sept.)
falling in A.D. 1076" (FLEET 1896 pp. 442-443).

### 2d. The era: "rubbed out the Saka-varsha"
FLEET 1896 p. 447 (Gadag, Vira-Narayana temple, A.D. 1098, from an ink-impression; Fleet's text in
Ind. Ant. VIII p. 187, not opened):
> "By his amplitude, and unaided, Tribhuvanamalla, the king Chalukya-Vikramaditya, caused all the
> hostile kings to bow down, and became the lord of the world. Having rubbed out the brilliant
> Saka-varsha, he, the impetuous one, the most liberal man in the world, who delighted in religion,
> published his own name throughout the world, under the form of the Vikrama-varsha"
And (Yedarave, A.D. 1094-95):
> "Having said 'Why should the glory of the kings Vikramaditya and Nanda be a hindrance any
> longer?', he, with a loudly uttered command, abolished that (era) which has the name of Saka, and
> made that (era) which has the Chalukya counting."
Fleet's hedges: n. 3 "an ink-impression is still required, to give the exact reading of the
original, and to shew whether it really contains any reference to king Nanda". Body: "Instances have
not been found, of this era having been adopted by the kings of other dynasties. But nearly all the
records of his own time are thus dated ... the names of the samvatsaras, however, shewing exactly what
the corresponding Saka years are." n. 4: later records in its "fifty-second, fifty-third,
fifty-fourth, sixtieth, sixty-first, eighty-fourth, and ninety-fourth years" (A.D. 1127 to 1169-70).
EC VII's text section also has Shikarpur records dated in "Chalukya-Vikrama-kalada 67 neya" and
"Chalukya-Vikrama-varsha[57th]neya" (OCR of the Kannada texts; record numbers not checked). Tier:
inscription.
BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 217: "He abolished the Saka era and established his own; but it fell into disuse
not long after his death." (scholarship; note the EC VII counts show later local survivals.)

### 2e. The 1077 record that keeps both counts
SII XX No. 52 (Lakshmeshwar, B.K. No. 6 of 1935-36), summary: "This record, written in Sanskrit and
Kannada languages, is dated Saka 969 (in words), Chalukya-Vikrama year 2, Pingala, Ashadha su. 12,
Wednesday corresponding regularly to A.D. 1077 July 5. There is a second date in the record the
details of which are Saka 1000, Kalayukta, Madhumasa (Chaitra) Amavasya, Sunday. This corresponds to
A.D. 1078, April 14." It records the sallekhana death of Srinandi-Panditadeva "at Anesejjeya-basadi at
Purikaranagara". Tier: inscription (summary). NOTE: "Saka 969" is as printed on the whatisindia
page and cannot match 1077 (it should be about 999); the second date "Saka 1000" is the clean proof
that a Saka year was still written in his second year. Check the SII XX volume before quoting the
first figure.

## 3. Marriages: Chandaladevi and the svayamvara (poem or record?)

### 3a. The poem's svayamvara
BUHLER 1875 pp. 38-39 (VII.1 - IX.152): "He learnt that the Silahara prince, ruling over Karahata,
possessed a daughter Chandralekha or Chandaladevi by name, who, being endowed with marvellous beauty,
was by the order of Parvati to hold a Svayamvara." At Karahata "Chandralekha heard unmoved the praises
of the lord of Ayodhya ... of the kings of Chedi and Kanyakubja ... of the princes of Kalinjara,
Gopachala, Malava, Gurjara, Pandya and Chola. One by one she rejected them, signifying her displeasure
by various contemptuous gestures. Her eyes sought the Chalukya and remained fixed on his face. ...
Chandralekha obeyed, the acclamations of the assembled multitude approved her act, and the happy pair
at once proceeded to the nuptial Mandapa."
Buhler's judgment, p. 39 n. 1 - p. 40: "He has also succeeded in giving the story a very unreal
appearance by imitating the Raghuvamsa in the description of the Svayamvara. Nevertheless the main
facts related by him may be taken to be historical. For the name of Vikrama's wife Chandaladevi is
preserved in the inscriptions ... From the inscriptions of the Silaharas it is also certain that this
family ruled in Karahata, the modern Karhad". He adds: "Tod's annals of Rajasthan and other works show
that Svayamvaras occurred among the Rajputs until a very late period."
BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 218: "Whether the svayamvara was real, or imagined by the poet to give himself an
opportunity for the display of his poetic and descriptive powers, it is not possible to decide."
VERDICT: the marriage and the Karhad Silahara birth are `inscription`; the svayamvara itself is a
court poem's set piece modelled on Kalidasa: tell it as Bilhana's telling, disclose on the last page.
DIGNITY: in Bilhana's spy-and-lovesick scene the king's "limbs wasted away and pallor overspread his
face" (Buhler p. 38-39); keep to the garland moment, which honours both.

### 3b. Chandaladevi in the inscriptions
FLEET 1896 p. 449: "Chandaladevi, who also was styled piriy-arasi, and in one passage
agramahamahishi; Bilhana mentions her both as Chandaladevi and as Chandralekha, and in words which
shew that she was the daughter of one of the Silahara princes of Karad, - probably of Narasimha; and
she is spoken of in A.D. 1102-1103 as the mother of Jayakarna". Tier: inscription; father's name
"probably" only.

### 3c. A Kashmir king falls for her portrait (Kalhana, contemporary chronicle)
STEIN 1900 p. 355, Rajatarangini VII.1119-1124 (King Harsha of Kashmir):
> 1119. When he saw a picture of Candala, the beautiful wife of Parmandi, the lord of Karnata, he was
> struck by the god of love. ... 1121. Having his head turned by the parasites, he shamelessly vowed
> before his court to obtain Canda1a and to overthrow Parmandi. ... 1124. "The king of brilliant lustre
> has renounced the chewing of Potasa until he has slain the lord of Karnata; until he has embraced
> Candala; until he has effected his entry into the town of Kalyana".
Stein's note 1119: "For Parmandi, i.e. Vikramaditya of Kalyana ... His wedding with Candralekha or
Candaladevi, the daughter of the Silahara prince of Karahata, is described in the vii. and viii.
Cantos of the Vikramankadevacarita." Note 1122: "Harsa's vow is as ludicrous as his boast to invade
Karnata." Tier: inscription (contemporary chronicle, 1148-50). The mockery falls on Harsha, not on
Vikramaditya: usable with dignity as proof of the queen's fame.

### 3d. Other queens named in inscriptions
FLEET 1896 p. 448: the records "give the names of no fewer than six wives": Savaladevi (daughter of
the Mahamandalesvara Jogamarasa), Lakshmadevi ("invariably spoken of with the title of piriyarasi or
chief queen; she is mentioned in A.D. 1084-85, as ruling at the capital of Kalyana, in A.D. 1095-96,
as ruling the eighteen agraharas and the town of Dharmapura, i.e. Dambal ... and she was still alive
in A.D. 1125-26"), Jakkaladevi (of the Kadamba stock), Malleyamadevi, Chandaladevi, and Malaladevi or
Malika ("daughter of the Senabhova or village-accountant Rayaya"). Tier: inscription.
SII XI (summaries): A.D. 1084 grant of a village "by his queen Lakshmadevi, to Somesvara-panditadeva
of the temple of Achalesvara at rajadhani Sundi, for the benefit of the temple, for feeding ascetics
and for imparting education"; A.D. 1080 a gift "while the chief queen Lakshmidevi was governing the
town" of Dronapura. SII XX No. 51 (A.D. 1077): gifts by "Ketaladevi, queen of Tribhuvanamalladeva".
DISPUTED: SII XX (A.D. 1094) calls "Pattamahadevi Mailalamahadevi, queen of Vikramaditya"; FLEET 1896
p. 449 has "a daughter, Mailalamahadevi, who was married to Jayakesin II., of the family of the
Kadambas of Goa". Follow Fleet for the daughter; flag the SII XX wording.

## 4. Wars

### 4a. The youngest brother Jayasimha (court poem + silence of the stones)
BUHLER 1875 pp. 42-43 (XIV.1 - XV.87): the informer said Jayasimha "amassed treasures by oppressing
his subjects ... that he was seeking the friendship of the Dravida king and that, worst of all, he
tried to seduce Vikrama's soldiers from their allegiance"; Vikrama "sent out spies"; "addressed
friendly exhortations to his brother"; Jayasimha's men burned villages and he "sent insulting
messages"; "a battle was fought, in which Jayasimha at first gained some advantages by means of his
elephants. But Vikramaditya's personal bravery restored the fortune of the day. Jayasimha's army was
routed and its leader was captured on his flight, in the jungles. Though Vikrama would have had
reason enough to deal hardly with the captive, he spoke kindly to him and consoled him."
BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 218: "with remarkable bravery the king rushed forward on the back of his maddened
elephant, dealing heavy blows right and left." Buhler p. 43 n. 1: "Vikrama's war with Jayasimha is not
mentioned in the inscriptions ... Vikrama did not care to proclaim the fact, that he had fought with
both his brothers." FLEET 1896 pp. 449, 454: Jayasimha is yuvaraja at Banavasi 1077-1082; "Then,
however, he rebelled; and the authority entrusted to him was taken away. His name does not appear in
subsequent records." EC VII Intro p. 22 (Sk 297, c. 1080): Jayasimha was "on the most affectionate
terms with his brother, like Lakshmana to Rama". Tier: court poem + inscription.

### 4b. Cholas
Before the accession: see §2b. After: BUHLER 1875 p. 44 (XVII.43-68): "The Chola again became proud
and insolent. But Vikrama's army marched on Kanchi; a battle was fought in which the Chola fled as
usual. Kanchi was taken." Buhler's caution p. 38 n. 2: "The statement that he had again to fight the
Chola detracts from the magnitude of his former victory." FLEET 1896 p. 453 n. 1 links this campaign
to "so many inscriptions, referring themselves to the reign of Vikramaditya VI., at Draksharam and
other places in the Telugu country", and notes the Eastern Chalukya counter-claim that
"Kulottunga-Chodadeva I. pursued Vikramaditya VI. from Nangali in Mysore to Manalur on the
Tungabhadra". Tier: court poem; inscription (both sides). Exact year of the Vengi/Telugu conquest:
UNVERIFIED in a PD source this pass.

### 4c. Hoysalas: Vishnuvardhana, Kannegal, Achugi
FLEET 1896 p. 453: "about A.D. 1117, when the Hoysala Vishnuvardhana invaded Uchchangi and the Belvola
country, and carried his arms successfully so far to the north as to bathe his horse in the waters of
the Krishnavena, i.e. the Krishna. The Hoysalas, under the immediate leadership of a Dandanayaka named
Gangaraja, claim to have inflicted a serious disaster, in a night attack, on the army of Vikramaditya
VI. when it was in camp at Kannegala. And the records of the Sinda chieftain Achugi II., through whose
instrumentality the invasion was stopped, - stating that he pursued and prevailed against Hoysala,
took Gove, put Lakshma to flight in war, valorously followed after Pandya, dispersed at all times the
Malapas, and seized upon the Konkan; that he gave Gove and Uppinakatte to the flames ... As, however,
the succession shortly afterwards duly passed to Somesvara III., no lasting injury can have been done
to the Western Chalukya power."
Hoysala side (from `hoysala-verified.md` §4, EC II 1889 No. 45): "the army of the Chalukya emperor
Tribhuvana-Malla Permmadi-Deva, including twelve tributary chiefs, was left in camp at Kannegala".
The emperor himself is NOT said to be present. Same year FLEET 1896 p. 452 lists "the Hoysala
Mahamandalesvara Vishnuvardhana, who in A.D. 1117 was ruling the Gangavadi ninety-six-thousand" as a
feudatory.
A Hoysala king's own tribute to him: BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 219, from Vira-Ballala II's Gadag inscription
(Ind. Ant. II p. 300): Vishnuvardhana "was again and again reminded by his servants of the honour done
to him by the king Paramardideva (Vikramaditya), who said, 'Know the Hoysala alone among all princes
to be unconquerable.'" Tier: inscription (Hoysala, a generation later; Bhandarkar corrects Fleet's
translation of this verse).

### 4d. Paramaras (Malwa) and the Narmada
BUHLER 1875 p. 31 (III.67): "He lent his assistance to the king of Malava, who came to him for
protection, to regain his kingdom" (as prince). FLEET 1896 p. 452: "A record of A.D. 1088-89 speaks of
Vikramaditya VI. crossing the Narmada, and conquering kings on the other side of that river. And
another, of A.D. 1098, shews that then again he was in the northern part of the kingdom, on the banks
of the Narmada." BUHLER 1875 p. 21: the poem "knows nothing of the great expedition into Central India
beyond the Narmada which Vikrama undertook in 1088." Tier: inscription; the named Paramara opponent:
UNVERIFIED.

### 4e. Kalachuris
Of Chedi/Dahala: the poem credits the father with destroying "the power of Karna, king of Dahala"
(BUHLER p. 27); EC VII Intro p. 22 (Sk 297) credits the brother Jayasimha: "He had burnt Dahala". No
Kalachuri war of Vikramaditya himself was found. Of Kalyani (Bijjala's house): they rise only later;
Bijjala's father Permadi is first dated 1128 under Someshvara III (FLEET 1896 p. 471). A Hirekerur
transcript naming Bijjala under Vikramaditya VI is judged faulty by Fleet ("I feel convinced that there
must be something wrong", p. 471 n.). So: no war with the Kalachuris of Kalyani in this reign.

### 4f. Vengi, Gauda, Kamarupa, Ceylon (the prince's raids in the poem)
BUHLER 1875 p. 31: "carried his arms as far north as Gauda and Kamarupa. He attacked also the king of
Simhala ... He, finally, conquered Gangakunda (IV. 21), Vengi (IV. 29) and Chakrakota (IV. 30)." n. 3:
"Bilhana's rhapsodic treatment of this portion of Vikrama's career makes it impossible to determine
the chronological order of these wars. ... The assertion that Vikrama defeated the kings of Gauda and
Kamarupa sounds very strange. It is, however, possible that he made with his cavalry a raid into their
territories." Tier: court poem; Gauda and Kamarupa = do not present as fact.

### 4g. The overall picture
FLEET 1896 p. 452: "This long reign seems to have been a fairly peaceful one." BUHLER 1875 p. 43:
"After Vikrama had subdued all his enemies, his dominions enjoyed peace and prosperity. The elements
even showed themselves propitious, neither famine nor pestilence visited his kingdom."
BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 219 (Bilhana): his subjects "did not care to close the doors of their houses at
night, and instead of thieves the rays of the moon entered through the window openings."

## 5. The court: Bilhana, Vijnaneshvara, Someshvara III, Kalhana's parasol

### 5a. Bilhana's own words (canto XVIII)
BUHLER 1875 p. 15, verse 101 (Buhler's translation): "There the lucky poet received from the Chalukya
king, the terror of the Cholas, the dignity of Chief Pandit, distinguished by the grant of a blue
parasol and a mast elephant." Verse 102: "has composed this truly lovely poem out of affection for the
prince of Karnata". pp. 16-19: born at Khonamukha near Pravarapura (Kashmir); travelled Mathura,
Kanauj, Prayaga, Banaras, Karna of Dahala's court, by Somanatha and sea to the south, Ramesvara, "and
finally arrived in Kalyana, where king Vikrama gave him the office of Vidyapati or chief Pandit."
p. 23: "Bilhana left his country between 1062-1065 and wrote the Vikramankacharita at an advanced age
about 1085". Tier: contemporary (self-report) + scholarship (dates).

### 5b. Kalhana's line (independent, from Kashmir)
STEIN 1900 pp. 340-341, Rajatarangini VII.935-937:
> Bilhana, who had left Kasmir in the reign of King Kalasa, had been made by Parmadi, the lord of
> Karnata, his Chief Pandit (vidyapati), and when travelling on elephants through the hill-country of
> Karnata, his parasol was borne aloft before the king. When he heard that the liberal Harsa was like
> a kinsman to true poets, he thought even so great a splendour a deception.
BUHLER 1875 p. 21 gives the same passage as VII.936-938: "Whose parasol, when he travelled on elephants
through Karnata-land, was seen borne aloft before the king". Stein's note: "Parmadi is the well-known
surname" of Vikramaditya. Tier: inscription (contemporary chronicle, written c. 1148-50). NOTE the
verse numbering differs (Stein 935-937, Buhler 936-938); cite Stein. NOTE also the line's point in
Kalhana is Harsha's generosity making Bilhana regret leaving: the honour is real, the context is a
compliment to Harsha.

### 5c. Vijnaneshvara and the Mitakshara
BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 219: "Vijnanesvara, the author of the Mitakshara, which is at present acknowledged
over a large part of India, and especially in the Maratha country, as the chief authority on matters
of civil and religious law, flourished in the reign of Vikramaditya and lived at Kalyana. At the end
of most manuscripts of that work there occur three stanzas":
> "On the surface of the earth, there was not, there is not, and there will be not, a town like
> Kalyana; never was a monarch like the prosperous Vikramarka seen or heard of; and - what more? -
> Vijnanesvara, the Pandit, does not bear comparison with any other (person). May this triad which is
> like a celestial creeper exist to the end of the Kalpa!"
> (p. 220) "May the lord Vikramaditya protect this whole earth as long as the moon and the stars
> endure, - he whose feet are refulgent with the lustre of the crest jewels of prostrate kings from the
> bridge, which is the heap of the glory of the best scion of the Raghu race, to the lord of mountains,
> and from the Western Ocean ... to the Eastern Ocean."
Bhandarkar's text rests on the Bombay lithograph and "a manuscript of the Mitakshara, dated Samvat 1535
and Saka 1401". Tier: inscription (contemporary text, colophon verses). Colebrooke 1810 not opened.

### 5d. Someshvara III and the Manasollasa (next reign)
FLEET 1896 p. 456: "Somesvara III. is represented as the author of a work named Abhilashitarthachintamani
or Manasollasa, dealing with polity, the administration of justice, medicine, elephants, alchemy,
astrology, arms, and rhetoric, which was written in the fourth year of his reign, the Saumya
samvatsara, Saka-Samvat 1051 (expired)" (= 1129-30). p. 455: styled "Sarvajna-Chakravartin, 'the
omniscient emperor.'" BUHLER 1875 p. 41 n. 1: "Vikramaditya's son, Somesvara III surnamed Bhulokamalla
wrote a curious handbook of amusements for kings ... He enumerates twenty kinds of sports (vinoda) and
twenty amusements (krida)". Tier: inscription/scholarship. Use for court life only with care: it is
three years after Vikramaditya's death.

### 5e. A royal poem?
BUHLER 1875 p. 42 n. 1 (XIII): the king "composed also a poem, describing the breaking of the Monsun,
which he addressed to Chandaladevi"; Buhler: "I will not decide if we have really to recognize in
Vikramaditya a royal author or if Bilhana merely puts the poem into his mouth". EI XIII Ittagi v. 28
calls him "a gallant of the concert of naturally sweet music, a gallant of graceful speech". Tier:
court poem; disputed.

## 6. Building and gifts: Itagi, Vikramapura, Gadag, Lakkundi, Kalyana

### 6a. Itagi (Ittagi) Mahadeva temple, 1112: "emperor of temples" is the inscription's own phrase
EI XIII p. 36 (Barnett): the temple, "in or shortly before A.D. 1112 ... in the massiveness of its
construction and the richness of its decoration it fully deserves some of the praises lavished on it
in the inscription, including a mention of it in verse 67 as devalaya-chakravartti, 'a very emperor
among temples.'" Barnett quotes Fergusson (it "must be regarded as one of the most highly finished and
architecturally perfect of the Chalukyan shrines") and Meadows Taylor ("No chased work in silver or
gold could possibly be finer").
Translation, verse 67: "When one compares temples on the ocean-bounded earth, are any, whether it be
those formerly built or those yet to be built or those now building, equal (to this)? They are not
equal. So, after previously constructing with joy of soul this one, which is worthy to be called the
Emperor of Temples, in the everlastingly brilliant precinct of the god Mahadeva". Verse 65: its
flagstaff "lifts a finger to point a comparison with all other abodes of gods on the earth ... saying:
'Look! such are they, (and) this!'"
Builder: "an officer of Vikramaditya VI, the General Mahadeva", born at Ittage; besides Dandanayaka he
was "Mahapradhana or High Minister, Kannada-Samdhivigrahi ... and Mane-vergade or Master of the
Household". "the king Vikramaditya VI himself gave to the temple an entire village named Bennekallu".
Date: the 37th year of the Chalukya-Vikrama-varsha, Nandana; Fleet (in Barnett) = 7 or 8 September
1112, "but the date is an irregular one, at any rate in respect of the alleged eclipse".
The king in this record: v. 24 "lion to the raging elephants who were haughty hostile kings ... a
fortunate Dilipa among kings"; v. 25 "what heroes are equal to the Chalukya-Rama?"; v. 27 "the Earth
stands upon the mighty arm of the king whose ornament is valour [Vikramabharana]"; v. 29 "who would be
able to describe in eulogy the heroic career of the terrible emperor Vikrama?". Tier: inscription.
CORRECTION to `kalyani-chalukya.md` §6: "Devalaya Chakravarti" is not Cousens's phrase; it is verse 67
of the 1112 record itself (Barnett's rendering "a very emperor among temples").

### 6b. Vikramapura (Arasibidi), the temple and the tank
BUHLER 1875 p. 44 (XVII.1-42): "He built a temple of Vishnu-Kamalavilasi; in front of it he dug a
splendid tank. Near it he built a city with splendid temples and palaces." n. 1: "The building of the
city, which was called Vikramapura is mentioned in the inscriptions ... and Sir W. Elliott adds that
'an enormous tank and other works attest its former splendour.'" FLEET 1896 p. 450 n. 5: Vikramapura
"existed before the time of Vikramaditya VI. But it seems to be the town which, Bilhana says, he
'built'". SII XI No. 135 (A.D. 1087): grant "to the Forty-Two learned Mahajanas of the Brahmapuri, at
the capital Vikramapura". Tier: inscription + poem.

### 6c. Gadag
The era verses (§2d) are on the Vira-Narayana temple record of 1098; the Bachaladevi verse on the
Trikutesvara record of 1102 (FLEET 1896 p. 438 n. 1, p. 426 n. 3). FLEET p. 451 n. 3 places the
Anantapalayya record of 1103-04 "at the temple of Trikutesvara at Gadag". These show his reign's
records on Gadag temples; that he BUILT a Gadag temple is not stated in anything read. UNVERIFIED.

### 6d. Lakkundi and the gold coin
SII XI No. 136 (Alur, A.D. 1091), summary: the Mahajanas "received from this Mahadevayya-Nayaka, 130
Lokkigadyanas and gave in exchange 200 mattar of land". Lokki-gadyana = the gold gadyana of
Lokkigundi (Lakkundi): the town's coin was money in his reign. Tier: inscription (summary). Any
Lakkundi temple built by Vikramaditya himself: UNVERIFIED (none found).

### 6e. Kalyana, the capital
FLEET 1896 pp. 427-428: Kalyana "was made the capital by Somesvara I., and is mentioned as such, in one
of his inscriptions, dated in A.D. 1053; and it continued to be the capital of all his successors, and
of the Kalachuryas after them"; n. 3: "For an account of the place, which shews that there are now no
ancient remains at it, see Archaeol. Surv. West. Ind. Vol. III., p. 23." FLEET p. 450: his other
seats were Nadaviyuppayana-vidu (1077), Etagiri = Yadgir (1077-78), Vijayapura = Bijapur (1091-92),
Manneyakere (1125-26), and Vikramapura. BUHLER 1875 p. 28 n.: the verb "he made" (of Someshvara I and
Kalyana) "might be also taken to indicate that he founded Kalyana. But this was not the case, as the
town existed long before his time" (Buhler; FLEET p. 427 disagrees: Kalyana "is nowhere mentioned in
the records of the earlier Chalukya period"). DISPUTED (minor).

### 6f. Gifts beyond Shaivism
FLEET 1896 p. 452: "One of the most interesting of the records is the Dambal inscription of A.D. 1095,
which records grants made to viharas of Buddha and Arya-Taradevi at that town, and thus shews that
Buddhism still held a place in the Kanarese country as late as the end of the eleventh century."
EC VII Intro p. 21: the emperor sanctioned a village to the Permmadi (Jain) basadi at Balligave that he
had founded as prince. BUHLER p. 44: "His liberality to the poor of all countries was unbounded."

- Silaharas of Karad: Gandaraditya (1109-1119); of the Konkan: Anantadeva (1095).
- Rattas of Saundatti: Kannakaira II (1087-88), Kartavirya II (1096-97).

## 8. Death in 1126 and what followed

FLEET 1896 pp. 446-447: "When once on the throne, he had a long and uninterrupted reign of at least
fifty years, extending to at any rate some date in A.D. 1126. There are several records of his fiftieth
year, the Visvavasu samvatsara ... The latest of them registers grants that were made on Magha sukla 7,
corresponding to the 3rd January, A.D. 1126. ... it seems highly unlikely that he was alive much longer
after the date in January of that year". Successor, p. 455: Someshvara III, first year "the Parabhava
samvatsara, Saka-Samvat 1049 current, = A.D. 1126-27"; "His capital, throughout the whole of it, was
Kalyana" (p. 456). Place and manner of death: NOT RECORDED in anything read. Tell it plainly
(no invented death scene); disclose.
Fate of the kingdom (dynasty page): Jagadekamalla II (1138/39-1149), Taila III (from 1150; "died
certainly before the 19th January, A.D. 1163", FLEET p. 459). Bijjala, of a Kalachurya feudatory
family, appears c. 1150 as Taila III's servant "devoted to the service of the Chalukyas" (p. 472),
starts his own reckoning in 1156-57 and in 1162 "completed the usurpation which he had been
contemplating" (pp. 474-475). Someshvara IV recovered the throne by 1183-84 with the general Brahma;
latest date 25 December 1189; "With him the dynasty of the Western Chalukyas of Kalyani came to an
end." (pp. 463-467). Tier: inscription/scholarship.

## 9. Dignity flags: where the only sources are hostile, or the king is humbled

Owner's rule: celebrate without inventing; never show him demeaned.
1. **Chola records (hostile)**: Virarajendra claims Vikramaditya "was driven from Gangapadi over the
   Tungabhadra", that the Chola tied the heir's necklace "on 'the liar's' neck" (SII III), and that
   Kulottunga "pursued Vikramaditya VI. from Nangali ... to Manalur" (FLEET p. 453 n. 1). These are
   enemy claims. Tell instead what both sides agree on: the Chola king gave him his daughter, and he
   went to Kanchi to set his brother-in-law on the throne.
2. **Kannegal (Hoysala claim)**: a night attack on "the army ... including twelve tributary chiefs";
   the emperor is not said to be there. Tell it, if at all, as a raid on a camp of his vassals that
   his general Achugi answered by driving the Hoysala back (FLEET p. 453), and the later Hoysala
   memory that Paramardi named the Hoysala "unconquerable" (a compliment the Hoysalas themselves kept).
3. **Taking the throne from his brother**: the stones say "seized in battle" and "punished or
   confined him"; Buhler judges him a deliberate usurper. Dignified telling: the poem's account (the
   brother's alliance with the Chola usurper Rajiga, the battle, the hesitation over the captive);
   the last page carries Buhler's verdict and the fact that Someshvara II's end "is not known".
4. **The grieving son "disarmed"** (BUHLER p. 32): tell the grief, not the restraint.
5. **The lovesick king and harem scenes** (BUHLER pp. 38-41, drinking and water-sport cantos): Buhler
   calls them "merely conventional". Leave out; at most one garden scene with Chandaladevi.
6. **War with the youngest brother**: only the court poem; it ends with the king speaking "kindly"
   to the captive. That clemency is the dignified beat.
7. **Kalhana on Harsha and Chandala**: humiliates Harsha of Kashmir, not Vikramaditya; safe to use.
Nothing found shows him humiliated in his own records; no episode rests ONLY on a hostile source
except the Chola and Hoysala battle claims above.

## 10. For the visual bible

- **No portrait.** No relief, statue, painting or coin labelled with his name was found in any
  edition read. The Itagi tablet's top sculptures are "a linga ... two standing figures and a cow and
  calf, with the sun above them ... another standing figure and the bull Nandi, with the moon over
  them" (EI XIII p. 37): not the king. The face is an AI reconstruction, to be disclosed. Bilhana's
  only physical words: "a handsome and strong boy" (BUHLER p. 30). Fleet's arithmetic (§1a) makes him
  at least seventy at death: age him across the book.
- **Titles and names**: Tribhuvanamalla; Perma, Permadi, Permanadi, Sanskrit Paramardi; Vikramarka,
  Vikramanka, Vikrama; Kali-Vikrama ("The prefix may be either the Sanskrit word, meaning the Kali age,
  or the Kanarese kali, 'courageous, brave, heroic'"); Vikkala/Vikkila in Eastern Chalukya records
  (FLEET p. 446; BUHLER p. 30 n. 2). As prince "Chalukya-Ganga-Permanadi" (FLEET p. 440 n. 4) and
  "Vikrama-Ganga" (Sk 136). Biruda "Rayanarayana" (SII XI, A.D. 1080). "Chalukya-Rama",
  "Vikramabharana" (EI XIII vv. 25, 27).
- **Emblem: the boar.** BUHLER p. 32 n. 4: "The Kuladevata of the Chalukyas of Kalyana appears to have
  been Vishnu, as they use the boar as their emblem." EI XIII p. 38: "the Chalukyas had Vishnu as
  their family god, and the boar, typifying one of his incarnations, as their crest."
- **Insignia**: "the jewels, umbrella, and throne" (Sk 136, 1068 coronation of the brother); the
  pattabandha (fillet of coronation) festival (Wadageri, FLEET p. 446); "the five big drums" of the
  great vassals (Sk 136); a "blue parasol and a mast elephant" for the chief Pandit (BUHLER p. 15).
- **Coins**: ELLIOT 1886 p. 54: early Chalukya gold pieces "recognized by the figure of a boar in the
  centre"; later the padma-tanka form "was imitated by the Chalukyas ... the figure of the boar being
  substituted for that of the lotus". No coin attributed to Vikramaditya VI by name in what was read
  (UNVERIFIED). The Lakkundi gold "Lokkigadyana" was current in 1091 (§6d). Kashmir copied the
  Karnata coin type: STEIN 1900 VII.926: Harsha, "fond in his amusements of the Daksinatya (Dekkan)
  fashion, he introduced a coin-type (tanka) copied from that of Karnata"; CUNNINGHAM 1894 pp. 34-35:
  Harsha's gold "'elephant' type ... was copied from the coins of Karnata". So: an elephant on the
  Karnata gold of this period is attested indirectly. No portrait coins.
- **Dress and court, in words**: Kalhana's wandering poets tell a man "From speech and dress you are
  recognizable as a wanderer from the Dekkan" (VII.1123). Harsha's court, after the Deccan fashion
  (VII.926-927, context only, not stated as Deccani): "People appeared in his assembly hall with waving
  palm-leaves, adorned with big forehead-marks of sandal ointment, and wearing splendid long daggers."
  Bilhana's palace: bath rooms "cooled by flowing water", sandal ointment (BUHLER p. 41); hunting
  "lions and other large game, hunted boars with hounds and shot deer with arrows" (p. 43); Jayasimha's
  battle with war elephants; the king on "his maddened elephant" (BHANDARKAR p. 218).
- **What can be seen today**: Itagi Mahadeva temple (1112, his general's, the king's village grant);
  the Kuruvatti Mallikarjuna temple by the Tungabhadra (site of the father's death; temple dating per
  `kalyani-chalukya.md` §6, not re-verified); Gadag Trikutesvara and Vira-Narayana (records of 1098 and
  1102); Arasibidi (Vikramapura) tank and remains; Balligavi (Kedaresvara, the Permmadi basadi site);
  Dambal (the 1095 vihara record); Lakkundi (mint town). Basavakalyana: FLEET 1896 (from ASWI III)
  found "no ancient remains" at Kalyana; the present fort is therefore later than the Chalukyas (its
  builders: UNVERIFIED in a PD source); use only for terrain.
- **Anachronisms**: no Saka year on any document after 1076 (use "Chalukya-Vikrama year N"); no
  portrait coin; no Bahmani fort walls as "Chalukya Kalyana"; no Basavanna or Anubhava Mantapa in
  this reign (see §12).

## 11. Dynasty page: the Kalyani Chalukya rulers (ಕಲ್ಯಾಣಿ ಚಾಲುಕ್ಯರು)

Capital: very possibly Manyakheta (Malkhed) at first; Kalyana from Someshvara I (attested 1053) to the
end. FLEET 1896 p. 427: "Their capital, indeed, is not specified until the time of Somesvara I.; and
very possibly it was at first Malkhed." `scholarship` for Malkhed, `inscription` for Kalyana.
Dating note (for the page): Fleet's years are the earliest and latest dated records naming each king
as sovereign; years in a cyclic samvatsara straddle two Western years (hence "1076-77"); from 1076
the records count in the king's own Chalukya-Vikrama years.

## 12. Popular claims to avoid (with the source line that rejects or hedges each)

1. **"Chandaladevi chose him at a svayamvara" as plain fact.** BHANDARKAR 1896 p. 218: "Whether the
   svayamvara was real, or imagined by the poet ... it is not possible to decide." BUHLER p. 40: the
   poet gave it "a very unreal appearance by imitating the Raghuvamsa". Tell as Bilhana's telling.
2. **"Fifty years, never lost a war."** The Hoysala record of the Kannegal night attack (EC II 1889
   No. 45; FLEET p. 453 "claim to have inflicted a serious disaster"); the Chola claim that Kulottunga
   "pursued Vikramaditya VI." (FLEET p. 453 n. 1); BUHLER p. 38 n. 2: a second Chola war "detracts from
   the magnitude of his former victory". Say instead: a fifty-year reign, "fairly peaceful" (FLEET
   p. 452), with no lasting loss.
3. **"He abolished the Saka era."** He decreed it (Gadag 1098); but SII XX No. 52 of his second year
   still writes "Saka 1000", and FLEET p. 447 notes the samvatsara names always show the Saka year.
4. **"Basaveshwara was his minister."** Wrong reign. Basava's tradition belongs to Bijjala, whose
   own reckoning starts in 1156-57 (FLEET p. 474), thirty years after Vikramaditya's last date
   (3 January 1126). And FLEET p. 481: "No epigraphic mention of Basava and Channabasava has been
   obtained; which is peculiar, if they really held the high office that is allotted to them by
   tradition."
5. **"The Anubhava Mantapa met in his Kalyana."** No contemporary record of it was found in any source
   read; it belongs to the Basava tradition of Bijjala's time (see 4). Do not place it in this book.
6. **"Bijjala was assassinated in 1167/1168."** FLEET p. 477: Bijjala "transferred the burden of
   government to his dear or favourite son Sovideva"; latest date 19 July 1167, alive. p. 481: the
   Channabasava Purana dates the murder "A.D. 785, which is too early by close upon four centuries";
   the Jain poem places it "even before the time, in A.D. 1156, when Bijjala established himself as
   king". Year and manner: legend.
7. **"Cousens called Itagi the emperor of temples."** It is the 1112 inscription's own verse 67
   (EI XIII p. 36).
8. **"Vikramaditya was the son of a Ganga queen."** FLEET p. 440 n. 3: the Gadag records say the three
   brothers "were uterine brothers, born of one and the same mother", Bachaladevi.
9. **"He conquered Bengal and Assam."** BUHLER p. 31 n. 3: "sounds very strange ... possible that he
   made with his cavalry a raid".
10. **"Bilhana's poem covers his whole reign."** BUHLER p. 21: the poem "knows nothing of the great
    expedition into Central India beyond the Narmada which Vikrama undertook in 1088"; written c. 1085.
11. **"The Basavakalyan fort is the Chalukya palace."** FLEET p. 427 n. 3 (citing ASWI III p. 23):
    "there are now no ancient remains at it".
12. **"Somesvara I founded Kalyana."** Disputed: BUHLER p. 28 n. ("the town existed long before his
    time") vs FLEET p. 427 (not mentioned in earlier records). Say "made it the capital".
13. **"Vikramaditya killed his brother."** Not recorded: "What ultimately became of Somesvara II., is
    not known" (FLEET p. 445).

## 13. Corrections to `kalyani-chalukya.md`

1. Itagi: "Devalaya Chakravarti" is the inscription's phrase (EI XIII v. 67), not Cousens's.
2. Rajatarangini on Bilhana: Stein numbers the verses VII.935-937 (Buhler 936-938); Stein's wording is
   "Chief Pandit (vidyapati) ... his parasol was borne aloft before the king".
3. Vikramaditya's first regnal year = Saka 999 current (998 expired), not "990 current" (OCR slip).
4. SII XX No. 52's "Saka 969" is a transcription problem; quote only its second date (Saka 1000).
5. Mailalamahadevi: daughter (Fleet) vs "queen" (SII XX summary): disputed.
6. The Chola "liar" epithet (SII III) and Kannegal are the only hostile sources; mark them so.
7. Kalachuri wars of Vikramaditya himself: none found (Dahala burnt by Jayasimha, Sk 297).
8. Add: Lakkundi "Lokkigadyana" in 1091 (SII XI No. 136); Yuvaraja Mallikarjunadeva 1095 (No. 139);
   Lakshmadevi's 1084 grant "for imparting education"; Kalhana VII.926 on the Karnata coin type.
