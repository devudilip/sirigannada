# Kadalekai Parishe: verified facts (2026-11-24)

Every fact in `data/history-src/habba-jaatre/kadalekai-parishe/facts.json` carries its source URL. Notes from the review:

- **Temple.** Wikipedia (Dodda Basavana Gudi) gives 1537 and Kempe Gowda; Karnataka Tourism says 16th century. No inscription was found.
- **Size and surface.** The Nandi is 4.6 m × 6.1 m, a granite monolith blackened by oil and charcoal (Wikipedia). Other sources give 4.5 m × 6–6.5 m.
- **The neighbourhood's name.** Basavanagudi is named after the temple (Wikipedia, Basavanagudi).
- **Legend.**
  - The bull raided the crops and the farmers vowed their first crop (Karnataka Tourism).
  - In another version, the bull came on full-moon nights, was chased up a hillock, and an iron peg was nailed into its head to stop it growing (Wikipedia, Kadalekai Parishe).
  - The trident version is from trawell.in and India TV (2025).
  - We found no source saying the trident can be seen today; Citizen Matters does not mention it.
- **Vrishabhavathi.** The river is believed to rise at the Nandi's feet; an IISc report lists other sources (Wikipedia).
- **The fair.**
  - Held on the last Monday of Kartika. The 2025 Basavanagudi fair ran 17–21 Nov and was the first five-day fair (NIE); over five lakh visitors were expected (Asianet).
  - Malleshwaram has held its own fair since 2017 (Wikipedia). 2025 brought a plastic ban (NIE, Deccan Herald).
- **Groundnut history.**
  - The plant comes from South America; the oldest pods are about 7,600 years old (Wikipedia, Peanut).
  - S. Talawar's *Peanut in India* (University of Georgia, 2004) covers the Indian side:
    - the route to India is uncertain (Jesuits, China, Africa, the Philippines), and it may have arrived in the first half of the 16th century;
    - Buchanan found it grown in Mysore by 1800;
    - it became an important crop in the Madras region in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
- **The 1930 Mysore Gazetteer** (vol. V, Bangalore district) was searched; it does not mention the groundnut fair.
