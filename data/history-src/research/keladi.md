# Nayakas of Keladi (ಕೆಳದಿ ನಾಯಕರು), 1499 to 1763: research notes

Series `keladi`. Prepared on 2026-11-08 together with `keladi-shivappa-nayaka-verified.md`, which holds the verbatim quotations for story 1.

Every claim below names its source. Abbreviations follow the verified file:
- DV: della Valle
- RICE I and RICE II: the Mysore Gazetteer, 1897
- RICE 1909: Mysore and Coorg from the Inscriptions
- EC VI and EC VII: Epigraphia Carnatica
- KNV: Keladinripavijayam, ed. Shama Sastry, 1921
- DANVERS: The Portuguese in India
- EFI: English Factories
- KANARA: Bombay Gazetteer XV
- BUCH: Buchanan
- SCM: South Canara Manual
- HR: Hayavadana Rao
- SWAMI: Swaminathan, The Nayakas of Ikkeri, 1957

Swaminathan (1957) is the only modern monograph on the house. It is in copyright, so it is cited and paraphrased only. Other modern work exists, mainly by Kannada scholars of the Keladi Museum and Historical Research Bureau, Keladi, but none of it was reached.

**Capitals:**
- Keladi (Sagar taluk);
- Ikkeri (Sagar taluk), under Sadashiva Nayaka's successor (RICE II p. 431);
- Bidanur from 1639, "about 1640" or 1646 (RICE II pp. 431, 463; SCM p. 72). Bidanur is also Bidarur or Venupura, from "Bidaru halli, bamboo village". Its walls were 8 miles round, with 10 gates and the palace on a central hill (RICE II p. 464).

Haidar renamed Bidanur Haidar-Nagar; it is now Nagar, Hosanagara taluk.

**Sources disagree on Shivappa's years:**
- RICE I p. 593 and KANARA: 1648–1670.
- DANVERS fn: 1648–1670.
- SCM: 1649 plus 22 years.
- BUCH: 22 years from 1644.

The list above follows the inscriptions (SWAMI p. 87) and the death date in KNV and the Dutch records (HR, SWAMI p. 101). See the verified file §11.

## 2. Primary sources: what survives, where to read them, licence

### 2a. Court literature (Keladi side)
- **Keladinripavijayam** by Linganna, an 18th-century Brahman court poet, in Kannada champu. It runs from the founding of the house to 1763. Edited by R. Shama Sastry, Mysore Oriental Library 1921; archive.org `emzH_keladi-nripa-vijay-...-1921-oriental-lib` (public domain in India; Shama Sastry died 1944). A 2011 reprint exists (knv2011, OCR poor). SWAMI p. 7 says the early cantos are error-prone and the later ones valuable.
- **Shivatattvaratnakara** by Basappa Nayaka I (c. 1700), a Sanskrit encyclopaedia with a dynastic history (Kallola VI, VIII). Not read; SWAMI cites it throughout.
- **Shivatattva Chintamani** and other works: not reached.

### 2b. Inscriptions
- **EC VII and EC VIII** (Shimoga, 1902 and 1904). Keladi grants are listed in EC VII intro pp. 42–43. Shimoga 81 (1668) gives the lineage.
  - EC VIII: most archive.org copies are unusable, but the Staatsbibliothek Berlin scan is readable: https://archive.org/details/ec-08-1904-b (file EC_08_1904_B_djvu.txt), read 2026-11-08. Its intro p. 15 table lists Virabhadra's grants to 1645, one Virabhadra grant (Sagar 46) dated "? 1646", and a Shivappa grant of 1659 for the Hosangadi Mahattu matha (Tirthahalli; "A grant by Sivappa-Näyaka for the Mahattu matha built in Hosangadi", signed Sri-Sadasiva). The intro also says the Aghoreshvara temple floor has "effigies of three of the Keladi kings, doing obeisance", and that "The distance between the central pillars was adopted as the standard measure for garden land" (18 ft 6 in), against RICE I's "stone steps".
- **EC V** (Hassan), read 2026-11-08 (archive.org Epigraphia-Carnatica-12vols, Vol 5 part 1): Belur 80 (1659) is a Sri Ranga grant of Belur land; Belur 81 and 82 (1660) have him "seated on the jewel-throne in Vélapuri".
- **EC VI** (Kadur, 1901): the Sringeri grants. Sringeri Jagir 11 (1652) is Shivappa's.
- **EC V** (Hassan), Belur 80–82: see above (read).

### 2c. Foreign eyewitness and archival
- **Pietro della Valle**, letters of 1623, in the Hakluyt Society edition of 1892 (public domain). This is the fullest eyewitness of the Ikkeri court and the Portuguese coast.
- **Portuguese records:**
  - DANVERS 1894 (public domain) summarises the Goa archives for 1652–54.
  - The originals (Livros das Moncoes, Assentos, Instrucoes) were not reached.
  - Faria y Sousa was not reached.
- **English records:** EFI 1651–54 (Foster 1915) covers the 1650 visit to Bidanur. Fryer's account of 1673 is reported via RICE.
- **Dutch records:** the Dagh Register and Baldaeus (1672) are reported via KANARA and SWAMI; not read.
- **Jesuit and Carmelite:** Father Leonardo Paes is reported via RICE; Father Vincent's Shah Bandari Isak is reported via RICE II p. 434.

### 2d. Mysore side
- **Chikkadevaraya Vamshavali** (c. 1680) and **Kanthirava Narasaraja Vijayam**. These are hostile sources, read via HR I.

### 2e. Gazetteers and later compilations (reference tier, public domain)
- RICE 1897 I and II; RICE 1909.
- KANARA 1883.
- SCM 1894.
- BUCH 1807. He was in Nagar in 1801 and recorded the family history and the shist as it was still in force.
- Wilks 1810 (not read for this pass).

### 2f. Coins
The gold varaha, or Ikkeri pagoda, shows Shiva with a trident and an antelope and Parvati on his thigh. The reverse reads "Sri Sadasiva" in Nagari. The coins were struck at Ikkeri and later at Bidanur (SWAMI p. 30; KANARA currency section). Fanams were also struck. Haidar continued the Bidanur mint (RICE II p. 464).

## 3. Candidate stories

**A. Shivappa Nayaka, the coast and the shist (1645–1660).** Written as story 1. It has an eyewitness frame (DV), a day-dated siege (DANVERS), a copper grant (Sringeri) and a legacy still being asked for in 1801 (BUCH, RICE). Turning point: Gangolli, January 1653. See `keladi-shivappa-nayaka-verified.md`.

**B. Rani Chennammaji and the Maratha prince (1689–90).** The queen who sheltered Rajaram, Shivaji's son, against Aurangzeb's army. NOT VERIFIED in this pass: the shelter story is famous, but it was not checked in a source this session. It would fit `veera-naariyaru` or `keladi`. Needs SWAMI chapter X, the Maratha sources and the Mughal accounts.

**C. Della Valle at Ikkeri (1623).** A traveller's-eye book about Venkatappa Nayaka I. The court; his conversation with a widow about to commit sati (DV II p. 273 per the contents list); his meeting with the Queen of Olala (Ullal), whom the Ikkeri king had fought (DV II p. 306). All of it can be read in DV II, which makes this the best-documented single year.

**D. The fall of Bidanur (1763).** Rani Virammaji, Haidar's march and the palace set on fire. RICE II p. 464; Haidar's "fortune". It is a dark ending and needs care not to make the queen small.

## 5. Disputed or uncertain points

See the verified file, "Disputes". The main ones are:
- Shivappa's reign years;
- the year the capital moved to Bidanur;
- the Mysore charge that he murdered Virabhadra;
- the year Sri Ranga arrived (1646 or about 1656);
- the year Mangalore fell;
- the siege army (8,000 or 80,000);
- whether the shist trial is real history or revenue lore;
- whether the queens' samadhi was sahagamana.

Also disputed: the house's caste name in the sources (Malava Gauda in RICE, Lingayat by faith) and the early regnal years (SWAMI differs from RICE for 5 and 6).

## 6. Visual references for accurate AI images

### 6a. Portraits
No contemporary portrait of Shivappa is known. Modern statues, including the one at Shivamogga, and calendar art are not references.

Votive relief panels of chiefs exist at the Aghoreshvara temple, Ikkeri (Sadashiva Nayaka; Bhadrappa Nayaka) and the Veerabhadra temple, Keladi ("relievo portrait of a chief"). They are listed in SWAMI's plates but were not seen (UNVERIFIED). They show the general form of the period: a standing chief with hands joined, a turban, earrings and a waist-sash.

### 6b. Objects
- the gold varaha (§2f);
- the gilded poniard and silver-mounted sword, and the gilded wooden canopy (DV II pp. 248, 251);
- palm-leaf records (the ಕಡತ of the KNV record hall);
- the Lingayat ishtalinga in a silver case on a cord at the neck.

### 6c. Architecture and places
Commons files confirmed to exist by name:
- Keladi: Rameshwara temple, gandabherunda relief, yali pillars.
- Ikkeri: Aghoreshvara temple front and profile, Nandi.
- Nagar: town view; Kavaledurga fort entrance.
- Basrur fort.
- Gangolli: St Antony shrine.

The licences were not checked. The architecture is the Keladi style: granite, a mix of Dravida and late Vijayanagara forms, Hoysala-inspired mouldings, and yalis.

Coastal forts were built of laterite. That is an assumption from the surviving coastal forts of the region; no description of the Gangolli fabric was found.

### 6d. Dynastic emblem
The gandabherunda, a double-headed eagle, is carved at the Rameshwara temple, Keladi. Do not invent a flag design.

### 6e. Dress, arms, animals
- **Men at court** (DV II p. 248): long white garments; a short open surcoat on solemn days; small, almost square white turbans or little caps of cloth of gold; a gilded dagger at the girdle.
- **Arms:** swords, spears, matchlocks (the Portuguese side), bronze cannon (both sides; DANVERS gives 10 guns at Gangolli).
- **Landscape:** coconut palms, areca gardens, paddy, monsoon forest and bamboo on the ghats.
- **The Portuguese in the 1650s:** morions or broad hats, doublets, breeches, matchlocks, small carracks or galleys and pataxos.

### 6f. Anachronisms to avoid
- crescent or star flags, and modern Indian flags;
- Maratha-style pagri and Mughal jama for Keladi men (DV says small square turbans);
- flintlocks or later muskets on the Kannada side;
- Haidar-era rockets;
- 19th-century Mysore palace architecture;
- the modern Shivamogga statue face;
- text on banners;
- the Nagar fort as rebuilt by Haidar.

## 7. Key people beyond the kings

- **Venkatappa Nayaka II:** Shivappa's brother. With him in the record hall and against the khans; king 1660–61.
- **Siddappa Nayaka:** Shivappa's father, of the junior branch.
- **Sri Ranga Raya (VI in HR's numbering; III on the Vijayanagara dynasty page, II in EC V; the book uses no number):** the last Vijayanagara emperor; Shivappa's guest about 1656–59; installed at Belur.
- **Kanthirava Narasaraja / Devaraja Wodeyar of Mysore:** the rivals; Devaraja pursued Shivappa in 1660.
- **Dalavai Hamparajaiya:** the Mysore general captured at Grama (HR).
- **Francisco de Souza:** Portuguese captain of Honavar (SWAMI).
- **Father Leonardo Paes:** a Jesuit traveller in Kanara (via RICE).
- **Linganna:** author of KNV.
- **Pietro della Valle:** the Roman traveller of 1623.

## 8. Sources to avoid (or use only as finding aids)

- Popular websites and Wikipedia text on Shivappa. They often repeat the 1648–1670 dates and add sayings with no source (for example the "Shivappa's shist" proverbs).
- The modern map "Greatest Extent Of Keladi Kingdom during the reign of Shivappa Nayaka.jpg".
- Statues and paintings made after 1900.
- The English factory rumours that Shivappa was captured by Bijapur and ransomed, or died in 1663 (rejected by SWAMI p. 101).
