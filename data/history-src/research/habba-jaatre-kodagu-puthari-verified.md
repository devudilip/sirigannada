## 2026 date status (read first)

- **Announced: Wednesday 25 November 2026**, with Rohini Nakshatra. The Kodagu Express reports that the date was fixed at the Paadi Sri Igguthappa Temple, Kakkabe, at a meeting on 9 November 2026. Representatives of 13 Kodava families, temple office-bearers and astrologers from Ammangeri took part.
  - Source: The Kodagu Express, "Kodagu's Puthari harvest festival to be celebrated on November 25", published 9 Nov 2026. https://www.kodaguexpress.com/article/kodagu-s-puthari-harvest-festival-to-be-celebrated-on-novemb
  - Quote: "The annual Puthari harvest festival of Kodagu will be celebrated on November 25, coinciding with the Rohini Nakshatra"
- **The muhurta times (nere kattuvudu, kadiru, prasada) for 2026 are not published** in that article ("The date and timings for this year's celebrations were finalised", but no times are given). I found no other 2026 report giving times.
- **Conflict:** the Karnataka government holiday list, made in late 2025 before the temple fixed the date, gives the Kodagu-only local holiday as **Thursday 26 November 2026**. The list was confirmed on Asianet Suvarna Kannada, 31 Dec 2025: "ನವೆಂಬರ್ 26 (ಗುರುವಾರ) – ಹುತ್ತರಿ ಹಬ್ಬ". It is also on keka.com: "Kodagu district only". The festival night per the temple is 25 Nov, so treat 26 Nov as the holiday date only.
- Ignore any "Dec 23 2026" or "Dec 14 2026" dates on blogs (hindu-blog.com, thecurrentindia.com). They are recycled or tentative pages.

## A. Date, how it is fixed, timing

1. **The date is fixed each year at the Paadi Igguthappa temple, Kakkabe, by astrology with the takka families.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)
   - Source: Star of Mysore, "'Puthari' harvest festival of Kodagu on Nov. 27", 15 Nov 2023
   - URL: https://starofmysore.com/puthari-harvest-festival-of-kodagu-on-nov-27/
   - Quote: "as per the date decided at the Paadi Igguthappa Temple in Kakkabe"

2. **In 2023 the astrologers were from Ammangeri. They sat with the deva-takkas, nad takka heads and the devotees' association.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)
   - Source: Kannada Oneindia, "Huttari Habba 2023…", 14 Nov 2023
   - URL: https://kannada.oneindia.com/news/madikeri/huttari-habba-2023-date-and-time-fix-for-the-celebration-of-huttari-festival-327163.html
   - Quote: "ಕಕ್ಕಬೆಯ ಪಾಡಿ ಶ್ರೀ ಇಗ್ಗುತಪ್ಪ ಸನ್ನಿಧಾನದಲ್ಲಿ ಅಮ್ಮಂಗೇರಿಯ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಷ್ಯರು ದೇವತಕ್ಕರು, ನಾಡಿನ ತಕ್ಕ ಮುಖ್ಯಸ್ಥರು"

3. **The festival is on the full moon with Rohini nakshatra, in the Kodava month Birchiyar (Nov–Dec).**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)
   - Source: Star of Mysore, 15 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 1)
   - Quote: "will be celebrated on Rohini Nakshatra full moon night on Nov. 27"
   - Also: the Kodagu Express 2026 article says "observed during Birchiyar, a month in the traditional Kodava calendar that corresponds to November–December."

4. **The rite happens at night, in three timed steps: nere kattuvudu, then kadiru cutting an hour later, then food/prasada an hour after that. The Padi temple goes first and households follow a few minutes later.**
   - Tier: record (contemporary announcement)
   - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 7 Dec 2022
   - URL: https://kannada.oneindia.com/features/huttari-festival-celebration-in-kodagu-here-see-complete-details-about-speciality-277310.html
   - Quote: "ರಾತ್ರಿ 7:20ಕ್ಕೆ ಕೊಡಗಿನ ಮಳೆದೇವರು ಎಂದೇ ಕರೆಯಲ್ಪಡುವ ಪಾಡಿ ಇಗ್ಗುತ್ತಪ್ಪ ದೇಗುಲದಲ್ಲಿ ನೆರೆ ಕಟ್ಟಲಾಗಿದೆ. ನಂತರ 8:20ಕ್ಕೆ ಕದಿರು ಕೊಯ್ಯುವ"

5. **In 2024 the astrologer Shashikumar worked with the Kaniyara Nanjappa family, and the date was fixed at the Paadi temple.**

7. **The Kalladcha festival at the Paadi temple falls the day before Puthari.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news)

9. **Before the festival a "devara kattu" is placed at Malma hill, Igguthappa's first seat. Until Huthari loosens it, killing animals and other violence is forbidden.**
   - Tier: scholarship (news, describing a belief or custom)
   - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
   - Quote: "ಪಾಡಿಇಗ್ಗುತ್ತಪ್ಪದ ಆದಿಸ್ಥಳ ಮಲ್ಮ ಬೆಟ್ಟದಲ್ಲಿ ವಿಶೇಷ ಪ್ರಾರ್ಥನೆ ಸಲ್ಲಿಸಿ ದೇವರ ಕಟ್ಟು ಹಾಕಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ."
   - Note: Moegling (1855) records the same idea as the Dévara-kattu "God's peace" during the Huttari week (fact 33).

## B. What happens (modern accounts)

10. **The family gathers at the ain mane. Under the hanging lamp in the nellakki nadubade (central hall) they spread a mat and set the Huthari basket.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಕುಟುಂಬದ ಸದಸ್ಯರೆಲ್ಲರೂ ಐನ್‌ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಸೇರಿ ಮನೆಯ 'ನೆಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿ' ನಡುಬಾಡೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ತೂಗು ದೀಪದ ಕೆಳಗೆ ಚಾಪೆ ಹಾಸಿ"

11. **Leaves for the nere (modern Kannada account): five kinds, mango, peepal (arali), jackfruit, kumbali and wild cashew, with achchunaru (forest fibre).**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 and 7 Dec 2022 (same sentence in both)
    - URL: as fact 2
    - Quote: "ಮಾವಿನ ಎಲೆ, ಅರಳಿ ಎಲೆ, ಹಲಸಿನ ಎಲೆ, ಕುಂಬಳಿ ಎಲೆ, ಕಾಡು ಗೇರು ಎಲೆ ಹೀಗೆ ಐದು ತರಹದ ಎಲೆಯನ್ನು"

12. **Nere kattuvudu means stacking the leaves one on another and tying them with fibre while praying. Married women draw rangoli with rice flour first.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಎಲೆಗಳನ್ನು ಸಂಪ್ರದಾಯದಂತೆ ಒಂದರ ಮೇಲೊಂದು ಇಟ್ಟು ನಾರಿನಿಂದ ಕಟ್ಟಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ. ಇದನ್ನು ನೆರೆ ಕಟ್ಟುವುದು ಎಂದು ಕರೆಯುತ್ತಾರೆ."

13. **The Huthari kudike (pot) holds thambittu with milk, ghee, honey, sesame, ginger, coconut, bitter gourd and a thorn. Beside it go a sickle, the taliyakki bolcha (lamp) on a stool, and three betel leaves with three areca nuts.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಹುತ್ತರಿ ಕುಡಿಕೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ತಂಬಿಟ್ಟು ಹಾಕಿ ಅದರ ಜೊತೆ ಹಾಲು, ತುಪ್ಪ, ಜೇನು, ಎಳ್ಳು, ಶುಂಠಿ, ತೆಂಗಿನಕಾಯಿ, ಹಾಗಲಕಾಯಿ, ಮುಳ್ಳು"

14. **After the nere, boiled "Huthari genasu" (tuber) is eaten with honey, jaggery and ghee.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಬೇಯಿಸಿದ ಹುತ್ತರಿ ಗೆಣಸನ್ನು ಜೇನುತುಪ್ಪ, ಬೆಲ್ಲ ಹಾಗೂ ತುಪ್ಪದೊಂದಿಗೆ ಸೇರಿಸಿ ಸೇವಿಸುತ್ತಾರೆ"
    - Note: Richter (1870) also lists "sweet potatoes" in the supper (fact 31).

15. **An elder carries the "kutti" (basket) to the village ambala. The eldest of the house hands the sickle to the person who will cut the kadiru, and a married woman holds the taliyakki bolcha.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಮನೆಯ ಹಿರಿಯ ವ್ಯಕ್ತಿ ಕದಿರು ತೆಗೆಯಲು ಕುಡುಗೋಲನ್ನು ಕದಿರು ತೆಗೆಯುವವನ ಕೈಗೆ ನೀಡುತ್ತಾರೆ"

16. **In the field, milk and honey are poured at the base of the stalks, the nere is tied there, and a gunshot is fired at the muhurta.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ನೆರೆಯನ್ನು ಕದಿರಿನ ಬುಡಕ್ಕೆ ಕಟ್ಟಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ. ಆ ನಂತರ ಹುತ್ತರಿ ಮೂಹೂರ್ತಕ್ಕೆ ಸುಸೂತ್ರವೆನಿಸುವಂತೆ ಗುಂಡೊಂದನ್ನು ಹಾರಿಸಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ"

17. **The kadiru is cut in an odd number of stalks.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಕದಿರನ್ನು ಬೆಸ ಸಂಖ್ಯೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಕೊಯ್ದು ಹುತ್ತರಿ ಕುಕ್ಕೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಇಡಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ"
    - Also in the CHSA page: "tied in odd numbers" (fact 23).

18. **The basket of kadiru is carried home on the head to the cry "Poli poli deva". At the door a married woman washes the cutter's feet and gives milk.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ಕದಿರು ತುಂಬಿದ ಕುಕ್ಕೆಯನ್ನು ತಲೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಹೊತ್ತು 'ಪೊಲಿ ಪೊಲಿ ದೇವಾ' ಎಂದು ಘೋಷಣೆ ಕೂಗುತ್ತಾ ಬರಲಾಗುತ್ತದೆ"

19. **Meaning of "Poli Poli Deva": "May we prosper, Oh Lord". Older sources translate it as "increase, O God" (facts 31 and 36).**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: New Indian Express, 15 Dec 2024

    - Quote: "The chants of "Poli Poli Deva" (May we prosper, Oh Lord) resonated across Kodagu"

20. **Families without fields fetch kadiru from neighbours or temples and tie it in the prayer room. The sheaves are also tied to vehicles and weapons.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 14 Nov 2023 (URL as fact 2)
    - Quote: "ತಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿರುವ ವಾಹನ, ಆಯುಧಗಳಿಗೆ ಕಟ್ಟುವ ಸಂಪ್ರದಾಯ ಹಿಂದಿನಿಂದಲೂ ನಡೆದು ಬಂದಿದೆ"

21. **New-rice payasa is cooked at home and the family eats together.**
    - Tier: scholarship (news)
    - Source: Kannada Oneindia, 7 Dec 2022 (URL as fact 4)
    - Quote: "ಬಳಿಕ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಹೊಸ ಅಕ್ಕಿ ಪಾಯಸ ಮಾಡಿ ಸದಸ್ಯರೆಲ್ಲಾ ಒಂದೆಡೆ ಕುಳಿತು ಊಟ ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾರೆ"

22. **Thambittu made from fried kusubalakki and ripe banana was handed out at the Mysuru Kodava Samaja celebration. The same report covers gunfire, the puja to Igguthappa and Cauvery, and "Poli Poli Deva".**

23. **The CHSA account of the sequence: the family meets at the ain mane, the eldest hands a sickle to the head of family, a woman leads with a lit lamp, a gunshot is fired, and the crowd chants Poli Poli Deva.**

## C. Old written record (public domain, archive.org)

30. **Moegling (1855), *Coorg Memoirs*, pp. 45–53. This is the oldest full description found. On the great day, Ashvatha (peepal), Kumbali and Kéku leaves, Injadi creeper and Achchi bark are gathered before dawn.**
    - Tier: record (primary, 1855 missionary account)
    - Source: Hermann Moegling, *Coorg Memoirs: An Account of Coorg and of the Coorg Mission* (1855)
    - URL: https://archive.org/details/coorg-memoirs-hermann-moegling
    - Quote: "a quantity of Ashvatha-(ficus religiosa) Kumédali-(a wild tree growing all over Coorg) and Kéku-(also a Coorg jungle-tree) leaves" (OCR, p. 50)
    - On the nere: "rolls each piece into three leaves (one Ashvatha, one Kumbali, one Kéku-leaf) in the fashion of a native cheroot" (p. 51)

31. **Richter (1870), *Gazetteer of Coorg*, pp. 164–166. It repeats Moegling's account and gives the cry with its translation. The sheaf-cutter is chosen by the astrologer, and nobody may touch him.**
    - Tier: record (primary, 1870 government gazetteer)
    - Source: G. Richter, *Gazetteer of Coorg* (Mangalore: Basel Mission / Stolz, 1870)
    - URL: https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.08161 (second scan: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.212577)
    - Quote: "All return to the threshing floor shouting as they walk on: "Poly, poly, Déva!" (increase, O God!)"
    - Second quote: "The precedence is given to the person whom the astrologer has chosen in the morning for the ceremony of cutting the first sheaves."
    - Supper: "sugared rice and sweet potatoes, into which a handful of new rice is thrown"

32. **The name in the 1850s–1870s: "Huttari" from Pudi-ari, new rice.**
    - Tier: record (old scholarship; etymology is still an interpretation)
    - Source: Moegling 1855, p. 45 (URL as fact 30)
    - Quote: "The name Huttari is probably a contraction and corruption of two words Pudi Ari i. e. New Rice."
    - Richter 1870 (URL as fact 31): "The name is derived from the Malayálam Pudi-ari, new rice"

33. **The 1855 night sequence:**
    - The women set a lit lamp in a rice-strewn dish, and the household walks to the field behind it.
    - The cutter ties a leaf bundle to a rice bush, pours milk and cuts an armful.
    - Bundles are tied to "doors, stools, roofs, trees".
    - The Huttari dough is made of rice meal, plantain, milk and honey with "seven" of each token.
    - Tier: record (1855)
    - Source: Moegling 1855, pp. 51–52 (URL as fact 30)
    - Quote: "The dish with the burning lamp is carried in front. The sheaf-cutter follows with basket and sickle in one hand"
    - Quote: "who disperse to bind the festive token to every thing in house and garden, doors, stools, roofs, trees"
    - Quote: "No one during the seven holy days of Huttari dares quarrel, or fight, or even use offensive language to a brother-Coorg."

34. **In 1855 the festival lasted seven holy days plus four after-Huttari days.**
    - The six evenings before the full moon were spent in games such as chandukutti (ball and peg), mock plays and stick dances on the Uru-mandu.
    - On the eighth day came the Urukólu village stick-dance, on the ninth the Nadukólu, and on the tenth the Dévarakólu.
    - Tier: record (1855)
    - Source: Moegling 1855, pp. 46–54 (URL as fact 30)
    - Quote: "The real holidays are only seven in number, but both Coorgs and Holeyas … generally add two or three more days of feasting"
    - Quote: "Four after-Huttari-days are added to the holy week. On the eighth day the Urukólu, the village-stick-dance, collects the whole community."

35. **In 1855 village artisans brought new things for the festival: the Méda (basket-maker) the Huttari basket, the potter the Huttari pot, the carpenter a spoon, the Holeya a mat. Richter adds the blacksmith's new sickle. The astrologer came to announce the exact full-moon time.**
    - Tier: record (1855/1870)
    - Source: Moegling 1855, p. 51 (URL as fact 30)
    - Quote: "The Astrologer follows, communicates the important news of the exact time of the full-moon, and claims his share of the Huttari-bounty."

36. **In 1855 the date was keyed to the Malayalam new-rice festival, two months earlier.**
    - Tier: record (1855)
    - Source: Moegling 1855, pp. 45–46 (URL as fact 30)
    - Quote: "The Malayálam festival takes place two months before that of Coorg, because the rice on the coast ripens two months earlier."

37. **The 1965 Mysore State Gazetteer, Coorg District, says the head of family goes to the field "at the auspicious moment in the night", cuts stalks, and returns shouting "Poli, poli Deva" (Increase, increase O! God). The ears decorate the cradle, safe and granary, and Kolata follows for three or four days.**
    - Tier: record/scholarship (government gazetteer, 1965)
    - Source: B. N. Sri Sathyan (ed.), *Mysore State Gazetteer: Coorg District* (1965)
    - URL: https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.08413
    - Quote: "brings the sheaf to the threshing yard and then to the house shouting "Poli, poli Deva" (Increase, increase O! God)"
    - Quote: "The ears of the crop decorate various places of importance inside and outside the house such as cradle, safe and granary."

38. **In 1870, Huttari came before the main reaping: paddy ripens in November/December, "the feast of first fruits or 'Huttari' is celebrated, after which the paddy may be reaped."**
    - Tier: record (1870)
    - Source: Richter 1870 (URL as fact 31)
    - Quote: "In November or December the paddy gets ripe and the feast of first fruits or "Huttari" is celebrated, after which the paddy may be reaped."

## D. Legend: Igguthappa

39. **THE LEGEND IN FULL (oldest written version, Richter 1870, pp. 225–227, "taken from oral tradition"):**
    - Six brothers and a sister lived in Malabar. Five brothers came to Coorg by the Paditora ghat with their sister Ponnangalatamma.
    - At the Chouripade hill near the Kakkabe river they were hungry. Iguttappa gave rice and told her to boil it without fire; she agreed, if they would eat it without salt.
    - She milked a cow of the Paradandra house into the rice pot and buried it in the river sand, where it boiled.
    - Iguttappa threw rice in the air ("See how the hail is descending"), and she struck him with a ladle ("See how the thunder breaks in the monsoon").
    - The brothers tricked her with betel and declared she had lost caste. Iguttappa shot an arrow from Iguttappabetta and told her to live where it fell, in a mango tree at Ponnangala.
    - The brothers settled in separate villages, and all were worshipped as gods.
    - Tier: legend (recorded 1870)
    - Source: Richter 1870 (URL as fact 31)
    - Quote: "In ancient times there lived in the Malabar country six brothers and a sister."
    - Quote: "Iguttappa said: "I will give you rice, but you must boil it without fire.""
    - Quote: "Ponnangálatamma, seeing a cow, which belonged to the Paradandra house went and milked her, letting the milk fall into a chatty full of rice"
    - Quote: "The whole family were afterwards worshipped as gods."
    - Note for the writer: the rice-from-nothing and hail/thunder lines tie Igguthappa to rice and monsoon. That is the natural link to Puthari, but **no old source says this legend explains Puthari.** It is a legend of the god, not of the festival.

40. **Modern version: seven celestial siblings from Kerala. Three brothers stayed in Kerala (Taliparamba temples), and Igguthappa (the fourth) settled at Malma with a temple at Paadi nad. The sister (Thangamma/Ponnangala Thamme) settled at Ponnangala near Kakkabe.**
    - Tier: legend (encyclopedic retelling)
    - Source: Wikipedia, "Igguthappa"
    - URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igguthappa
    - Quote: "Legend has it that in ancient times from what is now Kerala arrived seven celestial children."
    - Quote: "The fourth brother Igguthappa took base at Malma in Kodagu and a temple was built for him at Paadi naad."

41. **Igguthappa is held to be the god of rain and crop, and gives the people of Kodagu "rain and rice". The siblings landed "in a conch shell".**
    - Tier: legend (travel writer's account of local belief)
    - Source: Inditales, "Padi Igguthappa Temple – Presiding Deity of Coorg"
    - URL: https://inditales.com/igguthappa-temple-presiding-deity-coorg/
    - Quote: "In ancient times, he was probably the God of rain and crop."
    - Quote: "Igguthappa decided to stay here and provide the people of Kodagu with rain and rice."
    - Kannada news calls him the rain god: "ಕೊಡಗಿನ ಮಳೆದೇವರು ಎಂದೇ ಕರೆಯಲ್ಪಡುವ ಪಾಡಿ ಇಗ್ಗುತ್ತಪ್ಪ" (Oneindia 2022, fact 4).

42. **Link to Puthari: paddy is first harvested in Igguthappa's own fields, and the people celebrate after that. Puthari is "normally celebrated 90 days after Onam".**
    - Tier: scholarship (historian C. P. Belliappa in Deccan Herald)
    - Source: Deccan Herald, "At summit, faith", C. P. Belliappa
    - URL: https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/at-summit-faith-2629803
    - Quote: "Every year, paddy is first harvested in fields belonging to Lord Igguthappa. People of Kodagu celebrate the festival the following day."
    - Note: this conflicts with the current practice, where the temple and households act on the same night a few minutes apart (facts 4 and 5).

43. **Name of the god: one reading is "Iggu means grain and thappa means give" (Belliappa). Richter (1870) instead glosses "Iggu, a Malayalam god; appa, father".**
    - Tier: scholarship (interpretations that disagree)
    - Sources: Deccan Herald (URL as fact 42) and Richter 1870 (URL as fact 31)
    - Quote (DH): "He is considered a provider of bounty and one who fulfills his devotee's wishes. Iggu means grain and thappa means give."
    - Quote (Richter): "the Iggudappa-kundu (Iggu, a Malayalam god; appa, father; kundu, hill) near the Paditora"

44. **Puthari as the god's own command (local priest's account): Igguthappa once lived in Coorg in human form and told people to offer him rice, "Since then Puttari harvest festival is celebrated".**
    - Tier: legend (oral, via a temple priest quoted by a blogger)
    - Source: Inditales (URL as fact 41)
    - Quote: "People here believe that once upon a time he lived in Coorg in human form. He directed them to offer him rice."

45. **The name Puthari = new rice (modern).**
    - Tier: scholarship
    - Source: New Indian Express, 15 Dec 2024 (URL as fact 19)
    - Quote: ""In Kodava language, 'Puthari' means new rice.""
    - Kodava Koota (URL as fact 26) also has: "Puttari is derived from pudiya = new and [ari] = rice". The second word is lost in the page's markup.

## Conflicts and gaps

- **2026:** the temple date is 25 Nov (Kodagu Express) and the government holiday is 26 Nov. The 2026 muhurta times were not found online as of 24 Nov 2026; a Shakti or Prajavani print notice may have them. Use "night of 25 November" without clock times, or verify locally.
- **Who cuts the first sheaf:**
  - 1855/1870: a person chosen by the astrologer, often "the young man".
  - 1965: the head of the family.
  - Modern Kannada news: the eldest hands the sickle to a designated cutter.
  - CHSA: the eldest hands it to the head of the family.
- **Leaves of the nere:**
  - 1855/1870: three, Ashvatha (arali), Kumbali and Kéku, plus the Injadi/Inyoli creeper and Achchi fibre.
  - Modern Kannada news: five, mango, arali, jackfruit, kumbali and wild cashew, plus achchunaru.
  - Only arali and kumbali are common to both lists.
- **The cry:**
  - Moegling 1855 writes only "póli! i.e. increase!".
  - Richter 1870 writes "Poly, poly, Déva!" (increase, O God!).
  - 1965 writes "Poli, poli Deva" (Increase, increase O! God).
  - NIE 2024 writes "May we prosper, Oh Lord".
  - Vanishing Kodavas records the men chanting "O O O" at the field.
- **Timing relative to Onam:** about 90 days (Belliappa; Sahapedia interview) against "two months" after the Malayalam new-rice feast (Moegling). These are not the same reference point.
- **Temple's deity:** Belliappa says the temple was "dedicated to Vishnu". The Inditales priest says Igguthappa = Subramanya/Kartikeya. Leave this out of the book or present both views.
- **Name etymology:** "Puthari = pudhiya ari / pudi-ari, new rice" is consistent across 1855, 1870 and today. "Iggu = grain" (Belliappa) and "Iggu = a Malayalam god" (Richter) disagree.
- **Legend versions:**
  - Richter 1870 gives six brothers and a sister, five of whom came to Coorg.
  - Wikipedia and Inditales give seven siblings, three staying in Kerala and three brothers plus the sister going to Kodagu.
  - Inditales has them arriving in a conch shell.
  - The caste-loss/betel trick in Richter is part of the oldest text. Per the "keep source story" rule, keep it, or choose the rice-without-fire episode, which is self-contained.
- **No source found ties the Igguthappa legend explicitly to the origin of Puthari.** The closest are Inditales (priest: "He directed them to offer him rice") and Belliappa (the god's fields are harvested first). Tell the legend as the god's story and state the link as belief.
- **Not found or not verified:**
  - The Kodava word "kadhir" as "sheaf" (sources write ಕದಿರು / "kadiru" / "Kadh" as the harvested ears; none gives a gloss).
  - "Mand pudikke" as a named dance.
  - A puthari "genasu" dish beyond the Oneindia line.
  - Dudi song lyrics.
  - B. L. Rice's *Mysore and Coorg* vol. 3 (archive.org tdl.46866): the OCR text had no "Huttari" hit, so it was not checked further.
- **Weak or tool-mediated quotes:** facts 7, 24 and 25 were read through the WebFetch summariser (the Vanishing Kodavas site blocks direct download). Check them by eye before printing. kodavaclan.com (lead P4) returned HTTP 409 and could not be read.
