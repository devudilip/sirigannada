# ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ (Belavadi Mallamma) and the siege of Belavadi, 1678: research dossier

Compiled 2026-10-18 for the new series `veera-naariyaru` (ವೀರ ನಾರಿಯರು), story 1. English with Kannada
names in Kannada script. Every claim carries a source and a tier (`inscription` / `scholarship` /
`legend`). The verbatim quotations, editions, pages and URLs are in `belavadi-mallamma-verified.md`
(cited below as **V§n**); this file is the working summary. "UNVERIFIED" means the claim was not found
in any source opened this session and must not be used as fact.

Working method: Wikipedia (English and Kannada) was used only as a finding aid. Everything below was
re-read in the source itself: Sarkar's *Shivaji and His Times* (1919, 1920, 1929), Sen's translation of
the Sabhasad bakhar (1920), Sen's *Foreign Biographies of Shivaji* (Martin), *English Records on
Shivaji* vol. II (1931), Grant Duff (1826), the Bombay Gazetteers for Belgaum (1884) and Kanara (1883),
Mehendale's modern biography, Arur's 1997 Pune thesis, and Betageri Krishnasharma's 1951 radio play.

## 0. The short answer

What the record supports: early in 1678, on his way home from the Karnataka campaign, Shivaji besieged a
small mud fort (ಗಡಿ, gadhi) at Belavadi near Sampgaon, held by a woman who was the Desai of the place.
She held out for about 27 days. An English letter written from Rajapur on 28 February 1678, while the
siege was still going on, says his own men called it a greater disgrace than anything the Mughals or
Bijapur had done to him, and that the conqueror of so many kingdoms could not reduce "this woman Desy".
The fort was then stormed and she was captured. The earliest Maratha chronicle says she was "punished";
a later one says she was released (and, per Mehendale, given a small estate). A Persian text of the late
18th century says she was "greatly dishonoured" by Sakhoji Gaikwad and that Shivaji had his eyes put out.

What the record does not support: that she defeated Shivaji; that he bowed to her, fell at her feet,
called her mother or sister, or gave her kingdom back; her birth, her father, her training, her
marriage; the women's army of 2,000 + 3,000; the death of a husband named Ishaprabhu in this war. All
of that is the Kannada tradition and a Marathi text of disputed date (`legend`), and the dossier keeps
it, because the owner's rule allows a legend told as ಐತಿಹ್ಯ and disclosed on the last page.

## 2. Sources: what survives, where, licence

### 2a. Contemporary
- **Rajapur factory letter, 28 Feb 1678** (F.R. Surat vol. 107 p. 60), printed in *English Records on
  Shivaji*, vol. II, no. 285 (Shiva Charitra Karyalaya, Poona 1931). archive.org/details/
  EnglishFactoryRecordsOnShivaji-1659To1682. The single contemporary witness. PD text.
- **François Martin's memoir** (Pondicherry), tr. Sen, *Foreign Biographies of Shivaji*: the unnamed
  blinded brother-in-law, 1678. Not about Belavadi.
- No inscription, no Kannada record, no Bijapur Persian record of the siege was found. The Belgaum
  Gazetteer of 1884 is silent about it.

### 2b. Maratha chronicles (all known here through translators)
- **Sabhasad bakhar** (1694): Sen, *Siva Chhatrapati* (1920) p. 128. Short; no name; "She was punished."
- **Chitnis bakhar** (1810): through Sarkar's and Mehendale's summaries only. "Mal Bai" / "Malawai";
  27 days; released; a small estate.
- **91-Kalami bakhar** ("A. K. Chronicle"): through Mehendale (name Savitribai) and Arur (Sakhoji
  coveted her; blinded). No English text reached.
- **Tarikh-i-Shivaji** (Persian, India Office MS. 1957; "based on Maratha tradition"): Sarkar's
  translation of f. 38a. Sortie, capture, "greatly dishonoured", Sakhoji blinded, imprisoned at
  "Manauli".
- **Shivadigvijay** (published Baroda 1895; Sarkar: "evidently fabricated" in its published form,
  with a c. 1760–75 kernel): Savitri Bai; "Balved desain loots transport"; its Prabhu Kayastha author
  "devotes page after page" to the defence of the Bhuikot of Belvadi by "a Prabhu Kayastha lady"
  (Sen p. 258).
- **Jedhe Shakavali**: through Mehendale only.

Licence: Sarkar (d. 1958) and Sen (d. 1962) are PD in India; the 1919/1920 editions are PD in the US.
Grant Duff and the Gazetteers are PD everywhere.

### 2c. Modern scholarship (reference only, in copyright, short quotations)
- **G. B. Mehendale**, *Shivaji: His Life and Times* (2011?, ISBN 978-0-9891538-0-5): the most careful
  modern account; names from Chitnis and tradition; the Yadwad slab; his verdict that the Samarotsava is
  "a modern fabrication".
- **Sushama Ashok Arur**, *House of Shivaji and Karnataka (1636–1707 AD)*, PhD, Pune 1997: summarises
  the Kannada tradition and the Samarotsava at length; treats the Samarotsava as "contemporary" (against
  its own editor). Use for what the tradition says, not for facts.

### 2d. The Kannada tradition (`legend`)
- Local tradition at Belavadi and Yadwad; Siddhanti Shivabasava Shastri's *Turukari Panchamara
  Itihasavu* (1929?; UNVERIFIED, not reached); the *Shivaji Mallammaji Samarotsava* (Marathi in Kannada
  script, published 1989; UNVERIFIED, not reached); a Sanskrit *ಶಿವವಂಶ ಸುಧಾರ್ಣವ* (named only on
  kn.wikipedia; UNVERIFIED).
- Betageri Krishnasharma (ಆನಂದಕಂದ), *ಬೆಳವಡಿ ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ*, 1951 radio play: read.
- Basavaraj Naikar, *The Rebellious Rani of Belavadi and Other Stories* (2001), English fiction: not

- Dinesh Nayak, *Bhavan's Journal* / eSamskriti / DIPR *March of Karnataka* (2021): popular retelling

## 4. Places

- **Belavadi (ಬೆಳವಡಿ)**, Bailhongal taluk, Belagavi district (formerly Sampgaon sub-division). Sarkar:
  "a small village 12 miles s. e. of Sampgaon and 30 miles s. e. of Belgaum"; Sen: "about 10 miles from
  Sampganv"; Mehendale: "about 20 km southeast of Sampgaon". South of the Malaprabha (map; BG XXI
  pp. 51–52 "Belevadi on the south side of the Malprabha"). Red-soil country (BG XXI p. 236); tiny gold
  in the streams (BG XXI pp. 16, 51–52). A mahalkari was stationed there in the 1850s (BG XXI p. 434).
- **The fort**: "a petty mud-fort" (Sarkar 1920 p. 355); "a gadhi : a small mud fort" (Mehendale);
  "Bhuikot", a fort on the plain (Shivadigvijaya via Sen p. 258). Nothing of it survives: the only old
  structure is the Veerabhadra temple, the family deity of the Desais (Betageri 1951; Nayak 2021).
- **Yadwad (ಯಾದವಾಡ)**, Dharwad district, about 10 km north of Dharwad: the Maratha camp in the
  tradition; the carved slab in the Hanuman temple (V§7).
- **Sampgaon (ಸಂಪಗಾಂವ)**: Adil Shahi mosque; fort besieged by Prince Muazzam in 1683 (BG XXI p. 600).
- **Torgal (ತೊರಗಲ್)**: Shivaji's route north from Belavadi (Sarkar 1920 p. 356; Duff "Toorgul").
- **Manoli (ಮನೋಳಿ)**: a sandstone fort on a Malaprabha peninsula (BG XXI pp. 586–588); possibly the
  "Manauli" where Sakhoji was imprisoned (UNVERIFIED identification).
- **Sonda / Sode (ಸೋದೆ)**, Uttara Kannada: seat of Madhulinga Nayaka (BG XV.2).
- Wrong Belavadis: the Hoysala Veeranarayana temple village in Chikkamagaluru district, and Belawadi
  near Mysore. Never use their images.

## 5. The owner's questions, answered on the evidence

1. **Birth, family, upbringing (Sode? which house?).** No record of her birth or parents. The Kannada
   tradition (Arur; Wikipedia; Nayak) makes her the daughter of Madhulinga Nayaka of Sode and sister of
   Sadashiva Nayaka. The Kanara Gazetteer confirms a Madhu Linga Naik ruled Sonda 1638–1674, followed by
   Sadashiv Raya: the names fit, the relationship is unproven. Birth 1624 (English Wikipedia) is
   uncited: UNVERIFIED. The house of the Sonda chiefs was Lingayat/Veerashaiva in tradition (the
   gazetteer's names Madhu Linga, Baswa Linga point that way; the gazetteer does not state their faith).
   Sen's note calls the lady "a Prabhu by caste" on Sarkar's word, and the Shivadigvijaya author calls
   her a Prabhu Kayastha: this contradicts the Veerashaiva tradition and may come from reading the Desai's
   title ಪ್ರಭು as a caste (this pass's guess, UNVERIFIED). Tell the tradition; disclose the conflict.
2. **Marriage to the Desai of Belavadi.** Tradition only (Ishaprabhu). The record says a Desai's widow
   held the fort (Duff; Sarkar; Mehendale). The swayamvara and the 21 tigers come only from Naikar's
   fiction via Wikipedia: `legend`, and a weak one.
3. **Training in arms and riding.** No record. Tradition (Wikipedia, Nayak): sword, archery, javelin,
   riding with her brothers under teachers "Shankar Bhat" and "Ranavir Singh". The T.S. does say she
   made a sortie and "kept the field heroically" for a day: that is the nearest the texts come to her
   fighting in person.
4. **The quarrel's cause.** Three versions. Sabhasad: "She carried off the transport bullocks of the
   Raje's army." Duff: her people killed his foragers and refused satisfaction. Kannada tradition:
   Maratha soldiers seized Belavadi's cows, and the Desai's commander was insulted when he went to fetch
   them. No source mentions an insult to her person as the cause.
5. **Her husband's death.** Not in any record. In the tradition he is mortally wounded in a sortie (Arur,
   Samarotsava) or felled by a treacherous blow (1951 play). The record has her a widow before the siege.
   Disputed; the story may follow the tradition and must disclose it.
6. **How long Belavadi held; what she did personally.** About 27 days (Chitnis, Duff, Sarkar); "one
   month" (T.S.); Sarkar's chronology c. 26 Jan–23 Feb 1678. Personally: she held the fort (all
   sources); she made a sortie, destroyed the siege works and fought in the field for a day before
   capture (T.S. only, `legend`). The women's army of 2,000 with 3,000 women bodyguards is the
   Samarotsava's (via Arur), `legend`.
7. **Sakhoji Gaikwad.** Only the *Tarikh-i-Shivaji* (and the 91-Kalami, via Arur) says he dishonoured
   her and that Shivaji put out his eyes and imprisoned him at "Manauli". Martin's 1678 memoir reports a
   brother-in-law of Shivaji blinded for a Mughal-inspired conspiracy, unnamed; Pagadi (via Arur)
   identifies him as Sakhoji and says the cause was treason, not Belavadi. So: a blinding of someone close
   to Shivaji in 1678 is reported by a contemporary; that it was Sakhoji and that it was for his conduct
   towards her is late Maratha tradition. Tell as "the Maratha chronicles say"; disclose.
8. **Did Shivaji meet her, honour her, call her mother or sister, restore her kingdom?**
   - Met her: he was present at the siege (Sabhasad, Rajapur letter), and she was captured, so a meeting
     is plausible; no source describes it except the tradition.
   - Released her: Chitnis (via Sarkar "captured and released"; via Mehendale "released and given a small
     estate"). `scholarship`, late source. Sabhasad says "punished".
   - Honoured her as mother: Samarotsava (via Arur "treating her like his mother"); 1951 play (ತಾಯಾ,
     ಅಮ್ಮಾಜಿ). Sister (ತಂಗಿ): kn.wikipedia, uncited. `legend`.
   - Restored her kingdom: Nayak 2021 and kn.wikipedia, uncited; the Chitnis "small estate" is the only
     chronicle hint. Belvadi is not among Shivaji's forts at his death (BG XXI p. 376).
   - The Yadwad slab (Shivaji feeding milk to her child, as described) is the tradition's monument of a
     reconciliation; its date and inscription are unknown.
9. **"ಶಿವಾಜಿ ಅವಳಿಗೆ ಬಾಗಿದ" / "she defeated Shivaji".** What each source supports:
   - Rajapur letter: he had not yet reduced her and felt disgraced. **Not** a defeat.
   - Sarkar: "This long check by a woman ... greatly lowered Shivaji's prestige." A check, not a defeat.
   - Sabhasad, Duff, Sarkar, T.S., Chitnis: the fort fell and she was captured.
   - Samarotsava: she ambushed him and he fell at her feet; its own 1989 editor admits that "the claim ...
     of Mallamma's victory over Shivaji is false" (Mehendale); Mehendale calls the text a fabrication not
     earlier than 1818.
   - The 1951 play: he stops the battle out of respect and calls her mother; she says he won her by
     generosity, not by valour. That is the most dignified folk ending and the one closest to the record.
   So: "she defeated Shivaji" is not supported; "she held Shivaji's army for 27 days and shamed his
   camp" is supported; "Shivaji bowed to her" is legend.
10. **Later life and death.** No record. The Samarotsava copy is said to have been made "after
    Mallamma's death in 1717" (Mehendale's summary); English Wikipedia says she died in 1678 (uncited).
    Tradition: she governed for her son with her brother's help. UNVERIFIED either way.

## 6. Disputed or uncertain points

All in the table at V§8. The five that shape the story:
1. Who started it (her raid on his bullocks / his men's raid on her cows / killed foragers).
2. Whether her husband died in this war or before it.
3. Whether Shivaji was personally at Belavadi (yes on the record) versus the Kannada Wikipedia line that
   he took no part.
4. What happened to her after capture (punished / dishonoured / released with an estate / honoured).
5. The name: Mallamma (Kannada; Chitnis's "Mal Bai" supports a Malla- name) against Savitribai
   (Persian and late Marathi). The story uses ಮಲ್ಲಮ್ಮ.

## 7. What the Kannada folk tradition adds (all `legend`, told as ಐತಿಹ್ಯ)

- Her birth in the Sonda royal house; a brother; a royal education in arms.
- The swayamvara with the tiger hunt (weakest; only via a modern English novel).
- Belavadi as a small free state of 160 or 360 villages (Wikipedia; 1951 play), founded 1511 by a
  "Raja Chandrashekhara" (kn.wikipedia, Nayak), loyal to Vijayanagara, Veerashaiva, worshipping
  Veerabhadra; the war cry "ಜಯ್ ವೀರಭದ್ರ".
- The first clash over cows; the insulted commander; the women's army; Ishaprabhu's death.
- The night ambush near a Bhavani temple at Lokur; Shivaji seeing in her the goddess (ಜಗದಂಬೆ); his
  apology; mother or sister; the milk for her child (the Yadwad slab); the escort to Torgal.
- Memorial stones in her country (1951 play: "the people of that country say").
- Modern claims to avoid as fact: "the first queen in the world to raise a women's army" (kn.wikipedia;
  *The Hindu* 2008); and *The Hindu* 2008's report that she formed a women's army "to fight against the
  British", which is simply wrong (she lived two centuries before Kittur Chennamma).
- Ballads (ಲಾವಣಿ) and films: none found. Treat any ಲಾವಣಿ quoted later as needing its own source.

What the folk layer gets right against the Marathi record: the fact of a woman Desai's long defence, the
shame it caused in Shivaji's camp, and Chitnis's "released" ending, which the tradition turns into
honour. What it adds without evidence: everything personal.

## 9. Visual bible

### 9a. The lead's look
- **No portrait, painting, sculpture or hero stone of her is known.** The Yadwad slab is said to show
  her standing with bow and arrow, sari in veerakachche (Nayak 2021), but its date is unknown and the
  only Commons photo is too poor to read and of doubtful licence (V§9a). **Her face will be an AI
  reconstruction**, disclosed as such.
- Character sheet guidance (owner rules: handsome and strong, distinct from every earlier lead, all of
  whom were men): a Kannada woman of the Deccan plateau in her late twenties to mid-thirties (the
  tradition gives her an infant son; the 1624 birth year is unsourced, so do not age her to 54); tall,
  upright, rider's and archer's shoulders; oval face with a strong jaw and high cheekbones; large dark
  eyes under straight brows; a straight nose with a small gold nose stud (ಮೂಗುತಿ); dark brown skin;
  long black hair in a firm knot at the nape (BG XXI p. 131: hair "in a knot behind the head"); a level
  streak of white ash on the forehead with a small kumkum dot (Veerashaiva sect mark, BG XXI p. 131); a
  ling in a silver box (ಕರಡಿಗೆ) on a cord at the neck or tied on the upper right arm (BG XXI p. 131).
  Widow's marks: the record makes her a widow during the siege, the tradition makes her one mid-story.
  Before the husband's death: bangles, mangalsutra, flowers; after it, plain wrists and no flowers. Keep
  dignity: no theatrical grief make-up.
- Dress: a nine-yard sari (ಸೀರೆ) in a deep colour (indigo, maroon, saffron-red), short-sleeved fitted
  bodice (ಕುಪ್ಪಸ); for riding and fighting the sari drawn between the legs and tucked at the back
  (ವೀರಗಚ್ಚೆ), as BG XXI p. 112 describes the between-the-feet style; end of the sari tied tight across
  the chest and waist; a cloth sash (ಪಟ್ಟಿ) holding a dagger (ಕಠಾರಿ). Gold ear ornaments (bugdi, bali),
  a few necklaces, bracelets (BG XXI p. 131). No crown; a Desai's wife wore jewellery, not regalia.
- Arms: a recurved bow and quiver (the Yadwad figure, as described); a straight sword (ಖಡ್ಗ) or the
  Deccani pata only if sourced; a spear. No pistols. A grey or bay horse with a padded saddle cloth.

### 9b. Belavadi fort and village (reconstruction)
- A small plains fort (ಗಡಿ, bhuikot): earth-and-rubble walls with mud plaster, perhaps a stone-faced
  gate, four round corner bastions, a ditch, a wooden gate with iron spikes; inside, a Desai's two-storey
  wada of mud, timber and tiles, a Veerabhadra shrine, granaries, a well. Height of walls modest
  (Sarkar's "petty mud-fort").
- Outside: a village of mud-walled houses with tiled roofs (BG XXI p. 131), cattle pens, red soil fields
  of jowar and cotton under a winter sky (January–February: dry, clear, cool mornings, dusty afternoons).
  The Malaprabha to the north with a sandy bed in the dry season (Commons: River Malaprabha near
  Bailhongal). Low ridges; neem and tamarind; black-soil patches.
- Siege works: Maratha trenches and earthworks around the fort (T.S.: "demolished all the siege
  trenches"), camp tents, horse lines, cooking smoke, transport bullocks.
- Yadwad: a separate open camp on the plain to the south, near Dharwad.

### 9c. Maratha soldiers (1678)
- Shivaji: match the c. 1680 Golconda-school portraits (File:Shivaji Rijksmuseum.jpg, File:Shivaji
  British Museum.jpg; open them before writing the sheet, they were not re-examined in this pass). The
  Yadwad rider, as described, carries sword and shield and has attendants with an umbrella and staffs
  (Nayak 2021). Keep him tall and composed, a king.
- Cavalry and foot: light horsemen with spears, swords and round shields, a few matchlocks; transport
  bullocks with packs (Sabhasad: bullocks "requisitioned for transport purposes"). Details of dress,
  banners and armour must come from period paintings in mysuru-verified.md §13c before any render; none
  were verified in this pass.
- No uniforms; no bayonets; no field artillery unless a source is found.

### 9d. Deccani (Bijapuri) context
- Bijapur court dress for any Adil Shahi figure: File:"Sultan Ali Adil Shah II of Bijapur with a Woman"
  1660-75 (PD). The Sampgaon Adil Shahi mosque for a Bijapur-era building on the plain.

### 9e. Anachronisms to avoid
- The modern Belavadi statue or the Kittur Chennamma iconography (1824) for Mallamma.
- Kittur fort (18th–19th c.) as Belavadi.
- The Hoysala temple of Belavadi, Chikkamagaluru.
- Cannon batteries, rifles, flintlocks; Mughal court dress on Belavadi people; Rajasthani ghagra-choli on
  a Kannada Desai's wife; tigers' heads as trophies in her hall (from the uncertain swayamvara tale).

## 10. Story shape and the goosebump moment

The true turning point is the letter: on 28 February 1678 an English clerk at Rajapur writes that the man
who has conquered so many kingdoms cannot take one woman's mud fort. Build the book so that the
reader is inside the fort when that sentence is being written outside it.

Candidate scene plan (each scene's facts in `facts.json`; legends disclosed):
1. Belavadi on the plain; the Desai's house; the Veerabhadra shrine (tradition).
2. Shivaji's army comes north from Gingee, Kopal, Lakshmeshwar (record).
3. The cattle quarrel (tell the Kannada version; disclose the other two).
4. The husband falls (tradition; disclose that the record makes her already a widow).
5. She takes command; the gates shut; the siege begins (record).
6. Days of the siege: trenches, hunger, the women of the fort (record + tradition).
7. Rajapur, 28 Feb 1678: the letter (record, verbatim in translation).
8. The sortie: the trenches burned, a day in the field (T.S.; `legend`, disclose).
9. The fort falls; she is taken (record).
10. Before Shivaji: the punishment of the guilty man (T.S.; disclose) and her release (Chitnis).
11. The milk for the child at Yadwad (tradition; the slab).
12. Belavadi today: one temple and a carved stone.

### Little-known facts (ನಿಮಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತೇ?)
- The only contemporary mention of her is an English East India Company letter from Rajapur (ERS II
  no. 285). `inscription`
- The earliest Maratha chronicle (Sabhasad, 1694) does not give her name at all; the Chitnis chronicle
  calls her "Mal Bai". `scholarship`
- Grant Duff (1826) put the siege at Bellary by mistake; Sen and Sarkar moved it back to Belvadi.
  `scholarship`
- Shivaji's army rules said cows were not to be taken and bullocks only for transport (Sabhasad, Sen
  p. 32). The quarrel at Belavadi was over exactly those animals. `scholarship`
- Specks of gold were washed from the streams around Belavadi in the 19th century (BG XXI pp. 16, 51–52).
  `scholarship`
- A real Madhulinga Nayaka ruled Sonda from 1638 to 1674 (Kanara Gazetteer). `scholarship`
- Belavadi was the station of a mahalkari (sub-collector) of Sampgaon in the 1850s (BG XXI p. 434).
  `scholarship`
