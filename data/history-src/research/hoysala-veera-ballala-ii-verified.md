# Veera Ballala II (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ವೀರಬಲ್ಲಾಳ), Hoysala, 1173–1220: verification pass (verbatim sources)

Prepared 2026-11-08 for the book `hoysala/veera-ballala-ii`. Everything below was read this session in
the editions named; nothing is from memory. Quotations are verbatim from the archive.org OCR text
(djvu.txt) with obvious OCR breakage silently repaired (line-end hyphenation rejoined, running heads
removed, diacritics normalised: "Sévuna", "Dévi"). Page numbers are the printed ones from the running
heads. Source texts were saved in the session scratchpad, not in the repo. Tiers as README §2.

The existing series research (`hoysala.md` Story E, `hoysala-verified.md`) was the starting point; this
file re-reads every primary passage the book uses and adds what those files lacked (Umadevi, the Chola
title, the Soratur topography, the Lakkundi fortress, the Garuda pillar text).

## 1. The Hoysalas as feudatories, and the falling empire

**V§1a** RICE 1909 p. 102 (Narasimha I): "the Chalukya throne was usurped by Bijjala and the
Kalachuryas." p. 102 (Ballala II): "The reign of Ballala II or Vira-Ballala vied in glory with that of
his grandfather Vishnuvarddhana, and the whole dynasty is in consequence sometimes called the Ballalas
after him." `inscription` (Rice's summary of the records).

**V§1b** RICE 1909 p. 102: "In Bl 86 an account is given of a royal progress made by him in his
father's lifetime through the hill countries in the west. On this occasion Tantrapala-Hemmadi claims to
have induced Kongalva, Changalva, and the other chiefs of Male to do homage, and eventually to have
gained the crown for Ballala, himself being made minister." `inscription`

**V§1c** DERRETT pp. 87–88 (paraphrase, in copyright): in 1183 the Chalukya Somesvara IV recovered
Kalyana from the Kalachuris; with the disappearance of the last Kalachuri in 1184 that dynasty ended;
Ballala "adopted the sensible policy of biding his time" and "had only six years to wait for the
harvest." `scholarship`

**V§1d** RICE 1909 p. 104 (end of the reign): "during which the Western Chalukyas and the Kalachuryas
came to an end, the Sévunas were driven back, and the Hoysalas remained as a dominant power in the
South." `scholarship`

## 2. Coronation, Coorg, Uchchangi

**V§2a** EC VI Introduction p. 18: "Ballala II was anointed to the crown on the 22nd of July 1173 (see
Kd 4, 136, 139) in the capital Dorasamudra." Translation of Kd 4, EC VI p. 26: "When (with usual titles)
the nissanka-pratapa Hoysana vira-Ballala-Déva was in the capital Dorasamudra (on the date specified,
22nd of July 1173) celebrating the festival of his assuming the crown, and ruling the kingdom". Also
RICE 1909 p. 102: "He was crowned on the 22nd of July 1173 (Kd 4, 136, 129) in the capital
Dorasamudra." `inscription`

**V§2b** RICE 1909 p. 102: "Hs 20 shows that in 1174 Ballala had to send an expedition under his
general Bettarasa against the Changalva king Mahadéva, who had retired to Palpare, a fort in Kiggatnad
in south Coorg. ... This is the earliest specific mention that has been found of the Kodagas or people
of Coorg." `inscription`

**V§2c** RICE 1909 p. 102: "An early conquest was that of Uchchangi, the Pandya fortress. This had been
besieged by the Chólas for twelve years and abandoned as hopeless; but Ballala easily overcame it, and
when Kama-Déva, the Pandya king, threw himself on his mercy, restored him to his kingdom. This was
before 1177 (Ck 36), and he, in consequence, assumed the titles Giridurgamalla and Sanivara-siddhi."
`inscription`

**V§2d** EC VI Introduction p. 18 (on Cm 21 and 22, dated 1178): "Of the hill itself it is said that
the king of birds (Garuda), who soars at will over the three worlds, is unable to fly over the highest
peak of Pandya's splendid droog; yet from desire for that hill of Uchchangi, vira-Ballala captured it
without trouble and tossed it about as if playing at (the games) ... Other inscriptions inform us that
on Pandya submitting to him, Ballala took pity on him and restored to him his kingdom." `inscription`
(The same passage says the Pandya "abandoned the hill and forsaking his prancing horse and the throne
on which he sat, took refuge in the middle of a forest": a victor's boast. The book does not use it,
by the house rule on the dignity of kings; the restoration is used.)

**V§2e** FLEET 1896 p. 68: Ballala's "conquests also included the Chola and Pandya kings, to the latter
of whom he restored his forfeited kingdom when he humbled himself before him". `scholarship`

## 3. Kuvara-Lakshma and the Garuda pillar (Bl 112)

**V§3a** EC V translations, Belur Taluq, Bl 112, "Date? about 1220 A.D.", pp. 71–74:
- p. 72: "His minister (with praises) was Kuvara-Lakshma. As if the (king's) palace were his cradle,
  (the king's) bounty the oil for his mouth, his nurses the members of the (King's) female apartments,
  thus did king Ballala cherish Kuvara-Lakshma-dandadhisa as a son."
- p. 72: "Between servant and king there was no difference; the glory and marks of royalty were equal in
  both ... his wealth and his life Kuvara-Lakshma devoted for the gifts and the victory of
  vira-Ballala-Déva; and conquered the world for him as far as the southern ocean".
- p. 72: "of only two things was he ignorant, how to say no to supplicants, and how to suffer defeat. He
  gave his word to king Ballala that he would keep him free from fear."
- p. 73: "He was thus both a hand-mirror and a dagger to Ballala-Déva."
- p. 73: his wife Suggala-Dévi "bound a todar on her left lotus foot" as her word not to desert him.
- p. 73: "the general Kuvara-Lakshma had heroes so bound to him to the number of one thousand. Those who
  abandoned instead of dying with their ruler, and escaped, such have we truly known, but not such as
  gave up their lives and died at the same time with him, the exceeding great warriors who had devoted
  themselves to the general Kuvara-Lakshma."
- pp. 73–74: "As evidence that in faithfulness to his master Garuda alone was his equal, and that he and
  no others were equal to Garuda, the images of himself and of Garuda were equally engraved thereon".
- p. 74: "what mind of a friend would not cause the hair on the body to stand up with pleasure".
- p. 74: "the dandésa Lakshma, together with his wife, mounted up on the splendid stone pillar, covered
  with the poetical vira-sasana, proclaiming his devotion to his master. And on the pillar they became
  united with Lakshmi and with Garuda (the inscription ends thus, in an unfinished verse)."
`inscription`

**V§3b** EC V Introduction p. xxiii: "Coincident with his death, it appears, was the self-sacrifice of
the prince Lakshma, recorded on a pillar near the Hoysalésvara temple at Halebid ... The sculptures on
the pillar point unmistakably to suicide, being all figures of men with swords cutting off their own
arms and legs, and even their own heads." RICE 1909 p. 104: "He was of royal blood, and perhaps a
half-brother. He and a thousand warriors had vowed themselves, as Garudas, to live and die with the
king, and at his death took their own lives as a sign of undying devotion to him." `inscription` for the
pillar; the timing ("it appears") and "perhaps a half-brother" are Rice's inferences: `scholarship`.

**V§3c** Commons photograph (above): the pillar stands today at the Hoysalesvara temple, Halebidu. Its
carved band shows kneeling men with swords; the book's picture shows the pillar, never the act.

## 4. Bhillama, the Seuna advance, Umadevi

**V§4a** FLEET 1896 p. 72: "Mallugi's son was Bhillama ... We have already had mention of him in
connection with the Hoysala king Ballala II., by whom, apparently in Bhillama's life-time, his son
Jaitugi I. was defeated in a battle fought, according to tradition, at Lakkundi in the Dharwad
District." Bhillama's earliest record "is dated Saka 1111 (A.D. 1189-90), the third year of his reign
... This inscription, therefore, fixes Saka 1109 (A.D. 1187-8) ... for the commencement of his reign."
Another of the same date "is at Annigeri ... and speaks of that place as the capital". "Bhillama was
succeeded, in Saka 1113 (A.D. 1191-2) ... by his son Jaitugi I., also called Jaitrasimha and
Jaitrapala, who had held the supreme command of his father's army when it was defeated by Ballala II."
p. 71: the Yadavas' "banner bore the device of a golden Garuda". `scholarship` (Fleet from the records)

**V§4b** DERRETT p. 88 (paraphrase): in 1189 Bhillama Sévuna, ruler at Dévagiri, had acquired large
parts of northern Kuntala and was moving south; in 1190 he occupied Annigere and Gadag; the princes of
the region were terrified. p. 89: Ballala attacked a Sévuna officer camped near Hadade south of the
Tungabhadra, chased the enemy back across the river, and came to terms. `scholarship`

**V§4c** EC V translations, Arsikere Ak 85, "Date? 1190 A.D.", p. 145: "Be it well. When, (with
various epithets), Hoysala vira-Ballala-Déva's other half, the senior queen, the madévi (or? Uma-Dévi)
was in Dorasamudra, ruling the kingdom of the world in peace and wisdom". EC V Introduction p. xxi:
"With Ak. 93 of 1189 we first meet with the queen Uma-Dévi. In Ak. 85 she is represented as ruling the
kingdom, and in Ak. 40 appears again in 1209." `inscription` (Rice's own query on the reading is kept
as `disputed`). DERRETT p. 89 (paraphrase): in April 1190 the queen ruled at Dorasamudra in Ballala's
stead and took the first steps against a rising in the south-east when his army went north.
`scholarship`. RICE 1909 p. 103: "His senior queen Uma-Dévi, mentioned in 1209 (Ak 40), appears in
many records for a long time."

**V§4d** EC VII Honnali Hl 28, "Date 1197 A.D.", p. 165: "when Umâ-Dêvi leaving the camp in the Kavudi
river, came by way of a raid to Belagavatti, and fighting, was defeated, and seizing the cattle in
Kattagi was departing, the nad people came out in a body and gave battle". Hl 37, "Date 1196 A.D.",
p. 166: "Umma-Bayi's warriors suddenly coming with horses, when seizing the cows, were defeated". EC VII
Introduction p. 86: "In Hl. 37, 38, 40 and 28 we have records of persistent attacks made in 1196 and
1197 by Uma-Dévi, who must have been the queen of Hoysala Ballala II. Her force is said to have been
defeated on each occasion, but she seems to have obtained a lot of booty." DERRETT p. 97: she "set upon
him and during two seasons ravaged his lands"; p. 102 suggests "the excessive severity" of her raids may
lie behind the Sinda's later defection. `inscription` (the records are the Sinda side's hero stones).

Verdict on the brief's question: Umadevi can be a named person. She is in the records as senior queen
from 1189, as ruler at the capital in 1190 (with Rice's query), and as the leader of raids in 1196–97
(recorded by her enemies, who say her force was beaten back but took cattle). She is not Narasimha II's
mother (that is Padmala-mahadevi: Dg 25, Ng 29).

## 5. Soratur and Lakkundi

**V§5a** EC XI translation, Dg 25 (1224, the Harihara temple record of Narasimha II), p. 34: "thus did
king Ballala cause it to be said in his slaughter of the Sévuna army in battle. Though he came with as
many as 200,000 infantry armed with thunderbolts, and 12,000 cavalry, conspicuous with high saddles and
jewelled breast-plates, vira-Ballala-Raya on his one elephant charged the Sévuna king's army, put them to
flight and slaughtered them from Soratur to the bank of the Krishnavéni river. When a host of hostile
kings with great pride formed a league against him with fixed determination, how can I describe the
might with which Giridurgga-malla suddenly besieged and took the forts within Erambarage, the honoured
Virata's fort, Gutti, Bellittage, Rattapalli, Soratur, and Kurugod?" Rice's footnote on "thunderbolts":
"exact meaning of this word is not known; the description seems to imply that they had fire-arms."
RICE 1909 p. 103 repeats the passage and places Soratur "near Gadag". `inscription` (a court poem written
some thirty years later; the numbers are a boast; the "fire-arms" reading is rejected as anachronistic,
no gunpowder weapons are attested in Karnataka in the 12th century).

**V§5b** EC V translation, Cn 179, "Date 1190 A.D.", pp. 202–203: "this emperor of the south prepared
the ground by his conquest, and from Soratur as far as Belvola made it fit for being turned up by the
plough-shares of the cultivators, having manured it with the bodies of the myriad brave warriors of the
Sévuna army." EC V Introduction p. xxi adds: "The composer of this inscription was Jannayya."
`inscription`

**V§5c** EC V translation, Cn 249, "Date? about 1190 A.D.", p. 230 (a hero stone): "When in Soratur,
Bala-déva fought with Jaitisiva and defeated him, Mara-gauda slew many". `inscription`

**V§5d** EC V translation, Bl 77, "Date 1198 A.D.", p. 64: "Moistening his valiant sword with the blood
of his enemy the Pandya king, he whets it on the grindstone the head of Billama, and sheathes it in the
lotus mouth of Jaitugi, the king Ballala." `inscription` (court poetry; not evidence that Bhillama died
by Ballala's hand)

**V§5e** DERRETT pp. 90–92 (paraphrase): in June 1191 Bhillama was at Hirur near Gadag; in the summer of
1192 Ballala marched on Belvola; the Sévuna general Jaitugi led his army through a gap in the chain of
hills that shields Gadag and Lokkigundi and deployed in front of it; Ballala met it at Soratur, his
camps stretching to Hosur four miles away; he drove the Sévuna back through the gap to Lokkigundi,
their headquarters, whose fortifications had ditches and towers, and took it by assault. Derrett quotes a
court poem: Bhillama boasts of "a multitude of elephants and warriors", and Ballala "crying aloud
pushed forward that (famous) elephant" and chased the enemy from Soratur. p. 91: Hemadri, writing
about 1270, claims Bhillama "made the Hoysala king lifeless"; Derrett calls this "very far from the
truth". p. 91: "Nothing is heard of Bhillama after 1193". p. 92: at the end of November 1192 Ballala
was still encamped at Lokkigundi; Gadag "was in his hands". `scholarship`

**V§5f** EC V translation, Ak 5, "Date 1194 A.D.", p. 114: "When he fought Jaituga, who was with an army
in the great fortress, which, with high ramparts, lofty bastions, mounted with astonishing flag-staves,
even combined masses could not attack and escape, he soon captured Lokkigundi, the king Ballala."
`inscription`. RICE 1909 p. 103: "By 1193 he had taken up his residence at Lokkigondi or Lakkundi in
Dharwar (Sk 105)." FLEET 1896 p. 68: "In Saka 1114, we find him established at the capital of
Lokkigundi, the modern Lakkundi near Gadag".

**V§5g** Date of Soratur, DISPUTED. Cn 179 is dated 1190 and already names Soratur, so Rice puts the
victory "before 1190". Derrett puts a first clash (Hadade) in 1190 and Soratur in summer 1192. Fleet
has Jaitugi's defeat "according to tradition, at Lakkundi". The book gives no year for the battle.

## 6. Emperor of the south

**V§6a** FLEET 1896 p. 68: Ballala's records "fix Saka 1113 (A.D. 1191-2) ... for the commencement of
his reign. It was he who first of his family assumed the regal titles". p. 67: his inscriptions are found
"at Belur, Halebid ... and at Balaganur ... Annigeri, Nagamve, and Gadag, in the Dharwad District".
RICE 1909 p. 103: "He was now sole ruler of the Seven-and-a-half Lakh country (Ci 64), and styled emperor
of the South (Sb 140)." DERRETT p. 95: from 1192 he bore the titles beginning *samasta-bhuvanasraya*,
"refuge of the entire Earth". `inscription`/`scholarship`

**V§6b** RICE 1909 p. 103: the royal residence at "Hallavur, also called Vijayasamudram ... on the
Tungabhadra in the Rani-Bennur taluq of Dharwar ... from 1200 (Hn 139) seems to have been living there
almost continuously till 1211 (Ak 137)". `inscription`

**V§6c** FLEET 1896 p. 68: "His first attempt on the last district was made in Saka 1118 (A.D. 1196-7),
when he besieged Panungal [Hangal] ... In this attempt he was unsuccessful, being repulsed by
Kamadeva's generals". `scholarship`

**V§6d** DERRETT pp. 99–103 (paraphrase): from 1211 Singhana, Jaitugi's son, struck into Banavase; by
1213 the Sévuna held Gadag and Erambarage; the Hoysala lost much of the north; towards the end of 1217
Ballala stayed at his capital, "worn down, it would seem, by more than forty years of almost ceaseless
warfare". `scholarship`

## 7. The Chola rescue and the title

**V§7a** EC IV.2 translation, Nagamangala Ng 29, "Date 1218 A.D.", pp. 118–119: "To Ballala-Déva and
Padmala-mahadévi was born the heroic Nrisimha, the only protector of the Chóla family, the uprooter of
the Magadha king." Then Ballala's titles: "displacer of the Magara kingdom, a grindstone to Adiyama,
establisher of the Chola kingdom, firm as mount Méru, hunter of the chiefs, a lion to the elephant
Pandya, a submarine fire to the Sévana army, the pratapa-chakravartti vira-Ballala". Kannada text
(EC IV.2 text volume, Ng 29): "Magara-rajya-disapatta Adiyama-gharatta Chóla-rajya-pratishthacharyyam
Méru-naga-dhairyyam mandalika-bentekaram Pandya-gaja-kesari Sevuna-bala-jaladhi-badavanalam".
`inscription`. (The same verse boasts that in Ballala's assembly "Chóla" begs "for a place in the line
for food": a court boast the book does not use.)

**V§7b** SASTRI pp. 396–397 (paraphrase): Maravarman Sundara Pandya invaded and defeated the Chola king
(Kulottunga III) in the joint reign with Rajaraja III (1216–18); among Ballala's queens was a
Chola-mahadevi, "probably of Tamil Chola origin"; a Hoysala record "clearly implies that when Ballala
was still living, his son Vira Narasimha marched against Srirangam" (note 112: EC VI Cm 56, "Hoysana
Sri-Viraballala-devana magam Vira-narasimha-devanu ... Rangana mele nadavandu"), probably 12 September
1217; "Another inscription describes Ballala himself as the establisher of the Cola kingdom" (note 114:
EC IV Nl [= Ng] 29), which "implies that Ballala II must have assumed his titles before the beginning of
A.D. 1218". `scholarship` over `inscription`

**V§7c** DERRETT p. 109 (paraphrase): Ballala, busy in the north, sent his son Narasimha with an army to
the rescue of the Chola; a special levy for the Chola's help was raised in 1217–18 and still collected
long after (p. 200: "Tax levied specially to help the Cola. ... it was still levied in 1291");
Narasimha beat the Adigaiman and the Bana, defeated Pandya detachments, "comforted Rajaraja", and came
home; "Ballala II added to his already multitudinous titles" the three titles of Ng 29. `scholarship`.
(The OCR prints the year as "1228"; context, Sastri and Ng 29 fix it at 1217–18.)

Verdict on the brief's question: the "establisher of the Chola kingdom" title IS Ballala II's (Ng 29,
1218), but he did not go himself: he sent his son. Rice 1909 p. 105 gives the fuller Chola rescue (the
Kadava Perunjinga, Gb 45) to Narasimha II's own reign; that is a later campaign and stays out of the
story.

## 8. The end of the reign

**V§8a** EC V translation, Cn 211b, p. 217: "Ballala-Déva being of full age, having established
Narasimha in the kingdom and gone to heaven". EC V Introduction p. xxiii: "This was in 1220, as Cn 172b
states that Narasimha was crowned on the 18th of April in that year. Ballala was thus at least 55 years
of age at his death and had reigned 47 years." RICE 1909 p. 104: "Narasimha II was crowned on the 16th
of April 1220 (Cn 172b)." DERRETT p. 104: Ballala was in the capital at the beginning of 1220 "and it
was there that he died in June or July of that year". `inscription` (day of the crowning DISPUTED,
16 or 18 April)

**V§8b** EC V Introduction p. xxiii: Narasimha II and his mother Padmala-Dévi in 1220 "granted
endowments for the temple of Kédarésvara at Dórasamudra or Halebid, which had been erected by his
father Ballala-Déva and his father's junior wife Abhinava-Kétala-Dévi." `inscription`

**V§8c** EC V Introduction p. xx: "Bl. 72 of about 1200, states that the king had latticed windows of
stone and other accessories made for the Belur temple." `inscription`

**V§8d** RICE 1909 p. 103 note: Ballala's daughter Sovala-Dévi "established the agrahara of
Somanathapura, which was equal to Valabhi, at Haruvanahalli (Haranhalli in Arsikere taluq)" (Ak 123).
`inscription`

**V§8e** RICE 1909 p. 103: "His son Narasimha was apparently associated with him in the government in
1205 (Cd 23). He was Yuvaraja in 1210". DERRETT p. 97: Narasimha "a mere lad of 13½ years" in 1196.
`inscription`/`scholarship`

**V§8f** EC V translation, Arsikere Ak 182, "Dates ? 1227 and 1235 A.D.": "the senior queen Uma-Dévi ... made
over those shares to her old friends among the Brahmans ... thus did she restore the grant." She outlived
the king. `inscription` (date queried by Rice; not used in the prose)
