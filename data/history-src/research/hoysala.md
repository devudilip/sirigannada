Key dated events (all `inscription` unless marked):

- 1006: name "Poysala" in TN 44 at Kaliyur (RICE 1909 p. 95).
- 1022, 1026: Kongalva king attacks Nripa-Kama-Poysala; in Mj 43 the Poysala general Jogayya dies killing
  the enemy general Kannama's horse (EC V intro p. x).
- 1047–1100: Vinayaditya; boundaries given as Konkana, Alvakheda (S. Kanara), Bayalnad (Wayanad), Talekad,
  Savimale (Ng 32) (RICE 1909 p. 98). Dorasamudra adorned "with splendid buildings by Vinayaditya" (SB 53)
  while the capital was at Sosevur then Belur (RICE 1909 p. 96).
- 1116: general Ganga-Raja captures Talakad; Cholas expelled from Mysore; night attack on the Chalukya army
  at Kannegala (SB 73 (59) of 1118 and 125 (45)) (RICE 1909 p. 99; EC II 1923 intro pp. 45, 51–52).
- 1116: Pandyas of Uchchangi defeated at Dumme (Cm 99) (RICE 1909 p. 100).
- 1117: Bl 58 (stone) and Bl 71 (copper plates): consecration of the Vijaya-Narayana (Chenna-Kesava) temple at
  Velapura/Belur; date Saka 1039 Hemalambi, Chaitra suddha 5 — Saturday in Bl 58, Sunday in Bl 71
  (EC V intro p. xiii n.). 1117 also: Ch 83 says he rules in peace at Talakad and Kolala (RICE 1909 p. 100).
- 1121: Bl 147 (Ghattadahalli) — Ketamalla erects a Siva temple at Dorasamudra named
  Vishnuvardhana-Poysalesvara (EC V intro pp. xiii, xxxvii).
- 1123: Shantala founds the Savatigandhavarana basti at Shravanabelagola (EC II No. 132 (56)) (EC II 1923 intro p. 55).
- 1123: Hn 102 — king on the bank of the Kaveri hears of the death of his younger brother Udayaditya
  (EC V intro p. xiii).
- 1131: death of queen Shantala at Sivagange (EC II No. 143 (53)); her mother Machikabbe then dies by
  sanyasana at Belgola (EC II 1923 intro p. 56; translation pp. 72–74).
- 1133: birth of Narasimha I (Bl 124) (RICE 1909 p. 101).
- 1137: Bankapura and Talakad stated to be the capitals (Ak 144); 1139 king at Bankapura (Cm 199, 200);
  1141 dies there, body conveyed to Sosavur (Cm 96) (RICE 1909 p. 101).
- 1173 (22 July): Ballala II crowned (RICE 1909 p. 102).
- before 1177: Ballala II takes Uchchangi (12-year Chola siege had failed), restores Pandya Kama-Deva; titles
  Giridurgamalla, Sanivara-siddhi (Ck 36) (RICE 1909 p. 102).
- before 1190 (Cn 179): victory at Soratur over the Seuna (Yadava) army of Bhillama, pursued "to the bank of
  the Krishnaveni" (Dg 25; Bl 77) (RICE 1909 p. 103). 1193: residing at Lakkundi (Sk 105).
- 1220: death of Ballala II; prince Lakshma and "a thousand warriors ... as Garudas" take their own lives
  (Bl 112, pillar beside the Hoysalesvara temple) (RICE 1909 p. 104).
- 1254: division of the kingdom between Narasimha III (Dorasamudra) and Ramanatha (Tamil districts, Kolar)
  (RICE 1909 p. 106).
- 1276 (2 April): battle of Belavadi, Seuna general Saluva-Tikkama routed (Bl 164, 165) (RICE 1909 p. 106).
- 1292 (31 Jan): Ballala III crowned (Cn 36).
- 1310/11: "the Turukas marched against Dorasamudra" (Hn 51, 52) — the earliest Kannada notice of the Delhi
  invasion (RICE 1909 p. 107). Khusrau dates Kafur's arrival to 5 Shawwal 710 AH (E&D III p. 88).
- 1313: the hostage prince restored (Sh 68) (RICE 1909 p. 107); Lal 1950 reads an inscription as Ballala himself
  returning from Delhi on 6 May 1313 (via Wikipedia "Siege of Dwarasamudra") — see §5.
- 1316: capital rebuilt (Md 100). 1326/27: second Delhi (Tughluq) expedition "completely demolished the city"
  (RICE 1909 p. 107; COELHO p. 247 "about 1328").
- 1328: Ballala at Unnamale/Tiruvannamalai (DB 11); an inscription of 1328 lists the princes and ministers
  "in the residence of the city of Unnamale, ruling the kingdom in peace" (EC XI Cd 4, quoted COELHO p. 247).
- 1329: residence at Virupakshapura / Hosavidu / Hosadurga (Ht 43) (RICE 1909 p. 107).
- 1340: anointing of his son Vira-Virupaksha-Ballala (Bn 111; Cm 105) (RICE 1909 p. 108).
- 1341: pillar of victory at Setu (Mr 82) (RICE 1909 p. 108).
- 1342 (8 Sept): "fell fighting against the Turukas in a battle at Beribi" (Kd 75) (RICE 1909 p. 108).
- 1343 (28 June): Ballala IV "obtained the crown" (Cm 105) (COELHO p. 250). 1346: last inscription (Bn 120).
- 1336: Vijayanagara founded "immediately on the disappearance of the Hoysalas" (RICE 1909 p. 110) —
  `scholarship`; Derrett sees Harihara's relation to Ballala as "a bird of prey towards a dying animal"
  and dates the swallowing-up of the Hoysala-nad to 1344–46 (DERRETT, "The collapse").

## 2. Primary sources: what survives, where to read, licence

### Inscriptions (the backbone; several thousand Hoysala records)
- Distribution: "from Tanjore in the south to Sholapur in the north, and from Coorg in the west to the east
  coast in South Arcot ... mostly on prepared slabs of black hornblende ... no space is left for the insertion of
  a single additional letter" (RICE 1909 pp. 95–96). `scholarship`
- EC V (Hassan district, 1902) — Belur and Halebid; "the great bulk of the inscriptions in this volume are
  Hoysala" (EC V intro p. ix). Contains Bl 58 (1117 Belur foundation, Sanskrit + Kannada), Bl 71 (copper plate
  twin), Bl 171 (Hale-Belavadi, earliest telling of the Sala legend per Rice), Bl 147 (Ketamalla 1121),
  Bl 124 (1133), Bl 112 (Lakshma's self-sacrifice 1220), Bl 164–165 (Belavadi 1276), Bl 38–51, 69 (sculptor
  signatures at Belur), Bl 289 (Kedaroja/Kalidasi at Halebid). Public domain.
- EC II (Shravanabelagola) — 1889 Rice ed. and 1923 Narasimhachar revised ed. Contains Shantala's records
  (Nos. 131, 132, 143), Gangaraja's (73, 118, 125, 240, 384, 397), Hulla's (137, 138 etc.). Public domain.
- Other EC volumes: EC III (Mysore district: Talakad, Tonnur, Melkote), EC IV, VI (Kadur: Cm 96, Kd 75
  Ballala III's death, Cm 105), VII (Shimoga), IX (Bangalore: Bn 111, 120), X (Kolar), XI (Chitaldroog: Cd 4),
  XII (Tumkur). All on archive.org (see search list: identifiers ec-05-1-1902, india.history.resource.93133
  (vol. III), epigraphia_carnatica_vol9_myso, etc.). Public domain.
- South Indian Inscriptions and Madras Epigraphical Reports for the Tamil-country records of Someshvara,
  Ramanatha and Ballala III (Kannanur = Vikramapura, Srirangam, Tiruvannamalai). Referenced by Rice and Derrett;
  not read for this sheet.
- New find (reported 8 Aug 2026 by The Hindu via Drishti IAS): a veeragallu at Neeragunda village,
  erected by Maravve for her husband Boppa who died "shortly after King Vishnuvardhana died at Bankapura", dated
  Durmathi samvatsara, Pushya ekadashi = 11 Dec 1141 (Drishti summary at
  https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/veeragallu-discovery-sheds-light-on-ruler-vishnuvardhanas-death).
  `inscription` but freshly reported and unpublished — treat as provisional.

### Copper plates and seals
- Bl 71 copper plates duplicate Bl 58 (EC V intro p. xiii n.). Seals of some copper plates show "a dead tiger and
  the rod" (as in Bn 6) (RICE 1909 p. 95). `inscription`

- Gold coins of Vishnuvardhana carry the reverse legends *Talakadu-gonda* and *Nonambavadi-gonda* (RICE 1909

### Persian chronicles (for 1310–11)

## 3. Candidate stories

### Story A — "The Skin on the Wall": Ballala III, the last emperor (ಮೂರನೇ ಬಲ್ಲಾಳ)
People: Vira Ballala III (b. c. 1262, crowned 31 Jan 1292); his son Vira-Virupaksha-Ballala (ವೀರ ವಿರೂಪಾಕ್ಷ ಬಲ್ಲಾಳ);
Malik Kafur (ಮಲಿಕ್ ಕಾಫೂರ್); Amir Khusrau (ಅಮೀರ್ ಖುಸ್ರೋ) as chronicler; Sultan Ghiyath-ud-din Damghani of
Madura (ಘಿಯಾಸುದ್ದೀನ್); his nephew Nasir-ud-din; Ibn Battuta (ಇಬ್ನ್ ಬತೂತ); the ministers Ballappa-dannayaka and
Singeya-dannayaka; Harihara of Vijayanagara (ಹರಿಹರ/ಹಕ್ಕ).

Arc (beats):
1. 1292–1310: a long, competent reign reunites the two Hoysala halves; 1301 he issues orders in Kolar "resumed
   from Ramanatha's kingdom" (Bn 51); 1305 marches against the Seuna "who was desirous of capturing him" (Sa 156)
   (RICE 1909 p. 107). `inscription`
2. Feb 1311: Kafur's picked cavalry arrive at "the fort of Dhur Samund after a difficult march of twelve days over
   the hills and valleys, and through thorny forests" (5 Shawwal 710). Ballala, away raiding the quarrelling
   Pandya brothers' "two empty cities", has hurried home (E&D III pp. 88–89). `inscription` (contemporary chronicle)
3. The envoy Balak Deo Naik is sent to the royal canopy: "your slave Billal Deo is ready to swear allegiance ...
   If you desire horses like demons, and elephants like afrits ... they are all present ... The fort is the fort of
   the king; take it." Kafur answers: Islam, or tribute as a zimmi, or death. "The Rai ... said he was ready to give
   up all he possessed, except his sacred thread." (E&D III p. 89). `inscription`
4. Sunday: Ballala "descended from his fortress ... and rubbed the forehead of subjection on the ground", then
   "was engaged all night in taking [his treasures] out" (E&D III p. 89). Barani: "Thirty-six elephants, and all
   the treasures of the place, fell into the hands of the victors" (E&D III p. 203). Kannada side: "the Turukas
   marched against Dorasamudra" (Hn 51, 52); the prince taken as hostage is restored 1313 (Sh 68) (RICE 1909 p. 107).
5. 1316 the capital is rebuilt (Md 100); 1326/27 a Tughluq army "completely demolished the city" (RICE 1909
   p. 107). The old king withdraws to Tondanur, then to Tiruvannamalai "among the mountains" where a 1328 record
   lists his princes and ministers around him (EC XI Cd 4, COELHO p. 247). He founds a new seat
   Virupakshapura/Hosavidu/Hosadurga (1329, Ht 43) which Heras and Coelho identify with the site of Vijayanagara
   (COELHO pp. 247–248 — `scholarship`, disputed).
6. 1340 he anoints his son (Bn 111; Cm 105); 1341 plants a pillar of victory at Setu/Rameswaram (Mr 82)
   (RICE 1909 p. 108). Ibn Battuta: the Hoysala army numbers 100,000 with 20,000 Muslim mercenaries ("scoundrels and
   criminals and run-away slaves") against 6,000 at Madura; Ballala routs them near Kubban (= Kannanur-Koppam) and
   besieges it ten months (REHLA pp. 228–229). `inscription` (near-contemporary witness)
7. The Friday sermon: the Sultan reads the garrison's letter to the people, who cry "to perish under the sword is
   much better for us"; next day they "pulled off their turbans from their heads and placed them on their horses'
   necks in token of their determination to die"; 300 picked men in the van, the Sultan in the centre with 3,000,
   3,000 in the rear. They strike "at the siesta-time while their soldiers were off guard and their horses had been
   sent out to pasture"; the Hoysalas mistake them for thieves (REHLA p. 229).
8. "Their raja attempted to mount a horse although he was eighty years of age. But he was captured by
   Nasir-ud-din, nephew of Sultan Ghiyas-ud-din ... not recognizing the raja, [he] intended to kill him. But one of
   his servants said to him, 'He is the raja.'" The Sultan "treated him apparently with respect until he had
   wrested from him his riches, his elephants and his horses while promising to release him. After he had stripped
   him of all his possessions he slaughtered and skinned him." (REHLA p. 229)

Goosebump moment: "His skin was then filled with straw and was hung up on the wall of Madura where I saw it
suspended." — Ibn Battuta, REHLA p. 229 (Defrémery IV pp. 195–196). The one line in all of Hoysala history
spoken by a man who was standing there. Pair it with the Kannada epitaph: Kd 75 says he "fell fighting against the
Turukas in a battle at Beribi on the 8th of September 1342" (RICE 1909 p. 108) — the home record gives him a
soldier's death; the foreign witness gives the truth of the wall.

Dramatised vs recorded: Recorded — every beat above. Dramatised — the king's inner life, the dialogue in the Belur
or Tiruvannamalai court, the relationship with the minister Ballappa (whom Derrett calls "a fully-fledged
king-maker ... biding his time" to transfer allegiance to Harihara — `scholarship`), Harihara's ambitions, the
reaction of the son. Do not dramatise a "ransom": the sources say he was promised release and then killed.
Date of death is DISPUTED (Kd 75 says 8 Sept 1342; Derrett argues for May–Aug 1343 because records in Ballala's
name continue into 1343; Ballala IV "obtained the crown" 28 June 1343, Cm 105).

Why it grips adults: a real king at eighty on a horse; the mercenaries fighting against their own co-religionists;
the betrayal after a promise; the son's hollow coronation; the servant Harihara who inherits everything. It is the
hinge between medieval Karnataka and Vijayanagara.

### Story B — "Poy, Sala!": the tiger and the making of an emblem (ಸಳ)
People: Sala (ಸಳ); the Jain muni Sudatta (per SB 28) / "Vardhamana-munindra" (Nr 46); the goddess Vasantika
(ವಸಂತಿಕಾದೇವಿ) of Sosevur/Sasakapura (ಸೊಸೆವೂರು, now Angadi, ಅಂಗಡಿ); Vishnuvardhana as the king who makes the
legend official; the sculptors who carve it at every doorway.

Arc:
1. 1006–1047: an obscure hill chief "Poysala" in a Chola-period inscription (TN 44); Kongalva enemies call him
   "the base (manda) Poysala" (Ag 76 of 1026) (RICE 1909 p. 95; EC V intro p. x). `inscription`
2. The hillmen's title: *Maleparol-ganda*, "champion among the Malepas or hill chiefs" (RICE 1909 p. 96) —
   the Hoysalas are of "indigenous origin" (p. 94). Vinayaditya "lays his sword" on arrogant Malepas and "his hand"
   on the fearful ones (EC II No. 132, tr. p. 59).
3. 1116: Talakad falls and the Chola tiger is beaten; 1117: Belur 58 is carved. Its Sanskrit opening, after
   the lunar genealogy, says a sage in a forest said to king Sala, of a fierce tiger, "poy Sala" (strike, Sala),
   "and from this circumstance he adopted the sage's words as his name, as also the tiger crest" (Bl 58 text, EC V
   p. 57: "kaschid vane muni-varena Salah karalat sardulakam prati hi poy Sala ity ato'bhut ... Poysala
   dvipi-lanchhanah"; same verse in EC II No. 132, tr. p. 58). `inscription` — the earliest DATED telling.
4. The variants multiply: Bl 171 (Hale-Belavadi; "the earliest" per Rice) — Sala hunting on the Sahya slopes sees
   a hare chasing a tiger, says "this is heroic soil", a rishi cries "poy Sala", and "before it could step a span
   (gen), Sala slew it with his dagger (gen)". Hn 66: the muni wanted to test Sala's bravery. Ak 71: the yogi was
   performing rites to bring Vasantika under his power; she sprang forth as a tiger to break the spell; the weapon
   is the ascetic's cane (betta/sele). Ak 108: the "rod of the yogi's fan or whisk" of peacock feathers. Bl 74: a
   salaki (iron rod) (EC V intro pp. ix–x). `inscription`
5. The emblem: "their crest on temples exhibits a free standing group of Sala stabbing the tiger ... while the seal
   of copper-plate grants shows a dead tiger and the rod" (RICE 1909 p. 95). Coins carry it too (EC V intro p. x).
6. The capital's later memory: a Vijayanagara record (Mg 25) has Bukka-Raya's chief councillor "unwilling to give
   up Sosavur ... the birthplace of the Hoysalas"; by 1539 it is renamed Angadi (RICE 1909 p. 110).

Goosebump moment: the realisation (scholarship, not inscription) that the tiger is the Chola emblem and the
story is first carved the year after Talakad: "the legend may have arisen or gained popularity after this event"
(Foekema 1996 p. 67; Kamath 2001 p. 123, via Wikipedia). Rests on Bl 58 (1117) and Sastri/Settar.

Dramatised vs recorded: Sala's life is `legend` entirely ("Of the time of Sala no records have been found", RICE
1909 p. 95). Everything about Vishnuvardhana's court choosing to carve it is `inscription`. A writer can honestly
frame it as a court inventing its own origin. Avoid the "lion" (CSMVS museum label calls it a lion; the inscriptions
say tiger/puli, EC V p. ix). The CSMVS claim that a Hassan record of 1149 is "the earliest inscriptional record" is
contradicted by Bl 58 of 1117.

Why it grips: a myth being manufactured in real time, and the men (sculptors, poets) paid to do it.

### Story C — "A rutting elephant to co-wives": Shantala (ಶಾಂತಲಾದೇವಿ) and the vow at Shivagange
People: Shantala-devi; her Saiva father Pergede Marasingayya (ಮಾರಸಿಂಗಯ್ಯ); her Jain mother Machikabbe (ಮಾಚಿಕಬ್ಬೆ);
her guru Prabhachandra-siddhanta-deva (ಪ್ರಭಾಚಂದ್ರ); her uncle Pergede Singimayya; King Vishnuvardhana; the
co-wives; the inscription's writer Bokimayya and engraver Kamvachari.

Arc:
1. Born at Balipura/Balligavi (Bl 58 says "born in Balipura", EC V intro p. xiii) to a mixed household: father a
   Saiva, mother "a devoted Jaina" (EC II 1923 intro p. 56). `inscription`
2. Bl 16: "Santala-Devi with the approval of king Vishnu assumed the crown (pattamam dhariyisidalu)"
   (EC V intro p. xiii). In 1121 she sits with the king and the five ministers when a grant is made (Cn 266) —
   a queen in council (EC V intro p. xiii). Derrett: she "was present" with the king on campaign in the far west
   (DERRETT, chapter on Vishnuvardhana's early campaigns; page not captured from the OCR — `scholarship`).
3. Her epithets, 1123 (EC II No. 132): "a Brihaspati in discrimination, a Vachaspati in ready wit, the cause of the
   elevation of the four samayas or creeds, expert in singing instrumental music and dancing, a rampart to the Jaina
   faith, and a rutting elephant to ill-mannered co-wives" (EC II 1923 intro p. 55; tr. p. 60). She founds the
   Savatigandhavarana basti — the temple is NAMED after the co-wife insult (p. 55). `inscription`
4. 1128 (EC II No. 131): she has the image of Santi-Jinendra made; the verse plays on her "defects": "crookedness
   (otherwise skill) in speech, fickleness (otherwise lustre) in the eyes ... and (yet) convert the defects themselves
   into charms. O Santala-devi, which poet on earth is able to describe adequately the wealth of your beauty?"
   (tr. p. 58). `inscription`
5. The king's turn to Vishnu (Belur 1117 is "dedicated to Vijaya-Narayana", Bl 58) while she remains "pure in Jaina
   faith" (No. 143, tr. p. 72). `inscription`. The 1133 birth of the heir Narasimha to the second queen Lakkuma/
   Lakshmi-Devi (Bl 124; RICE 1909 p. 101). `inscription`
6. "Saka-varsham 1050 ... Virodhikrit-samvatsarada Chaitra-suddha-panchami-Somavarad andu Sivagangeya tirthadalu
   mudipi svarggatey adalu" — "(On the date specified), she ended her life at the holy place Sivagange and attained
   heaven" (EC II No. 143 (53), Kannada text section and translation p. 73). The editor: "the inscription throws no
   light as to why Santaladevi went to Shivaganga", a Saiva tirtha thirty miles NW of Bangalore (intro p. 56).
7. "'The queen has attained the state of the gods; I cannot remain (behind)', thus saying, her mother ... Machikabbe
   came to Belugola, and, adopting severe sanyasana ... fasting cheerfully for one month, easily attained the state
   of the gods by samadhi in the presence of all the blessed" — before her guru Prabhachandra, Vardhamana and
   Ravichandra (tr. pp. 73–74). The father too "went with joy to the world of gods" (p. 73). `inscription`

Goosebump moment: the mother's line "I cannot remain behind" and the month-long fast, carved on Chandragiri
(EC II No. 143). Rests on the inscription itself.

Dramatised vs recorded: Recorded — all the above. NOT recorded — that Shantala herself performed sallekhana
(the record says only she "ended her life" (mudipi) at Shivagange; modern pages call it sallekhana; Wikipedia
hedges "traditionally"); any grief over a dead son or the husband's conversion (Wikipedia flags these as "local
tradition, as recorded in colonial-era gazetteers and Jain community histories", uncited); the "Natyarani" dancer
persona — the inscriptional basis is one epithet, "expert in singing, instrumental music and dancing" (No. 132,
143); the claim that she is the model of the Belur madanikas / Darpana Sundari — no inscription says so; the
earliest literary elaboration found is K. V. Iyer's Kannada novel *Shantala* (20th c.; year UNVERIFIED — Wikipedia
"K. V. Iyer" lists the title without date). Mark all of this `legend`.

Why it grips: a Jain queen, a Vaishnava-convert king, a Saiva father, a Jain mother; death at a Saiva shrine; a
temple named after a slur; a mother who follows her daughter. Three religions in one family, all documented.

### Story D — "Talakadu-gonda": Vishnuvardhana, Gangaraja and the year 1116 (ಬಿಟ್ಟಿದೇವ, ಗಂಗರಾಜ)
People: Bitti-Deva/Vishnuvardhana; the general Ganga-Raja (ಗಂಗರಾಜ), son of Echi and Pochikabbe, grandson of a
Ganga-descended line; his wife Lakshmi; the Chola samanta Adiyama/Damodara (ಅಡಿಯಮ) at Talakad; the Chalukya
overlord Vikramaditya VI and his "twelve samantas" at Kannegal; Hattagara Kete-Nayaka; the Vaishnava teacher
Ramanuja (ರಾಮಾನುಜ) in the background.

Arc:
1. c. 1100–1111: Bitti-Deva rules jointly with his elder brother Ballala I (Kd 164 of 1100); together the brothers
   repulse the Santara Jagaddeva's attack on Dorasamudra, "capturing his treasury and the central ornament of his
   necklace" (Bl 58, Ng 30) (RICE 1909 pp. 98–99). `inscription`
2. The Gangas' old capital Talakad has been Chola since 1004; the Hoysalas are Chalukya feudatories "but the bond
   was a loose and friendly one" (RICE 1909 p. 96).
3. 1116: "Seizing Talakadu, taking possession similarly of Kongu ... sending Narasinga to the abode of Yama — the
   general Ganga took Ganga-mandala and made it subject to the orders of king Vishnu. When Chola's samanta Adiyama,
   stationed in the camp of Talakadu, refused to surrender the nadu which Chola had given, saying 'Fight and take it',
   Ganga-Raja marched against him ... Is not Dama who, while the point of your sharp sword was lifting up the skin of
   his back, fled in the direction of Kanchi enough?" (EC II 1923 intro pp. 51–52, from Nos. 73, 125, 240, 384, 397).
   Cn 209: Adiyama, terrified by the sound of Kete-Nayaka's shield, "crossing the river from the south side, fled,
   and at the same moment Kete-Nayaka entered the fort of Talakadu behind king Vishnuvardhana" (EC V intro p. x n.).
   `inscription`
4. The same year a night attack: Ganga-Raja falls on the Chalukya army "encamped at Kannegal under the command
   of twelve samantas ... capturing all their stores and vehicles, which he presented to his own lord" (EC II intro
   p. 45; Nos. 73 (59) of 1118, 125 (45)). By these he "caused Vishnuvardhana ... to stand erect, that is, enabled
   him to assume independence" (SB 90; RICE 1909 p. 99). `inscription`
5. Bl 58 (1117) boasts the horror: he "burnt the chief city of the Gangas, polluted the water of the Kaveri by
   throwing the corpses of the enemy into it, so that Rajendra-Chola was driven to use the wells ... and delayed the
   setting in of the south wind, because it was stopped by filling the nostrils of the skulls of his enemies slain on
   the banks of the Kaveri. Such was the terror he created that even Yama was afraid to straighten his mustaches"
   (EC V intro pp. x–xi). `inscription` (court hyperbole, but recorded)
6. 1117: Belur consecrated to Vijaya-Narayana (Bl 58/71); Rice says six Narayana temples were raised, including
   Kirti-Narayana at Talakad (EC V intro p. xxxvi). Coins struck "Talakadu-gonda" (RICE 1909 p. 101). `inscription`
7. Gangaraja's reward: he is said to have donated Govindapadi village for Gommatesvara and the Kambadahalli
   basadi (Wikipedia "Ganggaraj", citing Settar — `scholarship`, inscription number not checked); what the EC II
   editor records is that he founded Jinanathapura at Belgola, held titles including "a purna-kumbha for the coronation of king Vishnuvardhana" and
   *drohagharatta* ("grindstone to traitors"), and that his father's guru was Kanakanandi (EC II No. 118 (44) of
   1120, intro p. 51). He is "a hundred-fold more fortunate than the former Raya of the Gangas, that is,
   Chamunda-Raya" (intro p. 46). `inscription`

Goosebump moment: the Jain general handing the recaptured Ganga homeland to a king who has just become a
Vaishnava — and then being praised, in a Jain temple, as the "purna-kumbha for the coronation". Rests on EC II
Nos. 73/118/125.

Dramatised vs recorded: the Ramanuja conversion is NOT in any Hoysala inscription (see §5); Rice 1909 p. 99 accepts
it "before 1116"; Derrett (note 3 to ch. 3) says the core tradition "is in no sense contrary to possibility or even
probability" but the embroidery (curing the daughter, grinding Jains in oil-mills, the "Bitti-deva before /
Vishnuvardhana after" story) "cannot now be substantiated by evidence, and have about them an improbable ring";
an inscription of 1098 (MAR 1944 no. 34) already names Vishnuvardhana-Hoysala. Keep Ramanuja off-stage or label
him legend.

Why it grips: a war story with a named enemy, a river of corpses, a night raid, a general's loyalty, and the
temple at Belur as the trophy.

### Story E — "A thousand Garudas": Ballala II (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ಬಲ್ಲಾಳ), Soratur and the men who died with him
People: Vira Ballala II; the Seuna Bhillama and Jaitugi; the Pandya Kama-Deva of Uchchangi; minister
Tantrapala-Hemmadi; general Bettarasa in Coorg; queens Uma-devi and Padmala-mahadevi; daughter Sovala-devi;
the poet Janna (ಜನ್ನ) at his court; prince Lakshma (ಲಕ್ಷ್ಮ) and his thousand Garudas.

Arc:
1. Bl 86: a royal progress "through the hill countries in the west" during his father's lifetime; Tantrapala-Hemmadi
   claims to have won him the crown (RICE 1909 p. 102). Crowned 22 July 1173.
2. 1174: Bettarasa sent against the Changalva at Palpare in south Coorg — "the earliest specific mention ... of the
   Kodagas" (Hs 20) (p. 102).
3. Before 1177: Uchchangi, which the Cholas besieged for twelve years, taken "easily"; Kama-Deva restored (Ck 36);
   titles Giridurgamalla ("wrestler of hill-forts") and Sanivara-siddhi (p. 102).
4. Soratur near Gadag: the Seuna came "with as many as 200,000 infantry (says Dg 25), armed with thunderbolts, and
   12,000 cavalry, conspicuous with high saddles and jewelled breastplates, Ballala-Raya on his one elephant charged
   the Seuna king's army ... and slaughtered them all the way from Soratur to the bank of the Krishnaveni". Bl 77:
   "he moistened his sword with the blood of the Pandya king, whetted it on the grindstone the head of Bhillama, and
   sheathed it in the mouth of Jaitugi" (p. 103). All before 1190 (Cn 179). `inscription`. Date disputed:
   Wikipedia (Sen 1999) c. 1191; Rice "before 1190".
5. 1193 at Lakkundi (Sk 105); "sole ruler of the Seven-and-a-half Lakh country (Ci 64), and styled emperor of the
   South (Sb 140)"; residence at Hallavur = Vijayasamudram on the Tungabhadra, 1200–1211 (p. 103). The Seuna titles
   *Yadava-Narayana*, *bhujabala-pratapa-chakravartti* "were appropriated by the Hoysalas on the latter defeating
   them" (p. 108).
6. The temples: Kedaresvara at Halebid built by Ballala II "and his junior wife Abhinava-Ketala-Devi ... about 1219"
   (EC V intro p. xxxviii); Amritesvara at Amritapura c. 1196 with the first Mallitamma signatures; Veeranarayana at
   Belavadi c. 1200 (Foekema 1996 via Wikipedia). `inscription`/`scholarship`
7. 1220: "being of full age, Ballala established Narasimha in the kingdom and went to heaven (Cn 211)". "Coincident
   with the king's death was the self-sacrifice of the prince Lakshma, recorded on a pillar by the side of the
   Hoysalesvara temple at Halebid (Bl 112). He was of royal blood, and perhaps a half-brother. He and a thousand
   warriors had vowed themselves, as Garudas, to live and die with the king, and at his death took their own lives"
   (RICE 1909 p. 104). Bl 112 adds that "images of himself and of Garuda were equally engraved" on the vira-sasana
   "as evidence that Garuda alone was his equal in fidelity to his master ... on the pillar they became united with
   Lakshmi and with Garuda" (EC V intro p. xviii). `inscription`

Goosebump moment: the Garuda pillar at Halebid — a thousand sworn men following a king into death, 1220. Rests on
Bl 112. (Compare Chokimayya calling himself "king Vishnu's Garuda", Hn 69, under Narasimha I, RICE p. 101.)

Dramatised vs recorded: the Garuda institution is recorded but its mechanics (how, where, whether all thousand
really died) are not; Soratur's numbers are court boasts; the Janna–Ballala relationship rests on literary
tradition (Narasimhacharya 1988), not inscription.

Why it grips: the Hoysala apex, a berserker charge "on his one elephant", and a suicide cult of bodyguards.

## 4. Little-known facts (one line + source + tier)

1. The six letters "Ra-kka-sa Po-ysa-la" were inscribed on Vinayaditya's flag, perhaps linking him to the Ganga
   king Rakkasa (RICE 1909 p. 98). `inscription`
2. Ballala I married the three daughters of Mariyane-dandanayaka on a single day in 1103 (RICE 1909 p. 99).
   `inscription`
3. The Hoysala general Punisa "frightened the Todavar" — the earliest mention of the Todas of the Nilgiris as a
   settled tribe (Ch 83 of 1117; RICE 1909 p. 100). `inscription`
4. Vishnuvardhana's Belur record claims he stopped the south wind by stuffing the nostrils of enemy skulls on
   the Kaveri bank (Bl 58; EC V intro pp. x–xi). `inscription`
5. Narasimha I's harem held "three hundred and eighty-four well-born women" (Bl 193; RICE 1909 p. 102). `inscription`
6. Ballala II's daughter Sovala-devi founded an agrahara "equal to Valabhi" at Haranhalli (Ak 123; RICE 1909 p. 103
   n.). `inscription`
7. Narasimha II vowed "the trumpet should not sound" until he had freed the Chola king from the Kadava Perunjinga,
   and did so, earning the title "establisher of the Chola kingdom" (Gb 45; RICE 1909 p. 105). `inscription`
8. The Hoysala Someshvara made a gift at Pandharpur in Saka 1159/1236 — a Hoysala record near Sholapur (Bombay
   Arch. Rep. 1897–8, cited RICE 1909 p. 105 n.). `inscription`
9. Narasimha III, aged about 15, went to the Vijaya-Parsva Jain temple at Halebid "and read the genealogy of his
   line as recorded in the inscription there" (Bl 124; RICE 1909 p. 106). `inscription`
10. Khusrau puns that the Hoysala capital's name rhymes with "samandar", a salamander (E&D III p. 88 n. 6).
    `inscription`
11. Ballala III fielded 20,000 Muslim mercenaries against the Madura Sultan (REHLA p. 228). `inscription`
12. The Belur temple's wooden tower had "rotted and fallen" by 1298 and was propped with pillars (Bl 24); the
    gopura burnt by "the Musalman army of Kalburga under Ganga-Salar" was rebuilt with seven storeys in 1397
    (Bl 8) (EC V intro p. xxxvii). `inscription`
13. The sculptor Dasoja of Balligrama also signed a standing Keshava now in the Metropolitan Museum, New York
    (acc. 18.41, "Standing Vishnu as Keshava", https://metmuseum.org/toah/works-of-art/18.41; the collection page
    returned HTTP 429 during this pass — details UNVERIFIED beyond the search snippet). `scholarship`
14. A Hoysala copper-plate seal shows the tiger dead with the ascetic's rod beside it (Bn 6; RICE 1909 p. 95).
    `inscription`

## 5. Disputed or uncertain points

- Vishnuvardhana's death: Rice 1909 p. 101: died 1141 at Bankapura (Cm 96), "his body being conveyed to Sosavur";
  Sastri, S. K. Aiyangar, Desai: c. 1152; Kamath: earlier, citing the Yalladahalli record c. 1145 naming Narasimha
  as king (Wikipedia "Vishnuvardhana"). The 2026 Neeragunda veeragallu (11 Dec 1141) supports Rice, if authenticated.
- Vishnuvardhana's accession: Rice gives no year (earliest sure date 1111); Wikipedia 1108.
- The Ramanuja conversion: no Hoysala inscription records it. Rice (1909 p. 99) accepts a conversion "before
  1116" under Ramanuja "who had fled from persecution by the Chola king"; Derrett (1957, note 3 to ch. 3) accepts
  the possibility but rejects the embroidery (daughter's cure, destruction of Jains, the name-change story) and
  cites a 1098 inscription (MAR 1944 no. 34) already using the name Vishnuvardhana. The Ramanuja hagiographies
  (Guruparampara-prabhavam, Yatiraja-vaibhavam) are centuries later (Wikipedia "Ramanuja", citing Raman 2003, Carman
  1974). EC V intro p. xxxvi speaks of the Belur temple being built "on his renouncing the Jain faith for that
  of Vishnu" — that is Rice's inference from the Vijaya-Narayana dedication, not a statement in Bl 58 (whose
  text I read only in part). Settar (1989 pp.
  152–155) stresses Shantala and the family remained Jain. → `legend` for the conversion narrative; `inscription`
  only that Belur was dedicated to Vijaya-Narayana in 1117 and that Jain foundations continued under him.
- Which inscription first tells the Sala legend: Bl 58 of 1117 (dated) contains it in Sanskrit; Rice calls Bl 171
  (Hale-Belavadi, undated in the OCR read; mentions Talakad so post-1116) "the earliest" version (EC V intro p. ix).
  Van der Geer 2008 p. 357 (via Wikipedia) says it "appears in the Belur inscription of c. 1117"; CSMVS museum
  label says 1149 Hassan is earliest — wrong on the evidence of Bl 58.
- Tiger vs lion: inscriptions say puli/sardula (tiger); some museum labels say lion.
- Shantala's death: date in No. 143 reads Saka 1050 with cyclic year Virodhikrit; editors render 1131 (Virodhikrit
  = 1131/32; Saka 1050 expired would be 1128/29) — note the internal tension; Rice and Narasimhachar both print 1131.
  Manner: "ended her life" (mudipi) at Sivagange; sallekhana is an inference. Reason: "throws no light" (EC II intro
  p. 56).
- Shantala as dancer/"Natyarani" and as model for the madanikas: `legend`, 20th-century elaboration.
- Hoysalesvara temple's founder: Bl 147 (1121) records Ketamalla's Vishnuvardhana-Poysalesvara at Dorasamudra,
  but Rice could not identify it with the great temple; Bl 289 proves the present temple no later than
  Narasimha I (c. 1141) (EC V intro p. xxxvii). Wikipedia says built 1121–1160 with Ketamalla and Kesarasetti.
- Someshvara's death: Rice 1254 (natural, kingdom divided); Wikipedia/Sastri 1262/63 killed by Jatavarman
  Sundara Pandya at Kannanur. Rice has Narasimha III's accession in 1254.
- Soratur: Rice "before 1190"; Sen 1999 c. 1191; Wikipedia "Bhillama V" has Bhillama winning first c. 1189.
  Rice: "all these successes being accomplished before 1190 (Cn 179)".
- 1311 tribute figures: E&D's Khusrau says the envoys came "accompanied by six elephants" (p. 89); Barani says
  thirty-six (p. 203); Saksena 1992 p. 414 (via Wikipedia) says thirty-six. Barani's grand total for the whole
  campaign: 612 elephants, 96,000 mans of gold, 20,000 horses (p. 204) — includes Ma'bar.
- Who went to Delhi: Rice reads Sh 68 as the king's son, taken as hostage, restored 1313; Lal 1950 p. 214 reads an
  inscription as Ballala himself "returned from Delhi on 6 May 1313"; Isami's robe/crown/million tankas unverified
  by Khusrau.
- Second sack: Rice 1326; Coelho "about 1328"; Wikipedia c. 1327.
- Virupakshapura/Hosadurga = Vijayanagara? Rice: Hosadurga in Chitaldroog; Heras and Coelho: the Vijayanagara
  site (COELHO pp. 247–248). Ferishta's "Beejanuggur named after his son Beeja" is a garbling (Coelho).
- Ballala III's death date: 8 Sept 1342 (Kd 75) vs Derrett's May–Aug 1343 (records in Ballala's name in 1343;
  Ibn Battuta's visit 1343; the son's crown on 28 June 1343). Place: Kd 75 says "Beribi"; Derrett discusses
  Tiruchirappalli (Chirichi-palli); Ibn Battuta places the capture near Kubban/Kannanur.
- Ballala IV's end: Chopra et al. 2003 p. 156 (via Wikipedia) say he "suffered the same fate" c. 1346 — no primary
  source found; Derrett only says the last Hoysala inscription is probably April 1346. UNVERIFIED.
- Janna's patron: Narasimha I "and possibly" Ballala II (Wikipedia, Narasimhacharya 1988); Wikipedia "Veera Ballala
  II" says court poet of Ballala II, title Kavichakravarti. Harihara: "little evidence that locates him in the
  court of a specific ruler" (Ben-Herut 2018 via Wikipedia). Raghavanka's Kakatiya patronage: low-quality source
  only — UNVERIFIED.

Dress, arms, animals (take from the friezes and madanikas, not from later Vijayanagara or Mysore paintings):
- Men: bare torso, dhoti, heavy ear-rings, tall crowns/kirita for royals, sacred thread (Ballala III "except his
  sacred thread", E&D III p. 89); swords straight and leaf-bladed, daggers (gen), spears, round shields
  (Kete-Nayaka's "glittering shield", Cn 209), bows; "high saddles and jewelled breastplates" on the Seuna cavalry
  (Dg 25); the ascetic's bamboo danda with peacock-feather whisk (EC V intro p. ix).
- Women: madanika coiffures, jewelled girdles, bare-breasted with ornament (as sculpted); the queen with a
  patta (crown) — Bl 16.
- Animals: war elephants (Ballala "on his one elephant" at Soratur); Kambhoja horses (Bl 58: "the tramp of his
  Kambhoja horse"); horse trade through Ma'bar ports (Wassaf, E&D III pp. 32–34).
- Royal paraphernalia: white umbrella (sveta-chhatra) "with a thousand petals" (Bl 58), the "band of five great
  instruments" (pancha-maha-sabda), the tiger crest; the Delhi "royal canopy" (chatr) before which the envoys bow
  (E&D III p. 89).
- Capitals: Dorasamudra with its great tank (the "sea" in the name — Khusrau: "the fort ... strong and
  surrounded by water", Saksena via Wikipedia); Belur = Velapura; Bankapura; Kannanur = Vikramapura north of
  Srirangam; Tiruvannamalai hill; Madura walls (where the skin hung).
- Ships: Hoysalas were not a sea power; Pandya "islands in the ocean" (E&D III p. 90); avoid Hoysala navies.

Anachronisms to avoid: Vijayanagara gopuras (1397+) at Belur; the Belur central tower (rebuilt 1736, 1774,
now gone — EC V p. xxxvii); Mysore-Wodeyar turbans and jackets; Mughal-style curved talwars for Delhi troops
(1311 — use early Sultanate mail, conical helmets, composite bows); Persian minarets at Dwarasamudra (Keay rejects
the Kafur mosque, Keay 2000 p. 258 via Wikipedia); firearms of any kind; Kannada script in modern letterforms
(use the Hoysala-period round Old Kannada as on the slabs); Shantala as a stage "Bharatanatyam" dancer (no
evidence of costume or style).

Anachronism in dating "Dwarasamudra" — the Sanskritised name Dvarasamudra/Dvaravatipura is used from
Vishnuvardhana's time (Bl 147 "residence of Dorasamudra", 1121); "Halebidu" ("old camp") is the post-1340s
name (RICE 1909 p. 96).

## 7. Key people beyond the kings (one line each)

- Sala (ಸಳ): legendary founder; "poy Sala"; no records (RICE 1909 p. 95). `legend`
- Sudatta (ಸುದತ್ತ): the Jain muni of the legend per SB 28; called Vardhamana-munindra in Nr 46 (RICE 1909 p. 95 n.).
  `inscription` (as a later naming)
- Vasantika-devi (ವಸಂತಿಕಾದೇವಿ): family goddess at Sosevur, "still represented by that of Vasantamma" (RICE 1909 p. 95).
- Keleyabbarasi (ಕೆಳೆಯಬ್ಬರಸಿ): Vinayaditya's queen, mother of Ereyanga (RICE 1909 p. 98). `inscription`
- Echala-devi (ಎಚಲದೇವಿ): Ereyanga's wife, mother of Ballala I, Bitti-Deva and Udayaditya (RICE 1909 p. 98). `inscription`
- Udayaditya (ಉದಯಾದಿತ್ಯ): younger brother, died 1123 at Kellavatti (Hn 102) (EC V intro p. xiii). `inscription`
- Ganga-Raja (ಗಂಗರಾಜ): minister-general; Talakad 1116; Kannegal night attack; founder of Jinanathapura; guru
  Subhachandra; wife Lakshmi built a Jina temple; titles drohagharatta (EC II 1923 intro pp. 45–52). `inscription`
- Pochikabbe (ಪೊಚಿಕಬ್ಬೆ) and Echi: Ganga-Raja's parents; Bamma-chamupa his brother (EC II intro p. 51). `inscription`
- Boppa (ಬೊಪ್ಪ): Ganga-Raja's son, built a Jina temple 1138 (EC II intro p. 55 region). `inscription`
- Punisa (ಪುನಿಸ): general who conquered the Nilgiris and Malabar, "frightened the Todavar" (Ch 83) (RICE 1909 p. 100).
- Chama-Deva: son of the Orissa king Chola-Ganga, took Uchchangi for Vishnuvardhana (Ck 29, 30) (RICE 1909 p. 100).
- Hattagara Kete-Nayaka: entered Talakad "behind king Vishnuvardhana" (Cn 209) (EC V intro p. x n.).
- Adiyama / Damodara (ಅಡಿಯಮ): Chola governor at Talakad who "fled in the direction of Kanchi" (EC II intro p. 52).
- Shantala-devi (ಶಾಂತಲಾದೇವಿ): senior queen, patta-mahadevi; Jain; died at Sivagange 1131 (EC II No. 143).
- Marasingayya and Machikabbe (ಮಾರಸಿಂಗಯ್ಯ, ಮಾಚಿಕಬ್ಬೆ): her Saiva father and Jain mother (EC II intro p. 56).
- Prabhachandra-siddhanta-deva (ಪ್ರಭಾಚಂದ್ರ): her guru, disciple of Meghachandra-traividya (EC II No. 132).
- Lakkuma / Lakshmi-devi (ಲಕ್ಷ್ಮೀದೇವಿ): second queen, mother of Narasimha I born 1133 (RICE 1909 p. 101).
- Chandaladevi: Kongalva princess married to Vishnuvardhana per Derrett (Wikipedia "Vishnuvardhana"). `scholarship`
- Ketamalla (ಕೇತಮಲ್ಲ): built the Vishnuvardhana-Poysalesvara at Dorasamudra 1121 (Bl 147).
- Hulla (ಹುಳ್ಳ): minister of Narasimha I, "one of the foremost upholders of the Jain faith" (SB 137, 138), builder of
  the Bhandari basti (RICE 1909 p. 101; EC II intro).
- Chokimayya: general who called himself "king Vishnu's Garuda" (Hn 69) (RICE 1909 p. 101).
- Echala-devi (II): chief queen of Narasimha I, mother of Ballala II (RICE 1909 p. 102).
- Tantrapala-Hemmadi: minister who claims to have won Ballala II his crown (Bl 86) (RICE 1909 p. 102).
- Bettarasa: general in Coorg 1174 (Hs 20) (RICE 1909 p. 102).
- Uma-devi (ಉಮಾದೇವಿ), Padmala-mahadevi, Abhinava-Ketala-devi: queens of Ballala II (RICE 1909 p. 103; EC V p. xxxviii).
- Sovala-devi (ಸೋವಲದೇವಿ): Ballala II's daughter, founded Somanathapura agrahara at Haranhalli (Ak 123).
- Madhuha / Madhusudana: Ballala II's general-administrator; the poet Trivikrama revised his inscription (Ak 118
  of 1194) (EC V intro p. xviii).
- Lakshma (ಲಕ್ಷ್ಮ): prince who died with a thousand Garudas in 1220 (Bl 112) (RICE 1909 p. 104).
- Kalale-devi: queen of Narasimha II, mother of Someshvara (Cn 203) (RICE 1909 p. 105).
- Bijjala-Rani and Devala-devi: Someshvara's queens, mothers of Narasimha III and Ramanatha (RICE 1909 p. 106).
- Saluva-Tikkama: Seuna general routed at Belavadi 1276 (Bl 164, 165) (RICE 1909 p. 106).
- Vishvanatha: Ramanatha's son, briefly ruled 1295–97 (RICE 1909 p. 107).
- Ballappa-dannayaka and Singeya-dannayaka: Ballala III's great ministers at Tiruvannamalai 1328 (Cd 4, COELHO p. 247);
  Ballappa the "king-maker" (DERRETT).
- Somayya Danayaka: Ballala III's officer who walled the Belur temple (Wikipedia "Chennakeshava Temple", citing
  Krishna 1937). `scholarship`
- Chikkayi-Tayi of the Alupas: Ballala III's queen (Wikipedia, citing *A History of South Kanara* pp. 138–139).
- Vira-Virupaksha-Ballala / Hampe-Vodeyar: Ballala IV; "obtained the crown" 28 June 1343 (COELHO pp. 250–251).
- Malik Kafur (ಮಲಿಕ್ ಕಾಫೂರ್): Alauddin's general, 1311 (E&D III pp. 87–90).
- Amir Khusrau (ಅಮೀರ್ ಖುಸ್ರೋ): court poet-chronicler, *Khazainu-l Futuh* (E&D III).
- Ziyauddin Barani: chronicler, *Tarikh-i Firoz Shahi* (E&D III p. 203).
- Ghiyath-ud-din Damghani (ಘಿಯಾಸುದ್ದೀನ್): Sultan of Madura 1340–44; killed Ballala III (REHLA p. 229; Wikipedia).
- Nasir-ud-din: his nephew who captured the king (REHLA p. 229).
- Ibn Battuta (ಇಬ್ನ್ ಬತೂತ): saw the skin on the wall of Madura, 1343 (REHLA p. 229).
- Harihara I (ಹಕ್ಕ) and Bukka (ಬುಕ್ಕ): Sangama brothers, "probably subordinates of or connected with the Hoysalas"
  (RICE 1909 p. 110); Bukka at Hosapattana 1354 (COELHO p. 248).
- Ramanuja (ರಾಮಾನುಜ): Vaishnava acharya; conversion of Bitti-Deva is tradition, not inscription (DERRETT n. 3 ch. 3).
- Janna (ಜನ್ನ): Jain poet, dandanayaka, Kavichakravarti; *Yashodhara Charite* c. 1209 (Narasimhacharya 1988 pp. 15–16
  via Wikipedia). `scholarship`
- Harihara (ಹರಿಹರ): Saiva poet of Halebidu, of a karanika family; *Girijakalyana*, 108 ragales; moved to Hampi
  (Ben-Herut 2018 via Wikipedia). `scholarship`
- Raghavanka (ರಾಘವಾಂಕ): Harihara's nephew; *Harishchandra Kavya*; the slapped-for-writing-about-a-mortal story
  (`legend`); shatpadi metre (Narasimhacharya 1988 pp. 18–19).
- Nemichandra, Rudrabhatta, Keshiraja: poets of the Ballala II–Narasimha II era (Wikipedia, citing Kamath/Narasimhacharya).
- Sculptors (ರೂವಾರಿ): Dasoja of Balligrama (ದಾಸೋಜ) and his son Chavana (ಚಾವಣ) — signed madanikas at Belur (Bl 38–51,
  69; EC V intro pp. xxxvi–xxxvii); Dasoja also made the western doorway of the Hoysalesvara (p. xxxvii).
  Others at Belur: Chikka Hampa, Kaliyuga Visvakarma of Lokkigundi, Kencha Malliyanna, Kesava, Kumara Mechari,
  Mayiua, Malliyera, Nagoja of Gadugu, Padari Malloja, Poisgananar Bita (p. xxxvii, alphabetical list).
  At Halebid: Ballanna, Bechana, Chauga, Devoja, Harisha of Odeyagiri ("Belur did not agree with him"), Harisha of
  Tanagandur, Kalidasi, Kedaroja (Narasimha-Deva's sculptor, Bl 289), Ketana, Mabalaki, Machanna, Manibalaki, Masa
  son of Kanjoja, Revoja (pp. xxxvii–xxxviii). Mallitamma (ಮಲ್ಲಿತಮ್ಮ): signs "Malli"/"Ma" at Amritapura (c. 1196),
  Nuggehalli (1246), Somanathapura (1268) — "forty" monuments over six decades (Wikipedia "Hoysala architecture",
  citing Collyer 1990/Kamath); the 1196 Amritapura Mallitamma may be grandfather of the Somanathapura one (older
  survey via search, UNVERIFIED). `scholarship`

## 8. Sources to avoid

- Any page giving 1313 as the year of Ballala III's capture/death (e.g. typefully.com post) — thirty years off.
- Pages calling the Sala animal a lion, or the CSMVS label making 1149 the earliest legend (Bl 58 is 1117).
- Pages that say Shantala "performed sallekhana" with details of grief over a dead son or the king's conversion —
  no inscription supports it (EC II No. 143 says only she ended her life at Sivagange).
- Pages that make Shantala the Darpana Sundari or the "Natyarani" with dance-school details — 20th-century fiction.
- Ferishta on the Hoysalas (mosque at Halebid; "Beejanuggur named after Beeja") — garbled, see Keay and Coelho.
- Wikipedia "Veera Ballala III" infobox internal contradictions (1342 vs 1343; sidebar vs infobox) and its claim
  that Ballala IV "suffered the same fate" — unsupported.
- Wikipedia "Hoysala Kingdom" monarch list for the 10th century (Arekalla 950, Maruga 960, Munda 1006) — no source
  in Rice; the first dated Hoysala record is 1006 and the first dated king is Nripa Kama 1022.
- indianetzone / exam-prep (drishtiias, visionias) summaries — secondary at best; use only to find the primary.
- Jain-heritage sites that say Shantala's basti dates 1123 vs the Santi-Jina image 1128 without distinguishing
  Nos. 132 and 131 — fine on dates but add uncited colour.
- Lee's 1829 Ibn Batuta for the Madura passage — it is not in that abridgement.
- Any claim that the Vijayanagara site was "founded by Ballala III" as fact — Heras/Coelho hypothesis, contested.
