# Kingdom of Mysuru (ಮೈಸೂರು ಸಾಮ್ರಾಜ್ಯ), 1399–1947 — research notes

Compiled 2026-10-10 against data/history-src/research/BRIEF.md. English with Kannada names in
Kannada script. Every claim carries a source and a tier (`inscription` / `scholarship` /
`legend`). "Unverified" means I could not reach a primary or academic source for it in this
pass; a writer must not use such a line as fact.

Working method: Wikipedia was used only as a finding aid; wherever possible the claim below
was re-read in the public-domain edition itself (Wilks 1810/1817, Beatson 1800, Kirkpatrick
1811, Dirom 1793, Kirmani/Miles 1864, Bowring 1893, Aitchison 1909, Hayavadana Rao 1943, Mysore
Gazetteer 1930). Page numbers are from the OCR running headers and may be off by one.

Search budget note: the web-search quota ran out at the end of this pass; a few items (the
exact Congreve wording, Gandhi's "Rajarshi" citation, the 1916 Mysore Archaeological Report
page for the Sringeri letters) are flagged below as still needing a lookup.

## 2. Primary sources: what survives, where to read them, licence

### 2a. Kannada chronicles and court literature (Wodeyar side)
- **Kanthirava Narasaraja Vijayam** (ಕಂಠೀರವ ನರಸರಾಜ ವಿಜಯಂ), Govinda Vaidya (ಗೋವಿಂದ ವೈದ್ಯ), 1648.
  Contemporary court poem; earliest text with the Dwaraka-origin story and the earliest
  description of a Mysore Navaratri/Dasara (HR I p. 45; Pranesh 2003 p. 2 via WP). Rao uses it
  for the 1639 siege and warns of "the poet's exaggerations" (HR I p. 121 fn 28). Tier
  `inscription` (contemporary) for 1638–48 events, `legend` for origins. Printed editions:
  Mysore University/Kannada Sahitya Parishat (20th c.); no free online edition located.
- **Chikkadevaraya Vamsavali** (ಚಿಕ್ಕದೇವರಾಯ ವಂಶಾವಳಿ), Tirumalarya (ತಿರುಮಲಾರ್ಯ), c. 1678–80 —
  "the earliest available Kannada prose historical writing" (WP "Tirumalarya", Narasimhacharya
  1934). **Chikkadevaraja Vijayam** (ಚಿಕ್ಕದೇವರಾಜ ವಿಜಯಂ), 16 chapters on his conquests; also
  *Chikkadevaraja Saptapadi* (1698). Modern ed.: Kannada Sahitya Parishat 1999 (Vamsavali).
  Tier `inscription` for 1673–1700 events. HR I cites both as C.Vam./C.Vi. throughout.
- **Mysuru Dhoregala Vamsavali / Mysuru Dhoralu Purana / Mysuru Nagarada Purvottara /
  "Annals of the Mysore Royal Family"** — 18th–19th c. palace chronicles; Rao calls them
  "chronicles of a quasi-historical character" needing "extreme caution" (HR I p. 9). The
  English *Annals of the Mysore Royal Family*, Part I, Mysore 1916 is the palace's own
  version (cited by WP for Krishnaraja III's birth). Tier `legend` for pre-1600, `scholarship`
  after. Online: not located in this pass (check archive.org "Annals of the Mysore Royal Family").
- **Chikkadevaraja's own works**: *Gitagopalam*, *Chikkadevaraya Binnapam* (Sri-Vaishnava
  devotional; GTA Press 1915 ed. via WP).
- **Sritattvanidhi** (ಶ್ರೀತತ್ತ್ವನಿಧಿ) and *Sougandhika Parinaya* by Krishnaraja III (WP, K. M. George
  1994); lithograph illustrations on Commons (File:Illustration from Sougandhika Parinaya lithograph.jpg).

### 2b. Inscriptions and copper plates
- Gajjiganahalli copper-plate, April 1639 — first record naming Kanthirava as king; says the
  kings "again obtained the Karna'ta portion of the earth, to protect it" (HR I pp. 25, 117).
- Halagere copper-plate, 1663 (HR I p. 45). Chamarajanagar plate of Tirumalaiyangar, 1675
  (HR I p. 328). 1551 inscription of Timmaraja II = earliest of the family (Stein 1989 p. 82).
- All Mysore-district records are in **Epigraphia Carnatica** vol. III (Mysore district) and
  vol. IX/X — Rice's edition is public domain; archive.org has multiple scans (search
  "Epigraphia Carnatica"). Tier `inscription`.

### 2c. Persian/Mysore-side chronicles of Hyder and Tipu
- **Nishan-i-Haidari** (ನಿಶಾನ್-ಇ-ಹೈದರಿ), Mir Hussain Ali Khan Kirmani, written c. 1802; tr. Miles as
  *History of Hydur Naik* (1842) and *History of Tipu Sultan* (1864). Mysore-court eyewitness-
  era; pro-Tipu. Online: KIRMANI (above); *Hydur Naik* PDFs on Commons (File:History of Hydur
  Naik... (IA dli.csl.5091).pdf). Public domain. Tier `inscription` (near-contemporary).
- **Tipu's letters**: KIRK 1811 — c. 1,000 letters selected from a register of c. 2,000,
  Feb 1785 – Nov 1793, "rescued from the captured archives" via Lt-Col Ogg (KIRK preface).
  Kirkpatrick is openly hostile ("cruel and relentless enemy ... intolerant bigot") — quote the
  letters, not his glosses. Public domain.
- **Tipu's dream register** — found in his escritoire by Kirkpatrick (BEATSON p. 197); tr. Mahmud
  Husain, *The Dreams of Tipu Sultan* (Karachi 1957; archive.org/details/dreamsoftipusult00tipprich,
  lending only). Reference only.
- **Fath-ul-Mujahidin** (army regulations, Zain-ul-Abidin Shushtari, c. 1783); Husain's 1953
  article in *J. Pakistan Hist. Soc.* (via WP "Mysorean rockets"). Reference only.
- **Tipu's Kannada letters to the Sringeri Jagadguru** (c. 30 letters) — discovered 1916;
  published in the *Annual Report of the Mysore Archaeological Department for 1916* (R.
  Narasimhachar). archive.org item "cwda_sringeri-by-r-narasimhachar-...-1916" (CC0 upload,
  partial scan). Tier `inscription`. The page/plate numbers were not verified in this pass.
- Tipu's **Regulations** (c. 1790), Royal Collection RCIN 1005094 (catalogue only).

### 2d. British eyewitness and official sources
- BEATSON 1800 (Harris's staff officer; official narrative + 47 appendices incl. the Partition
  Treaty, Malartic's proclamation, Mornington's letters). Public domain.
- DIROM 1793 (1792 campaign; text of the preliminary and definitive treaties; hostage ceremony).
- WILKS 1810–17 (Resident 1803–08; worked from Mysore manuscripts; vol. I is the base text for
  the Wodeyar centuries; vol. III for 1792–99). Public domain.
- Mornington/Wellesley despatches; *Official documents relative to the negotiations carried on
  by Tippoo Sultaun with the French nation* (Calcutta 1799) — propaganda compilation, use with care.
- Aitchison's *Treaties* vol. IX (1909) for every treaty text 1763–1901 (AITCH IX).

### 2e. Gazetteers and later compilations (reference tier, public domain in India)
- RICE 1897 (Mysore Gazetteer, 2 vols); Rice 1877 edition also on archive.org.
- MG II.4 (1930) — modern period; Appendix E reprints the Instrument of Transfer (p. 3079).
- HR I–III (1943–48) — the standard chronicle-plus-epigraphy narrative; vol. II (1704–66) and
  vol. III (1766–99) not located online in this pass (Rare Book Society of India has vol. I PDF).
- M. Shama Rao, *Modern Mysore* (1936), archive.org/details/modernmysore — for 1799–1936.
- Annual Reports of the Mysore Archaeological Department 1916–45 — dozens on archive.org
  (identifiers in.ernet.dli.2015.*, in.gov.ignca.*).

### 2f. Coins
- Wodeyar: Kanthirava's coins "named after himself, in April 1645" (HR I p. 143) — the
  Kanthiraya fanam/pagoda; Krishnaraja III rupee struck in the name of Shah Alam II, Mahisur
  mint 1805 (Commons file in Category:Wadiyar dynasty); gold pagoda of Krishnaraja Wodeyar.
- Hyder: pagodas keep the Hara-Gauri (Shiva–Parvati) image with the Persian letter "He";
  elephant copper from 1780–81 (indian-coins.com / coinindia galleries, dealer-grade sources).
- Tipu: Hijri dates for four years, then Mauludi (AM) dates; denominations Ahmadi, Haidari,
  Imami, Zohra, Qutb etc. (sources disagree on which name is which); elephant paisa; 15 mints
  (Patan, Nagar, Bangalore, Calicut, Dharwar, Bellary ...). Commons: Category:Coins of Tipu
  Sultan (8 files). Tier `inscription`.

## 3. Candidate stories (3–5)

### Story A — "The Last Day of the Tiger" (4 May 1799)
**People**: Tipu Sultan (ಟಿಪ್ಪು ಸುಲ್ತಾನ್); Mir Sadiq (ಮೀರ್ ಸಾದಿಕ್), the Dewan accused of treachery;
Sayyid Ghaffar (ಸೈಯದ್ ಗಫಾರ್), the loyal commander killed by a cannonball; Raja Khan, the servant
who fell with him; Major-General David Baird, who had spent 44 months in Tipu's dungeon twenty
years earlier (WP siege article; needs primary check); the Killadar who identified the body by
torchlight; Purnaiah (ಪೂರ್ಣಯ್ಯ), "the only Hindoo who remained in an office of high
responsibility under Tippoo Sultaun" (BEATSON p. 64 fn).

**Arc (8 beats)**
1. 5 April: Harris's 26,000 + the Nizam's 16,000 sit down before the island fortress; the river
   is at its lowest and can be forded if the assault comes before the monsoon (BEATSON pp. 87–88).
2. 9 April: Tipu writes that he "had adhered firmly to treaties" and asks why the British have
   come (BEATSON p. 93); c. 20 April he asks to negotiate (p. 109). Wellesley's terms are
   impossible by design (BOWRING pp. 189–190).
3. 2–3 May: the Nizam's battery and the six-gun battery open a practicable breach in the
   west curtain near the NW bastion; a shot blows up a rocket magazine inside the fort
   (BEATSON pp. 122–123).
4. Morning of 4 May: "Mahomedan and Brahmin astrologers" tell Tipu the day is inauspicious. He
   gives an elephant, jewels and money to Brahmins and the poor, holds his face over an iron pot
   of oil to see his reflection, eats a little (BEATSON pp. 162–163; KIRMANI pp. 267–268).
   News comes that "the faithful and devoted Syud Ghuffar was slain"; Tipu: "we also shall soon
   depart" (KIRMANI p. 268).
5. 1.30 p.m.: Baird — "come; my brave fellows, follow me, and prove yourselves worthy the name
   of British soldiers!" (BEATSON pp. 126–127) — leads two columns through knee-deep water at
   the ditch; the breach is 100 ft wide; the forlorn hope is a serjeant and twelve men each
   side; the colours are on the summit within six minutes (pp. 127–128, 131).
6. Tipu fires from behind a traverse on the north rampart, mounts, rides for the new sally-port
   (the Hoally/Hoolay Deedy); his horse is hit, he is shot through the left breast; Raja Khan is
   shot through the leg; both fall with the horse among the dead. A grenadier seizes his sword
   belt; Tipu strikes at two soldiers with his drawn sword; a musket ball through the right
   temple kills him (BEATSON pp. 164–165). Kirmani: the postern was shut against him — "at
   length having received several mortal wounds in the face, he drank of the cup of
   Martyrdom" (KIRMANI p. 271).
7. Dusk: Baird threatens the Killadar, who says the Sultan is not in the palace but was
   wounded "in a gateway on the north face"; the gateway is choked with dead and too dark to tell
   faces; the horse and palanquin are found first; the Killadar identifies the body — shot a
   little above the right ear, the ball lodged in the left cheek, three bayonet wounds in the
   right side; "still warm, and the eyes were open" (BEATSON pp. 136–137; BOWRING p. 200).
8. 5 May, 4.30 p.m.: the body goes out of the fort with an escort of European grenadiers to the
   Lal Bagh and is laid beside Hyder; that evening a violent storm kills two officers and
   others in the Bombay camp (BEATSON pp. 148–149). Mir Sadiq is struck from his horse at the
   Ganjam gate and "his impure body was dragged into a place of filth" (KIRMANI p. 270).
   Four men are hanged before the plundering stops (BEATSON p. 168).

**Goosebump moment**: the iron pot of oil at dawn — a king who has already been told by both
his Muslim and his Brahmin astrologers that the day is bad, looking at his own reflection to
avert it, then eating a little and going to the wall anyway. Evidence: BEATSON pp. 162–163 and
KIRMANI pp. 267–268 — two hostile-to-each-other sources (British staff, Mysore courtier) agree
on the astrologers, the alms and the meal. Tier `inscription`.

**Dramatised vs recorded**: Recorded — the hours, the breach, the wounds, the Killadar, the
burial, the storm, the alms. Dramatised — any dialogue beyond the quoted lines; Mir Sadiq's
motives (Kirmani asserts, Beatson only says the Killadar's men withdrew; WP says "alleged");
the "tiger vs sheep" saying (reputed, no primary found); Baird's thoughts.

**Why it grips adults**: it is a closed-room tragedy with a clock, a traitor, an omen, a
father's grave and a monsoon storm, and the two best sources are enemies who agree.

### Story B — "Two Boys on Elephants" (the hostage princes, 26 Feb 1792 – Mar 1794)
**People**: Abdul Khaliq (ಅಬ್ದುಲ್ ಖಾಲಿಕ್), about ten; Muiz-ud-din (ಮುಯಿಜ್-ಉದ್-ದೀನ್), about eight, "the
favourite and the Sultan's intended heir"; Cornwallis; the vakils; Tipu, watching from the fort;
Captain Doveton, who brings them home.

**Arc (7 beats)**
1. Feb 1792: Cornwallis's lines reach the island; Tipu's night attack of 6 Feb fails; the
   preliminary articles go in on the 22nd and come back signed on the night of the 23rd
   (DIROM pp. 222–227; MG II.4 p. 2602).
2. The terms: "One half of the dominions ... to be ceded to the allies"; 3 crore 30 lakh rupees,
   half at once; all prisoners "unequivocally restored"; "Two of Tippoo Sultan's three eldest
   sons to be given as hostages" (DIROM pp. 225–226, arts. I–IV).
3. 26 Feb, noon: the princes leave the fort on elephants in silver howdahs with pikemen,
   standard-bearers and 200 sepoys; the fort fires a salute and the park answers with 21 guns
   (DIROM p. 228). Tipu has asked that they be taken straight to Cornwallis's tent.
4. Cornwallis meets them at his tent door, embraces them and leads them in "one in each hand";
   he gives each a gold watch (DIROM p. 229). Next day they give him swords; he gives a fusee
   and pistols (pp. 230–231).
5. Tipu stalls: his men keep working on the fort during the armistice ("his Lordship must have
   been misinformed"), the vakils dispute the coin's value, he refuses to give up Coorg, then
   re-arms, then submits (DIROM pp. 236–246).
6. 19 Mar: the boys themselves hand the definitive treaty in triplicate to Cornwallis
   (DIROM pp. 246–248). John Smart paints Abdul Khaliq on 8 Aug 1792; the miniatures are sent to
   Tipu, presumably as proof of good treatment (V&A O1808225).
7. March 1794: the indemnity paid, the hostages return; Tipu receives Captain Doveton
   (WILKS III ch. XLII). Coda: after 1799 the sons go to Vellore; after the 1806 mutiny they are
   "transported to Calcutta" (BOWRING p. 201); Abdul Khaliq dies 1806 (V&A).

**Goosebump moment**: the 21-gun salute from the enemy's park answering the fort's own guns as
the two boys ride out — recorded by Dirom, an officer on the spot (p. 228). Tier `inscription`.

**Dramatised vs recorded**: Recorded — every ceremony detail above, the watches, the ages.
Dramatised — what the boys felt; what Tipu said to them; the mother(s). The Robert Home and
Mather Brown paintings are c. 1793 reconstructions, not eyewitness.

**Why it grips adults**: a father trading his sons for time; the enemy general playing the
kind uncle; the reader knows what 1799 will bring.

### Story C — "The Night Sortie at Arakere" (Kanthirava Narasaraja I, 1638–39)
**People**: Kanthirava Narasaraja (ರಣಧೀರ ಕಂಠೀರವ); Dalavai Timmarajaiya (ದಳವಾಯಿ ತಿಮ್ಮರಾಜಯ್ಯ),
appointed 26 Nov 1638; Ranadulla Khan (ರಣದುಲ್ಲಾ ಖಾನ್), the Bijapur general (with Shahji Bhonsle
in his army, per WP "Randaula Khan"); Govinda Vaidya, the poet who will write it up in 1648.

**Arc (6 beats)**
1. 1638: the young Immadi Raja dies — tradition says poisoned by Dalavai Vikrama Raya; Rao
   notes the tradition is uncorroborated and Vikrama Raya was alive in 1644 (HR I p. 116).
   Kanthirava, a kinsman living in obscurity, is brought to the throne.
2. Wilks's anecdote of his youth: travelling incognito to Trichinopoly he kills, with a
   broadsword before the court, a champion who had challenged all comers, refuses the Nayaka's
   offer to stay, and leaves in the night (WILKS I p. 48). `legend` (Wilks's manuscripts).
3. 18 Jan 1639: Ranadulla Khan lays siege to Srirangapatna (HR I p. 122; fn 19 discusses the
   date; Wilks "in his first year ... Rend Dhoola Khan").
4. "A section of his army delivered a surprise night-attack on the Bijapuris encamped at
   Arakere" (HR I p. 122); Wilks: the assault failed "with great slaughter" and the Raja's
   forces harassed the retreat (WILKS I p. 50).
5. The Khan "beat a hasty retreat"; truce with the Cauvery as boundary; withdrawal c. mid-Feb
   1639 (HR I p. 123). His father Bettada Chamaraja sends him the broadsword "Vijaya-Narasimha"
   in appreciation (HR I p. 124).
6. April 1639: the Gajjiganahalli copper-plate — the first record calling him king (HR I
   p. 117). April 1645: coins in his own name (p. 143). 1647: Ikkeri sends an embassy to his
   Mahanavami (p. 140). 1648: Govinda Vaidya's *Kanthirava Narasaraja Vijayam* — the city
   "swarmed with visitors ... chieftains, nayakas" at Dasara.

**Goosebump moment**: the copper plate of April 1639 — the first time anyone writes
"king" of a Mysore Wodeyar — cut within weeks of the Bijapur army leaving. Tier `inscription`.
**Disputed**: WP "Randaula Khan" says Mysore submitted and paid tribute after a month-long
siege; Rao (Kannada and Persian sources) says the Khan retreated after a truce. Present both.
**Dramatised vs recorded**: Recorded — the dates, the sortie at Arakere, the truce, the
plate, the coins. Dramatised — the Trichinopoly duel (legend), the poisoning (legend), all
dialogue. **Why adults**: a founding-of-a-kingdom story with a documentary spine, and the
poet who turns it into literature nine years later is a character.

### Story D — "The Emperor of the South" (Chikkadevaraja, 1673–1704)
**People**: Chikkadevaraja (ಚಿಕ್ಕದೇವರಾಜ); Tirumalarya (ತಿರುಮಲಾರ್ಯ), his co-student at Hangala from
c. 1668 and later minister-poet (HR I pp. 269–271, 328–329); Visalaksha Pandit (ವಿಶಾಲಾಕ್ಷ ಪಂಡಿತ),
the Jain prime minister who predicted the succession by astrology and was murdered at night
(WILKS I pp. 87–90); Karanika Lingannaiya (ಕರಣಿಕ ಲಿಂಗಣ್ಣಯ್ಯ), ambassador to Aurangzeb; Shivaji;
Qasim Khan, Mughal faujdar of Sira; the Jangama priests of Nanjangud.

**Arc (8 beats)**
1. Feb 1673: installed sixteen days after Devaraja's death; strikes east on the fifth day and
   defeats Chokkanatha of Madurai at Madhuvana (HR I pp. 272–275).
2. 1677: Shivaji's southern campaign takes Mysore's "ancestral possessions" Arni, Hoskote,
   Bangalore, Doddaballapur, Sira; Kannada works say local chiefs drew him toward Srirangapatna
   and Chikkadevaraja repulsed a surprise attack; Orme's sources say Marathas "plundered as
   far as Seringapatam" and retired "after having some bloody battles with the Naik of
   Mysore" (HR I pp. 281–284). No casualty figures survive.
3. c. April 1682: Sambhaji's generals under Haraji Mahadik attack Srirangapatna while the
   Mysore army is at Tiruchirapalli; repulsed (HR I ch. XI outline; WP "Maratha invasion of
   Srirangapatnam (1682)").
4. 1687: Ekoji (Venkoji) sells Bangalore for three lakhs; Qasim Khan's Mughal army gets there
   first, raises the Mughal flag 10 Jul 1687, then hands the city to the Raja's troops; the
   money goes to the Mughal (WILKS I pp. 91–92; Muddachari via WP).
5. A regular post is set up across the kingdom; postmasters double as police spies —
   "Hyder later improved this system" (WILKS I p. 89).
6. New petty taxes that soldiers do not pay spark ryot protests backed by Jangama priests
   (Stein 1985 pp. 400–401). Wilks: the priests are invited to meet the Raja at Nanjangud;
   "Expert executioners were in waiting in the square" (WILKS I pp. 206–207). D. R. Nagaraj
   (2003 pp. 378–379): over 400 priests strangled by royal wrestlers as they left one at a
   time. Suspicion for the Jain minister's murder falls on the Jangamas (WILKS I p. 90).
7. 1699–1700: embassy under Lingannaiya to Aurangzeb at Ahmednagar; returns with a letter,
   presents and insignia paraded through the town and shown at the Ranganatha temple; a new
   signet "Jug Deo Raj" / "Raja-Jagadev" (WILKS I pp. 99–100; HR I pp. 319–320). Wilks: the
   embassy's splendour "made little impression at court" — the ambassadors' entertainment cost
   about 200 rupees (p. 99 fn). The "ivory throne" claim is Wilks's; Rao says Mysore already sat
   on a jewelled throne and has "no independent evidence confirmatory of this account"
   (HR I p. 320, fn 178 p. 322).
8. The eighteen departments (ಅಠಾರ ಕಚೇರಿ, athara kacheri), Sri-Vaishnava devotion, his own
   *Gitagopalam*; death Dec 1704 (WILKS I p. 211) leaving a kingdom "from Medigeshi to Palni
   ... from Kodagu to the Baramahals" (Imperial Gazetteer 1908 via WP).

**Goosebump moment**: the Mughal insignia carried in procession and shown to Ranganatha
before the new seal "Jagadeva Raja — king of the world" is cut (WILKS I pp. 99–100; HR I
p. 320). Tier `scholarship` (Mysore manuscripts; no Mughal-side confirmation).
**Dramatised vs recorded**: Recorded — dates, Bangalore purchase, post, embassy, the massacre's
occurrence. Dramatised — the number 400 (one modern source), the wrestlers, Shivaji in person
at Srirangapatna (sources say his parties). **Why adults**: a clever, pious, ruthless state-
builder; the Jangama massacre keeps it from being hagiography.

### Story E — "Alamelamma's Curse" (1610) — LEGEND, usable only as frame or epilogue
**People**: Raja Wodeyar (ರಾಜ ಒಡೆಯರ್); Tirumala (ತಿರುಮಲರಾಜ), Vijayanagara viceroy of Srirangapatna,
nephew of Venkata I; his wives Alamelamma (ಅಲಮೇಲಮ್ಮ) and Rangamma (ರಂಗಮ್ಮ).
**What is recorded** (`scholarship`, chronicle-based): Tirumala lost support after the 1596
siege of Kesare; Raja Wodeyar rescued him from captivity c. 1609; Tirumala then plotted
against him and withdrew to Malangi leaving his family; Raja Wodeyar entered the city 8 Feb
1610 and held his first durbar 21 Mar 1610; Venkata I later confirmed the conquest (HR I ch. V).
He held the Mahanavami durbar at Srirangapatna 8–17 Sep 1610 "according to a traditional
account", and when his eldest son Narasaraja died on 7 Sep 1610 ruled that a royal death
should not stop the festival (HR I ch. V). Wilks rejects the "royal boil" bequest tale as
"contrary to all probability" (WILKS I p. 42) and notes manuscripts "now proved to be of
modern fabrication" (pp. 40–41).
**What is legend**: Rao's fn 92 gives the *Annals*' version — two wives went to Malangi;
Alamelamma drowned herself in the Cauvery after being made to return temple jewels — and
observes that the earlier sources "contain neither any reference to the two wives of Tirumala
nor to this incident" (HR I ch. V fn 92). Wilks (1810) does not mention a curse at all. The
curse text now current — ತಲಕಾಡು ಮರಳಾಗಿ, ಮಾಲಂಗಿ ಮಡುವಾಗಿ, ಮೈಸೂರು ದೊರೆಗೆ ಮಕ್ಕಳಾಗದೆ ಹೋಗಲಿ ("let Talakadu
become sand, Malangi a whirlpool, the Mysore kings be without heirs") — is cited on WP
"Talakadu" to Sashi Sivaramakrishna, *The Curse of Talakadu: A Legend in History* (Rupa),
a modern popular book. **Earliest written record: not established in this pass** — later than
the 1648/1680 court texts, present in the palace *Annals* (18th–19th c.). The golden image of
Alamelamma worshipped in the palace is reported by modern articles only. Tier `legend`.
**Goosebump moment** (if used): the archival silence itself — the two 17th-c. court poets who
praise Raja Wodeyar never mention her. **Why adults**: the legend is what every Kannadiga
knows; the honest version shows how a legend grows around a documented coup and a real
sand-buried town.

## 4. Little-known facts (one line + source + tier)

1. The first record to call a Mysore Wodeyar "king" is the Gajjiganahalli copper-plate of
   April 1639, weeks after Bijapur's retreat — HR I p. 117. `inscription`
2. Raja Wodeyar's Dasara of 1610 went ahead although his eldest son died the day before it
   began; he ruled that a royal death must not stop the festival — HR I ch. V. `scholarship`
3. Chikkadevaraja's ambassadors to Aurangzeb cost the Mughal court about 200 rupees in
   hospitality — Wilks's dry footnote on how little impression the "splendid embassy" made —
   WILKS I p. 99 fn. `scholarship`
4. Chikkadevaraja's postmasters were also the kingdom's police spies; Hyder kept and improved
   the system — WILKS I p. 89. `scholarship`
5. Hyder's father Fath Muhammad commanded 50 rocket-men for the Nawab of the Carnatic before
   entering Mysore service — WP "Hyder Ali" fn 7–8 (not checked in Wilks). `scholarship`
6. Hyder kept the defeated Khande Rao "like a tota (parrot)" in an iron cage, fed on rice and
   milk for the rest of his life — BOWRING pp. 31–34. `scholarship`
7. Hyder's death (Dec 1782) was hidden from his own army by Purnaiah and Krishna Rao; the
   embalmed body travelled to Kolar in a chest and the army was told he was ill — BOWRING p. 118.
   `scholarship`
8. Tipu's 1792 hostage sons were ten and eight; Cornwallis gave each a gold watch, and the
   boys later handed over the definitive treaty themselves — DIROM pp. 229, 246–248. `inscription`
9. In Jan 1786 Tipu wrote that "about forty thousand Koorgs" had been "raised ... to the honor
   of Islam" and put in the Ahmedy corps — KIRK Letter CXCVI to Runmust Khan, 5 Jan 1786.
   Historians (Hasan) doubt the number; the letter is Tipu's own. `inscription`
10. Tipu wrote to the Sringeri Jagadguru after Parasurama Bhau's Marathas sacked the matha in
    1791, sending money and gifts for re-consecrating Sharada — c. 30 Kannada letters found
    1916 (Mysore Archaeological Report 1916; Hasan 2005 via WP). `inscription`
11. Tipu abolished the Hijri on his coins in his fifth year for his own "Mauludi" era, and
    renamed months and denominations (Ahmadi, Haidari, Zohra ...) — KIRK preface; coin
    catalogues. `inscription`
12. A British shot blew up a rocket magazine inside Srirangapatna on 2–3 May 1799; the storming
    columns were met "with rockets and musketry" in the river — BEATSON pp. 123, 127. `inscription`
13. Tipu's dream register was found in his escritoire by Kirkpatrick; Beatson remarks that
    "war and conquest" filled his sleeping as well as waking thoughts — BEATSON p. 197. `inscription`
14. Four British soldiers were hanged for plunder before order returned to the fallen city —
    BEATSON p. 168. `inscription`
15. The 1799 settlement left the five-year-old Maharaja a guaranteed income of one lakh star
    pagodas plus one-fifth of net revenue — AITCH IX no. XLIII art. 5, p. 222. `inscription`
16. The 1881 Instrument of Transfer let the Governor-General resume Mysore on any breach
    (art. 23) and fixed the subsidy at Rs 35 lakh a year (art. 5) — AITCH IX pp. 232–234.
    `inscription`
17. The Nagar rebels used margosa (neem) leaves as a mobilisation signal; the 1833 Company
    inquiry blamed the Maharaja's "style of rule and personal character" more than the taxes —
    WP "Nagar revolt" (Lind 2004; Hawker et al. 1833). `scholarship`
18. Thomas Hickey painted Purnaiah c. 1801 (Yale Center for British Art) — the only Dewan of
    Mysore with an eyewitness oil portrait — Commons file page. `inscription`

## 5. Disputed or uncertain points

- **Origin of the Wodeyars.** Court texts (Govinda Vaidya 1648; Tirumalarya c. 1680) give
  Dwaraka and Yadu descent; Rao: 1399 "uncorroborated", bracket 1399–1420; the Jaina/Hoysala
  version "a later attempt to connect the Ruling House of Mysore with the Hoysalas" (HR I p. 26);
  Ikegame 2013 pp. 76–77 and others argue local lords who adopted a puranic pedigree; Wilks has
  the Vijeya/Krishna-at-Hadana story with a change of religion (WILKS I pp. 31–33). No side has
  a contemporary document.
- **1610: conquest or peaceful transfer?** Rao: "an act of conquest" with Venkata I's later
  confirmation; Wilks: "peaceable occupation" and rejection of the bequest tale; popular pages:
  "without military action". Alamelamma: see Story E.
- **1639 siege outcome.** Rao/Govinda Vaidya/Muhammad-Namah: Bijapur retreats after truce;
  WP "Randaula Khan" (Persian-side secondary): Mysore submits and pays tribute.
- **Chikkadevaraja and Shivaji.** Sources say Maratha parties reached Srirangapatna in 1677 and
  were beaten off; whether Shivaji himself was present is not stated (HR I pp. 281–284).
  The "ivory throne" from Aurangzeb is Wilks only (HR I fn 178). Death: 12 Dec 1704 (Wilks) vs
  16 Nov 1704 (Imperial Gazetteer).
- **Jangama massacre numbers.** Wilks gives the method, not the count; "over 400" is Nagaraj
  2003. Treat the number as uncertain.
- **Hyder's birth year** (1717–1722) and the date of his death (6 vs 7 Dec 1782; calendar).
- **Tipu's birth** (1 Dec 1751 per Hasan; 1750 in older works) and accession (22 vs 29 Dec 1782).
- **Treaty of Madras date**: 29 Mar 1769 (Bowring, WP) vs "April 1769" (Aitchison TOC).
- **Treaty of Mangalore**: 11 Mar 1784 (Aitchison) vs "January 1784" (Bowring) vs 28 Jun 1784
  (one secondary) — signing/ratification confusion.
- **Pollilur casualties**: 2,016 killed/1,000 captured (WP infobox) vs "some three thousand
  ... killed" and c. 200 Europeans captured (Jasanoff) vs "700 Europeans were killed" (Bowring
  p. 91). Whether a rocket blew up Baillie's tumbrils: "thought to have been" (Narasimha, via WP).
- **The Daria Daulat Pollilur mural**: WP/popular: painted 1784; Sotheby's 2022: shortly after
  1780, over-painted 1791 at Tipu's order after the treaty, restored under Wellesley; three
  paper copies exist (one sold 2022, 962 × 200 cm). Size of the wall mural itself is quoted
  variously (31.6 × 6.6 ft vs 31 × 17 ft).
- **Tipu's religious policy.** Kirkpatrick/Wilks/Bowring (Coorg, Nair and Canara Christian
  deportations and conversions; Tipu's own letters CXCVI, CCXXIV give numbers and intent) vs
  the Sringeri letters, temple grants and Hasan/Brittlebank/Habib (political not religious
  motive; numbers inflated). Both bodies of evidence are primary; present both. The Melkote
  "1,200 Iyengars killed" figure is uncited on WP — do not use.
- **The Jacobin Club** (1797): Boutier 2005 argues Company fabrication; the printed
  "Proceedings" (1799) are the only source. Napoleon's letter is dated Feb 1798 (not 1799) and
  never reached Tipu.
- **Mir Sadiq's treachery**: asserted by Kirmani (Mysore side) and repeated by Hasan; Beatson
  records only that the defenders at the breach had been withdrawn; WP says "alleged".
- **Congreve's acknowledgement of Mysore rockets.** The line that "the British at Seringapatam
  had suffered more from them than from the shells or any other weapon used by the enemy" is
  quoted in secondary sources (e.g. Military Wiki, Dawn 2014) as Congreve's; the edition and page
  of *A Concise Account of the Origin and Progress of the Rocket System* (1807/1814/1817) were
  NOT verified in this pass (Gutenberg has only the 1814 *Details of the Rocket System*). The
  Roddam Narasimha paper (NAL 1985) that documents Congreve's debt is at
  web.archive.org/web/20110727034357/https://www.nal.res.in/pdf/pdfrocket.pdf — not fetchable
  from this tool; read it in a browser before quoting Congreve.
- **Gandhi's "Rajarshi".** Universally attributed, never cited to a volume. WP cites a 2009
  Bangalore Mirror piece and dates it 1925; the only Gandhi text on Mysore it quotes is
  *Navajivan* 8 Feb 1925: "His Highness the Maharaja of Mysore has taken up spinning." A Deccan
  Herald piece says Gandhi (1927 Nandi Hills stay) held Nalvadi's rule "came close to his
  concept of Ramarajya". Neither CWMG volume/page nor the Young India 1927–28 file
  (archive.org/details/HindSwaraj.yi.10973.33464, yi.10973.33464_djvu.txt) was searched for the
  word. Until that is done, write "Gandhi is said to have called him Rajarshi" and cite nothing.
- **Nagar 1831 hand-over**: 19 Oct 1831 (Rice via WP "Mysore Commission") vs 9 Oct (WP lead).
- **Adoption date 18 Jun 1865**: WP uncited; MG II.4 TOC confirms recognition in 1867 only.
- **Instrument of Accession**: 9 Aug 1947 (PIB) vs "eve of 15 Aug" (WP).
- **Bhadravati 1923, KRS "completed 1931/1932", Mysore University 27 Jul 1916**: secondary only.

### 6e. Dress, arms, animals (sourced pointers)
- Mysore court dress c. 1800: the Hickey Purnaiah (white muslin, red turban), the Home/Brown
  hostage paintings (boys in long jamas and turbans, pearl strings), the Mysore gouache of Tipu
  (green coat, turban with jewelled ornament — V&A record). Tipu's own habits: "disliked
  palanquins and was fond of riding"; rose at daybreak, read the Koran, breakfast of nuts,
  fruit and milk (BEATSON pp. 153, 159–161); about 5 ft 8 in, short neck, square shoulders,
  "rather corpulent", brown complexion (p. 152).
- Weapons: Tipu's inscribed swords (Commons); "Tippu Swords in summer palace.JPG"; Mysore
  rockets (above); matchlocks and flintlocks; the 1799 breach was made with 18- and
  24-pounders (BEATSON pp. 122–123).
- Elephants: the hostage princes rode "elephants in silver howdahs" (DIROM p. 228); Tipu's
  howdah survives (File:Howdah of tipu sultan.jpg); the 1839 Keddah photographs
  (File:Sanderson Kheddah.jpg) show 19th-c. capture.
- Horses: Mysore cavalry was the core of Hyder's army (BOWRING ch. VI–VIII); the Amrit Mahal
  cattle breed is Wodeyar/Hyder-era (RICE 1897 pp. 201–204).
- Ships: Hyder's navy (ketches, dhows built by the Dutchman Azelars 1778; grabs and gallivats
  lost 1768) — WP "Hyder Ali" fn 55–56; Tipu's coppered ship for Pegu (KIRK Letter CCCXXVI).
- 17th c. (Raja Wodeyar to Chikkadevaraja): no contemporary portraits except the HR I
  miniatures (probably 18th-c. palace copies). Use Vijayanagara-style dress (Hampi reliefs),
  Nayaka-period Madurai/Srirangam sculpture, and the Keladi reliefs for costume.

### 6f. Anachronisms to avoid
- The present Mysore Palace (1912) in any scene before 1897; Lalitha Mahal (1921).
- Brindavan Gardens (1927–32) at KRS before the 1930s.
- Tipu's Tiger as a battlefield object (it is a palace automaton, c. 1790s).
- Gandabherunda arms-with-supporters before the 19th c.; the Karnataka state emblem (1973).
- Mughal-style Persian dress on 17th-c. Wodeyar courts (they were Vijayanagara/Nayaka in
  style; Chikkadevaraja's Mughal honours came only in 1700).
- Modern Dasara jumbo-savari with the golden howdah on Mysore streets before the 1800s
  (the 1610–1799 Dasara was at Srirangapatna; the move to Mysore city is post-1799).
- Rockets with fins or nose-cones (Mysore rockets are iron tubes lashed to bamboo).
- Red-coated "redcoats" for the Company's sepoys in all scenes — mix; and no Union Jack with
  the St Patrick saltire before 1801.
- Railways, Bangalore cantonment (1809), Cubbon Park (1870) before their dates.

## 7. Key people beyond the kings (one line each)

- Govinda Vaidya (ಗೋವಿಂದ ವೈದ್ಯ) — court poet, *Kanthirava Narasaraja Vijayam* 1648, first Dasara
  description and first Dwaraka-origin text (HR I p. 45; Pranesh 2003).
- Tirumalarya / Tirumalaiyangar (ತಿರುಮಲಾರ್ಯ) — Chikkadevaraja's co-student, Sri-Vaishnava councillor
  "like Brihaspati" (1675 plate), author of the *Vamsavali* and *Vijayam* (HR I pp. 328–329).
- Visalaksha Pandit (ವಿಶಾಲಾಕ್ಷ ಪಂಡಿತ) — Jain prime minister of Chikkadevaraja, astrologer-prophet of
  the succession, murdered at night (WILKS I pp. 87–90).
- Karanika Lingannaiya (ಕರಣಿಕ ಲಿಂಗಣ್ಣಯ್ಯ) — ambassador to Aurangzeb 1699–1700 (HR I p. 319).
- Dalavai Timmarajaiya (ತಿಮ್ಮರಾಜಯ್ಯ) — Kanthirava's commander, appointed 26 Nov 1638 (HR I p. 117).
- Ranadulla Khan (ರಣದುಲ್ಲಾ ಖಾನ್) — Bijapur general of the 1639 siege.
- Devaraja and Nanjaraja of Kalale (ಕಳಲೆ ದೇವರಾಜಯ್ಯ, ನಂಜರಾಜಯ್ಯ) — the Dalavayis who ruled through the
  Wodeyars c. 1730s–1759; Nanjaraja gave Hyder his first commands (WILKS I ch. VII–IX; BOWRING).
- Fath Muhammad (ಫತೇ ಮಹಮ್ಮದ್) — Hyder's father, peon to faujdar of Kolar; Budikote jagir (WILKS I
  pp. 239–246).
- Khande Rao (ಖಂಡೇರಾಯ) — Hyder's accountant-turned-rival; the iron cage (BOWRING pp. 31–34).
- Purnaiah (ಪೂರ್ಣಯ್ಯ, 1746–1812) — Hyder's and Tipu's finance minister, "the only Hindoo who
  remained in an office of high responsibility" (BEATSON p. 64 fn), hid Hyder's death, Dewan of
  the restored state 1799–1811, jagir of Yelandur, died Srirangapatna (MG II.4 p. 2824).
- Mir Sadiq (ಮೀರ್ ಸಾದಿಕ್) — Tipu's "Sahib Dewan", "held in execration by the peasantry" for
  rapacity (BOWRING p. 225), killed at the Ganjam gate 4 May 1799 (KIRMANI p. 270).
- Sayyid Ghaffar (ಸೈಯದ್ ಗಫಾರ್) — loyal commander killed by a cannonball on 4 May 1799 (KIRMANI p. 268).
- Lakshmi Ammani (ಲಕ್ಷ್ಮಮ್ಮಣ್ಣಿ, 1742–1810) — widow of Krishnaraja II, regent 1799–1810; her and the
  other widows' letter of 24 Jun 1799 preceded the restoration (BOWRING p. 204).
- Col. William Baillie — defeated at Pollilur 10 Sep 1780, died in Srirangapatna captivity
  13 Nov 1782 (Tritton 2013 via WP); "Baillie's dungeon" survives.
- Lord Cornwallis — 1792; the hostage ceremony (DIROM).
- Alexander Dirom; Alexander Beatson; William Kirkpatrick; Mark Wilks — the four British
  writers whose books are the primary printed record; Wilks was Resident 1803–08.
- Gen. George Harris; Maj-Gen. David Baird; Col. Arthur Wellesley (commanded the reserve, then
  Srirangapatna) — 1799.
- Mir Hussain Ali Kirmani — Mysore-court historian, *Nishan-i-Haidari* c. 1802.
- Hyder's and Tipu's French: Lally (at Pollilur), Ripaud (Jacobin club), Chapuis (1799).
- Dhondia Wagh (ಧೋಂಡಿಯಾ ವಾಘ್) — ex-Tipu officer, 1799–1800 rising in the north (MG II.4 pp. 2720–28).
- John Briggs; Sir Mark Cubbon (ಕಬ್ಬನ್, Commissioner 1834–61); Lewin Bowring (1861–70, later the
  biographer); Richard Meade (1870–81).
- Budi Basappa (ಬೂದಿ ಬಸಪ್ಪ) and Rangappa Naik — leaders of the Nagar rising 1830–31.
- C. Rangacharlu (ರಂಗಾಚಾರ್ಲು, Dewan 1881–83) — Representative Assembly; K. Seshadri Iyer (1883–1901)
  — Shivanasamudra power, Vani Vilasa Sagara; Sir M. Visvesvaraya (1912–18) — KRS, University,
  Bhadravati; Sir Mirza Ismail (1926–41) — not researched here.
- Kempananjammanni, Vani Vilasa Sannidhana (ವಾಣಿವಿಲಾಸ ಸನ್ನಿಧಾನ) — regent 1895–1902 (MG II.4 p. 2998).
- H. V. Nanjundaiah — first Vice-Chancellor, Mysore University 1916.
- Sashi Sivaramakrishna — modern author of *The Curse of Talakadu* (the popular source of the
  curse text; not a historian).

## 8. Sources to avoid (or use only as finding aids)

- homegrown.co.in, hindutone.com, knowledgeumacademy.in, karnataka.com, sakshipost, kupi.com on
  the "400-year curse" — undated, no primary; give 1610 or 1612 interchangeably and invent the
  golden-image and nose-ring details.
- "Fact-checked by Grok" / grokipedia.com pages — AI-generated, uncited.
- gktoday, vajiramandravi, prepp, anantamias, pw.live exam-prep pages — compressed, error-prone
  (e.g. "Treaty of Mangalore 1786", "Jacobin club 1794").
- indianetzone.com, mapsofindia history pages — uncited rewrites of Wikipedia.
- Wikipedia infoboxes where they conflict with their own body text (Pollilur casualties;
  Jayachamaraja's reign start; Mysore Commission 9 vs 19 Oct 1831; Mysore University vs Visvesvaraya
  "founder").
- WP "Raja Wodeyar I" — cites karnataka.com; its "Alamelamma" section is absent and its dates
  are uncited.
- WP "Chikka Devaraja" lead — Sambhaji "venom arrows / leather suits" claims are uncited and
  absent from the body.
- Popular pieces that say Shivaji himself was "halted at Srirangapatna" (Deccan Herald 2020) —
  the sources speak of Maratha parties.
- Any page giving Gandhi's "Rajarshi" with a date but no CWMG reference (Bangalore Mirror 2009,
  indiandownunder 2018, nestproperties, X posts).
- Sotheby's/auction copy for the Pollilur painting — good on condition history, promotional on
  significance; Dalrymple's "greatest Indian picture of the defeat of colonialism" is an opinion.
- Military Wiki / fandom for the Congreve quote — unpaged.
- *Official documents relative to the negotiations carried on by Tippoo Sultaun with the French
  nation* (Calcutta 1799) and the Annual Asiatick Register "Jacobin" proceedings — Company war
  propaganda; cite only with Boutier's objection.
- Kirkpatrick's and Bowring's editorial judgements ("intolerant bigot", "rivalled Mahmud of
  Ghazni") — period polemic; quote the documents, not the glosses.
- Deccan Herald "How Bengaluru was bought for Rs 3 lakh 333 years ago" — fine for the 1687 date,
  wrong on "Shivaji halted".
- Anything citing "Mysore Dasara started 1610" without Ikegame 2013 p. 123 or HR I ch. V.
