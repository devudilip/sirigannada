## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, southern Karnataka around Srirangapatna and Mysore in the winter of 1638 to 1639: the Kaveri splitting round a long flat island, green-brown water, sugarcane and paddy fields, coconut and areca groves, banyan and tamarind trees, grey granite boulder hills, red earth, misty cool dawns, dusty golden afternoons, cold starry nights. Dramatic natural light, river haze, dust, torch and campfire smoke where the scene calls for it, shallow depth of field on people; high detail in grey granite, earth-and-rubble walls with lime plaster, timber, cotton and silk, pearls and dull gold, blackened iron. Colour: Kaveri green-brown, red earth, ochre, white cotton, saffron, deep red, turmeric yellow. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

### kanthirava (lead; cover, scenes 1–3, 5–7, 9, 11; statue in 12)
Every prompt says "FIRST open the file bible/characters/kanthirava-sheet.png and match this man exactly".
Face and build: Kanthirava Narasaraja Wodeyar, king of Mysore, about 24, a wrestler's build: massive neck, very broad shoulders, deep broad chest, thick strong arms, hard and muscular, never fat; a BROAD face with wide cheekbones tapering to a firm rounded chin, LARGE prominent dark eyes under thick level brows, a strong broad straight nose, a THICK black moustache swept out and curled UP into round loops at the ends, a clean-shaven chin and jaw with NO beard, a small old scar nick through the outer end of the left eyebrow, warm medium-brown skin, a small musk-black tilaka dot on the forehead, LARGE gold ring ear-rings set with pearls and a blue sapphire in both ears.
Court dress: wearing a ROUNDED wound turban of deep red cloth with narrow gold bands, a jewelled band across the front and a short forward-curving top knob at the crown carrying a small diamond-and-pearl crest; a long white muslin coat (dagale) reaching below the knee, tied at the right side, over white trousers; a broad TURMERIC-YELLOW silk waist sash with a jewelled dagger (katari) tucked in front; pearl necklaces; a broad straight sword in a dark scabbard and a large ROUND dark shield with brass bosses slung at his LEFT side; a gold hero's anklet (virapendeya) on the RIGHT ankle ONLY, left ankle bare; gold-embroidered slippers.
War dress: in war dress: a quilted coat sewn all over with pearls (pearl armour) with a jewelled breastplate over it, a close iron helmet wrapped with a red turban cloth with the same forward-curving knob, the TURMERIC-YELLOW silk waist sash with a jewelled dagger, a broad straight sword in his hand, the large round dark shield with brass bosses, the gold hero's anklet on the RIGHT ankle ONLY, left ankle bare.
Ages: scene 1 about 16 (same face, light first moustache, bare-chested, no turban); scene 2 about 20 (plain
wrestler's dress, legend scene); scenes 3–11 about 23–24. Scene 12 is the stone statue only, no living man.
Distinct from every earlier lead: NOT a crown, NOT a tall ribbed conical cap (not Krishnadevaraya), NOT a white turban with an aigrette and pointed chin-beard (not Chikkadevaraja), NOT a hair knot or bare chest or drooping walrus moustache (not Chavundaraya), NOT a beard of any kind, NOT a jiretop, NOT ugly, NOT fat; handsome, strong, dignified. Also not Mayurasharma (young, curly hair), Pulikeshi (long
curls, short beard), Vishnuvardhana (full cropped beard), Amoghavarsha (thin moustache, loose long hair),
Vikramaditya II (heart-shaped, clean-shaven), Vikramaditya VI (receding hairline, beard), Shivaji (pointed
beard, green turban). His marks: wrestler's neck and shoulders, broad face, looped upswept moustache, no
beard, eyebrow scar, big ring ear-rings, rounded red turban with forward knob, yellow sash.

## PERIOD RULES
- 1639: the Srirangapatna walls are earth and rubble with some granite; rebuilt in large flat stones only
  after the siege (scene 11 shows the work starting). Nothing of Tipu's 18th-century town.
- Arms: swords, spears, bows, round shields, a few matchlocks and a few heavy iron cannon. No rockets.
- Banners: plain or striped coloured cloth, no crescents, no writing.
- Bijapur figures in Deccani jama and flat-topped turbans; Mysore figures in dagale, waist-cloths, turbans.
- The Trichinopoly scene is a legend: show the moment before the blow, never a death.
- Nose-cutting is stated in the prose; never shown.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
Exclusions: No crescent flags or crescent emblems; no Tipu Sultan era buildings (no Daria Daulat, no Gumbaz, no tall-minaret Jamia Masjid), no present Mysore Palace with domes and arches, no Indo-Saracenic architecture; no rockets, no flintlocks with bayonets, no British or European soldiers or uniforms; only matchlocks and a few heavy iron cannon; no Mughal court dress on Mysore people (Bijapur figures wear Deccani dress of about 1640); no gore, no severed heads or noses, no blood in close-up; no king or general kneeling, fleeing, cowering or sneering; no modern objects, roads, wires, concrete or signboards; no legible text or letters anywhere.
