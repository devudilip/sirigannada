The arc in one breath: the WORLD is a broken Vijayanagara south where Marathas, Mughals, Bijapur,
Golconda and Nayakas all grab; a passed-over prince in a village learns everything with two friends,
a Jain and a Sri-Vaishnava. WHY he matters: he turns a river-island chiefdom into a state with a post,
spies, a treasury, eighteen departments and a seal the emperor himself (says Mysore) sent. HOW: speed
(war on the fifth day), cunning (oxen with torches; buying a city), ruthlessness (the taxes, the
rising, Nanjangud), and luck with his friends, until one is murdered. ENDING: an old devotee writing
petitions to Vishnu, a full treasury, a kingdom from Palni to Midagesi, and a son who cannot hear or
speak. Two goosebump moments: the field at Kottatti lit by thousands of moving torches (scene 6), and
the Mughal insignia carried to the feet of Ranganatha (scene 11). One dark centre: Nanjangud (scene 8).

Storytelling rule (README step 4): scenes tell events with confidence, in order; no "the record says"
inside a scene. Every chosen telling is disclosed in the scene's dramatised[] and on the last page.
The one legend (the stars, f-prediction) is told as ಐತಿಹ್ಯ: the scene shows it as the story people
in Srirangapatna told about the Pandit, not as a recorded event.

## ಪೀಠಿಕೆ
The Wodeyars as Vijayanagara's local lords around Srirangapatna (f-wodeyar-origin), Raja Wodeyar's
capture of the island fort in 1610 (f-srirangapatna-1610), the south in the 1670s: Shivaji rising,
Aurangzeb coming south, the Nayakas of Madurai and Ikkeri, Bijapur and Golconda failing. The voices:
a court poet who was the king's school friend, an English Resident who gathered the palace manuscripts
and village memories in 1805–1810, a copper plate of 1686, Jesuit letters from Madurai, a Persian
stone in Dod-Ballapur, and Hayavadana Rao, the court historian of 1943 who argued with Wilks.
