# Visual bible: Mysore and Srirangapatna, 1668–1704 (ಚಿಕ್ಕದೇವರಾಜ ಒಡೆಯರ್)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/mysuru-verified.md §10 and §13 (the palace miniature of the king, HR I
Plate XXIV, Commons File:Chikkadevaraja.jpg; the Gundlupet devotee relief, HR I p. 387; the court-dress
passage from contemporary poems, HR I p. 392; the description of Srirangapatna and Mysore forts, HR I
pp. 388–389; Aurangzeb, Shivaji and Deccani noble portraits of c. 1680 on Commons).

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, southern Karnataka in the late 17th century (1670s to
1700s). Dramatic natural light, river haze, dust, torch smoke where the scene calls for it, shallow
depth of field on people, high detail in grey granite, whitewashed lime plaster, red earth, cotton and
silk, pearls and dull gold, blackened iron of matchlocks. Colour: Kaveri green-brown water, dry
ragi-field ochre, white silk, saffron, deep red. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a
painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

### chikkadevaraja (lead; scenes 1–12)
Locked to the approved character sheet (video folder bible/characters/chikkadevaraja-sheet.png); every
prompt says "FIRST open the file bible/characters/chikkadevaraja-sheet.png and match this man exactly".
Handsome, sharp and dignified, a scholar-king and trained archer: medium-tall, lean and upright with
strong shoulders, NOT bulky. A long handsome face with a prominent straight nose that reads strongly in
profile, narrow penetrating dark eyes under level brows, light-brown skin, a thin arched black moustache
curling at the ends and a small neat pointed chin-beard; hair hidden under the turban. The poets' words
("charming face, large eyes, well-proportioned nose", HR I p. 501) and the palace miniature's sloping
turban and jaw-line beard are the starting point; the sheet is the final face.
Forehead: a plain sandal mark in scenes 1–8; the Sri-Vaishnava upright mark (white U with a red centre
line) in scenes 9–12 and on the sheet.
Headgear is his signature: a white turban wound with a gold brocade band and a jewelled aigrette
(the miniature's turban; the Gundlupet relief's "crest sloping on one side"). Never a tall pointed
kirita.
Dress (HR I p. 392 and the miniature): a long white muslin angarkha coat with gold-embroidered borders
tied at the chest, a gold brocade sash with a jewelled katar, several pearl strings and an emerald
pendant, pearl earrings, white churidar, gold-embroidered slippers; a straight jewelled sword for war
scenes; a quilted white coat over the angarkha in battle.
Ages: scene 1 = 23 (thin moustache only, no beard, no grey); scenes 2–8 = 27–41 (as the sheet); scenes
9–11 = 41–55 (grey streaks from scene 11); scene 12 = 59 (moustache and chin-beard white, face a little
fuller, still upright). Calm, alert, watchful; rarely smiles.
Distinct from the earlier leads, say so in every prompt: NOT Mayurasharma (young, curly hair,
clean-shaven), NOT Pulikeshi (long curls, short beard, tall kirita crown), NOT Chavundaraya (square
jaw, thick moustache with no beard, hair knot, grey temples, bare chest), NOT the Hoysala king (round
face, full cropped beard, gold crown). Chikkadevaraja is the only lead with a white turban and aigrette,
a thin curled moustache with a small chin-beard, a long aquiline profile and a long white coat.

### tirumalarya
Born 1645, the king's school friend, Sri-Vaishnava Brahmin scholar and later prime minister (1686–1704).
Invented face (no likeness survives): slim, a little taller than the king, long narrow face, thin
straight nose, clean-shaven, head shaved at the front with a tied tuft, Sri-Vaishnava upright mark on
the forehead, white dhoti and white upper cloth, a palm-leaf bundle and stylus; in later scenes grey,
spectacles NOT allowed. Quiet, warm eyes.

### visalaksha-pandit
Jaina Brahmin of Yelandur, prime minister 1673–1686. Invented face: about 45 in 1673, heavy-set,
broad forehead, close-cropped grey-black hair, small neat moustache, no beard, no Vaishnava or Saiva
mark (a small sandal dot at most), white cotton garments and a white shoulder cloth, an astrologer's
palm-leaf almanac. Commanding, impatient. In scene 9 lying wounded, an old man in white.

### kumaraiya (scenes 2, 6)
Dalavai of Kalale, the king's father-in-law, old soldier: white moustache, a red turban, quilted
coat, sword; elephant or horse.

### doddaiya (scene 6)
Kumaraiya's nephew, the new Dalavai: 30s, lean, hard face, red turban, quilted cotton armour coat,
sword, mounted.

### qasim-khan (scene 10)
Mughal faujdar. Dress from late 17th-c. Deccan Mughal portraits (Commons: File:Murad Baksh, younger
brother of Aurangzeb, Deccan, late 17th century.jpg; File:Portrait of the Emperor Aurangzeb MET
136889.jpg): full dark beard, a flat-topped Mughal turban with a jewelled sarpech, a long fine white
jama tied at the side, a patterned patka sash with a katar, pearls, riding boots; middle-aged,
shrewd, courteous.

### marathas (scenes 5, 6, 10)
Light cavalry: small hardy horses, riders in short coats or bare-armed, tight turbans, long spears,
curved swords, round shields, a few matchlocks; saffron pennants. Shivaji, if shown, only far off on
horseback after the c. 1680 Golconda portraits (Commons File:Shivaji Rijksmuseum.jpg; File:Shivaji
British Museum.jpg): pointed beard, turban with a plume.

### jangama-priests (scenes 7–8)
Vira-Saiva priests in orange (saffron) robes (Wilks I p. 207), linga in a casket on a cord at the chest,
rudraksha, sacred ash on the forehead; varied ages; dignified. Shown with respect; never as villains.

### lingannaiya (scene 11)
Karanika Lingannaiya, ambassador: Smartha Brahmin official, 40s, white turban, white coat, horizontal
ash lines on the forehead, carrying a sealed letter in silk.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### hangala
A small village in red-earth dry country below blue hills (Gundlupet taluk): low tiled and thatched
houses, a stone well, a small Dravidian village temple (Commons File:Hangala Varadarajaswamy temple,
Gundlupet.jpg for the landscape only), ragi fields, tamarind trees.

### srirangapatna-fort
The island in the Kaveri with the river all round (HR I p. 388: "lofty ramparts, newly constructed rows
of spikes, deep moat, wickets, bastions, flag-staffs and banners"); granite-faced ramparts with round
bastions, a moat, iron-spiked gates (one of them the Mysore Gate, HR Plate XXV), a few cannon on the bastions;
inside, the Ranganatha temple with its gopura and pillared courts (Commons File:Ranganathaswamy Temple,
Srirangapatna - Wide Shot.jpg), the palace as a two-storey pillared timber-and-plaster building with
balconies (as in the miniature's background), elephant stables, a market street.

### durbar-hall
"Saundarya-vilasa" (HR I p. 392): a pillared hall of carved wooden pillars and beams on a raised
platform; the king on a jewelled throne-platform with a bolster, a round sunshade and a long fan behind
(as in the miniature); courtiers standing, poets, musicians.

### madhuvana / kottatti
Madhuvana: the forest edge of the Sendamangalam hills, scrub jungle and boulders. Kottatti and
Honnalagere: flat irrigated and dry fields near the Kaveri, palm groves, a Maratha camp of small tents.

### nanjangud
The Srikanteshwara temple as a walled granite Dravidian compound with a modest entrance
tower (NOT today's giant gopura; its date was not verified), outer courtyards; for scene 8, an
enclosure of cloth tent walls (qanat) set up outside, torches, guards.

### bengaluru-fort
A fort of earth and stone walls with a gate and bastions on a plain, a small temple inside (the later
Kote Venkataramana; Commons File:Kote Venkataramana Temple in Bangalore, Karnatak 01.jpg for its
pillar style only).

### melkote
Hilltop town with the Cheluvanarayana (Yadugiri-Narayana) temple and its pillared halls (Commons
File:Cheluvanarayana Swamy Temple, Melkote - during PHMSTBGP-2020 (161).jpg).

## PERIOD RULES
- Gunpowder is normal now: matchlock muskets, cannon on fort bastions (HR I p. 389 lists "cannons" on
  Mysore fort), but most fighting is horse, spear, sword, bow. No flintlocks with bayonets, no
  uniforms, no rockets (the iron Mysore rockets belong to Hyder and Tipu, a century later).
- Elephants and horses with cloth housings; howdahs and umbrellas for the king; the Mysore army
  marches at night by many torches (Wilks I p. 98).
- Court dress: long jama-type coats and sashes, turbans, pearls, alongside dhotis and upper cloths for
  Brahmins and townsmen. The king's dress is Deccani and southern together (silk coat, pearls, tilaka,
  sloping turban); full Mughal court dress only on Qasim Khan and Mughal figures. Women: saris worn
  over the shoulder, gold and pearl ornaments, flowers in the hair.
- Marks: Sri-Vaishnava upright mark (Commons File:Urdhava pundra symbol inside a Karnataka Vishnu
  Hindu temple.jpg) for the king from 1686 and for Tirumalarya; horizontal ash for Saivas and
  Smarthas; none for Jains.
- Banners and insignia: the Garuda banner for the king (HR I p. 502); the boar (varaha) and the
  two-headed gandabherunda as emblems on banners and on the 1700 insignia; the red ensign in the 1700
  procession. A round Persian seal (after File:Seal of Krishnaraja Wadiyar II.jpg, without the 1163
  date).
- Script on documents: Kannada of the 17th century on palm leaf and copper plates; Persian nasta'liq on
  the Mughal letter and the seal; abstract strokes only, never modern glyphs.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No present Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas, 1897–1912) or Lalitha Mahal, no Tipu-era buildings (Daria Daulat,
the Gumbaz), no Mysore rockets, no British soldiers or Union flag, no flintlock-and-bayonet uniforms,
no heraldic Mysore coat of arms with supporters, no modern Dasara golden howdah or jumbo procession, no
Brindavan Gardens or KRS dam, no modern buildings, wires or vehicles, no Mughal dress on the Mysore king
before scene 11 beyond a long silk coat, no tall kirita on the king outside the coronation, no gore in
close-up, no executions shown, no mocking of any community.
