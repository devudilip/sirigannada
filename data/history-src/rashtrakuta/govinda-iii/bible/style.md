Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the full CHARACTER blocks of everyone in frame, the SETTING, and the

- Not used: the "gold dinara of Govinda III" horseman on Commons is an auction attribution, not used

### govinda-iii (all scenes; sheet: govinda-iii-sheet.png)
Govinda III, the Rashtrakuta king, about 30, handsome, strong and heroic: tall, broad-shouldered and deep-chested with long powerful arms and a horseman's straight back, lean waist, never fat; a BROAD face, wide at high prominent cheekbones, tapering to a strong angular jaw and a clean-shaven chin; deep-set dark eyes under a heavy straight brow, a thin pale old scar cutting through the LEFT eyebrow; a strong Roman nose with a slight bump at the bridge; a full black moustache swept upward into points, long sideburns to the angle of the jaw, no beard; thick wavy black hair gathered into a HIGH topknot on the crown of the head bound with a gold ring; deep bronze-brown skin; heavy gold ear-rings in stretched lobes, a close gold necklet (kanthika) at the throat with a single red stone, long pearl strings across a bare chest, a thin gold sacred thread, gold armlets and wristlets; a deep-red silk waist-cloth with a gold border wound to the knees, a white uttariya over the left shoulder, a jewelled waist-band; a straight double-edged sword with a plain round pommel; calm, warm, steady gaze; never cruel, never sneering. As king (scene 3 on): a gold makuta crown with a makara motif set around the topknot (moderate in
scenes 3–6, taller and tapering from scene 7), except bathing, at night and in the rain. Scenes 1–2: about
25, no crown; scene 1 without the necklet. Scenes 7–12 and cover: about 40 to 45, as in the AGE PLAN.

### stambha (scenes 1, 5; sheet: stambha-sheet.png)
Stambha, Govinda's elder brother, about 38, handsome, dignified and powerful: a little shorter and heavier-boned than Govinda, broad-chested with thick muscular arms (muscle, never fat); a broad SQUARE face with a wide brow, a short dense neatly trimmed black beard along jaw and chin joined to a full moustache; straight black hair worn long to the shoulders under a flat gold band across the brow (no topknot); wide-set dark eyes, a straight broad nose; dark brown skin; gold ear-rings, a heavy gold chain, armlets; an indigo silk waist-cloth with a gold border, a saffron uttariya; a straight sword; proud and grave, never sneering, never beaten-down.

### dhruva (scenes 1, 2)
Dhruva, the old Rashtrakuta king, about 60, lean, hard and upright, a long weathered face, a white moustache and a short white beard, white hair in a topknot under a tall gold crown, deep brown skin, heavy gold ear-rings, pearl strings, armlets, a white silk waist-cloth with a gold border and a crimson uttariya; commanding and warm, never frail.

### ganga-king (scene 6)
the Ganga king, about 50, tall and lean, proud and upright, a long face with a long greying beard and moustache, grey-black hair in a knot at the nape, wheat-brown skin, plain white cotton waist-cloth and shawl with no jewels except small gold ear-rings, no chains on him; head high, dignified, never cowed.

### malava-lord (scene 8)
the king of Malava, about 45, slim and upright, a narrow clean-shaven face, hair in a side knot under a jewelled band, gold ear-rings, a white and green silk waist-cloth and uttariya, standing straight with dignity.

### army
Rashtrakuta soldiers: bare-chested infantry in white and red waist-cloths with round hide shields, straight swords, spears and long bows; horsemen on small Deccan horses with cloth saddles and NO stirrups; war elephants with red cloth coverings, bells and mahouts with goads; the Garuda standard (a frontal squatting bird-man with a hooked beak-nose and spread wings, hands joined palm to palm at the chest, feet joined sole to sole, NOT an eagle, NOT a crescent) embroidered on a long red cloth banner and a row of pennants (pali-dhvaja).

### gurjara-army (scene 10)
the Gurjara army of the north: elephants in dark cloth with brass bells, infantry in white with long bows and straight swords, horsemen without stirrups, tall saffron and white banners with no Garuda and no crescent.

## PERIOD RULES
- Emblem: Garuda, frontal and squatting, hands joined, no lotus (Paithan), or holding snakes (Sanjan); never an
  eagle, never a crescent.
- Insignia: white parasols, chowries, pali-dhvaja pennants, conches, horns, kettle-drums.
- Dress: men bare-chested with waist-cloth and uttariya, heavy ear-rings in stretched lobes, armlets,
  pearl strings, sacred thread for the king. No stitched coats, no turbans of later type.
- Arms: straight swords, spears, long bows, round hide shields; elephants are the main war-mounts;
  horses small, cloth saddles, no stirrups; no mail, no helmets.
- Dignity: no king (Govinda, Stambha, the Ganga king, Nagabhata, Malava, Dharmapala) is ever shown
  kneeling, fleeing, chained in close-up or mocked. Submission is shown as standing palms joined.
- Pratishthana: present-day Paithan ghats and temples are later; show plain banks and a few steps.
