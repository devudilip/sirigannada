# Visual bible: the Rashtrakuta Deccan, 9th century CE (ಅಮೋಘವರ್ಷ ನೃಪತುಂಗ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/rashtrakuta-verified.md §11 (the Sanjan and Cambay Garuda seals; the
Nilgund and Sirur descriptions of the royal pomp, 866; Sulaiman 851); research/rashtrakuta.md §6
(Ellora Kailasa reliefs and the Rashtrakuta murals for dress, arms and elephants).

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, 9th-century Deccan (central India). Dramatic natural
light, monsoon haze, lamp smoke and dust where the scene calls for it, shallow depth of field on
people, high detail in black basalt, cloth, gold and skin. Colour: dark basalt grey, red-brown Deccan
earth, monsoon green, white cotton, saffron and indigo cloth, dull gold, unpolished iron. No text,
captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

No portrait of Amoghavarsha survives (no relief, statue or coin with his face; EI XVIII calls the
Badami "royal ascetic" idea "nothing more than a surmise"), so the face is invented and disclosed.
The lead must not repeat earlier leads: Mayura (young, curly hair, clean-shaven), Pulikeshi (long
curls, short beard, tall kirita), Chavundaraya (square jaw, thick moustache, hair knot, grey temples).

### amoghavarsha-boy (scenes 1 end, 2, 3)
Age 13–15. Slim, already tall for his age, long neck, serious watchful eyes. Same face family as the
adult below: long oval face, fine high-bridged slightly aquiline nose, deep-set almond eyes under
straight dark brows. Straight black hair worn long and loose to the shoulders, a thin gold band across
the brow. No facial hair. Heavy gold ear-rings in stretched lobes (Sulaiman: "the men who have their
ears pierced"), a pearl string, bare chest, white and saffron waist-cloth.

### amoghavarsha-king (scenes 4–10)
Age 20–45. Handsome and strong: tall and lean-muscled, long-limbed, broad shoulders, a horseman's
straight back. LONG OVAL face with high cheekbones and a narrow firm chin; fine high-bridged,
slightly aquiline nose; deep-set almond eyes, calm and grave; straight dark brows; a THIN, neatly
trimmed moustache, clean-shaven otherwise (no beard, no sideburns). Straight
black hair, long and open to the shoulders, held by a low jewelled gold band or a small gold makuta
with a makara motif (Sanjan v. 19 "makara-shaped crowns"); NOT a tall kirita. Wheat-brown skin. Ornaments: heavy gold ear-rings in
stretched lobes, pearl necklaces, armlets, a jewelled waist-band; white or saffron silk waist-cloth to
the ankle, an uttariya over one shoulder; straight long sword. Under three white umbrellas with
chowries in court scenes (Nilgund/Sirur).

### amoghavarsha-elder (scenes 11, 12)
Same face at 55–75: the moustache white, hair long and grey-white, the face thinner, eyes
still calm; in scene 11 and 12 in plain white cloth, few ornaments; LEFT hand bound in white cloth in
scene 11 and missing one finger in scene 12 (show from a distance, never gore).

### govinda-iii (scenes 1–2)
Age 50s. A massive, heavy-shouldered conqueror, broad face, thick grey moustache, grey hair in a top
knot under a tall gold crown; war scars; in scene 2 lying weak on a couch.

### patalamalla (scene 4)
Age 55. Identity unknown: an older loyal household commander, grey short beard, shaved head with a
cloth fillet, plain iron-studded leather over a white tunic, a long straight sword.

### karka (scene 4)
Age 30s. The Gujarat cousin: compact, clean-shaven, curly short hair, gold crown band, sword drawn.

### bankesa (scene 7)
Age 40. The general: stocky, short black beard, cloth fillet with an upright plume, sword at the side.

### jinasena (scene 9)
Old Digambara Jain teacher: nude, shaven head, thin, a peacock-feather whisk (pichchi) and a water
gourd (kamandalu); seated on a stone bench. Shown with tact (seated, side view, behind a pillar or
lamp, from the waist up); no close frontal nudity.

### srivijaya (scene 10)
Court poet, age 40s: lean, clean-shaven, hair in a side knot, white cloth, palm-leaf manuscript.

### sulaiman (scene 8)
9th-century Arab merchant of the Gulf: full dark beard, wrapped turban of plain cloth, long loose
cotton robe and cloak, writing tablet or ledger; NOT Ottoman or Mughal dress.

## SETTINGS (locked)

### rainy-camp (scene 1)
Forested Vindhya ridges, a broad river in monsoon flood (the Narmada side), rows of cloth tents and
thatched shelters, war-elephants under cloth awnings, horses in lines, rain and lamplight.

### palace-court (scenes 2, 10, 12)
A pillared hall of stone and carved timber; dark polished basalt floor; a wide low stone lion-throne
(simhasana) on a dais; three white umbrellas, chowries, oil lamps; drums (dhakka), kahala horns and
conches at the gate (Sanjan v. 20, v. 37; Nilgund).

### battle-plain (scenes 4, 5)
Open red-earth plain, dust; elephants with plain or patterned cloth and bells, mahouts with goads;
small horses with cloth saddles; infantry with long straight swords, round shields, bows; the Garuda
standard and the pali-dhvaja row of pennants.

### manyakheta (scenes 6, 12)
A river bend (the Kagina) in flat black-soil country; new walls of dressed stone and brick, timber
galleries, a palace hall; the exact plan is unknown, so keep it generic. Not the present Malkhed fort.

### konkan-port (scene 8)
A west-coast harbour with coconut palms, Arab dhows (sewn-plank hulls, lateen sails), bales and
pepper sacks, local officials with ear-ornaments.

### jain-hall (scene 9)
A rock-cut or stone pillared Jain hall in the Ellora Indra Sabha manner: seated Tirthankara figure in
the sanctum, lamps, scholars with palm-leaf bundles.

### goddess-shrine (scene 11)
A small, dark, plain stone shrine of the goddess, low doorway, oil lamps, flowers; NOT the present
large Kolhapur temple.

## PERIOD RULES
- Emblem: Garuda, frontal, squatting on a lotus, beak-nose, wings spread, a snake in each hand (Sanjan
  and Cambay seals). Use it on seals, banners and the copper-plate ring seal; no other design.
- Copper charters: three plates on a ring with a square seal (Sanjan: plates c. 18 x 10 inches).
- Royal insignia: three white umbrellas, chowries, the pali-dhvaja banner and other flags, conches,
  battle horns, the tivili drum (Nilgund/Sirur 866).
- Dress: men bare-chested with waist-cloth and uttariya, heavy ear-rings in pierced lobes, armlets,
  necklaces; women with waistbands, ear-rings, armlets, necklaces (Nilgund). Reference: Ellora Kailasa
  reliefs and the Rashtrakuta-period Ellora mural.
- Arms: straight swords, daggers, bows and arrows, round shields; elephants as the main war-mounts;
  no mail, no helmets.
- Scripts: Old Kannada letters on stone (Nilgund 866 photo), Nagari-type letters on copper plates;
  abstract ancient strokes only, never modern glyphs.
- Religion shown evenly: the king bows to Jain teachers AND worships Mahalakshmi AND grants to
  Brahmans (Sanjan); never show him as of one faith only.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No gunpowder, cannons or firearms; no Mughal or Ottoman dress, turbans of Mughal type, or curved
talwars; no mail armour or helmets; no stirrups; no European ship forms; no Hoysala soapstone or
Vijayanagara gopurams; no present-day Kolhapur temple or Malkhed fort walls; no tall kirita on the
king; no full beard on the king; no modern script on stone or copper; no portrait coin; no close
frontal nudity of Jain monks; no gore in close-up (the finger never shown being cut).
