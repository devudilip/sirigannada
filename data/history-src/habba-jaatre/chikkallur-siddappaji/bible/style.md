# Style bible: ಕೆಂಡ ತಾವರೆಯಾದಾಗ (Chikkalluru Siddappaji Jatre)

Book data: `tmp/new-books/chikkallur-siddappaji/story.json` in the kannada repo worktree. Series habba-jaatre, order 4.

## Look
- Cinematic photorealistic stills at 16:9, 1792x1024 or larger. Natural light, shallow depth of field on people. No text, logos or watermarks. Never a painting, cartoon or film poster. This matches the Kambala and Puthari books.
- **Legend scenes (cover excluded; scenes 1-7).** "A scene from an ancient legend": simple, timeless rural Old Mysore dress. Men wear undyed or white cotton panche, bare chests or a plain cloth over the shoulder; women wear plain handloom saris without blouse prints. Lighting is warm, smoky and dramatic. Faces are realistic, with no halos and no multiple arms.
- **Record scenes (cover, 8-12).** Present-day jatre at Chikkalluru, Kollegal taluk: dry scrub forest and fields, tents, a crowd of ordinary rural Karnataka people in everyday clothes. Nights are lit by fire and the full moon.
- **Palette.** Forge orange and ember red against deep blue night and moonlight. The Neelagaras' red turbans and blue rudraksha are recurring accents.

## Setting
- **Maralli house (legend):** a mud-walled smith's house grown rich: copper-tiled roof, a coral-red lime-washed wall, gold heaped in a bamboo winnowing tray (Hiriyanna pp. 78-79).
- **Forge:** an open-sided smithy with a charcoal hearth, leather bellows, an anvil, iron tongs and hammers, workers in loincloths and head cloths.
- **Kunduru hill:** bare granite boulders and scrub, with a cave mouth.
- **Halaguru-Chillapura (legend):** seen from a hill at night, hundreds of small forge fires on a mound between two clusters of streets (p. 151).
- **Chikkalluru today:** a modest temple front with a raised stone platform (the Chandramandala katte) before it (Andolana 2026). The Chandramandala is a tall frame of green bamboo shaped like a kandaya (a spear-blade standard) or a chariot, with cloth torches wound at every node (Hiriyanna p. 240; DH 2013). It lies flat until lit, then is lifted upright.
- No tamarind tree is claimed at the temple. Do not paint a famous tree.

## Characters (re-describe fully in every prompt)
- **Manteswamy (guru):** about 45, tall and lean, with a long oval face, deep-set calm eyes, high cheekbones, a grey-streaked beard and long matted hair tied on top. He wears a saffron kaavi cloth and a patched shawl, rudraksha strings, and carries a kandaya on his shoulder (a tall staff of kakke wood topped with a spear-blade-shaped metal emblem, flowers and areca-flower sprays; Hiriyanna p. 79 "ಕಕ್ಕೆಮರದ ಕಂಡಾಯ", p. 241 "ಶಕ್ತ್ಯಾಯುಧದ ರೀತಿ"), a tamburi and a peacock-feather fan. An iron anklet is on his left ankle; he is barefoot (pp. 79-80). He is handsome, ascetic and serene.
- **Kempanna, child (scenes 1, 3, 4, 5):** 7 years old, sturdy, with a round face, bright fierce eyes and dark curly matted locks piled on his head. He wears a white cotton loincloth and a waist thread, with thick gold rings on all ten fingers. There is no sacred thread and no caste mark.
- **Siddappaji, adult (scenes 6-7):** about 20, with a broad smith's shoulders and arms, a square jaw, a short dark beard and long matted locks tied high. He wears a single blue-faced rudraksha at his neck, a kaavi cloth at the waist and a cloth over one shoulder, and is barefoot. He carries a kandaya and a tamburi. He is handsome and strong, and must not resemble the guru.
- **Muddamma:** about 40, a prosperous village mother: plain dark-red handloom sari, gold earrings and nose stud, hair in a low bun. Her face is strong, proud and expressive.
- **Ajja (Neelagara grandfather, scenes 8-12):** about 68, lean and upright, with a lined, kind face, a white moustache and bright eyes. He wears the real Neelagara dress (Hiriyanna pp. 195, 219-220):
  - a big red turban
  - a black coat over a white shirt, a white panche, and a saffron kaavi cloth on the shoulder
  - a single blue-faced rudraksha at the neck
  - bronze rings on two fingers of the left hand
  - three horizontal ash stripes with a black dot on the forehead
  - a four-string tamburi with a carved cobra-hood head, and a cloth bag at the side
  He is barefoot.
- **Gowri:** 12, slim, with an oval face, large curious eyes and a long plait. She wears a langa-davani: a green skirt and a yellow half-sari.
- **The brother:** 8, small, with a round face and a fresh short haircut. He wears new white clothes for the diksha.
- **The parents:** late 30s, ordinary rural clothes, in the background only.
- **The swamy and the math elder:** seen from behind or only as hands. No likeness of any real person.

## Never show
- Any animal slaughter, an animal tied for sacrifice, blood, meat being cut, or live sheep, goats or fowl at the jatre.
- A pipe, smoke from a pipe, ganja or bhangi. Any caste marker: sacred thread, caste-specific marks or dress.
- Anyone in a crouching or lowly pose, particularly Neelagaras or workers; serving shown only as family members sharing food in the same row.
- Disease or Mari goddesses, gore, anyone burning or touching the Chandramandala flames, or a stunt.
- Legible text, banners, political posters, logos, smartphones in the foreground, police, or plastic litter in the foreground.
- Halos, extra arms or a cartoon look.
