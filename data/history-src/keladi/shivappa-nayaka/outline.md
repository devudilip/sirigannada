# ಶಿವಪ್ಪ ನಾಯಕ: working outline

Series `keladi` (ಕೆಳದಿ ನಾಯಕರು), story 1. This is a new series. Built to README §6 and the BRIEF.

Source base: research/keladi.md and research/keladi-shivappa-nayaka-verified.md. The facts are in facts.json: 48 ids, of which 9 are `inscription`/eyewitness, 33 are `scholarship` and 6 are `legend` (the Virabhadra handover and the KNV praise verses are now `legend`, as the Keladi court's telling).

Eyewitness and contemporary sources:
- Della Valle 1623, for the coast and Ikkeri a generation earlier.
- The English factory letters of 1650.
- The Sringeri copper grant of 1652.
- Shimoga 81 (1668), for the lineage.

The Portuguese siege record is reached through Danvers (1894), a summary of the Goa archives.

The Keladinripavijayam (KNV) is an 18th-century court poem. Its personal episodes (the cellar, the record hall, the khans) are `legend`. Its later facts (the forts, Belur, the death date) are `scholarship`, because they are cross-checked against Dutch and Portuguese records by Swaminathan and Hayavadana Rao.

The shist method comes from Rice's 1897 compilation of the revenue records. No document from Shivappa's own time has been found for it.

## Capital and court

His court was at Bidanur (Bidarur, Venupura; today's Nagar), not Keladi or Ikkeri. The sources:
- He was crowned "in the Venupura palace" (KNV VII p.106).
- The English went "to Biddrow the Kings court" in 1650 (Foster, EFI 1651–54 p.75).
- The Sringeri grant of 1652 says "when we visited Bidurur".
- Ikkeri appears only in scene 1 (1623) and as the source of the measuring rod.

## The spine (one line)

(en) Shivappa is the dalavai from the junior branch of a farming house. He wants two things: his country's river mouths should belong to its own people, and a farmer should know his tax before he sows. In his way stand the palace plotters, Bijapur's army and the Portuguese forts on the river mouths. The story turns in January 1653, when the Gangolli garrison boards the third fleet and leaves the dismantled fort. It costs him years of war and a sick retreat from Srirangapatna. It matters because the coast from Mirjan to Kasaragod was a Kannada king's, and 140 years later farmers still asked for his rates by name.

## Turning point and goosebump moment

**Turning point (scene 10).** On 17 January 1653, two days after the third Goa fleet arrives, the Gangolli garrison boards the ships. They carry what they can and leave the fortress entirely dismantled (Danvers II p.304; Danvers does not say who dismantled it). That shows the coast can be taken. Honavar (with Dutch help) and Mangalore follow in the same campaign (Kanara Gazetteer p.125, KNV VII p.109).

**Goosebump moment.** The third fleet does little harm, and two days later the garrison boards it (the source does not say the fleet came to take it off). Shivappa's men walk into an empty fort and watch the sails go north (scenes 9 to 10).

**Resolution (scene 12).** The twelve harvests become the shist. He dies on 25 Sept 1660. Farmers still ask for his rates 140 years later.

## Greatness through an act

- **Scene 11.** The master of the coast seeks out the wandering last emperor of Vijayanagara stands before him with joined palms, gives him the honour due to the Vijayanagara throne, and shelters him. The source, the rival Mysore chronicle quoted by Hayavadana Rao, says "kandu kalgeragi" (fell at his feet); the book shows the homage standing, by the owner's rule, and discloses the wording in `dramatised`. This comes from the rival Mysore chronicle, quoted by Hayavadana Rao.
- **Scene 6.** He gives the Sringeri matha back its lands, though he was a Lingayat king (from a copper grant).

## Title

Recommended: **ಹನ್ನೆರಡು ಸುಗ್ಗಿ, ಒಂದು ಕಡಲು: ಶಿವಪ್ಪ ನಾಯಕ** ("Twelve Harvests and One Sea"). It pairs his two achievements (the shist trial and the coast) in the house pattern.

Alternatives:
- **ಖಾಲಿ ಕೋಟೆ, ತುಂಬಿದ ಕಣಜ** ("The Empty Fort and the Full Granary")
- **ಶಿವಪ್ಪನ ಶಿಸ್ತು** (a pun on ಶಿಸ್ತು as both "discipline" and the assessment). Short, but it hides the coast.
