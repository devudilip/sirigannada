# Visual bible: Keladi, Bidanur and the Kannada coast, 1623 to 1660 (ಶಿವಪ್ಪ ನಾಯಕ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the EXCLUSIONS. The sources are listed below; each line names the source in the research file (data/history-src/research/keladi.md §6 and keladi-shivappa-nayaka-verified.md):

- **Court dress:** Pietro della Valle's 1623 eyewitness account of the Ikkeri court (Hakluyt Society 1892, vol. II). It gives the white garments, the short surcoat, the small almost-square white turbans or caps of cloth of gold, and the gilded daggers (p. 248), plus the gilded canopy and silver-mounted sword (p. 251).
- **Ikkeri:** the bamboo-hedge walls (pp. 244–245).
- **Honavar fort:** on a freestone hill with two churches (pp. 202–203).
- **Mangalore fort:** small and water-bound (p. 301).
- **Gangolli:** the site from Swaminathan (1957) p. 57.
- **The siege:** 8,000 men and ten guns, from Danvers II p. 303.
- **Bidanur:** "bamboo village", walls 8 miles round, palace on a central hill (Rice II pp. 463–464).
- **Temples:** Commons photographs of the Rameshwara temple at Keladi (gandabherunda relief, yali pillars) and the Aghoreshvara temple at Ikkeri. Their licences were not checked.

No contemporary portrait of Shivappa Nayaka is known. The Keladi and Ikkeri votive relief panels of chiefs (Swaminathan's list of plates) were not seen. Every face is therefore a reconstruction, disclosed as AI reconstruction. The modern Shivamogga statue is NOT a reference.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, the Malnad and the Kannada coast of Karnataka in the mid-seventeenth century (1623 to 1660): monsoon forest and tall bamboo on the Western Ghats, green paddy fields and areca gardens, red laterite earth, coconut palms along sandy river mouths and the Arabian Sea. Soft humid light, monsoon cloud, sea haze at dawn, oil lamp and torch light at night; shallow depth of field on people; high detail in white handloom cotton, laterite and granite, teak timber, palm-leaf manuscripts, bronze, iron, wet sand. Colour: paddy green, red laterite, white cotton, sea grey and blue, dull gold. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## SETTINGS (locked)
- **honavar-1623**: The Honavar estuary in 1623: two river arms meeting the sea, a sandy shore with fishing boats and nets, a thick coconut grove with thatched cottages, and on a high hill of freestone a Portuguese fort with a long wall, a gate and the whitewashed front of a small church. Reconstruction from Della Valle II pp. 202-203. Ref: shots/scene-01.png.
- **ikkeri-1623**: Ikkeri in 1623: a city on a plain behind high hedges of thick-planted bamboo instead of walls, long streets shaded by tall trees, tanks, and the granite Aghoreshvara temple. Reconstruction from Della Valle II pp. 244-245.
- **keladi-village**: Keladi near Sagar: green paddy fields on a valley floor, mud-walled houses with clay-tile roofs, areca palms, a small granite temple with a pillared porch (the Rameshwara temple form), forested hills of the ghats behind. Reconstruction. Ref: Commons, Rameshwara Temple in Keladi.jpg. Ref: shots/scene-02.png.
- **sagar-cellar**: An earthen cellar under a timber-floored house at Sagar: low mud walls, rice sacks and clay pots, thin lines of lantern light through the floorboards above. Invented. Ref: shots/scene-03.png.
- **record-hall**: The record hall (kadatada chavadi) of the Bidanur palace: a long timber-pillared room, wooden shelves of palm-leaf bundles tied with red string, a long low wooden table, heavy teak double doors with iron straps, barred wooden windows. Invented. Ref: shots/scene-04.png, scene-12.png.
- **bidanur-hall**: The palace hall at Bidanur (Venupura): carved teak pillars on granite bases, a raised dais with a gilded wooden canopy, oil lamps on brass stands, bamboo-forest hills seen through an open side. Reconstruction (Della Valle's gilded canopy at Ikkeri). Not used by any shot since scene 5 moved to Kandalur; kept for the film.
- **kandalur-camp-hall**: A camp hall of plain timber pillars on granite bases beside a small stone temple at Kandalur, oil lamps on brass stands, a lamp pillar and temple tower through the open side. Invented; Kandalur is the chronicle's place of the handover. Ref: shots/scene-05.png.

- **gangolli-spit**: Gangolli: a long sandy strip between the sea and the wide Kundapura backwater, coconut palms, the siege lines of trenches and coconut-log screens with bronze cannon, and half a mile north of the village a small laterite fort cut off by a canal. ## PERIOD RULES
- **Dress:** white cotton is the court colour (della Valle). Turbans are small and almost square; no Mughal or Maratha headgear on Keladi men.
- **Faith marks:** Lingayat marks for the Keladi house (three ash lines, the linga case); a Vaishnava namam for Sri Ranga.
- **Coastal forts:** red laterite, small. Hilltop Honavar is grey freestone (della Valle). Nothing of Gangolli fort survives, so it is a reconstruction.
- **Guns:** bronze cannon on wooden carriages on both sides. Portuguese matchlocks. No flintlocks.
- **Ships:** Portuguese sailing ships of the 1650s with square sails. No steam, no modern hulls.
- **Bidanur:** a bamboo-forest town on the ghat edge with a palace on a hill; timber and tile, not 19th-century masonry.
- **Records:** palm-leaf bundles tied with string, written with an iron stylus. No paper books and no spectacles.
- **Violence:** fighting is shown only as lines, smoke and distance. No wounds, no bodies.
