# Visual bible: the Hoysala country, 1100–1142 CE (ವಿಷ್ಣುವರ್ಧನ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. Sources: research/hoysala-verified.md §10 (Commons files with licence), research/hoysala.md §6;
Bl 58 (1117) for the court, the horse, the umbrella and the queen's arts; Bl 124 (1133); the Belur and
Halebidu reliefs for dress, crowns, arms, horses and elephants. Where a real depiction exists, match it
and cite it in the prompt file; the faces themselves are invented and disclosed as AI reconstruction
(the Belur reliefs traditionally identified as the king and queen are too small and worn to give a face).

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, 12th-century Hoysala Karnataka (Malnad hills, Mysore
plateau, the Kaveri). Dramatic natural light, morning mist on green hills, river haze, torch smoke at
night, dust of horses on campaign; shallow depth of field on people; high detail in grey-green soapstone,
black stone inscription slabs, cotton, gold and iron. Colour: grey-green and blue-grey soapstone, red
laterite earth, paddy and areca green, white and indigo cotton, dull gold, burnished iron. No text,
captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; reference renders in the video folder bible/characters/)

### vishnuvardhana-prince (scene 2)
Age about 25. Face type, distinct from every earlier lead (Mayura: young, shaven head, beardless;
Pulikeshi: long shoulder curls, short beard, tall kirita; Chavundaraya: square jaw, thick moustache, hair
knot, grey temples; Gangaraja: slim, pointed beard): a broad, wide-cheekboned face, oval rather than
square, a high rounded forehead, large heavy-lidded almond eyes under straight dark brows, a strong
curved (aquiline) nose, full lips, a short close-cropped dark beard with a full moustache turned up at the tips (as on the character sheet), long
straight black hair worn high in a crown-knot. Handsome, strong and heroic: tall, deep-chested, broad
shoulders, muscular arms of a rider and swordsman; warm medium-brown skin. Heavy round gold ear-rings
(kundala) stretching the lobes, a pearl string, armlets, sacred thread, bare chest, a short pleated
lower garment for war with a cloth waist-sash; straight broad sword. No crown.

### vishnuvardhana-king (scenes 3–10)
Same face at 30–45. Tall tiered conical Hoysala crown of gold with jewelled bands, as
on the Belur durbar panel; heavy necklaces in tiers, armlets, wrist-rings, a jewelled waist-belt, long
pleated lower garment with a sash; in court seated at ease with the broad straight sword across the lap.
On campaign: the crown replaced by a gold-banded cloth cap, a round shield, a Kambhoja horse (Bl 58 "the
tramp of his Kambhoja horse"), white umbrella held over him. Expression: open, confident, quick to smile.

### vishnuvardhana-elder (scenes 11–12)
Same face at about 55–60: grey streaks in the hair and moustache, heavier body, deep lines at the eyes;
the same crown; dignified. Never frail or ugly.

### shantala (scenes 9–11)
Age 25–40. Slender and upright, an oval face with a small pointed chin, long dark eyes, fine brows,
a straight nose with a small gold nose-stud; warm light-brown skin; long dark hair in a high side bun
wound with jasmine strings and a jewelled pin (Belur madanika coiffure); a small queen's diadem (Bl 16:
she "assumed the crown"); jewelled girdle, pleated silk lower garment to the ankle, a fitted breast-cloth
with a light scarf (the sculptures are bare above the waist; for the book she is dressed, by house rule),
anklets. Intelligent, amused, commanding. At Belgola in white with few ornaments. Never a stage
Bharatanatyam costume.

### gangaraja (scenes 3–8; keep consistent with the Chavundaraya book, scene 11)
Age about 45 in 1116. Slim, wiry; long face; pointed black beard and moustache; Hoysala-style tall
conical cap with a gold band; white dhoti, gold necklaces; no plume fillet. In battle: a quilted cotton
jacket, round shield, straight sword, on a dark horse. At Belgola: barefoot, hands joined, before a monk.
Reference: history-video/ganga/chavundaraya/bible/characters/gangaraja.json and shots/scene-11.png.
Ages to about 60 by 1133 (death, off-stage).

### ballala (scene 2)
Elder brother, about 30: a gentler, rounder face than Vishnu's, short beard, same family jewellery.

### adiyama (scenes 4–5)
Chola samanta at Talakad, about 45: heavy build, hair in a side knot (Tamil fashion), large gold ear
ornaments, white dhoti, a Chola tiger banner behind him (the tiger as Chola emblem: scholarship, see hoysala.md §3 Story B). Proud, then panicked. Seen mostly at a distance.

### kete-nayaka (scene 5)
A Hattagara shield-bearer: young, broad, a huge round metal-rimmed glittering shield, spear.

### chalukya-camp (scene 7)
Twelve tributary chiefs' tents with their banners; Chalukya boar (varaha) standard at the centre; men in
the same period dress, asleep or rising in confusion.

### prabhachandra and the Belgola monks (scenes 10–11)
Digambara Jain monks: nude, shaven head, peacock-feather whisk, water gourd, seated; shown with tact
(seated, side view, behind a pillar); no close frontal nudity.

### machikabbe (scene 11)
About 60, in white, seated in fast before three monks; gaunt, serene.

### jain-priests (scene 12)
Lay priests (not monks) in white dhotis carrying a covered tray with sandal water and consecrated food.

### lakshmi-mahadevi (scene 12, optional)
The second queen, about 20, glimpsed with a newborn; a different face from Shantala (round face, wide
eyes).

## SETTINGS (locked)

### belur-velapura (scenes 1, 3, 9)
A hill-country town with a timber palace hall (pillared, carved wooden brackets). The Chennakeshava
temple in 1117: a star-shaped plan on a raised platform, walls of grey-green soapstone with dense
friezes, bracket figures under the eaves, Sala-and-tiger groups at the doors, scaffolds of timber and
rope still on the upper parts. NO gopura (Vijayanagara, 1397), NO enclosure wall (later), NO pierced
stone windows (about 1200, Ballala II), NO tank pavilions of later date.

### dorasamudra (scenes 2, 10, 12)
The capital beside a great tank (samudra): earth-and-stone walls, timber halls, temples under
construction from 1121 (Ketamalla's Siva temple), Boppa's Jain basti from 1133. Do not show the finished
Hoysalesvara temple before the 1140s.

### talakad (scenes 4–6)
The Kaveri wide and deep, the old Ganga fort in the bend of the river on its north bank, a Chola camp on
the south bank, old Ganga and Chola brick-and-stone temples, coconut and areca groves; Kirtinarayana
under construction in scene 6. Avoid the great sand dunes of later centuries.

### kannegal (scene 7)
Undulating dry country with rocky outcrops at night; a camp of tents, carts and tethered horses and
elephants.

### belgola (scenes 8, 10–11)
Vindhyagiri with the colossus now enclosed by Gangaraja's new cloister wall (after 1116); Chandragiri with
small plain granite basadis and pillars; Shantala's new basti (1123). Keep consistent with the
Chavundaraya bible's belgola-hills, plus the wall.

### sivaganga (scene 11)
A tall conical rocky hill near Bangalore with Saiva shrines and rock-cut steps; no Jain buildings.

### bankapura (scene 12)
A northern fort in black-soil country; royal tents within the walls.

## PERIOD RULES
- Dress from the Belur and Halebidu reliefs: men bare-chested with jewelled necklaces, armlets,
  ear-rings, sacred thread, pleated lower garments, waist-sashes; royals in tall tiered conical crowns;
  officers in conical caps with gold bands. Women in pleated silks, girdles, high side buns with flowers.
- Arms: straight broad swords and daggers, spears, bows, round shields (Kete-Nayaka's "glittering shield",
  Cn 209); quilted cotton coats at most; no plate armour.
- Horses: Kambhoja horses (Bl 58) with cloth saddles and breast ornaments as on the friezes; elephants
  with cloth housings and bells; white royal umbrella.
- Emblems: Hoysala banner and door groups of Sala stabbing a tiger; Chola tiger banner; Chalukya boar.
  Copper-plate seals with a dead tiger and a rod.
- Script on stone and coin: 12th-century Old Kannada (rounded letters on black stone slabs); abstract
  ancient strokes only, never modern glyphs.
- Faiths side by side: Jain basadis, Vaishnava and Saiva temples in the same town; nobody shown
  destroying a temple.
- Legend scenes: the Sala story only as a sculpture being carved; Ramanuja not shown.

## EXCLUSIONS (paste verbatim)
No Vijayanagara gopura or granite architecture, no Mughal or Maratha turbans, jackets or curved talwars,
no firearms or cannon, no plate armour or helmets with face guards, no stirrups unless copied from the
Halebidu friezes, no modern Kannada letters, no Bharatanatyam stage costume, no Ramanuja figure, no
close frontal nudity of monks or the colossus, no gore in close-up, no sand dunes at Talakad, no finished
Hoysalesvara temple before the 1140s, no pierced stone windows at Belur in 1117.
