# ವಿಷ್ಣುವರ್ಧನ: working outline (book and screenplay share this)

Source spine: the Shravanabelagola records of Gangaraja and Shantala in Rice, Epigraphia Carnatica II
(1889) and Narasimhachar's revision (1923): nos. 73 (59), 125 (45), 240 (90), 118 (44), 132 (56),
143 (53), 384 (144); the Belur records in EC V (1902): Bl 58 and Bl 71 (1117), Bl 124 (1133), Bl 171,
Bl 16, Cn 209; Ml 31 at Tippur (1117) in EC III (1894); Rice's *Mysore and Coorg from the Inscriptions*
(1909) pp. 95–101. Derrett (1957) and Sastri (1958) for the reconstruction only. Research:
research/hoysala.md and research/hoysala-verified.md. Facts: facts.json (38 ids).

Dating: c. 1108 (accession, Derrett), 1116 (Talakad, Rice and Sastri; inferred from 1117 records),
1117 (Bl 58/71, Ml 31; the Kannegal grant), 1121 (Dorasamudra; Shantala in council), 1122–3 (submission
to the Chalukya, Sastri), 1123 (Shantala's basti), 1131 (Shantala's death), 1133 (Gangaraja's death;
Narasimha born), death at Bankapura 1141 / 1142 / c. 1152 (disputed: the prose gives no year).

Title (working): ಹೋರಾಡಿ ತೆಗೆದುಕೊ: ವಿಷ್ಣುವರ್ಧನ ('Fight and Take It': Vishnuvardhana), from
Adiyama's answer at Talakad (f-talakad-adiyama).
Alternative: ಕಾವೇರಿಯ ಕತ್ತಿ, ಬೇಲೂರಿನ ಚೆನ್ನ: ವಿಷ್ಣುವರ್ಧನ.
Subtitle: ತಲಕಾಡುಗೊಂಡ ಹೊಯ್ಸಳ ರಾಜ, ಸು. ೧೧೦೮–೧೧೫೨ (keep the dynasty page's years; the death year is
disputed, see f-death).

Arc in one line: a hill-country king who is still a Chalukya feudatory, with a Jain Brahman general
at his side, takes back the old Ganga capital from the Chola (world, why), wins it by a river and a
night raid (how), builds Belur, loses his queen and his general, and ends as an emperor who bows to
a Jain image on the day his son is born (ending).

Legend rule for this book: two legend passages, both introduced as ಐತಿಹ್ಯ inside the scene and never
illustrated as events: the Sala-and-tiger story (scene 1; the image shows the stone group being carved
at a temple door, i.e. the court making its emblem) and the Ramanuja conversion (scene 9; told as what
the Srivaishnavas of Melukote say, no image of Ramanuja). Ramanuja stays off-stage. Shantala's
"sallekhana", dancer persona and madanika-model story are NOT used.

## ಪೀಠಿಕೆ
Malnad hill chiefs who called themselves champions of the Malepas (f-hill-chiefs), feudatories of the
Chalukya emperor at Kalyani (f-chalukya-feudatory), and a Chola governor sitting since 1004 in the old
Ganga capital, now called Rajarajapura (f-chola-talakad). The voice: Gangaraja's own Belgola stones, the
Belur stone of 1117, a queen's epitaph on Chandragiri, and a Jain image named by a Vaishnava king.

## ಸಾರಾಂಶ
Ten lines: hill feudatory, Talakad 1116, Kannegal, Belur 1117, the queen and the general, the Jain image.

## ನಿಮಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತೇ?
f-kaveri-corpses (the south wind and the skulls), f-gangaraja-titles (the seven hells), f-shantala-basti
(a temple named after an insult to co-wives), f-name (the name in 1098), f-belur-1117 (Saturday on stone,
Sunday on copper), f-gangaraja-death (the Vaishnava king names a Jain god), f-sala-variants (the hare that
chased a tiger), f-first-poysala.

## ಇದು ಕಥೆ, ಇದು ಇತಿಹಾಸ
All dialogue except the quoted lines ("Fight and take it", "Let go!", "I am delighted ... ask", "The queen
has attained to godhead ...", "By the merit of the consecration ..."); the workshop, the coronation pot,
the envoy, the mint, the tent at Bankapura; the order and year of Kannegal; Adiyama and Dama as two men;
Shantala at the Belur rite; the two legends (Sala, Ramanuja), told as ಐತಿಹ್ಯ; every face (the Belur
relief identifications are traditional); the death year.

## ಇಂದು ನೋಡಬಹುದು

(Dorasamudra; Boppa's Parsvanatha basti); Talakad (Kirtinarayana, excavated from the sand);
Shravanabelagola (Gangaraja's enclosure and lines under the colossus; Shantala's Savatigandhavarana basti
and No. 143 on Chandragiri); Sivaganga hill; Rice's EC II and EC V online.
