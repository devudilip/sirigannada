# Visual bible: Veera Ballala II, Hoysala Karnataka and the Gadag plains, 1173–1220 CE (ಇಮ್ಮಡಿ ವೀರಬಲ್ಲಾಳ)

Every prompt pastes the STYLE LINE, the relevant CHARACTER and SETTING blocks verbatim, and the
EXCLUSIONS. The series bible for the grandfather (hoysala/vishnuvardhana/bible/style.md) is the base for
dress, crowns, arms and emblems; this book is two generations later and moves north to the black-soil
plains around Gadag and Lakkundi. Sources: data/history-src/research/hoysala-veera-ballala-ii-verified.md
§9 (Commons files checked), Bl 112 (EC V pp. 71–74) for Lakshma, his wife's todar and the pillar, Dg 25
(EC XI p. 34) for the Seuna cavalry's "high saddles and jewelled breast-plates", Ak 5 (EC V p. 114) for
Lokkigundi's "high ramparts, lofty bastions, mounted with astonishing flag-staves", Fleet 1896 p. 71 for
the Seuna golden Garuda banner, EC VI Intro p. 18 for Uchchangi. No likeness of any person in this book
survives; every face is invented and disclosed as AI reconstruction.

## STYLE LINE (paste verbatim)
Cinematic photorealistic historical still, 16:9, Hoysala Karnataka and the north Karnataka plains in the late 12th and early 13th century (the Malnad hills, the Mysore plateau, the dry rocky black-soil country around Gadag). Dramatic natural light, monsoon rain and morning mist in the hills, red and ochre dust on the northern plains, torch smoke at night; shallow depth of field on people; high detail in grey-green soapstone, black stone, granite boulders, cotton, silk, gold and iron. Colour: grey-green soapstone, red laterite and black cotton soil, paddy and areca green, white and indigo cotton, maroon silk, dull gold, burnished iron. No text, captions, logos or watermarks. Not a painting, not cartoon, not watercolor, not a film-poster look.

## CHARACTERS (locked; json blocks in bible/characters/)

### ballala (lead; sheet bible/characters/ballala-sheet.png)
Face type, checked against every earlier lead and against the books being made alongside it.
Mayurasharma: shaven head. Pulikeshi II: curls, short beard. Vikramaditya II: heart-shaped face, broad
cheekbones, cleft chin, clean-shaven. Amoghavarsha: long oval face, thin moustache, long loose hair.
Govinda III: broad face, upswept pointed moustache, long sideburns, topknot, Roman nose, scar through the
left eyebrow. Chavundaraya: square jaw, thick moustache, hair knot. Vishnuvardhana: round face, short
dense beard. Vikramaditya VI: receding hairline, close beard. Harihara: long full beard, receding
hairline. Bukka: full wavy beard, long loose hair. Krishnadevaraya: clean-shaven oval, kullayi cap.
Kanthirava: broad face, moustache curled into loops, eyebrow scar. Chikkadevaraja: turban, thin arched
moustache, chin-beard. Shivappa Nayaka: long lean face, deep-set narrow eyes, hooked nose, drooping
moustache. Shivaji: pointed full beard. Ballala's own marks: widow's peak with hair swept back, long
narrow hollow-cheeked face, wide-open almond eyes, a full straight moustache trimmed level, no beard, no
scar, short sideburns. (The scar and the upturned moustache of the first draft were dropped because they
repeated Govinda III and Kanthirava.)
Ballala, the Hoysala king: tall, rangy and sinewy, long-limbed with hard rider's shoulders and corded forearms, never heavy; a long narrow face with an angular jaw and lean hollow cheeks under high angular cheekbones; a long straight nose with a high bridge; long wide-open almond-shaped dark eyes under straight level brows; a pronounced widow's-peak hairline with black hair swept straight back; clean-shaven cheeks and chin, a full straight black moustache trimmed level at the corners of the mouth (not curled, not upswept, not drooping), short neat sideburns ending above the ear-lobe; no scar; deep dark-brown skin.
- Young, about 20 (sc-01 to sc-04): here about 20, smooth-faced and keen; hair tied in a high knot at the back with no crown, heavy round gold ear-rings, a single pearl string and a gold necklace, gold armlets, sacred thread across the bare chest, a short pleated white lower garment for riding with an indigo waist-sash holding a straight broad sword; handsome, alert, never boyish or soft
- Coronation (sc-02): water is poured on his bare head (hair in a knot, no crown; an attendant holds the crown on a gold tray), then here about 20; a tall tiered conical gold Hoysala crown with jewelled bands and a back-plate (as on the Belur durbar panel), large jewelled ear-discs, layered pearl and gold necklaces, broad gold armlets and bracelets, a jewelled belt with hanging tassels over a long pleated silk lower garment in white and gold, a sash, bare feet; handsome and grave
- On campaign, about 22 (sc-03, sc-04): here about 22; a short conical gold war-crown with a single jewelled band, heavy gold ear-rings, gold necklaces over a quilted white cotton war-jacket open at the chest, gold armlets, an indigo waist-sash, a short pleated lower garment, a straight broad sword with a lotus pommel; dust on his arms and face; handsome, fierce, upright
- King, about 40 (cover, sc-08 to sc-10): here about 40, the first grey threads in the moustache, fine lines at the eyes; a short conical gold war-crown with a jewelled band, heavy gold ear-rings, layered gold necklaces over a quilted white cotton war-jacket open at the chest, broad gold armlets, an indigo waist-sash, a short pleated lower garment, a straight broad sword with a lotus pommel; handsome, hard, commanding
- Elder, about 65 (sc-11): here about 65, the hair at the temples and the level moustache white, deep lines at the eyes and mouth, still lean, tall and upright, never frail; the tall tiered conical gold Hoysala crown with jewelled bands, large jewelled ear-discs, layered pearl and gold necklaces, a white shawl over one shoulder, a long pleated white and gold silk lower garment; dignified and kingly
Ages are assumptions: crowned 1173 at about 20 (his father was then in his 40th year, Derrett p. 79), so about 37 at Soratur and about 65 when he sends Narasimha south (c. 1218); EC V Intro p. xxiii has him at least 55 at death in 1220.

### lakshma (companion; sheet bible/characters/lakshma-sheet.png)
Lakshma, the Hoysala general: compact and powerfully built, shorter than the king, broad-shouldered and thick-necked with a wrestler's arms; a broad square-browed face, a short full black beard trimmed close all round joined to a moustache, close-cropped tight curly black hair, a broad straight nose, wide-set calm dark eyes, warm medium-brown skin; a small gold pendant of Garuda with spread wings on a black cord at his throat.
- Boy (sc-02): a small boy of about seven, Lakshma: sturdy, close-cropped tight curly black hair, a broad square-browed face, wide-set dark eyes, warm medium-brown skin, a small gold Garuda pendant on a black cord, a white waistcloth and a little gold armlet; wide-eyed and serious.
- General, about 30 (sc-08): conical red cloth cap with a gold band, quilted indigo jacket, dagger and sword.
- Recorded marks (Bl 112, EC V p. 73): a gold todar ring and a pearl anklet (pende) on his LEFT ankle, given by the king; shown in sc-08 and sc-12.
- About 55 (sc-12): grey beard, bare-headed, plain white, no weapon, the todar and pende on the left ankle.

### umadevi (sc-06)
the senior queen Umadevi, about 35: tall and upright, a long oval face with a high forehead, strong straight brows, large dark eyes and a steady gaze, a straight nose with a small gold nose stud, a firm mouth, medium-brown skin; black hair in a high side bun wound with jasmine strings and a jewelled pin (the Belur bracket-figure coiffure) and a small gold queen's diadem; a long pleated deep-maroon silk lower garment with a gold border, a fitted breast-cloth and a light gold-edged shoulder scarf, a jewelled girdle, armlets, layered necklaces, anklets; commanding, intelligent, never sexualised.

### bhillama (sc-05)
the Seuna king Bhillama of Devagiri, about 45: a big-framed powerful man, a broad face, a full rounded black beard and moustache with grey streaks, heavy brows, a proud level gaze, warm wheat-brown skin; a tall conical gold crown, large gold ear-rings, layered gold necklaces, broad armlets, a red silk sash over a white pleated lower garment, a straight sword at the hip; mounted upright on a tall bay horse with a high-cantled saddle and a jewelled breast-strap; strong and dignified, never sneering.

### narasimha (sc-11)
the prince Narasimha, about 35: his father's long narrow face and widow's peak but fuller cheeks and a softer jaw, a thin black moustache only, warm dark-brown skin; a short conical gold war-crown, gold ear-rings, a quilted white cotton war-jacket over gold necklaces, an indigo sash, a straight sword; mounted on a grey horse with a cloth saddle and a breast ornament; eager and serious.

### pandya-king (sc-04)
the Pandya king of Uchchangi, about 40: heavy-shouldered and strong, hair in a high side knot, a thick moustache and a short black beard, large gold ear-discs, a gold-banded crown-cap, gold necklaces, a white and gold dhoti, dust and sweat on his chest, a straight sword; standing fully upright with his head high, proud in defeat.

### suggala (sc-12)
Lakshma's wife Suggala, about 50: a calm round face, grey-streaked black hair in a low bun, a plain white pleated cotton garment and shoulder cloth, a few gold ornaments, a plain gold chain-ring anklet (todar) on her left ankle only; serene and steadfast. The todar is recorded (Bl 112); its metal is not (gold is a guess).

### priest (sc-02)
an elderly priest: shaven head with a tuft, white dhoti and shoulder cloth, sacred thread, sandal-paste marks, pouring water from a gold vessel.

### seuna-host (sc-05, sc-07, sc-09)
the Seuna army: masses of infantry with long spears, bows and round shields, white and red cotton, bare heads or cloth wraps (no metal helmets), cavalry on tall horses with high-cantled saddles and jewelled breast-straps, a few armoured war elephants with cloth housings and bells, many red banners each bearing a golden Garuda figure with spread wings.

### hoysala-host
Hoysala soldiers: spearmen and swordsmen with round shields, bare heads or cloth wraps (no metal helmets), white and indigo cotton, quilted jackets on the officers, cloth banners: the Hoysala tiger banner: a leaping tiger with the hero Sala stabbing it, as on the Belur emblem sculpture.

### war-elephant (cover, sc-09)
a great tusked war elephant with a red and gold cloth housing, bells and a jewelled forehead plate, a mahout at its neck with a goad, the rider seated on a padded cloth saddle behind him (no howdah, no canopy), as on the Halebidu friezes.

## PERIOD RULES
- Dress from the Belur and Halebidu reliefs: men bare-chested with jewelled necklaces, armlets, ear-rings,
  sacred thread, pleated lower garments, waist-sashes; royals in tall tiered conical crowns, on campaign a
  short conical gold war-crown; officers in conical cloth caps with gold bands; quilted cotton war-jackets
  at most. Women in pleated silks, girdles, high side buns with flowers, dressed above the waist.
- Arms: straight broad swords and daggers, spears, bows, round shields. Dg 25's "thunderbolts" are NOT
  shown as firearms (no evidence of gunpowder in 12th-century Karnataka).
- Horses: cloth saddles and breast ornaments; the Seuna cavalry with high-cantled saddles and jewelled
  breast-straps (Dg 25). No stirrups.
- Elephants: cloth housings, bells, forehead plate, mahout at the neck, rider on a padded saddle; no howdah.
- Emblems: Hoysala banners with a tiger (Bl 112, EC V p. 71, records the tiger on the flag); Sala
  stabbing it is a guess from the temple emblem, and so are the colours. Seuna banners
  with a golden Garuda (Fleet p. 71; red cloth is a guess). No crescent anywhere.
- Script on stone: abstract rounded Old Kannada strokes, never legible or modern.
- The Garuda pillar is shown only as a monument with two calm people before it; never the self-sacrifice.

no gunpowder, firearms, cannon or rockets; no Mughal, Maratha or Vijayanagara dress, turbans, jamas or curved talwars; no crescent or star on any flag; no gopurams, domes or Vijayanagara granite halls; no stirrups; no plate armour, chain mail or helmets with face guards; no howdah canopy on any elephant; no modern objects, roads, wires or signboards; no legible text and no modern Kannada letters; no gore, severed limbs or blood in close-up; no king kneeling, grovelling, fleeing or humiliated; no belly or weak look on the king; no film-poster look.
